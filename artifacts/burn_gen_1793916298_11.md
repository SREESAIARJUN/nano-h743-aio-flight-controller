## 0. Architecture and Baseline Assumptions

**Architectural point: 48 kHz PWM does not run on the H743.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and performs commutation. The H743 only:
- sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs, and
- receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In each 60° commutation sector, exactly two legs carry the current:

| Leg | Behavior | Conduction share |
|---|---|---|
| PWM leg | High-side on for D; synchronous low-side on for (1−D) | 100 % of the sector |
| Static leg | Low-side on for the full sector | 100 % of the sector |

The current therefore always flows through two channel resistances in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2I^2R_{hot}$$

At I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.8\text{ m}\Omega) = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Per-FET loss**

- Each FET conducts during 2 of the 6 sectors, either as the PWM leg or the static leg.
- Time-averaged loss per FET: 23.0 / 6 ≈ **3.84 W**.
- Instantaneous loss on the static low-side: (80)²(1.8 mΩ) = **11.5 W**. This matters for die-level Z_th (see Section 4.2).

**Copper path loss**

Assume shunt plus traces total R_pcb ≈ 0.5 mΩ per path:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) rises with T_j, so the true operating point satisfies:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0059/\text{K}$$

The system is stable while $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot R_{\theta} < 1$. With roughly 23 W, α·P ≈ 0.10 W/K. The die-local loop gain is about 0.1 × 1.5 ≈ 0.15, which is stable. The **board-level** loop is not stable in steady state, which is why the 80 A rating must be treated as a burst rating (Section 4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Use V = 25.2 V as the worst case.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | **1.452 W** |
| Body-diode conduction during dead time | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k | **1.229 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, 10 V) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.048 W (in the driver, not the FET) |
| **Total switching (FETs)** | | **≈ 2.75 W** |

**Design levers**

- **Dead time is about 45 % of switching loss.** Reducing it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per ESC. First verify shoot-through margin using t_d(off),max + t_f,max of the driver and FET.
- **Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw.** At 24 kHz it falls to about 0.73 W, at the cost of more current ripple. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance racing motors.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 (all motors at burst) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Switching | 2.75 W | 11.0 W |
| Copper and shunt | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **29.3 W** | **≈ 117 W** |

**Electrical efficiency at full burst:** P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 with sag, that is about 1.6 kW per motor, so the ESC stage is about **98.2 % efficient**. The losses are small relative to throughput but enormous relative to the board's thermal mass.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped board model (burst)

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{ba}}$$

**Thermal capacitance, C_th**

- FR4 at about 3.5 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 3.9 J/K
- Copper at about 4 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.5 J/K
- FETs and other components ≈ 0.6 J/K
- **Total C_th ≈ 6 J/K**

**Board-to-air resistance, R_ba**

- Area: A = 2 × (0.0305)² = 1.86 × 10⁻³ m²
- Convection coefficient in propwash: h ≈ 100–250 W/m²K
- R_ba = 1/(hA) ≈ **2.2–5.4 K/W**

**Steady-state check:** 117 W × 2.2 K/W = 257 K rise. That is physically impossible, so **80 A per motor is a burst-only rating.**

**Burst duration.** The time constant τ = R·C ≈ 13–32 s, much longer than a burst, so the response is near-adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_b \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}} = \frac{117}{6}\,t = 19.5\ \text{K/s}$$

From T_b0 = 50 °C to a board limit of 110 °C:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{60}{19.5} \approx \mathbf{3.1\ s\ (all\ 4\ motors)}$$

A single motor at burst gives about 12 s.
