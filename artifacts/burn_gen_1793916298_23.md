## 0. Architecture Review: Two Issues Before the Math

**0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
In a flight controller with an integrated 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot commands. Each motor channel (or one shared MCU) runs ESC firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture below reflects this split.

**0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally unrealistic.**
Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm board at 4×80 A heats at roughly 60 K/s, which is unsurvivable beyond about 1 s. The model below quantifies this so you can choose a realistic option:

- derate the specification, or
- move to a 30×30 mm board with paralleled FETs, or
- define "burst" strictly, for example 80 A on one motor for 1 s or less.

## 2. Loss Model (per channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification in six-step operation, the current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- the active high-side or its complementary low-side on the PWM leg, plus
- the static low-side on the return leg.

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Here α ≈ 0.005–0.007 K⁻¹, which gives k_T ≈ 1.4 at about 100 °C.

$$R_{ds}(100^\circ C) = 1.6 \times 1.4 = 2.24\text{ mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.24\text{ mΩ} = 6400 \times 4.48\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{28.7\text{ W}}$$

That is 14.3 W per conducting FET.

Rds(on) depends on temperature, which depends on loss, so this is a positive-feedback loop. Solve it iteratively, or check for thermal runaway with:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = I^2\,2R_{25}\alpha \approx 0.13\text{ W/K}$$

The system is stable while this is smaller than 1/θ_ja,eff.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (only the PWM leg switches)

**V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.968\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (dissipated mainly in the turning-on FET):
$$P_{rr} \approx Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.015\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.307\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{ n} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.038\text{ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.36\text{ W/channel}}$$

### 2.3 Shunt and Copper Loss

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m} = 1.6\text{ W}$$

Copper loss, for each current path segment:
$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \frac{\rho_{Cu}\,\ell}{w\,t\,N_{layers}}$$

Example: a 10 mm path, 3 mm wide, 4 layers of 2 oz (70 µm) copper gives about 0.2 mΩ, so roughly 1.3 W. Include this term; it is not negligible.
