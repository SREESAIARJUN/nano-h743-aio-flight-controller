## 3. Thermal and Loss Model

### 3.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation each phase leg conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical. The high-side FET of a leg carries the motor current I_m for duty D of its 1/3 window. With synchronous rectification, the low-side of the same leg carries it for (1−D).

Per-FET RMS currents:

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_m\sqrt{\frac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I_m\sqrt{\frac{1-D}{3}} + \text{(low-side commutating partner: } I_m/\sqrt{3}\text{)}$$

Simplified worst case at full throttle (D → 1): at any instant exactly two FETs conduct I_m.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With R_DS(on)(110°C) = 0.70 × 1.5 = 1.05mΩ:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per-FET I_rms | 80/√3 = 46.2A |
| Per-FET P_cond | 46.2² × 1.05m = **2.24W** |
| Per motor (2 × I²R) | 2 × 6400 × 1.05m = **13.4W** |
| 4 motors | **53.8W** |

**FOC variant:** sinusoidal phase current gives I_ph,rms = I_peak/√2. Per-FET RMS is about I_peak/2, so per-motor loss ≈ 3 × (I_pk/√2)² × R. The loss is comparable at equal torque.

**PCB copper loss.** Bus and phase traces add loss on top of the FETs. For 2oz copper (70µm), ρ_Cu(80°C) = 2.1×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, and a 10mm path × 6mm wide × 2 layers:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w t n} = \frac{2.1\times10^{-8}\times0.01}{0.006\times70\times10^{-6}\times2} = 0.25\,m\Omega$$

This gives about 1.6W per motor at 80A. Do not ignore it: it is a large share of the FET loss.

### 3.2 Switching Loss at 48kHz

Only the PWM-switching (high-side) FET in each motor sees hard switching. The synchronous low-side sees dead-time diode conduction and Q_rr.

**(a) V–I overlap.** Assume t_r = 20ns and t_f = 15ns, set by gate resistors:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(tr)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{k} = 0.023\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** of the synchronous FET body diode:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.073\text{ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature, so budget ×2: **0.15W**.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, with V_F = 0.8V and 2 edges × 100ns:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_m (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
