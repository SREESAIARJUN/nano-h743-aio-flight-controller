# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 80A-Burst ESC (Job #35): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Map and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

The analysis forces three decisions up front.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run field-oriented or six-step control on the flight controller. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by four dedicated ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them and receives eRPM telemetry. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports.
2. **"80A per channel" in a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~28×28 mm board) is a burst rating only.** The math below shows:
   - total burst dissipation of about 80 W,
   - an adiabatic burst limit of about 5 s,
   - a realistic continuous rating of about 30–35 A per channel.
3. **Pack current at 4 × 80 A and high duty approaches 320 A.** That stresses the connector, pads, shunt and battery leads beyond what nano boards normally carry. Size the input path for burst duration, not for current alone.

All component values below are representative design-intent parameters. Replace them with the datasheet maximums of the parts you actually procure, then validate on a thermal-camera bench before flight.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_DS = 1.45 mΩ.

### 2.2 Current paths (trapezoidal, synchronous complementary PWM)

During each 60° sector, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:**
  - the high-side FET conducts for duty D,
  - the low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2R_{DS}\,[D+(1-D)]+I^2R_{DS}=2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification eliminates diode freewheeling loss.
