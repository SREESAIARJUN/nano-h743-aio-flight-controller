## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step commutation
At any instant, current flows through exactly **two** switching positions in series: one high-side path and one low-side path.

- When the HS is PWM'd with duty D, the high-side position is the HS FET for a fraction D of the time.
- For the remaining (1−D), it is the complementary LS FET of the same leg, which is synchronously rectifying.
- The other leg's LS FET conducts 100% of the sector.

The conducting resistance is therefore always ≈ 2·R_ds(on), independent of D (ignoring dead-time intervals).

**Per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

With flat-topped trapezoidal current, I_ph,rms in the conducting interval ≈ I_pk.

**Per FET** (each FET is in the current path 1/3 of the electrical period, on average):

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point
Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve P(T_j) and T_j(P) together:

$$T_j = T_a + Z_{th}(t)\cdot P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + Z_{th} P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - Z_{th} P_0 \alpha}$$

Here P_0 = 2I²R_ds,25 and the denominator uses the per-FET share of Z_th.

**Thermal-runaway condition:** Z_th · P_0 · α ≥ 1. This is your hard limit for burst duration.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.56 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 3.71 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W** | **4.10 W** |

### 1.4 Copper losses (often forgotten)
On a 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner stack, assume ~0.25 mΩ effective per path (bus, phase pad, vias).

- P_Cu ≈ 80² × 0.25 m ≈ **1.6 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET in its sector switches ZVS-like after dead time but contributes diode and Q_rr loss.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_g} = \frac{10-3.5}{15} = 0.433\ \text{A} \Rightarrow t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 23\ \text{ns}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_g} = \frac{3.5}{12} = 0.292\ \text{A} \Rightarrow t_{off} = 34\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components (per channel)

| Term | Equation | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f | ½·25.2·80·57 n·48 k = **2.76 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 0.03 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr(I, di/dt)·V·f (use 3× datasheet at 80 A) | ~0.11 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f | 2·0.8·80·300 n·48 k = **1.84 W** |
| Gate drive (all 6 FETs, averaged) | Q_g·V_drv·f·(switching count) | ~0.03 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 4.8 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Cutting dead time to 150 ns halves the diode term.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves everything in this table but raises current ripple.
