## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Key Correction

**Partitioning (important).** The STM32H743 does **not** drive the gates. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated ESC MCU per motor, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU generates its own 48 kHz complementary PWM, does commutation and BEMF sensing, and returns eRPM telemetry over the bidirectional DShot line. The H743 only sources DShot600 and captures the replies.

> ⚠️ **6S with 40 V FETs:** this is common practice but has thin margin. Ringing on a 25.2 V bus can exceed 35 V. Mandatory mitigations are a TVS (SMBJ28A/33A class), ≥4× local MLCC per bridge, and a low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads. For more margin, use 60 V parts and accept roughly 1.5× R_DS(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D and the low side (synchronous) for 1−D. The leg conducts current 100 % of the time.
- **Static leg:** the low side is on for 100 % of the sector.

Per motor, therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{HS} D + I_{ph}^2 R_{LS}(1-D) + I_{ph}^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of D, which is why burst current, not throttle, sets conduction loss.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.3825 ≈ **2.07 mΩ** (use 2.1 mΩ).

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.1\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$
