## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Clarifications

Two architectural points shape everything that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and *emits* bidirectional DShot600. Four (or one shared quad) dedicated ESC MCUs perform 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running Bluejay. I specify **4× AT32F421 running AM32** because the dedicated per-motor MCU decouples ESC faults and is well supported for bidirectional DShot.

2. **The form factor and current rating conflict physically.** A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot sink 4×80 A. The math in §3 shows this. I therefore specify a **30.5×30.5 mm** board and treat 80 A as a **per-motor burst of ≤ 3 s**. The continuous rating is derived in §3.6.

**Baseline parameters.** All values are stated so they can be swapped for your actual BOM.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current (per motor) | I_pk | 80 A |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

During any 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats. With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D:

- PWM leg: the high-side conducts for D, and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D − 2t_dt·f_sw).
- Return leg: the low-side conducts 100% of the sector.

Ignoring the small dead-time fraction, the leg current I flows through exactly **two channels at all times**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For a burst at full throttle, motor current ripple at 48 kHz is small relative to 80 A. With motor L ≈ 15 µH: ΔI ≈ V/(4·L·f) ≈ 22.2/(4·15e-6·48e3) ≈ 7.7 A pk-pk, so I_rms ≈ I_avg·√(1 + (ΔI/I)²/12) ≈ 80.03 A, and I treat I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 2.3 Self-consistent junction temperature (fixed-point iteration)

Per-FET average dissipation is roughly P_motor/6, since each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The worst-case *instantaneous* FET loss is I²R plus switching loss, while that FET is the active one.

Iterating T_j = T_board + P_FET,inst · R_θJC, starting from T_board = 85 °C:

| Iter | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_inst/FET = I²R (W) | ΔT_jc (K) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 85 | 1.143 | 7.32 | 8.8 |
| 1 | 93.8 | 1.179 | 7.54 | 9.1 |
| 2 | 94.1 | 1.180 | 7.55 | 9.1 (converged) |

For margin I adopt **R_DS,hot = 1.35 mΩ**, which covers T_j ≈ 135 °C late in a burst.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{17.28\ W}$$

### 2.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses (often ignored, and they are significant)

- **Phase/bus copper.** 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner, with 4 layers paralleled. The effective path is ~0.25 mΩ per motor across two conducting legs: 80² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.6 W**.
- **Current shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt in the common return gives 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W** while it carries current. I recommend sensing on the *battery* side (one 0.1 mΩ shunt with an INA240A2), where average current is lower than phase current.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (the PWM leg high-side only)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 35\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The recovery current also adds to the turn-on overlap. A rule of thumb is I_rr·t_rr/2 extra, which is about 0.1 W here.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot40\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns (AM32 `dead_time` setting, validated on the scope against shoot-through) halves it.
