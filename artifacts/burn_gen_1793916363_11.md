**Scope.** This document covers system architecture, the 80 A burst loss model, the 48 kHz switching loss model, the thermal transient model, the bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, schematic netlists, and design rules.

## 0. Architecture Corrections

These issues change the design, so they come first.

1. **ArduPilot cannot be the ESC firmware.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each motor bridge needs its own commutation MCU (e.g., STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230) running AM32 or Bluejay-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The resulting architecture is **1× H743 + 4× ESC MCUs**, either stacked or on a combined PCB.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) at 80 A per channel is only a short-burst rating.** Section 3 shows roughly 90 W of total dissipation at burst against roughly 6 W of sustainable cooling. Burst duration is limited to about 1.5–2 s. A continuous rating of 80 A is not achievable at this size.
3. **H743 packaging.** LQFP100 (14×14 mm) leaves almost no room on a 20×20 board. Use TFBGA100 (8×8 mm), or split into a 20×20 two-board stack (FC + ESC).
4. **6S with 40 V FETs at 80 A has a real avalanche risk** because of loop-inductance overshoot (Section 2.5). The recommendation is 60 V FETs, or ≤4S operation with 40 V parts.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

With six-step commutation and complementary PWM, two legs carry current at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high side (HS) during D and through the synchronous-rectification low side (LS) during 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low side is on continuously for the 60° sector.

As a result, two R_ds(on) are always in series with the phase current, independent of duty cycle.
