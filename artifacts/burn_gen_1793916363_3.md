# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Reference Model

This model covers conduction and switching losses, transient thermal limits, the ArduPilot bidirectional DShot timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Two corrections to the brief:**

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor is commutated by the ESC's own MCU (AM32 on STM32G071/AT32F421, or BLHeli_32). The H743 only sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back. The model therefore splits into an **FC domain** (timers and DMA) and an **ESC domain** (MOSFET losses).
2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is a burst-only rating.** The math below shows that sustained 80 A is thermally impossible at this size. The real deliverable is a **burst time limit**, not a continuous rating.

All component values are representative placeholders. Replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts before freezing the design.

## 1. ESC Power Stage Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_ds tempco factor at T_j = 125 °C | k_T | ≈1.6, giving R_hot = 1.92 mΩ |
| Rise + fall time (gate driver dependent) | t_r + t_f | 20 + 20 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body diode V_f | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Q_rr / Q_oss | | 50 nC / 40 nC |
| Total gate charge, gate drive voltage | Q_g, V_gs | 60 nC, 10 V |

The bridge uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

## 2. Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly **two** FETs. During the on-time it flows through the high side of one phase and the low side of another. During freewheel it flows through two low sides. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2(80)^2(1.92\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.6\ W/channel}$$

**Per-FET loss.** Each FET conducts during a 120° electrical window, 1/3 of the time:

- Peak during its window: I² · R_hot = 12.3 W
- Averaged over an electrical cycle (electrical periods are sub-millisecond, much shorter than the FET thermal time constant): P_cond/6 · 2 ≈ **4.1 W per FET** (worst-case FETs ≈ 5 W)

**Copper loss.** Add this separately. 80 A through roughly 0.3 mΩ of 2 oz pours, vias, and solder pads adds about 2 W per channel. This is easy to underestimate.

## 3. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg contributes switching loss.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40n·48k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8·80·200n·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | V · Q_oss · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈2.7 W/channel** |

**Effect of switching frequency.** Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about +1.3 W per channel. In exchange you get lower current ripple and quieter motors. At 80 A, switching loss is only about 10% of total loss, because conduction dominates.

### Per-channel and stack totals at 80 A

$$P_{ch} \approx 24.6 + 2.7 + 2.0 \approx 29\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{stack} = 4 \times 29 \approx \mathbf{117\ W}$$

The stack figure assumes all four motors are at 80 A simultaneously, which means 320 A from the battery. That only happens in an unrealistic full-punch scenario, but it is the correct worst case for design.

## 4. Thermal Model and Burst Limit

### Lumped two-node model

```
P_fet ──[R_th,jc + R_th,cb]── T_board ──[R_th,ba]── T_amb
                                  │
                              C_board
```

- **R_th,jc:** about 1.5 K/W (DFN exposed pad)
- **R_th,cb:** about 8 K/W, from pad through via array into inner planes (≥ 12 × 0.3 mm filled vias per FET)
- **C_board:** about 6 g of PCB, copper, and parts at c ≈ 0.7 J/(g·K), so C ≈ **4.2 J/K**
- **R_th,ba:** with prop-wash convection, h ≈ 80 W/m²K over about 8 cm² gives roughly 15 K/W. This is negligible during a burst, which makes the burst essentially **adiabatic**.

### Board heating rate

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{P_{stack}}{C} = \frac{117}{4.2} \approx \mathbf{28\ K/s}$$

### Junction offset above board

$$\Delta T_{jb} = P_{fet}(R_{jc} + R_{cb}) \approx 5 \times 9.5 \approx \mathbf{48\ K}$$

### Maximum burst duration

$$t_{burst} = \frac{T_{j,max} - T_{board,0} - \Delta T_{jb}}{P_{stack}/C}$$

With T_j,max = 150 °C (derated from 175 °C) and T_board,0 = 45 °C:

$$t_{burst} = \frac{150 - 45 - 48}{28} \approx \mathbf{2.0\ s}\ \text{(all four channels at 80 A)}$$

With a single channel at 80 A (P ≈ 29 W, about 7 K/s board rise), the limit is about **8 s**.

### Iterate for R_ds temperature dependence

R_ds rises with temperature, so solve iteratively:

$$R(T) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /K$$

At a 45 °C start, losses are about 25% lower than the hot-case numbers, so the iterated limit is about **2.3 s**.

### Continuous rating sanity check

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{(T_{j} - T_{amb})/R_{total}}{2 R_{hot}}}$
