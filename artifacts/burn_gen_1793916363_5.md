## 0. Architectural Decisions and Findings

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and contains no sensorless BLDC commutation. The correct architecture is:
   - H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× bidirectional DShot600 → **four ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware.
   - Candidate ESC MCUs: STM32G071 or AT32F421. Each drives one 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.
2. **A nano board cannot sustain 80 A per motor.** On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 80 A per motor is a short burst rating only (≈0.5–1.5 s, derived in §3.4). The thermally sustainable continuous rating is ≈18–25 A per motor. The ESC firmware must enforce I²t limiting. The math below shows why.
3. **DMA is not the bottleneck on the H7.** The DMAMUX lets any request map to any of the 16 DMA1/DMA2 streams, so the problem becomes a stream budget rather than a fixed-mapping conflict (§4.5).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current. With complementary PWM on the "PWM leg" and the opposite leg's low-side held ON:

- **PWM ON (duty D):** current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B. Two FETs are in the path.
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** current freewheels LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B. Two FETs are in the path.

So **exactly two channels conduct the phase current at all times**, independent of duty:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

The phase inductance L_ph produces triangular ripple:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

Worked example, a typical 5" motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, at D = 0.5 and V_bemf ≈ 12.6 V:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\,\text{A}
\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6.4\,\text{A}^2
$$

This is negligible against 80² = 6400 A² (0.1 %). At 80 A, I_rms ≈ I_avg.

### 2.3 Temperature-coupled solution

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on loss, so the problem is a fixed-point iteration:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\, R_{\theta},\qquad R_{DS}=R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

For burst sizing, evaluate at a hot design point T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS,hot} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1 + 0.0045 \times 100] = 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\ W}
$$
