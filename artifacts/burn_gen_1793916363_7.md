## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Caveats

### 0.1 Architectural clarification

In a 4-in-1 stack, the **STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller**. Each ESC channel is commutated by its own small MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32 target. The H743 never drives gates directly. It emits bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

The 48 kHz figure is therefore the **ESC's** PWM carrier, not an H743 timer.

### 0.2 Feasibility caveat on "80A burst on a nano board"

Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate 4 × 80 A for more than about 1 second. Treat 80 A as a **≤1–2 s, single- or dual-motor transient rating**. Do not treat it as a continuous or all-motor rating. Any marketing that claims otherwise will not survive the thermal math.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS,hot} = 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.4 \times 1.4675 = 2.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd (HS for D·T, LS for (1−D)·T) and phase B's low side is held on. Because the PWM leg is complementary, the motor current always flows through exactly **two** conducting channels, independent of D. Ignoring dead time, which is handled in §2:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS,hot} + I_{ph}^2 R_{shunt} + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}}$$

At I_ph = 80 A:

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET channels | 2 × 6400 × 2.05 mΩ | **26.2 W** |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ per ESC, low-side common) | 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Copper (pads + planes + vias, ~0.3 mΩ path) | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| **Total conduction per ESC** | | **≈ 29.7 W** |

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each of the 6 FETs is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. The split between "held-on low side," "PWM high side," and "freewheel low side" depends on D.

- **Electrical-cycle average per FET:** 26.2 W / 6 ≈ **4.4 W**.
- **Worst-case instantaneous (held-on LS):** I²R = 6400 × 2.05 mΩ = **13.1 W** for 120° of each cycle.

At a 7-pole-pair motor at 30 k rpm, f_e = 3.5 kHz, so the period is 286 µs. That is far below the junction thermal time constant (Z_th,jc(100 µs) ≈ 0.2 K/W). Junction ripple is therefore ≈ 13.1 × 0.2 ≈ 2.6 K, and the **average** model is valid.
