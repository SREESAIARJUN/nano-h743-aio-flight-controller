## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors in this design. Commutation is handled by a separate ESC MCU per channel. The split is:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot master, telemetry decode | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM reply | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 | AM32 |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET (6 per channel) | n/a |

The TFBGA100 package is recommended because the LQFP100 (16×16 mm body including leads) leaves no routing room on a 20×20 mm mount pattern.

**Feasibility verdict, stated up front.** 4 × 80 A simultaneous on a nano-class board is only a **~1–4 second burst** capability, as derived in §3. Do not market it as continuous. The realistic continuous rating is in the 25–35 A per channel range depending on airflow.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through **two** FETs at any instant:

- During ON time (duty D): HS of phase A and LS of phase B conduct.
- During OFF time (1−D): LS of phase A (synchronous rectification) and LS of phase B conduct.

So the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 2.25 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}\ (14.4\ \text{W per conducting FET})$$

Because R_ds rises with temperature, this is a positive-feedback relationship. The self-consistent operating point is found by iterating:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}$$

Thermal runaway occurs if the loop gain d(P·R_θ)/dT_j = P₂₅·α·R_θ approaches 1. Here 9.6 W × 0.005 × 1.5 ≈ 0.07, so the junction itself is stable. The board is not, as shown in §3.

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only the active high-side FET hard-switches. The low side commutates near zero voltage, but its body diode incurs reverse recovery.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40n·48k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50n·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100n·48k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 FETs | **0.048 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.33 W** |

### 2.3 Totals

| Scope | Conduction | Switching | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per channel | 28.8 W | 2.33 W | **31.1 W** |
| 4 channels | 115.2 W | 9.3 W | **≈124.5 W** |

Two observations follow:

- **Conduction dominates (~93%).** Lowering f_sw buys almost nothing at burst current. Lower R_ds(on), or paralleling FETs, is the only real lever.
- **Rule of thumb:** each 0.5 mΩ reduction in R_ds(on),25 removes about 32 W (hot) across the four channels at 80 A.

### 2.4 Efficiency at Burst

Electrical output is approximately 25.2 V × 80 A
