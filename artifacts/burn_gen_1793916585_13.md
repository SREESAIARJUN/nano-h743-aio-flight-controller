## 1. Power Stage Topology

Each ESC channel is a 3-phase half-bridge inverter with six FETs. It is driven with trapezoidal (6-step) commutation and complementary PWM on the active phase (synchronous rectification).

In any commutation sector:
- The high-side FET of phase A switches at duty D.
- The low-side FET of phase A conducts during (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- The low-side FET of phase B is held on.
- Phase C floats (BEMF sensing).

The current path therefore always contains **exactly two conducting FETs in series**, regardless of D.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The hot on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds25} = 1.6 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Because of synchronous rectification, two FETs are in the path at all times (HS or LS of phase A, plus LS of phase B). The duty cycle therefore cancels:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.48\ W}$$

Copper and terminal loss:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}\times 2\ (\text{in + out phase}) = 80^2 \times 0.4\text{m}\Omega \times 2 = \mathbf{5.12\ W}$$

> **Note:** P_Cu is distributed across the board and motor wires. Assume about 50% (≈2.6 W) lands on the PCB.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched edges, 48 kHz)

Use the linear V-I overlap approximation:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Capacitive (Coss) Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2\ (\text{HS + LS node}) = 0.5 \times 1.2\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.037\ W}$$

### 2.5 Reverse Recovery Loss

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then the HS turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}$$
