## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two parts of the brief need clarifying before the math, because they change the model.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Bi-directional DShot600 is a protocol between a flight controller and separate ESC MCUs. If the H743 drove the gates directly, there would be no DShot. The architecture is therefore:

- **H743 (ArduPilot)** sends DShot600 frames to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32) each generate the 48 kHz PWM and commutation for one motor.
- **Gate drivers and FETs** form one 3-phase bridge per motor.

**2. "Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot sustain 4×80A.** The math below shows about 24 W per channel at 80A, which is about 96 W total. On a 20×20 board that is a destructive heat flux within seconds. This design assumes:

- a 30.5×30.5 mm board,
- 8-layer construction with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes,
- with 80A treated as a ≤5 s burst rating, not a continuous one.

If 20×20 is a hard requirement, the burst spec must drop to roughly 45–50A.

**Baseline parameters.** All results scale from these values. Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) = **1.41 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal BLDC with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases carry I_ph. The current path always passes through exactly two FETs:

- the PWM'd leg's high side during D·T_sw, or its synchronous low side during (1−D)·T_sw, and
- the static low side of the return phase.

Because the PWM'd leg's high and low FETs share the PWM period complementarily, the instantaneous conducting-FET count is always 2, independent of duty:

P_cond,FET-path = 2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T_j)

P_cond,PCB = I_ph² · R_pcb

**At 80A, T_j = 100 °C:**

- FET path: 2 × 6400 × 1.41 mΩ = **18.05 W**
- PCB: 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.20 W**
- **Total conduction per ESC: 21.25 W**

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts on average one third of the electrical period, so:

- I_rms,FET = I_ph / √3 = 46.2 A
- P_FET,cond = 46.2² × 1.41 mΩ = **3.01 W average per FET**

The thermal model uses the average because the electrical period is ≤1 ms, which is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant of 10–100 ms.

### 1.4 Thermal runaway stability criterion

Conduction loss rises with temperature, so the loop is stable only if:

(dP/dT_j) · Z_th < 1

where dP/dT_j = I_rms² · R_ds,25 · α = 2134 × 1e-3 × 0.0055 = 0.0117 W/K per FET.

With per-FET θ_j-board of about 4.2 K/W, the loop gain is 0.05, so the junction-to-board loop is stable. The board-level loop with θ ≈ 7 K/W is still below 1 (gain ≈ 0.49 using total ESC dP/dT). It is stable but significantly amplified. The effective board resistance becomes θ/(1−0.49), roughly a 2× multiplier. This is why continuous ratings collapse quickly.
