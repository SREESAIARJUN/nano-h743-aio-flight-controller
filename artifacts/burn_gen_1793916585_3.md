**Architecture note.** The H743 does not drive the bridges. It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and returns eRPM telemetry. The "48 kHz" in the loss model is an ESC-MCU parameter.

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 "nano" footprint with single 3.3×3.3 mm FETs is not survivable beyond a fraction of a second. The numbers below assume a 30.5×30.5 board, or paralleled FETs on 20×20. §4 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\text{–}0.008\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.007:

$$R_{DS,hot} = 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.70 = 1.53\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During any 60° electrical sector, one phase is the PWM leg, one is the return leg (low-side held on), and one floats.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side (synchronous) for (1−D)·T_sw. The leg therefore always carries current through exactly one FET.
- **Return leg:** one low-side FET conducts 100 % of the sector.

So there are always two series FETs in the motor current path, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS,hot}$$

Assuming flat-topped trapezoidal current with I_rms ≈ I_pk:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.53\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.6\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. Averaged over one rotation:

$$\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{19.6}{6} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}$$

While actively conducting, a FET dissipates I²·R = 9.8 W. At typical eRPM (10–40 kHz electrical) the conduction window is 8–33 µs, well below the junction thermal time constant (~ms). The junction therefore sees roughly the average, with a small ripple (§3.3).

### 1.4 Copper and parasitic conduction

The PCB phase trace and pad resistance (R_cu) adds loss:

$$P_{cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{cu}$$

For 0.2 mΩ effective path resistance (2 oz, 3 paralleled layers, short run): P_cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.2 m = **2.6 W per motor**. This is not negligible. Use 4–6 parallel copper layers on phase and power nets.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches (one high/low complementary pair per motor at any instant).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Recovery current also adds turn-on overlap loss in the high-side. A practical multiplier of 1.5–2× on P_rr captures this, giving about 0.1 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF}\times 635\times48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

With two dead-time intervals per cycle (t_dt = 50 ns, V_f ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, 125 °C) | 19.6 |
| Copper | 2.6 |
| V·I switching | 1.94 |
| Q_rr (with multiplier) | 0.10 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **24.7 W** |

**All four motors at 80 A: ≈ 99 W.** Add about 1.5 W for the FC, regulators and the ESC MCUs.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f} \approx \frac{2.4\text{ W}}{48\text{ kHz}} = 0.05\ \text{W/kHz per motor}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.2 W per motor, roughly 5 % of burst loss. Conduction dominates, so 48 kHz is justified if it improves current ripple and acoustics on low-inductance motors. Check the ripple:
