# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The numbers below depend on component choices that have not been confirmed. Each assumption is marked **[A]**. Replace these with datasheet values before release.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFET [A] | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 / 5×6, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ typ / 1.2 mΩ max at 25 °C, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(er) ≈ 2 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive [A] | 3-phase driver (FD6288-class), V_GS = 10 V, t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns at 80 A |
| Dead time [A] | 100 ns per edge |
| PCB [A] | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz copper on all layers, no heatsink |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM at 48 kHz |
| Architecture | H743 = flight controller only. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). |

**Flag 1 – Topology.** The H743 should not commutate four motors directly. Production designs all use per-channel ESC MCUs. The H743's job is to generate bidirectional DShot.

**Flag 2 – "Nano" vs. 80 A.** 80 A per channel on 6S is about 2 kW per motor. As §1.5 shows, a 20×20 or 30×30 board with no heatsink can hold 80 A × 4 for **roughly 1–2 seconds**, not the 10 s implied by typical marketing "burst" ratings. Specify a burst duration and duty cycle in the requirements before committing to this rating.

## 1. Loss and Thermal Mathematical Model

### 1.1 Conduction Path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- the high-side FET of one phase (or its complementary low-side during the PWM off-time), and
- the low-side FET of another phase.

Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the per-FET average is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

The per-channel total, summed over the six FETs, simplifies to:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with R_25,max = 1.2 mΩ:

$$R = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

For comparison, at 25 °C typical (0.9 mΩ) the same calculation gives 11.5 W. Because loss and temperature feed each other, solve the loop iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\, Z_{th}(t)$$

### 1.3 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge is PWM-switching at any instant. Per channel:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw (dissipated in the driver/gate resistor) | **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |

Conduction loss dominates by about 14:1 at 80 A. Raising f_sw (for example to 96 kHz for smoother low-throttle behavior) would add roughly 1.7 W per channel, which matters far less than reducing R_DS(on).

### 1.4 Total Dissipation

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Switching | 1.73 W | 6.9 W |
| Bus copper, shunts, connectors [A: 0.3 mΩ shared bus, 320 A total] | — | ~30 W (I²R on the shared bus) |
| **Total** | **~26.3 W** | **~105 W FETs + ~30 W bus** |

The bus-copper term is significant at 320 A aggregate. It requires battery pads on all layers with via stitching. Budget at least 2 mm² of copper cross-section per 10 A of burst current.

### 1.5 Transient Thermal Model

**Junction-to-case.** For a 1 s pulse, Z_th(jc) ≈ 0.4 K/W [A], so:

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 26.3/6 \times 0.4 \approx 1.8\ \text{K}$$

This term is negligible. The board is the bottleneck.

**Board thermal mass (30.5 × 30.5 mm):**
- Copper: 9.3×10⁻⁴ m² × 8 layers × 70 µm × 8960 kg/m³ ≈ 4.7 g → C ≈ 1.8 J/K
- FR-4 (effectively coupled fraction ≈ 50%): ≈ 1.5 J/K
- FETs and components: ≈ 0.4 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 3.7 J/K**

**Board-to-air resistance** with prop-wash airflow (h ≈ 50 W/m²K, two sides plus component surfaces): R_ba ≈ 8 K/W.

This gives a time constant:

$$\tau = R_{ba}\,C = 8 \times 3.7 \approx 30\ \text{s}$$

**First-order rise** with P = 105 W:

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{ba}\,\big(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\big) = 840\,\big(1 - e^{-t/30}\big)$$

**Allowed burst time.** Starting from 45 °C board temperature (hover) and limiting to 110 °C board (T_j about 125 °C), ΔT_allowed = 65 K:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{65}{840}\right) \approx \mathbf{2.4\ s}$$

Including the R_DS(on) rise during the burst reduces this to **about 1.8 s**. A 20×20 board (C ≈ 1.7 J/K) is limited to **about 0.8 s**.

**Continuous rating** at 65 K rise: P_max = 65/8 ≈ 8 W total. That supports roughly 23 A per channel continuous (all four channels, 25 °C ambient).

**Firmware protection.** Set ESC temperature protection (AM32 `TEMPERATURE_LIMIT`) to 105 °C. In ArduPilot, set `ESC_TLM`-based current limits or `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX` to enforce the burst budget.
