# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, DShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Two Issues to Resolve First

**Issue 1: Who drives the FETs?**
ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends bidirectional DShot to the ESC. DShot only makes sense if a separate ESC MCU does the commutation. This design therefore assumes:

- **H743 (ArduPilot)** sends DShot600 bidirectional frames to **4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 ESC MCUs (AM32 firmware)**.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

If you meant the H743 to drive the FETs directly, DShot is irrelevant and ArduPilot would need custom motor-control code. That is a different project.

**Issue 2: 4×80A on a nano board does not survive thermally.**
Section 3 shows roughly 80 W of heat for 4 s or less before the board reaches its limit. The model is still valid. It tells you what has to change.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction loss

On-resistance depends on junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.9 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**. Use 1.4 mΩ to allow for layout and copper.

In 6-step synchronous operation, exactly two switch positions carry I_ph at every instant: one in the high-side group and one in the low-side group. The complementary FET carries the off-time current, so the duty cycle cancels out:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

Per FET:
- Instantaneous: 80² × 1.4 mΩ = 9.0 W while conducting.
- Average: about 3 W (each switch position is active for 1/3 of the electrical period).

### 2.2 Hard-switching loss

Only the PWM leg switches at any moment.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse-recovery and output-capacitance losses

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f I_{ph} t_d f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_g f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Total per channel and per board

| Loss term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.92 |
| Switching | 1.94 |
| Reverse recovery + Q_oss | 0.11 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **≈20.6** |
| **4 channels** | **≈82 W** |

Efficiency check: about 2.2 kW of motor power at 80 A per motor gives roughly 96% stage efficiency. That is electrically fine. The problem is purely thermal.

### 2.7 Sensitivity rules

- Conduction loss scales with I² and with R(T). This creates positive feedback, so iterate T_j until it converges.
- Switching loss scales with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1 W per channel. That is only a 1 W gain, so frequency is not the lever here.
- **Paralleling two FETs per switch halves conduction loss** (17.9 → 9.0 W per channel). This is the dominant lever.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

```
P_FET ─ Rθjc(0.8 K/W) ─ case ─ Rθ(solder+via array, ~3 K/W per FET) ─ PCB node (C_pcb) ─ Rθ_conv ─ T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm board, 8 layers, 2 oz copper, plus 24 FETs)

- FR4: ~1.5 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 1.7 J/K
- Copper: ~2.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.0 J/K
- 24 FETs + solder: ~2.0 J/K
- **Total C_pcb ≈ 4.7 J/K**

### 3.3 Convection in prop wash

Assume h ≈ 100 W/m²·K over A = 8×10⁻⁴ m² (both sides).

$$R_{θ,conv} \approx 12.5\text{ K/W}$$

This removes only about 0.08 W/K. During a burst the board is effectively adiabatic.

### 3.4 Burst survival time

Start at T_amb = 40 °C and allow T_pcb,max = 105 °C (ΔT = 65 K).

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C\,\Delta T}{P} = \frac{4.7\times65}{82} \approx \mathbf{3.7\ s}$$

Junction temperature rides above the PCB node:

$$T_j \approx T_{pcb} + P_{FET,avg}(Rθ_{jc}+Rθ_{attach}) \approx T_{pcb} + 3\text{ W}\times3.8 \approx T_{pcb}+11\text{ K}$$

A 9 W per-FET ripple at the commutation frequency is filtered by die thermal mass and adds about 3–5 K at low eRPM.
