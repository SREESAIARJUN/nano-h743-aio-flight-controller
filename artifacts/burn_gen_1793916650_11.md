# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching and Bidirectional DShot Design Model

This document builds the model from first principles. Part values are reference assumptions and are labelled as such. Replace them with datasheet values from your actual BOM before freezing the design.

**The 80 A burst requirement cannot be met by a bare nano board for more than about 2 seconds at 6S.** The derivation is in §3, and the mitigation options are in §8.

## 0. System Architecture and Assumptions

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does not commutate motors. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which performs 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over one bidirectional DShot600 wire.

```
 6S Battery ─┬─ Bulk caps ─┬─ 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6 FET bridge] → Motors
             │             └─ Current shunt → amp ─────┐
             └─ 5V/3V3 BEC ─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ◄──┘ ADC
                               │ TIMx CHy ×4 (bidir DShot600)
                               └──────────────► ESC MCU signal inputs
```

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V | worst case for switching loss |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A | requirement |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | requirement |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 class | typical nano-ESC part |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{2.3}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.60 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-FET duty in 6-step synchronous commutation

Each phase spends 120° of the electrical period high-active, 120° low-active and 120° floating.

- **High-side FET:** PWM at duty D during its 120° window.
- **Low-side FET:**
  - complementary (synchronous rectification) during the high window, giving (1−D);
  - held fully on during its own low window.

The RMS-squared currents per FET are therefore:

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3} I_{ph}^2
\qquad
I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3} I_{ph}^2
$$

Check: summed over the three legs, the total is 2·I_ph². Exactly two FETs carry I_ph at any instant, so the model is consistent.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D ≈ 1 (full-throttle burst)

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per HS FET | I²R/3 | 6400 × 1.92 mΩ / 3 = **4.10 W** |
| Per LS FET (D = 1) | I²R/3 | **4.10 W** |
| Per motor bridge (6 FETs) | 2·I²R | **24.6 W** |
| Four motors | — | **98.3 W** |

If the H743 ever commands mid-throttle at high current (for example, a heavy prop under load), the worst case for the low-side FET is D → 0. That gives 2/3·I²R = 8.2 W in a single FET.
