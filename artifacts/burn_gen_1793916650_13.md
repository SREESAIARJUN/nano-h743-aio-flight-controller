## 0. Executive Summary and Feasibility Flags

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU (AM32 on STM32G071 or AT32F421). ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors directly from the H743. |
| **80A burst, per motor** | Achievable only as a **time-limited burst (about 3–5 s from a 40 °C start)**. Steady-state 80A × 4 is thermally impossible in a nano footprint. |
| **"Nano" footprint** | 24 × PDFN5×6 MOSFETs (about 30 mm² each, about 720 mm² total) **do not fit on a 20×20 mm board** (400 mm² per side). Minimum viable is **30.5×30.5 mm**, or 20×20 with dual PDFN3.3 parts. Dual PDFN3.3 parts have higher R_DS(on), which pushes burst capability down to about 45–55 A. |
| **Bi-dir DShot600** | Fully feasible on the H743 thanks to DMAMUX. Placing all four motors on one timer (TIM1) minimises the stream count. |
| **Total burst dissipation** | About **94 W** for the ESC stage at 4 × 80 A on 6S. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]^{\,\kappa}\approx R_{DS,25}\,(1.0045)^{(T_j-25)}
$$

At T_j = 115 °C this gives (1.0045)^90 ≈ 1.50, so **R_DS(hot) = 1.35 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current Path in 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phases:

- **PWM phase:**
  - High-side (HS) conducts for fraction D.
  - Low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D − 2t_d f_sw).
- **Return phase:** the LS is on continuously.

The conducting path therefore always contains **two MOSFET channels in series**, except during dead time. Dead time is handled in §3.4.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot(1-2t_d f_{sw})
$$

$$
= 80^2 \times 2(1.35\,\text{m}\Omega)\times(1-0.0192) = 6400\times 2.70\text{m}\times 0.981 = \mathbf{16.95\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical cycle, so 1/3 of the time, and there are 6 FETs:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{16.95}{6}\approx 2.83\ \text{W},\qquad P_{cond,FET,peak\ (during\ conduction)} = I^2R = 8.64\ \text{W}
$$

At commutation frequencies above about 100 Hz electrical, the junction thermal time constant (around 1–5 ms) averages this. **Use 2.83 W average plus a ripple term ΔT ≈ 8.64 W × Z_θ(t_on).**
