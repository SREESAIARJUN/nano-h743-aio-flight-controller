# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist (Job #65)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### Partitioning

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In practice the AIO board is split into two halves:

- **STM32H743 (flight controller):** runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC section.
- **ESC section:** four small ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Each drives one 3-phase gate driver and six N-MOSFETs.

The losses below therefore belong to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer allocation belongs to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

The motor is driven with 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous complementary PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path, each realised by either the active FET or its complementary FET.

Per motor channel, the conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}\ \text{per motor}$$

**Copper loss.** Add the trace and plane loss: P_Cu = I²·R_trace. Each 1 mm × 10 mm segment of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ, so roughly 1.6 W per such segment at 80 A. Budget about 1.5 W per motor:

$$P_{Cu}\approx 1.5\text{ W/motor}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is in the current path for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, split between active and freewheel roles. That gives:

$$P_{FET,cond}\approx P_{cond}/6 \approx 3.2\text{ W average}$$

The peak instantaneous loss per FET is I²R = 9.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on one leg per PWM edge pair.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(30 ns)(48 k) | 1.45 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 60 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.073 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| C_oss | ½ Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Gate drive (driver loss, 2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| **Total switching-related loss** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

### Totals at 80 A Burst

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 19.2 + 1.5 + 2.2 ≈ **22.9 W** |
| All four motors at full burst | **≈ 92 W** |

Conduction dominates at 83%. To reduce losses:

1. **Lower R_DS(on)** first. Even 0.7 mΩ saves about 6 W per channel.
2. **Shorten dead time** next. 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
3. **Lower f_sw** last. 24 kHz saves only about 0.75 W per channel while adding current ripple and acoustic noise.

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above Board

Assume R_θJB ≈ R_θJC plus the solder and via field, about 2 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{jb} = P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JB} \approx (3.2+0.4)\times 2 \approx 7\ \text{K average}$$

Using the transient impedance Z_θ at the electrical frequency adds only a few K of ripple. Junctions are therefore not the limit. The board's bulk heat capacity is.

### 3.2 Board Heat Capacity (Adiabatic Burst Model)

| Material | Mass × specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ~5 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 5.5 J/K |
| Copper | ~4 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.5 J/K |
| FETs and components | — | ~0.5 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 7.5 J/K** |

The board heats at:

$$\frac{dT}{dt}=\frac{P}{C_{th}}=\frac{92}{7.5}\approx 12\ \text{K/s}$$

### 3.3 Convective Model (First-Order RC)

Prop-wash convection is taken as h ≈ 100 W/m²·K over A ≈ 2 × (0.0305)² ≈ 1.9×10⁻³ m², giving:

$$R_{\theta BA}=\frac{1}{hA}\approx 5.3\ \text{K/W}, \qquad \tau = R_{\theta BA}\,C_{th}\approx 40\ \text{s}$$

The board temperature then follows:

$$T_b(t)=T_{amb}+P R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

To reach a board limit of T_b,max = 105 °C (giving T_j ≈ 112 °C):

$$t_{burst} = -\tau\ln\!\left(1-\frac{T_{b,max}-T_{amb}}{P R_{\theta BA}}\right) = -40\ln\!\left(1-\frac{65}{488}\right)\approx \mathbf{5.7\ s}$$

The sustainable continuous dissipation is:

$$P_{cont}=\frac{65}{5.3}\approx 12\text{ W total}$$

This corresponds to about **~3 W per motor**, i.e. roughly **≈ 28 A continuous per motor** when you solve the loss model backwards. That figure assumes cold R_DS(on) and partial throttle; validate it on a thermal bench.
