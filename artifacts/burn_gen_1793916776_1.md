## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

During each 60° sector, one phase leg is PWM'd and another phase's low-side FET is held on:

- **PWM leg, high side (Q_H):** conducts for fraction D of each PWM period.
- **PWM leg, low side (Q_L, synchronous rectification):** conducts for 1 − D (minus dead time).
- **Return-phase low side:** conducts 100% of the sector.
- Each switch position is active for 120° = 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

The electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to a few kHz) is far faster than the FET thermal time constants (≥ ms). Thermal analysis can therefore use **cycle-averaged power**.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

With phase-to-phase inductance L_pp:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{pp}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5. Example: L_pp = 20 µH (a typical 5" class motor), V = 25.2 V:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\times10^{-6}\cdot 48\,000} = 6.56\text{ A} \Rightarrow \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 3.6\text{ A}^2
$$

This is negligible against 80² = 6400 A². Use I_rms ≈ I_m for burst analysis. For low-inductance motors (< 5 µH), the ripple term matters.
