# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #13): Power, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three issues change how the model is set up.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.**
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600.
   - Each of the 4 ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_S.
   - So the design is an AIO: one H743 plus four ESC MCUs, gate drivers, and 24 MOSFETs.

2. **"80 A burst" is ambiguous.** I model 80 A **per channel** (320 A total) as the worst case. Section 3 shows this cannot be sustained on a nano board for more than a few seconds.

3. **Form factor.** A nano board is 20×20 mm mounting (~25.5×25.5 mm board) with 6S max (25.2 V full charge).

Component assumptions:

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**How current flows in 6-step commutation.** In any commutation step, two legs carry the motor current I:
- Leg A is PWM'd. Its high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- Leg B's low-side FET conducts continuously.

Therefore at every instant exactly **two FETs** carry I. Ripple is neglected here (I_rms ≈ I_dc at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance).

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2I^2R_{DS,hot}$$

The loss is independent of D, which makes this the correct worst-case form for a burst.

**At 80 A, 1.5 mΩ:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(0.0015) = 19.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each leg's high side carries current about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, and its low side carries the remainder of the conduction share. The worst-case FET is a low-side FET at low D:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}(1 + (1-D)) \to \le 6400 \cdot 0.0015 \cdot \tfrac{2}{3} = 6.4\ \text{W}$$

**Copper and trace loss** (add explicitly; this is often ignored):
- Assume the phase path is 2 oz copper on 4 layers in parallel, with ~0.3 mΩ effective resistance per channel, including the battery-pad share.
- P_Cu = 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. Switching occurs once per PWM period per edge pair.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the synchronous low-side body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** Two FETs are switching in the active leg. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** ≈ **3.3 W per channel**.

**Design notes on the switching terms:**
- Dead-time loss is comparable to V–I overlap. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns, if the driver propagation-delay match allows, saves about 0.6 W per channel.
- Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles P_sw. The benefit is lower current ripple and smoother low-RPM behaviour.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 3.3 W | 13.2 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Drivers + LDO + MCU | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **24.7 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is about 1.8 kW per channel, giving ~98.6% efficiency. The electrical efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

### 3.2 Steady-state limit

**Thermal resistance to ambient.** Assume convective cooling over both board faces:
- Area: A = 2 × (0.0255 m)² = 1.3×10⁻³ m².
- In prop-wash, h ≈ 100 W/m²K (forced air). In still air, h ≈ 15.

$$R_{\theta,BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{100\cdot1.3\times10^{-3}} \approx 7.7\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 99 \times 7.7 \approx 760\ \text{K}$$

→ **Continuous 80 A per channel is physically impossible on this board.**

**Maximum continuous per channel** for T_board ≤ 100 °C at 40 °C ambient, with ΔT = 60 K:
- Allowed total loss: P_max = 60 / 7.7 ≈ 7.8 W, i.e. about 1.95 W per channel.
- Conduction scales as ~2.4 mΩ·I² (2R + copper), and switching scales roughly linearly with I.
- Solving gives **I_cont ≈ 22–25 A per channel**, which matches real nano AIO ratings.
