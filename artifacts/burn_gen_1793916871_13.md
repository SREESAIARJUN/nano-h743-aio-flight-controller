**Architectural clarifications:**

1. **The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate the ESC.** It runs ArduPilot on the FC side. A 4-in-1 ESC uses four independent ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The netlist below reflects this split. On an AIO "nano" board both share one PCB but remain separate MCUs.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs 80 A.** On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 4 × 80 A is a *transient* rating only. Section 2.6 shows the continuous rating lands near 35–40 A per channel with good airflow. Present 80 A as a ≤ 2 s burst rating, never continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, two phase legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) conducts as a synchronous rectifier for (1 − D − 2·t_dt·f_sw).
- **Leg B (return leg):** one FET is held fully ON.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

With synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through exactly **two channels** in series (one in Leg A, one in Leg B), regardless of D. The exception is dead time, which is handled in §1.3.

### 1.2 Channel conduction loss

Temperature-corrected on-resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

Per-channel conduction loss (excluding dead-time fraction):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,(1 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\text{ m}\Omega \cdot (1 - 0.0288) = 19.2 \cdot 0.971 = \mathbf{18.65\ W}$$

### 1.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

During each dead-time interval, current freewheels through the LS body diode of the PWM leg. There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2\,t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 600\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** at 48 kHz dead time is expensive. Cutting t_dt to 150 ns halves this loss to 0.92 W. Only do so if the driver propagation-delay mismatch plus FET t_d(off) leaves margin against shoot-through. Verify on the bench at minimum gate-drive voltage.

### 1.4 Shunt and copper

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{sh} + R_{trace}) = 6400 \cdot 0.55\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.52\ W}$$

The shunt contributes 1.6 W of this; plating, vias and solder contribute the rest. Use 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper, with via arrays under the drain tabs.

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Each FET's duty over a full electrical revolution is about 1/3 of the time. The average per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \approx 3.1\text{ W}$$

However, the **instantaneous** loss while conducting is I²R = 9.6 W. For short bursts the junction sees a pulsed waveform at the commutation frequency. At high eRPM (commutation period ≪ thermal time constant of the die, about 1–10 ms), the averaged value governs.

**Low-RPM / stall caveat.** At startup or stall, one FET may carry the full 9.6 W continuously for tens of milliseconds. This makes stall/desync protection a thermal requirement, not merely a control feature.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (HS hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The HS turns on into the LS body diode after dead time:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and temperature. Budget ×2 if R_g is small: about 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 60\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.4 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per PWM period in the active leg. This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, **not** in the FET die:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{ nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$
