## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Design Flags

### Flag 1: Partition the controllers

The STM32H743 should not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. The robust and standard partition is:

- **H743:** runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **4 × ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32): each handles commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, and dead-time for one motor.

All switching-loss math below applies to the ESC MCU domain. All DMA math applies to the H743 domain.

### Flag 2: "Nano" and 4 × 80 A burst conflict physically

As Section 3 shows, 80 A on all four channels dissipates about 125–135 W in the power stage. A 20 × 20 mm board cannot hold the FET count, copper area, or thermal mass. This document therefore assumes:

- **ESC board:** 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting pattern, about 42 × 45 mm outline.
- **FC:** H743 on a stacked 20 × 20 or 30.5 × 30.5 FC board, connected by board-to-board connector.

If "nano" is a hard mechanical requirement, the burst rating must drop to about 35–45 A per channel. Section 3.6 shows the scaling.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.80 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step BLDC with complementary PWM

At any instant, current flows through exactly two bridge positions:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D; the synchronous low-side (LS) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** the LS is fully on.

The current path therefore always contains two R_DS in series, independent of D:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase-current ripple with winding inductance L_ph:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), ΔI is about 4–8 A. The correction is under 1 %, so it is neglected.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle each FET carries current one-third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

Worst-case FET at low duty is the LS, because it carries (1 − D) of the PWM leg plus the full-on return leg.

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.8 × 10⁻³ = **23.0 W per channel**
- Average per FET: 3.84 W. Worst-case LS FET: about 5.5 W.
- Copper and shunt add about 3 W per channel. The estimate assumes 0.25 mΩ shunt plus about 0.2 mΩ of trace/pad: 80² × 0.45 mΩ ≈ 2.9 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return leg is static.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·40n·48k = **1.94 W** |
| Body-diode conduction (dead time) | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·400n·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | 2·½C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1.5n·635·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs per leg) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·50n·10·48k = **0.05 W** (dissipated in driver and gate resistors) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 3.3 W per channel** |

### Design levers, in order of payoff

1. **Cut dead time from 200 ns to 80 ns.** This saves about 0.7 W per channel. In AM32 the parameter is `dead_time`; it must be validated against the driver's propagation skew.
2. **Lower f_sw to 24 kHz.** This halves all switching terms, saving about 1.6 W per channel. The cost is audible noise and higher ripple. 48 kHz is retained per the specification.
3. **Use a driver with ≥ 1.5 A sink current.** This reduces t_f.
