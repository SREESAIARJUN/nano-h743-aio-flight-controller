# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

The 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU per channel or shared MCUs, such as AM32 on STM32G071 or AT32F421, or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate the motors. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

During any 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs (HS at duty D, LS at 1−D, synchronous) and one leg's LS is held fully on. Phase current I flows through:

- **PWM leg:** HS for time D·T_sw, LS for (1−D)·T_sw (minus dead time)
- **Return leg:** LS for 100 % of T_sw

So the instantaneous conduction resistance is always two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty. That is why synchronous 6-step ESCs heat almost equally at partial and full throttle for the same phase current.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.007\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.595 ≈ **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**PCB copper and solder loss.** Assume 0.25 mΩ per phase path, including the shunt, with two paths in series:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ W$$

### 1.4 Per-FET averaged loss

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries its share:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{20.5}{6} \approx 3.4\ W$$

The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R = **10.2 W**. This matters for the transient thermal analysis in §3, because Z_th at the commutation period is non-negligible at low eRPM.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check

Positive feedback through R(T). The closed-loop gain must stay below 1:

$$G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{\theta,JA} = \alpha \cdot P_{cond,FET}(25) \cdot R_{\theta,JA}$$

With P(25) ≈ 2.1 W/FET, α = 0.007, and R_θJA ≈ 35 K/W (nano board, no heatsink, propwash airflow): G ≈ 0.52.

The loop is stable, but it amplifies temperature rise by 1/(1−G) ≈ **2.1×**. This is the hidden reason small boards "die suddenly" under sustained load.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

All losses are per channel (one PWM leg active at a time).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (HS FET)

Gate-charge-based transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,src}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,sink}}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 22 nC: t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 12 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(27\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (1.5\text{ nF})(25.2^2)(48\text{k}) = 0.023\ W$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so derate upward ×1.5–2 for 80 A.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{ nC} \cdot 10\text{ V} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ W$$

### 2.6 Summary per channel at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, T_j = 110 °C) | 20.5 |
| Copper / shunt | 3.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.31 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated ×1.5) | 0.09 |
| C_oss + gate | 0.08 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.8 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneous** | **≈ 103 W** |
