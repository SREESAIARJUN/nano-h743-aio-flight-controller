## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005: **R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current-path analysis (6-step, high-side PWM, sync rectification)

In each 60° sector, phase X sources current and phase Y sinks it, at motor current I:

- **High-side X** conducts for a fraction D of each PWM period.
- **Low-side X** conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous freewheel path.
- **Low-side Y** conducts for 100 % of the sector.

Each phase sources for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and sinks for 1/3. Averaging per FET gives:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R\,\frac{D}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R\left[\frac{1-D}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right]$$

Summed over the 6 FETs of one channel:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 3\left[I^2R\frac{D}{3} + I^2R\frac{2-D}{3}\right] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

This result is independent of D, because two FETs are always in series with the winding.

### 1.3 Worst-case FET

The low-side FET carries the most loss, and it is worst at low D (high current at low throttle, e.g. a punch-out from hover):

$$P_{LS,max} = I^2R\,\frac{2-D}{3}\ \xrightarrow{D\to 0}\ \frac{2}{3}I^2R$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Channel conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Worst LS FET (D = 0.3) | 6400 × 1.5 m × 1.7/3 | **5.44 W** |
| Worst HS FET (D = 1) | 6400 × 1.5 m / 3 | 3.2 W |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, 4-in-1 common) | (4 × 80)²… see §1.5 | — |

### 1.5 Common-path copper and shunt losses

With one shared battery-return shunt, the shunt current is the sum of all channel currents:

$$P_{shunt} = R_{sh}\Big(\sum I_k\Big)^2 = 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \times 320^2 = 25.6\ \text{W}$$

That figure is unacceptable. **Decision:** use per-channel 0.5 mΩ shunts in each low-side source return, which also gives AM32 per-motor current limiting:

$$P_{shunt,ch} = 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 80^2 \times \tfrac{2}{3}\ (\text{shunt sees the sinking phase}) \approx 2.1\ \text{W}$$

Copper spreading resistance of the bus, using 4 layers of 1–2 oz copper over about 15 mm with an effective ~0.3 mΩ shared:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega\times 320^2 \approx 30\ \text{W}\ \text{(worst case, all motors at 80 A)}$$

This is reduced by bonding the battery pads directly to the bus planes with ≥ 4 stacked layers and stitching vias (≥ 60 × 0.3 mm). The target is 0.1 mΩ, which gives **≈10 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches: the HS FET hard-switches, and the LS FET soft-switches after dead time.

### 2.1 Gate-drive transition time

Miller-plateau model, with V_drv = 10 V (FD6288Q from a 10 V regulator), V_pl ≈ 3.5 V, and R_g,tot = R_ext + R_int + R_drv ≈ 3 + 1 + 6 = 10 Ω:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g}} = 0.65\,\text{A},\quad t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$
$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} \approx 0.5\,\text{A}\ (\text{with turn-off diode},\ R\approx 7\Omega),\quad t_f \approx 20\ \text{ns}$$
