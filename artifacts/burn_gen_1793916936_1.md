# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #60): Power, Thermal, and DShot DMA Model

**Document class:** Architecture-level design model. This is not a fab-ready release.

**Basis for all numbers:** 6S operation (25.2 V max), 48 kHz PWM, and representative MOSFET parameters. Every parameter marked **[A]** is an assumption. Before release, replace each one with datasheet values for the selected parts.

---

## 0. Architecture Findings You Should Read First

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. A typical choice is one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor, or one per two motors. | The netlist below separates the FC and ESC domains. |
| F2 | "80 A" is interpreted as **80 A per motor, burst**, which is 320 A aggregate. Section 2 shows a **~88 W** total dissipation at full burst. A 20×20 mm board cannot sustain this. It is viable only as a time-limited burst of about 1–3 s from a warm start. | A stacked FC (20×20) + ESC (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) is strongly recommended over an AIO layout. |
| F3 | An H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) plus 24 power MOSFETs plus gate drivers will not fit on one 20×20 board with adequate copper. | This confirms the stacked architecture in F2. |
| F4 | On the H743, DMAMUX removes fixed stream/request pairing. The binding constraints for bi-directional DShot are therefore **timer channel pairing** and **total stream count**, not request mapping. | This simplifies Section 4. |

| t_dt | 100 ns **[A]** | Dead time per edge |
| R_θJC | 0.8 K/W **[A]** | Junction to case |

**Commutation model:** six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM.
- At any instant, one high-side and one low-side FET carry I_ph. The PWM-switching FET pairs with its complementary partner.
- Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, at 80 A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

The hot on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Per-FET RMS current with 1/3 conduction is:

$$I_{rms,FET} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1/3} = 80/\sqrt3 = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

Per-FET conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 46.2^2 \times 1.5\text{m} = 3.20\ \text{W}$$

Per motor, two FETs are always in the current path. Under synchronous rectification the PWM duty D is shared between the switch and its complement, so the total is independent of D to first order:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

**Sensitivity:** P_cond scales as I². At 60 A it falls to 10.8 W. Paralleling two FETs per position halves it to 9.6 W.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched FET per motor at any time.

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction (2 edges per cycle):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 actively switching gates; this power is dissipated in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Loss summary

| Term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **21.9** | **≈ 87.5 W** |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ **[A]**, I_DC ≈ D·I_ph) | ≤ 1.6 | ≤ 6.4 |
| Copper traces/pours (~0.3 mΩ path **[A]**) | ~1.9 | ~7.7 |

At 80 A burst, total board dissipation is **≈ 100 W**.

**Why 48 kHz is affordable:** switching loss is only ~11 % of the total. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ≈ 1.3 W per motor. That saving buys less than doubling the copper weight or paralleling FETs would. 48 kHz is justified by lower current ripple and quieter motors.
