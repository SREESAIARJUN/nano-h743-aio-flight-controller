## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Red Flag

### 0.1 System partitioning

The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors.

| Function | Device | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz, 200 MHz timer clock) | ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class | AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, one per motor, bidirectional DShot capable |
| Gate drivers | 3 half-bridges per motor (e.g. FD6288Q-class), 12 per board | |
| Power FETs | 6 per motor, 24 total | 40 V, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm, R_DS(on),25 ≈ 1.0 mΩ |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | |
| Board | 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes | |

### 0.2 Red flag on the "nano" form factor

"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst per motor" are physically incompatible except for burst durations of about **1–2 seconds**. Section 3.5 shows the math.

**Design decision required:** do one of the following:
- move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint, **or**
- redefine the rating as "80 A for under 1.5 s, 30 A continuous."

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

At any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side FET of phase *x* and one low-side FET of phase *y*. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- the high-side FET conducts for fraction D,
- its synchronous low-side partner conducts for fraction (1 − D).

As a result, the loop always contains **two R_DS(on)** in series, independent of D (dead time is neglected here and treated in §2.4).

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{trace}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

This creates positive electrothermal feedback. Solve it by iterating to a fixed point:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + R_{\theta,ja}\cdot P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)$$

A stable solution exists only if:

$$R_{\theta,ja}\cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} < 1$$

Otherwise the result is thermal runaway.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | 25 °C | 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Per conducting FET (I²R) | 6.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Per motor (2 FETs) | 12.8 W | **19.2 W** |
| Copper (≈0.15 mΩ per motor path, 2 oz, short pours) | 0.96 W | 1.3 W |
| **Per motor total conduction** | 13.8 W | **20.5 W** |

Per-FET average (rotation spreads loss across 6 FETs, each conducting about 1/3 of the electrical cycle): **P_FET,avg ≈ 3.2 W hot**.

The **peak-sector instantaneous** value is the one that matters for junction temperature on a slow rotor: 9.6 W.

### 1.4 Battery-side conduction

- Total bus current, 4 motors × 80 A × D. Worst case D → 1: **320 A**.
- XT60 contact resistance ≈ 0.5 mΩ → 51 W. **The connector cannot support this.** Use XT90 or a direct solder pad.
- 12 AWG lead at 5.2 mΩ/m over 0.1 m: 0.52 mΩ → 53 W during burst. **Use 10 AWG.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched FET in each motor (one at a time) incurs hard-switching loss. Its synchronous partner switches near zero voltage (ZVS) after the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times are set by the Miller charge and the gate-driver current: t ≈ Q_gd / I_g. With Q_gd ≈ 10 nC and I_g ≈ 1 A, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 10 ns.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period, with t_dt = 200 ns:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the second-largest loss. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, but requires verifying shoot-through margin with the actual driver propagation skew.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

This loss is mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| C_oss | 0.06 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Total switching** | **2.37** |

**Note on 48 kHz:** switching loss is about 10 % of conduction loss at 80 A. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz costs about +2.4 W per motor. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice: it gives low current ripple for low-inductance motors and moderate loss.

| Item | W |

### 3.2 Junction-to-board thermal path (per FET, peak sector)

- R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W (5×6 mm package)
- R_θ,solder+vias ≈ 1.5 K/W

$$\Delta T_{j-board} = 9.6 \times 2.7 \approx \mathbf{26\ K}$$
