# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #51): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Issues

Three findings shape everything that follows.

1. **80 A per motor on a nano-format board is a short burst, not a rating.** At 80 A, each ESC channel dissipates about 26 W. Four channels together dissipate about 104 W. A board this size can shed roughly 8–15 W continuously in prop-wash. Section 4 shows that 80 A on all four channels is survivable for about **2–4 s**. A sustainable continuous rating is about **35–40 A/channel**, and it should be marketed that way.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot cannot run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops at 48 kHz. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which handles bidirectional DShot and eRPM telemetry. The H743 is the DShot master. "4-in-1 ESC" here means four ESC MCUs plus a shared power stage on one PCB.
3. **The device values below are representative**, not a qualified BOM. They model a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET and an FD6288Q-class gate driver. Substitute datasheet values for the selected parts before sign-off. The equations are part-agnostic.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Phase current (burst) | I_ph | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | κ(T) | ≈ 1 + 0.0062·(T_j − 25) |
| Junction temperature target | T_j | 110 °C → κ = 1.53 |
| R_DS(on) hot | R_hot | 1.84 mΩ |
| PCB copper + solder per current path | R_pcb | 0.3 mΩ |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM |

### 2.2 Derivation

In 6-step commutation, at any instant exactly two legs carry I_ph. One leg is the PWM leg and the other is the low-side "freewheel-ON" leg.

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high side (HS) for duty D and through the synchronous low side (LS) for 1−D. Instantaneous loss is therefore always I²R_hot, independent of D.
- **Static leg:** the LS stays on for the full 60° sector. Its loss is I²R_hot.

So the conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot} + 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.84\times10^{-3}) + 2(80)^2(0.3\times10^{-3}) = 23.55 + 3.84 = \mathbf{27.4\ W}$$

The 3.84 W term is copper loss. It lands in the PCB, not the FETs. FET-only conduction loss is **23.6 W/channel**.

### 2.3 Per-Device Distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, averaged over a rotation:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{23.55}{6} = 3.93\ \text{W (average)},\qquad \hat P_{FET} = I^2R_{hot} = 11.8\ \text{W (instantaneous, during its sector)}$$

The sector period at typical eRPM is a few hundred µs, which is much shorter than the die thermal time constant. Use average power for junction temperature and check the peak against the Z_th(10–100 µs) curve.
