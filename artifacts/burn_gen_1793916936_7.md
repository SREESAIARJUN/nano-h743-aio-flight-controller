## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Red Flags

Before the math, three points will drive design reviews:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model below reflects this split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a seconds-scale rating, not a capability.** The math in §3 shows roughly 2 s with all four channels at 80 A and roughly 18–20 A continuous per channel. Any marketing figure must state duration and ambient.
3. **All component parameters below are representative placeholders.** They are not datasheet-verified for a specific part number. Replace each with values from your selected MOSFET and driver datasheets and re-run the model. The spreadsheet form of these equations is given in §3.5.

## 2. Loss Model, Per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss in 6-Step Trapezoidal Mode with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd (complementary high/low) and one leg holds its low-side FET fully ON. With duty D:

$$
P_{cond,sector} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[\,D + (1-D)\,] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

The result is independent of D. Sync rectification makes the PWM leg always conduct through exactly one FET.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

The non-FET resistive losses add to this:

$$
P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts during 2 of 6 sectors. The fully-ON low side sees I²R = 11.5 W instantaneous during its sector. The cycle average is P_cond/6 ≈ 3.8 W per FET.

At motor electrical frequencies above about 500 Hz, the FET's junction-to-case transient impedance Z_th(jc) smooths this to near the average. At low eRPM under high current (stall or desync), the instantaneous 11.5 W governs. That condition is the real killer.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched V-I Overlap)

Gate current during the Miller plateau (V_plateau ≈ 3 V):

- Turn-on: I_g ≈ (10 − 3)/5 = 1.4 A, so t_r ≈ Q_gd/I_g + Q_gs2 portion ≈ **10 ns**
- Turn-off: I_g ≈ 3/5 = 0.6 A, so t_f ≈ **20 ns**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss V² f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5n × 635 × 48k | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2 t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | Q_g · V_drv · f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.04 W |

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A

| Loss term | W / channel | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.0 | 92.0 |
| Copper | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| Shunt | 1.6 | 6.4 |
| Switching (V-I) | 1.45 | 5.8 |
| Dead-time + Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.72 | 2.9 |
| **Total** | **≈ 30 W** | **≈ 120 W** |

**Takeaway:** conduction is about 93% of losses. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per channel. Lowering R_ds(on) and copper resistance is the only lever that matters at 80 A.
