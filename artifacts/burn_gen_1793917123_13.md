# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: System Model (Job #39)

## 0. Architecture Note and Basis of Calculation

**Architecture clarification:** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any DShot system, each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. Here that is one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per channel (or a quad-MCU arrangement). The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600.

The model below assumes:
- A stacked or AIO design with the FC on top of a 4-in-1 power stage.
- 6S operation.
- An **80 A per-channel burst rating**.

All device parameters below are **representative assumptions**. Substitute values from your actual MOSFET datasheet, and verify against bench thermal imaging.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant in trapezoidal (6-step) commutation:
- One phase is the PWM leg. The high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1−D.
- One phase is held low, so its low-side FET conducts continuously.
- One phase is floating, which is the BEMF sensing window.

With complementary (synchronous) rectification, **exactly two FETs carry the full phase current at all times**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 × (1 + 0.51) ≈ **1.81 mΩ**.

Conduction loss is coupled to temperature. Iterate T_j ↔ P until converged, or use the hot value for worst case.

### 1.3 Numbers

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

**Copper and interconnect** (often ignored, but significant at 80 A):
- Phase pad + via field + bus trace: assume 0.5 mΩ per channel round trip.
- P_cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W** per channel.
- Battery lead pads + main bus at 320 A total, R ≈ 0.15 mΩ: P = 320² × 0.15 mΩ ≈ **15 W**. This applies only in the all-4-at-burst case.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Electrically, each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical period. On average the loss is shared across all 6 FETs:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{23}{6} \approx 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous loss is 11.5 W in a conducting FET. At burst RPM, electrical frequency is in the kHz range (for example, 14-pole at 30 krpm gives 3.5 kHz). This is far faster than the junction thermal time constant (~ms), so the average value governs T_j rise.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The low-side sync FET soft-switches (ZVS) after the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

The induced extra turn-on loss in the HS FET is often 2–3× this value. Allow **0.15 W**.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$
P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

For the FD6288Q-class driver (×3 legs ≈ 0.15 W), check the bootstrap/LDO budget.

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A burst

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 23.0 |
| Copper / vias | 3.2 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (incl. induced) | 0.15 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| **Total / channel** | **≈ 28.0 W** |
| **4 channels + bus** | **≈ 127 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. At full throttle the loss is ≈ 28 W / 2016 W ≈ **1.4 %** per channel.

**Design insight:** Conduction losses dominate (≈ 82 %). This means:
- Raising f_sw from 24 → 48 kHz costs only ~0.8 W per channel.
- Choosing lower-R_DS(on) parts, or paralleling FETs, pays off far more.
- Paralleling two FETs per switch halves conduction loss to 11.5 W, at the cost of ~2× Q_g and C_oss. These switching-side losses are still under 0.3 W.
