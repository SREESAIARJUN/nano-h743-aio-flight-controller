# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Design Basis Document

**Rev A, Preliminary Engineering Model**

> **Scope note.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. In any practical 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (here an AT32F421 running AM32) because 48 kHz six-step control with zero-cross sensing needs dedicated timer and comparator hardware per motor. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document is structured around that architecture. All component values are representative. Verify every number against the datasheet revision you procure.

### Feasibility flag (read first)

An 80 A burst per channel on a "nano" form factor is only credible as a **short-duration burst** of seconds, not continuous operation. A 20×20 mm board cannot physically host 24 FETs rated for that current. This design therefore assumes:

* a 30.5×30.5 mm board,
* 8-layer, 2 oz copper,
* 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN FETs.

Section 4 bounds the burst duration quantitatively.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous complementary PWM. In any 60° sector, exactly two phases conduct:

* **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side (LS) conducts for (1 − D), minus dead time.
* **Static leg:** the LS stays on for the entire sector.

The motor current I therefore always flows through **two FET channels in series**, whatever the duty cycle (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left(1+\alpha\right)^{(T_j-25)}, \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

This empirical fit is valid for typical 40 V trench FETs. At T_j = 125 °C the multiplier is ≈ 1.57. Read the actual multiplier from the datasheet's normalized R_DS(on)-vs-T_j curve.

### 2.3 Candidate device

Representative device class: 40 V, R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ max at V_GS = 10 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 (e.g., the Infineon OptiMOS 6 / Toshiba U-MOS IX class).

Per-device loss share:

* **Static LS:** conducts 1/3 of each electrical cycle.
* **HS:** conducts D/3 of each electrical cycle.
* **Complementary LS:** conducts (1 − D)/3 of each electrical cycle.

Summing over all six devices recovers the 2I²R total.

### 2.4 Conduction loss at 80 A, per channel

| Case | R_DS(on) per FET | Path R (2 FETs + 0.4 mΩ copper/shunt) | P_cond |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold start, T_j = 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2.4 mΩ | **15.4 W** |
| T_j = 100 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 3.2 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| T_j = 125 °C | 1.57 mΩ | 3.54 mΩ | **22.7 W** |

**Hottest single FET:** the static LS, worst case, takes I²R/3 averaged:

$$80^2 \times 1.57\text{ mΩ} / 3 \approx 3.35\ \text{W}$$

Commutation is sequential and sectors rotate, so each LS device sees 1/3 duty on average at high eRPM.

**Electrothermal stability check.** The loop gain is

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\, R_{th} = P\,\alpha\,R_{th}$$

and must be < 1. With 3.35 W × 0.0045 × R_th,j-a,eff, this requires R_th < 66 K/W, which is easily met. No thermal runaway.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The static leg contributes only commutation events at f_comm = 6·f_elec, which is negligible versus 48 kHz.

Operating parameters:

* V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full charge)
* I = 80 A
* f_sw = 48 kHz
* FD6288Q drive capability: ≈ 0.3 A source / 0.6 A sink (weak, so this dominates t_r / t_f)

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Overlap time is set by Miller charge and drive current:

* t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_source = 10 nC / 0.3 A ≈ 33 ns
* t_f ≈ 10 nC / 0.6 A ≈ 17 ns

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 50\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

### 3.2 Remaining switching terms

| Term | Formula | Assumed values | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | Q_rr = 40 nC | **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | Q_oss = 45 nC | **0.027 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 250 ns | **1.54 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | Q_g = 45 nC, V_GS = 10 V | **0.043 W** |

**Totals:**

* **Switching subtotal per channel: ≈ 4.0 W.**
* **Total per channel at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C: ≈ 24.5 W.**
* **Board total (4 channels): ≈ 98 W.** Add ≈ 1.5 W for the FC, regulators and MCUs.

### 3.3 Design levers (ranked by payoff)

1. **Dead time 250 → 100 ns.** Saves ≈ 0.9 W per channel. Only do this if FET switching is fast enough. Validate shoot-through on the bench with a current probe on the bus cap.
2. **Lower R_DS(on).** Each 0.25 mΩ removed saves ≈ 3.2 W per channel at 80 A.
3. **Stronger gate driver.** A 2 A-class driver cuts P_sw to ≈ 0.6 W. The cost is higher dv/dt, so watch BEMF comparator noise.
4. **Lower f_sw to 24 kHz.** Halves P_sw and P_dt, but increases current ripple on low-inductance motors.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state: not achievable at 80 A

Lumped thermal resistances:

* R_th,j-c(bottom) ≈ 1.5 K/W per FET
* R_th,c-b ≈ 2 K/W per FET (solder plus via array)
* R_th,board-air ≈ 3–5 K/W total for a 30×30 board, depending on prop-wash airflow (2–10 m/s)

At 98 W:

$$\Delta T_{b-a} = 98 \times 4 \approx 390\ \text{K}$$

This confirms that 80 A is **burst only**. The continuous rating is set by T_board ≤ 100 °C at T_amb = 40 °C:

$$P_{max,cont} = 60/4 = 15\ \text{W total} \Rightarrow \approx 3.5\ \text{W per channel}$$

Solving 2I²·1.4 mΩ + switching ≈ 3.5 W gives **I_cont ≈ 30 A per channel** with good airflow. Below that current, switching loss scales roughly linearly and is re-solved iteratively.
