# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These constraints shape every number below, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot runs the flight stack and has no motor-commutation firmware. Bi-directional DShot also implies a separate ESC MCU that replies with eRPM telemetry. The architecture is therefore:
   - **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot.
   - **ESC:** four AM32 or BLHeli_32-class MCUs (e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421), one per motor, each driving a 3-phase bridge.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows that the board's heat capacity, not the MOSFET rating, limits burst duration to roughly 1–2 s. The continuous rating under prop wash is about 15–25A per channel. I recommend specifying 80A as a ≤1 s peak with firmware-enforced I²t limiting.
3. **Device parameters are representative.** They describe a 60V, 3.3×3.3 mm-class N-FET suitable for 6S. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; the equations stay valid.
4. **The netlist is an architecture-level reference netlist.** It is not a DRC/ERC-verified, fabrication-ready design.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step drive, two legs conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D and the low side (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low side conducts continuously.

Instantaneous bridge conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Including PWM current ripple ΔI (from motor inductance L):

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

With L = 20 µH, the worst case is at D = 0.5: ΔI = 6.6 A, which adds 3.6 A². This is negligible against 6400 A².

Evaluated at 80 A:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | Per FET (avg, ×1/6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.0 mΩ | 25.6 W | 4.3 W |
| 100 °C | 2.9 mΩ | 37.1 W | 6.2 W |
| 125 °C | 3.2 mΩ | 41.0 W | 6.8 W |

Each FET conducts for one-third of the electrical cycle. During that interval it dissipates about 3× its average. At typical eRPM (commutation of 1–5 kHz), this ripple is filtered by the die and package thermal mass.

**Partial-throttle note:** I_ph = I_batt / D. An "80A battery current" at D = 0.6 means 133 A phase current. Define the burst rating as **phase** current.

### 2.2 Switching losses (PWM leg only)

**Turn-on**, with gate current I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl)/(R_drv,src + R_g) = 7.5/5 = 1.5 A:
- t_ri = Q_gs2 / I_g = 3.3 ns
- t_fv = Q_gd / I_g = 6.7 ns
- **t_on ≈ 10 ns**

**Turn-off**, with gate current I_g,off = V_pl/(R_drv,snk + R_g) = 4.5/4 = 1.125 A:
- t_rv = 8.9 ns
- t_fi = 4.4 ns
- **t_off ≈ 13.3 ns**

Energy per PWM period:

$$E_{on} = \tfrac12 V I t_{on} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(10\,\text{ns}) = 10.1\,\mu J$$

$$E_{off} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(13.3\,\text{ns}) = 13.4\,\mu J$$

$$E_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus} = 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 = 1.5\,\mu J$$

$$E_{oss} = C_{oss}V^2 = 800\,\text{pF} \times 25.2^2 = 0.5\,\mu J \quad (\text{both devices})$$

The sum is E_sw ≈ 25.5 µJ, so P_sw = E_sw · f_sw = **1.22 W**.

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.31\,\text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(50\,\text{nC})(12)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.058\,\text{W}$$

### 2.3 Total per channel at 80 A burst (T_j = 100 °C)

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 37.1 |
| Switching | 1.22 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) + copper (~0.3 mΩ path) | 3.5 |
| **Per motor** | **≈ 42 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 170 W** |

**Conclusion:** at 48 kHz, conduction dominates (about 88%). Lowering f_sw buys almost nothing. The levers are lower R_ds(on), parallel FETs, and heavier copper.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W per motor, so a loss of 42 W gives about 97.9%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power *density*.
