## 0. Architectural Clarifications

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In any 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running the commutation firmware, typically AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The math below shows it cannot be sustained. The model gives you the allowable burst duration.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.70 = **1.70 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-switch duty in 6-step commutation

Each phase leg spends 120° electrical high-active, 120° low-active, and 120° floating. With complementary PWM at duty D on the high-active leg:

$$P_{HS} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\frac{(1-D) + 1}{3}$$

The low-side switch carries synchronous-rectification current during the off-time of its own high-active window. It also conducts fully during its low-active window.

### 2.3 Worst-case burst (D ≈ 1, T_j = 125 °C)

$$I^2R = 80^2 \times 1.70\,\text{m}\Omega = 10.9\ \text{W}$$

- P_HS ≈ P_LS ≈ 10.9 / 3 = **3.6 W per MOSFET (averaged)**
- Two switches conduct at any instant, so per channel: P_cond = 2 × 10.9 = **21.8 W**
- **All four channels: ≈ 87 W**

### 2.4 Electrothermal feedback

R_ds rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The loop converges if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} = P_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} < 1$$

On a small board with poor heat sinking, this margin narrows quickly. That is one more reason the 80 A figure must remain a burst rating.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching loss, which is one high-side/low-side pair per channel.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Coss | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 1.7 W** |

**Key sensitivities:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw and with t_r + t_f. Drivers like the FD6288-class have limited source/sink current, so measure actual edges. They are often 30–50 ns rather than 20, which doubles this term.
- Dead-time loss is significant. Tuning dead time down from 100 ns to 50 ns roughly halves it, at the cost of shoot-through margin.

## 4. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 4.1 Totals

$$P_{ch} \approx 21.8 + 1.7 = 23.5\ \text{W/channel}$$

$$P_{total} \approx 94\ \text{W}\quad (\text{plus shunt and copper } I^2R\text{, see 4.5})$$

### 4.2 Junction rise per MOSFET

Assume R_th,jc ≈ 1.0 K/W (bottom pad). With 3.6 W average plus switching share:

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 4\ \text{K}$$

This is not the limit. The board-to-air path is.

### 4.3 Steady state: not achievable

Board convection resistance, assuming a 30×30 mm board (two faces), h ≈ 50–100 W/m²K under prop wash, and A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m²:

$$R_{th,ba} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 5.5 \text{–} 11\ \text{K/W}$$

A steady 94 W would require ΔT ≈ 500–1000 K. **Continuous 80 A is physically impossible on this form factor.**
