# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, and Thermal Analysis (Job #37)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

An STM32H743 does not commutate MOSFETs on a 4-in-1 design. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) generates the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The model below covers both layers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During each 60° sector, two legs conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D). Phase current flows through exactly one FET of this leg at all times.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole sector.

Because one FET in each leg always carries the current, conduction loss per channel is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: a 2306-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line) at D = 0.9 and V_bemf ≈ 21 V gives:

- ΔI_pp ≈ (4.2 × 0.9)/(15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 5.3 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 2.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A²

At low duty with high torque (punch-outs from low throttle), ripple can reach 20–30 A_pp. Recheck the correction there.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence (solved iteratively)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 0.90 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.36 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.36\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.4\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries current about 1/3 of the time. Its average loss is therefore ≈ P_cond,ch / 6 ≈ 2.9 W.

During its active sector, however, a single static-leg FET dissipates:

$$80^2 \times 1.36\ \text{mΩ} = \mathbf{8.7\ W}$$

This instantaneous figure matters at low eRPM. At high mechanical RPM, the FET's thermal time constant smooths it out. At near-stall (prop strike, or high-torque start), the full 8.7 W sits on one die.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz (per channel, one switching leg)

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr rises with di/dt and I_F. Real values are often 2–3× the datasheet figure, so budget **0.15 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Dead time is the dominant secondary loss. Cutting t_dt to 50 ns (AM32 allows this, if the driver propagation-delay mismatch permits) saves 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$
