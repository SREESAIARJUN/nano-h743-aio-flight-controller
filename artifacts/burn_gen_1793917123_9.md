# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #8)

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Notes

These points govern every number below. Change any of them and the numbers move.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU: an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, with bi-directional DShot600 in and eRPM telemetry out. The H7 does not commutate motors. Putting 4× FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz on the FC MCU would conflict with ArduPilot's scheduler and is not supported upstream.
2. **No component part numbers were provided.** I use a representative 40 V DFN5×6 MOSFET (parameters in §1). Re-run §2–§4 with the actual datasheet values before freezing the BOM.
3. **Battery:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum.
4. **Commutation:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz.
5. **Form factor.** "Nano" is taken as a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board area (≈9 cm²). §4 shows that **80 A × 4 simultaneous is a burst-only rating of roughly 2–3 s at this size.** This is the single most important finding in the package.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step

At any instant, two phases conduct. The current path is:
- the high-side FET of phase A (PWM duty D) or its synchronous low-side partner (1 − D), plus
- the low-side FET of phase B (fully on for its 120° sector).

So at every instant the phase current I flows through **exactly two R_DS(on) in series**, regardless of D. That holds because synchronous rectification replaces diode freewheeling.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot}$$

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period, and in the PWM leg the conduction is shared D : (1−D). Averaged over the electrical cycle, the loss spreads across all six devices:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}}{6}$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.9\times10^{-3} = 24.3\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = 4.05\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET is the one fully on for 120°. During its sector it dissipates

$$80^2 \times 1.9\,\text{m}\Omega = 12.2\ \text{W (instantaneous)}$$

The electrical period at typical burst eRPM is about 0.3–1 ms, which is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms to tens of ms). The die therefore sees close to the average, with a small ripple. Ripple estimate:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx 12.2\,\text{W} \times Z_{\theta JC}(t_{sector}) \approx 12.2 \times 0.1 \approx 1.2\ \text{K}$$

### 2.3 Self-consistency check (R_DS(on) vs T_j)

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}$$

The model is iterated in §4. At T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.92 mΩ, which matches the assumed R_hot.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only **one leg per motor** is hard-switching at a time (the PWM phase). All terms below apply per motor.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (sync FET body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead-time windows per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 Totals per motor at 80 A

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.30 |
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q_oss | 0.07 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Gate drive (driver) | 0.06 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈25.8 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | **≈103 W** |

**Interpretation.** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 6 % of the total. Conduction dominates. The levers that matter, in order:
1. Lower R_DS(on), or paralleled FETs. Two in parallel halve P_cond.
2. Lower f_sw. This gives little benefit here. Note that 48 kHz is already chosen for motor ripple and acoustics.
3. Shorter dead time. This gives a modest benefit.

### 3.7 PCB copper loss (frequently forgotten)

Battery-to-bridge current is I_bat ≈ D · I_ph per motor. At full throttle with 4 × 80 A, the input is ≈320 A
