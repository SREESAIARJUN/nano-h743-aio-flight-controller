**Critical feasibility flags. Read these first:**

1. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A per channel.** On a 20×20 mm board, 24 MOSFETs in 5×6 packages do not physically fit with adequate copper. 80 A-class 4-in-1 ESCs are 30.5×30.5 mm mounting at minimum, usually on a 36–45 mm board. I modeled 36×36 mm. If "nano" means a 20×20 mm mount, the realistic burst rating is roughly 35–45 A.
2. **Total current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A, about 7 kW at 22 V. No XT60 or XT90, nor most packs, will deliver that. In practice 80 A is a *per-channel short burst* rating. Total battery current is bounded by the pack and connector, typically 150–200 A peak.
3. **System partitioning.** The H743 does not drive the gates. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. Section 3 covers the H743 side.
4. **Netlists.** Section 4 gives reference-level topology netlists. They are not a production-verified BOM. Pin numbers for the H743 assume the LQFP100 package; re-verify them against your package.
