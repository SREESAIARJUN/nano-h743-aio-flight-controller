# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points need fixing before the math, because they change what gets modeled.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Bidirectional DShot means the FC sends throttle frames and receives eRPM telemetry from four separate ESC MCUs. The usual choice is an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not by the H743. The architecture is therefore:
   `H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU ──► 4× FD6288-class 3-phase gate driver ──► 24 MOSFETs`
2. **"Nano" plus 80 A is a burst-only rating.** A 20×20 or 30×30 mm board physically cannot reject the steady-state dissipation of four motors at 80 A. Section 2.5 quantifies this, so the spec must be expressed as a time-limited burst with firmware thermal derating.

All device parameters below are **representative assumptions**. Replace them with your actual MOSFET and driver datasheet values before release.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM. During each 60° electrical sector, current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

- During the PWM on-time, the high-side FET of the switching leg and the low-side FET of the return leg conduct.
- During the PWM off-time, the low-side FET of the switching leg (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of the return leg conduct.

The conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

The temperature-dependent on-resistance closes a thermal feedback loop:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad k \approx 0.006\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.28 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.28\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C the same calculation gives 10.2 W, which shows how strongly the loop matters.

Copper losses in the phase traces add to this. 2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of about 0.245 mΩ per square. A phase run of 3 squares therefore adds 0.74 mΩ, giving 80² × 0.74 m = **4.7 W per phase path**.

**Recommendations:**
- Use thick copper (3 oz or more on inner layers).
- Solder busbar wire directly onto phase pads.
- Keep phase runs under 1 square.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; the synchronous low-side FET switches with zero voltage. The loss terms per motor are:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40n·48k | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss energy | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Gate charge (mostly dissipated in the driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### 2.3 Total Loss at 80 A Burst (Hot)

| Scope | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 16.4 + 2.7 + ~3 (copper share) | **≈ 22 W** |
| Board, all four motors at 80 A | 4 × 22 | **≈ 88 W** |

Two consequences follow:

- **Battery bus current.** At full duty, the bus approaches 320 A. Battery leads, the XT60 connector, and the bulk capacitor ripple rating all become first-order problems.
- **Capacitor ripple current.** For a single motor, the RMS capacitor current is approximately I_ph·√(D(1−D)), which peaks at 40 A RMS at D = 0.5. You need several low-ESR polymer capacitors plus a low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads.

### 2.4 Junction Rise (Local Thermal Network)

The thermal path is: junction → case (Z_th,jc) → pad and copper spreading (R_sp) → board thermal mass (C_brd) → air (R_conv).

The heat is unevenly shared, so worst-case per-FET loss is roughly 22 W / 6 FETs × a 1.5 hotspot factor ≈ **5.5 W**.

| Path element | Value |
|---|---|
| Z_th,jc (transient, around 1 s) | about 0.5–1.0 K/W |
| R_sp (2 oz copper, filled-via array) | about 3 K/W |
| Resulting local junction-over-board rise | 5.5 × (≈4) ≈ **+22 K** |

### 2.5 Board-Level Transient and Steady State

**Convection resistance.** Assuming a 30×30 mm board with both sides exposed (A = 1.8×10⁻³ m²) and propwash h ≈ 80 W/m²K:

$$R_{conv} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 6.9\ \text{K/W}$$

**Steady state is impossible at full load.** At 88 W:

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 88 \times 6.9 \approx 600\ \text{K}$$

**Thermal capacitance.** The board (FR4, copper, and components, about 6 g) has C_brd ≈ 4 J/K, giving a time constant τ = R·C ≈ 28 s. The burst is therefore nearly adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_{brd}(t) = P R_{conv}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C} = 22\ \text{K/s at 88 W}$$

**Burst budget.** Assume T_j,max = 125 °C (derated from 150 °C), board start temperature 40 °C, and a 22 K local rise. That leaves 63 K for the board:

| Scenario | Allowed burst duration |
|---|---|
| All four motors at 80 A | t_burst ≈ 63 / 22
