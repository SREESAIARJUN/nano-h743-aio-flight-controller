## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in six-step commutation

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase has its low-side FET held on. The third phase floats for BEMF sensing.

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D. Combined, the leg conducts current I for the full period.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts current I for the full period.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, which is a useful property for burst analysis.

### 1.3 Parasitic resistances

$$P_{par} = I^2 \left(R_{shunt} + R_{Cu,phase} + R_{Cu,bus}\right)$$

Estimate copper resistance as R_Cu ≈ ρL/(wt). For 2 oz copper, sheet resistance is 0.25 mΩ/□. Assume about 1 square on each of 2 paralleled layers per path, giving about 0.25 mΩ total. **[A2]**

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **23.7 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. That is two FETs per channel, with one hard-switched edge pair per period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{1.5\text{ A}} \approx 8.7\text{ ns (ideal)}$$

Layout inductance and driver resistance typically stretch this to about 20 ns per edge. I use 20 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery, C_oss, and gate charge

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\text{ W}$$
$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs} = 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\text{ W}$$

Gate-charge loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Switching subtotal

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.04 + 0.61 \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W}$$

At 80 A, switching is about 10 % of total loss. **Conduction dominates.** Lowering f_sw below 48 kHz buys little thermally and costs current ripple and acoustic noise. Spend effort on R_DS(on) and copper.
