## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

**Park transform**, with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

The **inverse Park transform** is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Plant and decoupling

For a surface PMSM (L_d = L_q = L):

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q$$
$$v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda)$$

Here λ is the flux linkage and λ = Kt / (1.5 p).

Add decoupling feedforward to cancel the cross terms:

$$v_d = u_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = u_q + \omega_e(L i_d + \lambda)$$

After this, each axis behaves as a first-order plant:

$$G(s) = \frac{1}{Ls + R}$$

### 1.3 PI tuning by pole-zero cancellation

Use the controller C(s) = K_p + K_i/s with:

$$K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c$$

The PI zero (at R/L) then cancels the plant pole, and the closed loop becomes:

$$T(s) = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}$$

**Discrete-time limits.** One PWM period of computation delay plus the ZOH gives an effective delay of about 1.5·T_s. To keep ≥ 45° phase margin, choose ω_c ≤ 0.2·(2π f_pwm).
