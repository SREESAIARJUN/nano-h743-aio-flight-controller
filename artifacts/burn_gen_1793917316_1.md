# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Model

**Document scope:** Electro-thermal loss model, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

---

## 0. Up-Front Engineering Findings

These are stated first because they change the design.

1. **ArduPilot does not commutate motors.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not the H743. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally burst-limited, not continuous.** The math in §3 shows that a 20×20 mm board at 4×80A becomes adiabatic in about 1–3 s. You must define the burst duration and duty in the spec. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 2 FETs in parallel per switch, or derate to 80A for ≤2 s on one or two motors.
3. **Assumptions.** I don't have prior Job #10 documents, so I assume:
   - 6S LiPo (25.2 V max)
   - 40 V FETs in 5×6 PQFN, about 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C
   - 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM

   Substitute your actual BOM values into the equations.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (worst case) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current (burst) | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | R_DS(on) at 25 °C, per FET | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | Temperature coefficient | ≈ +0.6 %/K → ×1.5 at 110 °C |
| t_r, t_f | Current rise/fall at I_ph (driver ~1 A sink/source) | 20 ns, 15 ns |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery | 50 nC |
| C_oss,tr | Time-related output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 60 nC @ 10 V |
| t_dt | Dead time | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward drop at 80 A | 0.8 V |
| N_p | FETs in parallel per switch | 1 (also evaluated at 2) |

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation, current at any instant flows through one high-side switch of phase A and one low-side switch of phase B. With synchronous rectification, the PWM off-time current freewheels through the complementary FET instead of a diode. So there are always **two R_DS(on) in series**.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] \approx 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2 \frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{N_p} = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}\quad (N_p=1)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = \mathbf{9.6\ W}\quad (N_p=2)$$

**Per FET.** Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, split between its high- and low-side switches. Each switch is therefore on for about 1/3 of the cycle at full throttle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)/N_p^2 \cdot N_p$$

- N_p = 1: 3.2 W per FET (6 FETs → 19.2 W ✔)
- N_p = 2: 0.8 W per FET (12 FETs → 9.6 W ✔)

**Parasitics.** Add phase copper and the shunt as R_par. A 2 oz trace 3 mm wide × 15 mm long ≈ 1.25 mΩ
