## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several points in the brief need correcting before any modelling, because they change the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 frames to four ESC microcontrollers running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model below is split along that boundary:
   - **Domain A:** H743 → DShot600 (ArduPilot, timers, DMA).
   - **Domain B:** ESC MCU → gate driver → 24 MOSFETs (losses and thermal behaviour).

2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor phase current.** This implies about 320 A battery-side at full duty. That is not physically sustainable on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board. Section 3 quantifies how long it actually survives. If the spec means 80 A total (20 A per motor), every conduction-loss number below scales by (20/80)² = 1/16.

3. **Baseline parameters.** All values are parametric; the numbers are illustrative for a typical 3.3×3.3 mm DFN 40 V MOSFET.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| R_ds(on),25 | MOSFET at 25 °C | 1.6 mΩ |
| α | R_ds(on) tempco (normalized) | ≈1.5× at T_j = 100 °C |
| f_sw | PWM frequency (AM32) | 48 kHz |
| t_r + t_f | Switching transition, ~1 A gate driver | 30 ns |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC @ 10 V |

| D | PWM duty | 0 → 1 |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad k \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives R_ds(100 °C) ≈ 1.5 × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

In every 60° electrical sector, one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) device carry the phase current. The PWM leg alternates between HS (fraction D) and its complementary LS (fraction 1−D). The return leg's LS conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}$$

Including inductor ripple, with ΔI_pp the peak-to-peak phase ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

The ripple itself is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH), D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 21 V:
ΔI_pp ≈ (4.2 × 0.9)/(2 × 15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 2.6 A → negligible next to 80 A (adds 0.009 %).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,4\times} = \mathbf{122.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET average

Each of the 6 FETs per motor conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period (HS share D, LS share 1−D plus return duty), so the average is roughly even:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 5.1\ \text{W}$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation in a conducting device is 80² × 2.4 mΩ = **15.4 W**. This peak matters for the junction because the electrical period (≈0.3–1 ms) is comparable to the die's thermal time constant.

### 1.5 Copper/interconnect losses (often ignored, critical here)

Battery current at D ≈ 1 is I_bat ≈ 4 × 80 × D ≈ 320 A.

- **Power plane:** 2 oz, 4 parallel layers, ~15 mm path, 25 mm wide. Sheet resistance is ≈0.25 mΩ/□ per oz-layer, so the parallel stack is ≈31 µΩ/□. That gives R ≈ 0.6 × 31 µΩ ≈ 19 µΩ, and P = 320² × 19 µΩ ≈ **1.9 W**.
- **Battery pads plus XT60 lead:** ~0.5 mΩ, giving P ≈ **51 W**. The connector is in fact a bigger limit than the FETs. (XT60 is rated ~60 A continuous; even XT90 is marginal.)

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; the return leg is static within a sector. Per motor:

### 2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover) loss in the hard-switched HS device

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 C_oss charge/discharge

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
