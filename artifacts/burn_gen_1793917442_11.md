## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is AM32's default. At any instant, two phase legs are active:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw, and the low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on.

Phase current therefore always flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**, regardless of duty cycle. Dead-time intervals are handled separately in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.8\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 2.49\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FETs | 2 × 80² × 2.49 mΩ | **31.9 W** |
| Copper and shunt, R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |
| **Per channel** | | **≈ 35.1 W** |
| **Four channels at burst** | | **≈ 140 W** |

### 1.4 Distribution across FETs

Over one electrical revolution, each of the six FETs in a channel carries current for about one third of the time. When the electrical period is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (true above roughly 1,000 eRPM), the average loss per FET is:

$$\bar{P}_{FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6}\cdot 3\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = \frac{31.9}{6} \approx 5.3\ \text{W}$$

**Warning:** at stall or very low eRPM, one FET pair can carry the full 16 W each. Desync and stall protection must cap current in that case.

### 1.5 Electrothermal stability

Rising temperature increases R_DS(on), which increases loss. The loop gain is:

$$G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,JA}$$

At 80 A: ∂P/∂T = 2 × 6400 × 1.8 mΩ × 0.0045 = 0.104 W/K.

Thermal runaway occurs when G ≥ 1, i.e., when R_θ,JA ≥ 9.6 K/W per channel. This sets a hard upper bound on per-channel thermal resistance. Because the loop gain is significant, solve the operating point iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + R_\theta\, P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg's low-side FET is static.

| Loss mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 27 ns × 48k = **1.31 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48k = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.04 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V² · f | **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs/leg, mostly dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g · V_GS · f · 2 | **0.048 W** |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |
| **Four channels** | | **≈ 6.9 W** |

**Key conclusion:** at 80 A, conduction loss is about 20 times switching loss. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.85 W per channel, while gaining lower current ripple and quieter motors. 48 kHz is the right choice. Spend design effort on R_DS(on) and copper, not on switching speed.
