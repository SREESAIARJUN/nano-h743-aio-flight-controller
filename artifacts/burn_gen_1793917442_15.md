> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** "Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot physically host 24 FETs rated for 80 A. Even the 30×30 format shown here only supports 80 A as a **time-limited burst of about 3 s** (derived in §1.4). Continuous rating on this footprint is about 35–45 A per motor with prop-wash cooling. Treat 80 A strictly as a burst spec and enforce it in firmware (§1.5).

## 1. Power Stage Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). During each 60° sector, phase current *I* flows through:
- one high-side path: HS for duty *D*, and that leg's LS (synchronous) for 1−*D*, and
- one low-side FET that is fully on.

So at any instant, two R<sub>DS(on)</sub> drops are in series, independent of *D*:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 100 °C:
- R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**
- P<sub>cond</sub> = 2 × 80² × 1.45 m = **18.56 W per motor**

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts during 120° of the electrical cycle (1/3). The electrical frequency is about 3 kHz for a 14-pole motor at 25k RPM, which is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (> 10 ms). Averaging is therefore valid:

$$P_{FET,cond} = 18.56/6 \approx 3.1\ \text{W (average)}, \quad 9.3\ \text{W (instantaneous)}$$

**Copper and shunt add-on:**
- Phase and power traces, R<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 0.3 mΩ per motor path → 2 × 6400 × 0.3 m ≈ **3.8 W** (the factor of 2 accounts for out and return).
- Bus shunt, 0.1 mΩ, at the 320 A total burst → 10.2 W.
  - This is unacceptable. **Use per-motor current sensing via R<sub>DS(on)</sub> or a 0.2 mΩ shunt per ESC** → 1.3 W per motor.

### 1.2 Switching Losses at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. That is one hard-switched HS and one complementary LS per motor.

| Term | Formula | Values | Loss per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t<sub>r</sub>+t<sub>f</sub>)·f<sub>sw</sub> | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k | **1.452 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q<sub>rr</sub>·V·f<sub>sw</sub> | 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.048 W |
| C<sub>oss</sub> | ½·Q<sub>oss</sub>·V·f<sub>sw</sub> × 2 | ½·30 nC·25.2·48k·2 | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V<sub>F</sub>·I·2t<sub>d</sub>·f<sub>sw</sub> | 0.8·80·2·200 ns·48k | **1.229 W** |
| Gate drive | Q<sub>g</sub>·V<sub>GS</sub>·f<sub>sw</sub> × 2 | 60 nC·10·48k·2 | 0.058 W (in the driver) |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 2.82 W** |

Rise/fall time basis: t<sub>r</sub> ≈ t<sub>f</sub> ≈ Q<sub>gd</sub>/I<sub>gate</sub> ≈ 15 ns, assuming about 1 A driver peak and a 4.7 Ω gate resistor.

**Key insight.** Dead-time diode conduction is roughly equal to the overlap loss. Cutting t<sub>d</sub> from 200 ns to 100 ns (AM32 dead-time setting, validated on the scope with no shoot-through) saves about 0.6 W per motor.
