**Form-factor warning.** 80 A × 4 channels is not physically realistic on a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint. There is not enough copper cross-section, thermal mass or FET area. The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm** board. On a 20×20 board, plan for roughly 35–45 A burst per channel.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, each commutation step has three conducting devices:

- The PWM leg's high-side FET carries I for duty D.
- The PWM leg's low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) carries I for (1−D).
- The opposite leg's low-side FET carries I continuously.

The total loss is:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful design invariant.

**Temperature dependence of R_ds(on).** Use a temperature coefficient α ≈ 0.0045 /K:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Ripple contribution.** Assume phase-to-phase inductance L ≈ 20 µH. Ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2\times 0.25}{20\,\mu H\times 48\,kHz} \approx 6.6\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The corresponding RMS term is

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2+\Delta I_{pp}^2/12 = 6400 + 3.6$$
