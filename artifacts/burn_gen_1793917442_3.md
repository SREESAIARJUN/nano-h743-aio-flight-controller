# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFET gates. In every practical 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. The model below keeps these two domains separate.

```
6S LiPo ──┬── 4× [ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071) → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET → Motor]
          │            ▲ DShot600 bidir (1-wire, per motor)
          └── 5V/3V3 BEC → STM32H743 (ArduPilot) + IMU + Baro + OSD
```

All numeric results depend on component parameters I have to assume. Replace them with datasheet values for the actual BOM.

| Symbol | Assumed value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | DC bus |
| I_burst | 80 A per ESC channel, 5 s | Burst phase current |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS=10 V | 40 V PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 FET |
| α (TC) | 0.0055 /K → ~1.5× at 115 °C | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient |
| t_r, t_f | 10 ns each (at ~1.5 A gate drive) | Switching edges |
| Q_g | 40 nC @ 10 V | Gate charge |
| Q_rr | 30 nC | Body diode reverse recovery |
| C_oss(er) | 1.0 nF | Energy-related output capacitance |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body diode forward drop |
| t_dead | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_θJC | 1.2 K/W per FET | Junction to case |

**Feasibility warning:** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint is extremely aggressive. The numbers below show it is survivable only as a short burst with heavy copper and active airflow. It is not survivable as a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry phase current:

- **During the ON interval (D):** current flows through HS(phase A), the motor, then LS(phase B).
- **During the OFF interval (1−D):** current flows through LS(phase A), recirculates through the motor, then LS(phase B).

So the current always passes through exactly two channels, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Per-FET split: the HS FET sees $D \cdot I^2 R$ and its sync-rectifier partner sees $(1-D) I^2 R$. Commutation rotates these losses across all 6 FETs, so the average per FET is $P_{cond,ESC}/3$.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This creates a positive-feedback loop that the thermal model in section 3 must solve:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per ESC | P_cond, 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 80 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 16.6 W | 66.6 W |
| 115 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss (often forgotten)

Assume the phase path has 1.5 mΩ of total PCB and solder resistance (2 oz inner layers, vias, pads). Then:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 1.5\text{ mΩ} = 9.6\text{ W per ESC}$$

This is comparable to the FET loss. Use 2–3 oz copper, stitch with via arrays (about 1 A per 0.3 mm via for burst), and solder motor wires directly to the pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per ESC, there is one hard-switched commutation cell per PWM period. Its losses are:

**(a) V-I overlap (hard-switching edges):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

Edge time from gate charge: $t_{sw} \approx Q_{gd+gs2}/I_{g}$. For example, 12 nC / 1.2 A = 10 ns. Raising R_G to slow the edges for EMI scales this term linearly.

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.03\text{ W}$$

**(c) Body diode reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

Real Q_rr at 80 A and high di/dt is often 2–3× the datasheet value. Budget 0.1 W.

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD}\, I\, t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (HS + LS per period; dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET):**

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.038\text{ W}$$

**Switching total per ESC ≈ 1.75 W (≈ 7 W for all four).** At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by roughly 10:1.

The 48 kHz trade-off:
- Doubling to 96 kHz adds about 1.7 W per ESC.
- 24 kHz saves about 0.85 W but increases current ripple and audible noise.

The phase ripple current is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A pp.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation (all motors at 80 A)

| Source | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot, 115 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 1.75 W | 7.0 W |
| PCB copper | 9.6 W | 38.4 W |
| **Total** | **30.6 W** | **≈122 W** |

### 3.2 Junction-over-case (steady, per FET)

Per-FET average is 19.2/3 + 1.75/2 (switching lands on the HS-PWM device), giving about 7.3 W worst device.

Instantaneously, the device carrying the full 80 A conducts $I^2 R$ = 9.6 W during its 1/3 commutation window. Its thermal time constant (~ms) is much longer than one electrical period, so the average applies:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 7.3 \cdot 1.2 \approx 9\text{ K}$$

### 3.3 Board-level transient (two-node RC model)

P_tot ──►[C_board]──R_θ,conv──► T_amb

          T_board ── R_θJC ── T_j

$$C_{th}\frac{dT_b}{dt} = P(T_j) - \frac{T_b - T_a}{R_{\theta,BA}}$$
