# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC, Job #5: Design Basis and Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility

Three points shape the whole design.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz switching.** In a standard FC + 4-in-1 stack, the H743 sends DShot600 commands. The ESC-side MCUs (AM32 or BLHeli_32, on AT32F421 / STM32G071-class parts) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The DMA/timer analysis (§4) applies to the H743, and the loss analysis (§2–3) applies to the ESC power stage.
2. **80 A per channel is not physically achievable on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board.** Section 3.4 shows this quantitatively. The model below is parametric, so you can apply it to whatever form factor and FET is chosen. My recommended realistic targets are in §3.5.
3. **The component values are representative placeholders.** They are not part selections. Replace them with datasheet values before release.

## 1. Commutation Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

AM32 and BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM:

- At every instant, phase current flows through **exactly two conducting FETs**:
  - the high-side (HS) or synchronous low-side (LS) FET on the PWM leg, and
  - the continuously-on LS FET on the return leg.
- Each FET carries current for 120° of each electrical cycle, i.e. 1/3 of the time.
- Hard switching occurs on **one leg** at a time.

For ripple-free current I, the channel conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\right)$$

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

Adding inductor ripple Δi, the RMS current becomes:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}$$

At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~20 µH phase-phase), Δi is about 5 A. This adds only about 0.03 % and is neglected.

## 2. Loss Derivation per Channel at 80 A Burst

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds}(100) = 0.9 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 75) = 1.20\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.20\times10^{-3} = 15.36\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.20\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Switching (V–I Overlap) Losses

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 1.16\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitive, Recovery, Dead-Time and Gate Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | ½ C_oss,er V² f | 0.5·1.2n·635·48k = **0.018 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f | 0.8·80·100n·48k = **0.307 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2 Q_g V_gs f | 2·40n·10·48k = **0.038 W** |

### 2.4 Channel and Board Totals

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET-dissipated | 16.90 W | 67.6 W |
| Copper | 3.20 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈20.1 W** | **≈80.4 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates at 94 % of FET loss. Switching frequency matters far less than R_ds,on, copper, and thermal mass.

The sensitivity of FET loss to switching frequency is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 32\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs only about 0.77 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET Junction Rise

Each FET's average loss is about 16.9 W / 6 ≈ 2.8 W. Its instantaneous loss while conducting is about 7.7 W.

The electrical period at 30 k eRPM is 2 ms. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~10–50 ms), so the average loss can be used:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET} \cdot (R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta,solder+spread})$$

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 2.8 \times (1.5 + 3.5) = 14\ \text{K}$$

### 3.2 Board Lumped Thermal Model (RC)

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P_{tot} R_{\theta ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/(R_{\theta ba} C_{th})}\right)$$

Effective heat capacity C_th is built from the FETs, copper planes, FR4, and solder.
