**Scope:** Electrothermal loss model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The commutation MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The model below follows this partition.

**Feasibility verdict, stated up front.** A nano (20×20 mm) or 25.5 mm board **cannot dissipate 80 A continuous** per motor. The math in §3 shows that "80 A" on such a board is a **thermally-limited transient rating of roughly 1–3 s**. The design must enforce this limit in firmware and in ArduPilot current limiting. Do not treat 80 A as a steady-state rating.

**Assumed parameters.** Replace these with datasheet values once you select parts.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction D. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous freewheel path.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the 120° sector.

Therefore exactly **two FET channels carry I_ph at all times**, independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor (80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for one-third of the period, with the PWM/return split rotating. The average per FET is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET}\approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} \approx 3.1\text{ W at }100\,°\text{C}$$

At low duty with the motor near stall, the instantaneous distribution is uneven. The low-side return FET takes the full I²R for 120° of electrical rotation. Size the design to the **peak-sector** dissipation (≈ 6.2 W per FET during its sector) when the electrical frequency is low relative to thermal time constants.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt

$$P_{Cu}=I_{ph}^2 (R_{trace}+R_{shunt})$$

Take R_trace ≈ 0.3 mΩ (2 oz copper, 4-layer, short pours) and a shared 0.25 mΩ shunt.

At the burst condition, ~2.5 W per motor of PCB I²R spreads into the board. The shunt carries battery current, not phase current.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway criterion

The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA}<1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.001·0.006 = 0.077 W/K. This requires R_θJA < 13 K/W.

As shown in §3, the board is near this limit in still air. This is another reason 80 A must be a time-bounded burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **PWM leg** switches. That is one half-bridge per motor at any time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (inductive load)

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(100\text{n})(48\text{k})=0.31\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=0.06\text{ W}$$

This is a first-order estimate. The real value depends on di/dt, so measure it with a double-pulse test.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.058\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per motor

$$P_{sw}\approx 2.4\text{ W}$$

### 2.7 Frequency trade

P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, P_sw ≈ 1.2 W.

48 kHz is justified on low-inductance motors (< 20 µH) to limit current ripple. The ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is at D = 0.5: 25.2·0.25/(15 µH · 48 kHz) ≈ 8.75 A_pp. This adds roughly ΔI²/12 to I_rms², which is a ~0.1 % conduction penalty and negligible.
