## 0. Design-Basis Review

Before the math, several requirements conflict and need resolution. Otherwise the model will describe hardware that cannot be built.

| Issue | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **"Nano" + 80 A burst** | Each ESC channel needs 6 MOSFETs, so 24 FETs total. At 5×6 mm that is ~720 mm² of FET footprint alone, more than the whole 20×20 mm board (400 mm²). Commercial 20×20 AIOs are typically rated around 12–25 A per channel. | Model on a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** stack. 80 A is treated as a **short burst (seconds)**, not a continuous rating. Section 3 quantifies how short. |
| **Who drives the bridges** | The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each bridge is run by an ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071). The H743 runs ArduPilot and only *sends* bidirectional DShot600. | 48 kHz PWM is an **ESC firmware** setting. DShot DMA is an **H743 / ArduPilot** concern. The two are modeled separately. |
| **Bus voltage** | Not specified. | Assume **6S**: 25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal. Worst-case switching uses 25.2 V. |
| **MOSFET** | Not specified. | Parameterized generic 40 V, 5×6 mm device. Substitute datasheet values for the selected part (Section 1.1). |

All numbers below are **engineering estimates from stated assumptions**. They are not validated results. Thermal figures must be confirmed with thermocouple or IR testing on a prototype.
