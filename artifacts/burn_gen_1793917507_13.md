## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Key Conclusions

I have no prior design data for "Job #70", so this document fixes a baseline. Every assumption is tagged **[A#]** so you can substitute vendor data and re-run the equations.

| Tag | Assumption |
|---|---|
| A1 | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (design V_DS stress uses 30 V including ringing) |
| A2 | "80 A burst" = **80 A RMS phase current per channel**, ≤ 5 s, at full duty |
| A3 | Board: nano 20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm outline, 8‑layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes |
| A4 | Architecture: STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. **Each ESC channel has its own MCU** (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 never drives gates. |
| A5 | MOSFET: 40 V, R_DS(on),max = 0.9 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V, Q_g ≈ 90 nC, Q_rr ≈ 60 nC, Q_oss ≈ 80 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W. Six per channel, 24 total. |
| A6 | Gate driver: 3‑phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class), with t_r + t_f ≈ 35 ns at the chosen gate resistance |
| A7 | Commutation: 6‑step trapezoidal with synchronous complementary PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz and dead time t_dt = 100 ns per edge |

### Headline conclusions

1. **The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC‑firmware parameter (AM32/BLHeli_32), not an ArduPilot one.** ArduPilot only sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.
2. Per‑channel loss at 80 A is about **20 W**, so about **79 W** when all four channels burst together. A nano board cannot dissipate this continuously. Its continuous rating is thermally limited to about **25–30 A per channel** with prop‑wash airflow.
3. An all‑channel 80 A burst is **thermal‑mass limited to roughly 1–1.5 s**. A single‑channel burst can last about 4–5 s.
4. Bi‑directional DShot600 on the H743 is straightforward because DMAMUX allows any request on any stream. Put all four motors on **one advanced timer (TIM1 or TIM8)** with `BIDIR` tags and `DMA_NOSHARE`.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6‑step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **Leg X (PWM leg):** the high‑side FET conducts for duty D. The low‑side FET of the same leg conducts the freewheel current for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Leg Y (return leg):** the low‑side FET is on continuously for the whole sector.

So the current always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,\big[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,\big] = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

Phase‑current ripple adds a small term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) near full throttle, the voltage across the inductance is small and ΔI is about 2–5 A. The ripple term is therefore below 0.2 % and is neglected.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 0.9 mΩ × 1.47 ≈ **1.33 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.33\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.0\ W}
$$

The loss is **not shared evenly** between FETs. Over a full electrical cycle, each FET carries current for about one third of the time:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{17.0}{6}\times 2 \approx 5.7\ \text{W (time-averaged)}, \qquad P_{FET,inst} = 80^2 \times 1.33\text{ m}\Omega = 8.5\ \text{W}
$$

The FET therefore heats in 8.5 W pulses whose duration is set by the commutation period. That period is very short compared with the junction thermal time constant, so the average value is valid for junction temperature.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R (est.) | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battery pads to bus plane (2 oz ×2 layers, ~10 mm) | 0.15 mΩ | I_bat ≈ 4×I_ph×D ≈ 320 A (all channels) | 15 W (!) |
| Bus plane per channel | 0.2 mΩ | 80 A | 1.3 W |
| Current shunt (single, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 320 A | 25.6 W (!) |

**Design flag:** at a 4×80 A burst the battery current is about 320 A. That exceeds what a nano board and its battery leads can reasonably carry. Realistic burst ratings are "80 A on one or two motors" or "4×55 A". I recommend:
- splitting the shunt into four per‑channel 0.5 mΩ shunts, or using sense‑FET/R_DS(on) sensing;
- relabeling the product as "80 A single‑channel peak, 4×45 A burst".

The rest of this document carries 4×80 A as the worst case.
