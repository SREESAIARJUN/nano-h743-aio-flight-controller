# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #54): Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Spec Flags

This document is based on the assumptions below. Values marked **[A]** are placeholders. Replace them with the datasheet values of the parts you select before you freeze the design.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (8S is excluded, because 40 V FETs give too little avalanche margin) |
| Architecture | The H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 firmware. The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM) at f_sw = 48 kHz |
| "80 A burst" | **80 A per motor**, phase current, as a transient of 1–3 s or less |
| Form factor | "Nano" is interpreted as a 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, about 38 × 38 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper |
| MOSFET [A] | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3. R_ds(on) = 1.3 mΩ at 25 °C and V_gs = 10 V. Q_g = 35 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, C_oss = 1.1 nF, Q_rr = 30 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate driver [A] | FD6288Q (3-phase, 10 V V_cc, about 0.6 A source / 1.0 A sink) |

> ⚠️ **Spec flag (read first).** The model below shows that 4 × 80 A continuous is physically impossible on a nano-format board. At that load the stage dissipates about 120 W. The board can only sustain about 80 A for a few seconds before it saturates thermally.
>
> Before layout, the procurement spec must state:
> - the burst duration and duty cycle (for example, 80 A for 2 s, then a 20 % average), and
> - whether "80 A" means per motor or the total.
>
> The thermal capacitance model in section 3 gives a defensible burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in a 6-step synchronous bridge

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases are active:
- the **PWM leg**, where the high side switches at duty cycle D and the low side synchronously rectifies for (1−D), and
- the **return leg**, where the low side is held ON.

Neglecting ripple, the phase current I_ph flows through exactly two conducting FETs at every instant. Dead time is the exception and is handled in section 2.4. The conduction loss per motor is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

Each FET conducts during part of the 360° electrical cycle:
- High-side FET: (1/3)·D
- Low-side FET: (1/3)·(1−D) + 1/3

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ C
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × 1.38 = **1.79 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase-current ripple at 48 kHz, with a typical motor inductance of L_ph-ph ≈ 15 µH and D = 0.5, is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} = 8.75\text{ A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple term is negligible (+0.1 %).

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

| Path | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + via field + trace, about 0.25 mΩ per phase, two phases in path | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Shunt, if per-motor sensing (0.25 mΩ, average share about 1/3) | ~0.08 mΩ effective | 0.5 W |

### 1.5 Result per motor at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.79\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.9\ W}
$$

Adding copper and shunt gives **≈ 26.6 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The high-side FET of the PWM leg is hard-switched. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage because its body diode is already conducting.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (high side)

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g}
$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 4.5 V and R_g,total = 4.7 + 2 = 6.7 Ω:
- I_g ≈ 0.82 A, which the driver limits to about 0.6 A for turn-on
- t_r ≈ 12 nM/0.6 ≈ **20 ns**
- t_f ≈ **12 ns**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 32\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.55\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 1 = 0.5 \times 1.1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.017\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse-recovery loss (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature. Budget about 2× this, which gives **0.07 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Use t_dt = 150 ns per edge, with two edges per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times 80 \times 300\text{n}\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.92\ W}
$$

> **Design lever.** In AM32, reduce dead time to about 80 ns once you have scope-verified that there is no shoot-through. This saves about 0.45 W per motor.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FETs)

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs} = 35\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times 2 = 0.034\ \text{W/motor}
$$

### 2.6 Switching-frequency sensitivity

$$
P_{sw}(f) \approx (1.55+0.017+0.07+0.92)\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}} = 2.56\ \text{W}\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}}
$$

48 kHz costs about 1.3 W per motor more than 24 kHz. In return you get lower audible noise, lower current ripple and smoother low-throttle behaviour.
