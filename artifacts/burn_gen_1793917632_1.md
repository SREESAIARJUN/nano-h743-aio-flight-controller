# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bidirectional DShot DMA, and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three points would invalidate the design if left unresolved.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot/DShot system, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 frames. Each motor channel on the 4-in-1 ESC has its own small MCU (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 silicon). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The loss model below therefore belongs to the ESC power stage. The DMA model belongs to the H743.
2. **"Nano" plus "80A burst" is a physical contradiction.** On a 20×20 mm ESC, 24 MOSFETs at roughly 5×6 mm do not fit, and the board's thermal mass cannot absorb about 95 W (Section 3). I specify a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC.** If a 20×20 ESC is mandatory, rerate it to about 45–55A burst.
3. **All device parameters below are stated assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure. The equations are exact; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs carry current at any instant.

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET during duty D and the low-side FET during 1−D. With synchronous rectification, exactly one FET is always conducting.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

So two FETs are in the current path at every instant, regardless of duty cycle.

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25} \cdot k_T = 0.90 \times 1.6 = 1.44\ \text{m}\Omega$$

A more general form is $R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$ with α ≈ 0.006/K.

**Conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{pk}^2\, R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.44\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$

For a sinusoidal/FOC drive, use $I_{rms}^2$ per phase and sum over three legs instead.

**Copper path loss:** assume 0.5 mΩ of PCB copper from bus to phase pad and back (2 oz, 6-layer):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each switch conducts about 1/3 of an electrical cycle in 6-step:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\times 2 \approx 3.1\ \text{W (conduction only)}$$

The instantaneous loss while conducting is $I^2R = 9.2$ W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return-leg low-side FET is static within a commutation sector.

**Hard-switching overlap (inductive load, linear approximation):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**C_oss discharge (both FETs in the switching leg):**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### Per-Motor and Board Totals at 80A

| Loss term | W / motor | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 18.40 | 73.6 |
| PCB copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 | 5.8 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.17 | 0.7 |
| **Total** | **23.8** | **≈ 95 W** |

**Efficiency check.** At D ≈ 1, output power is about 80 A × 25 V ≈ 2 kW per motor. A 23.8 W loss gives about 98.8% efficiency. The trouble is the loss *density*, not the efficiency.

**Sensitivity.**
- Conduction dominates at about 77%. Paralleling two FETs per switch, as 30×30 80A designs do, cuts the conduction term by 4× per FET and 2× overall, bringing it to about 9.2 W per motor.
- Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz adds only about 1 W per motor. 48 kHz is affordable here.
