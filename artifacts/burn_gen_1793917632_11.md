## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phase legs are active at every instant. In each active leg, current flows through exactly one FET: the high-side (HS) during D·T_s, and the low-side (LS) during (1−D)·T_s.

Therefore exactly **two FETs conduct the phase current at all times**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple
With peak-to-peak ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

For r = 0.3, the ripple adds about 0.75%. It is negligible, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/^\circ\text{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.4 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET average
Each leg conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle, and the HS/LS split depends on D. Averaged over a full electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = \mathbf{4.1\ W}$$

At low throttle the LS FETs carry most of the loss. At high throttle the HS FETs do. Size every FET for the worst case: about 8.2 W during its 120° conduction window, filtered by the junction thermal mass.

### 1.6 Copper and shunt losses
- Assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective PCB copper plus solder per current path, giving 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ 1.9 W per channel.
- A common current shunt of 0.25 mΩ carrying the 320 A total gives 25.6 W. **That is unacceptable.** Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt or a Hall-effect sensor (e.g., ACS7xx-class, or a magnetic sensor placed over the battery bus), which reduces this to 10 W or less.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg hard-switches. Its HS FET carries the V·I overlap loss. Its LS FET sees dead-time diode conduction and reverse recovery.

### 2.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau (V_pl ≈ 3 V)

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10-3}{4.5} = 1.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{3}{4.5} = 0.67\ \text{A}$$

### 2.2 Transition times (Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 13 nC)

$$t_{on} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{1.56\text{ A}} = 8.3\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{0.67\text{ A}} = 19.5\ \text{ns}$$
