# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #39)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three issues change the shape of this design:

1. **The H743 should not run the motors.** Driving 4 × 3-phase bridges at 48 kHz with sensorless commutation needs 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead time, plus 12 back-EMF comparators. The H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 (2 × 3 complementary pairs) and lacks per-phase comparators. The standard and recommended architecture is:
   - **H743 = flight controller (ArduPilot)**, sending bidirectional DShot600 to
   - **4 × ESC MCUs** (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32), each generating 48 kHz complementary PWM for its own bridge.

   The rest of this document assumes that split.

2. **An "80 A burst" rating on a nano (20 × 20 mm) board is not physically sustainable.** The math below shows about 20 W dissipation per channel at 80 A. The board's thermal mass allows roughly **1–2 s** of 4-motor burst from a 40 °C start. Continuous capability is about **25–30 A/channel** with airflow. Even 30.5 × 30.5 mm boards rated 55–65 A depend on short bursts. For a genuine 80 A burst, plan for a 30.5 mm footprint with 5 × 6 mm FETs and a thermal pad or heatsink, or define "burst" as ≤1 s.

3. All device parameters below are **representative assumptions** (40 V shielded-gate MOSFET class). Before freezing the BOM, replace them with the datasheet values of the chosen parts.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, the current always flows through exactly two switch positions:
- the PWM phase (high-side during D, synchronous low-side during 1−D), and
- the low-side of the return phase.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.0\,[1+0.0045\cdot85] = 1.38\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Ripple correction.** The current RMS includes the PWM ripple ΔI ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_m f_sw). Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{15\mu\text{H}\cdot48\text{k}} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left[1+\tfrac{1}{12}(\Delta I/I)^2\right] = 6400\,(1.001) \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple correction is negligible at 80 A.

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(6406)(1.38\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400\cdot0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Hottest-device worst case.** At a given instant the 17.7 W is shared by two FETs, about 8.85 W each. Each switch position conducts one-third of the electrical cycle, so the cycle-averaged loss is about 2.95 W per FET. The instantaneous 8.85 W matters only at low eRPM, i.e., stall or punch-out from zero.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the PWM phase only.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r+t_f) f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30n·48k | 1.452 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.060 W |
| Coss | ½ Q_oss V f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f I (2 t_dt) f_sw = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2 Q_g V_gs f_sw | 0.058 W |
| **P_sw total per channel** | | **≈ 2.21 W** |

Design levers:
- Switching loss is linear in f_sw.
- Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify this against the driver's propagation skew and the FET's t_d(off).
- Reducing f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel at full load, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.

## 4. Total Dissipation

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 17.7 W | 70.8 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| ESC MCU, driver, LDO, FC | — | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | **23.1 W** | **≈ 94 W** |

Inverter efficiency at 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 1.76 kW per channel is about 98.7%, which looks good. The problem is not efficiency; it is that the absolute watts must be removed from a very small board.
