## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FET channels are always in the current path:
- During the PWM on-time: HS of phase A and LS of phase B.
- During the off-time: LS of phase A (sync rectifying) and LS of phase B.

Therefore, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per FET, each leg's HS+LS pair conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, split by duty D:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,D,\qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}(T_j)\ \text{(low-side return leg)}$$

The worst-case single FET at D → 1 (full-throttle burst) is:

$$P_{cond,FET,max} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (closed-loop)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{T_j-25},\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This is a fixed-point problem, since T_j depends on P, which depends on R_ds(T_j):

$$T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{\theta}(t)\,\Big[\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha)^{T_j-25} + P_{sw,FET}\Big]$$

Iterate from T_j = T_ref. It converges in about 4 iterations if the loop gain $\partial T_j/\partial T_j < 1$. If the gain is ≥ 1, the result is **thermal runaway**, and the design is invalid at that operating point.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j ≈ 125 °C (R_ds ≈ 1.5 × 1.8 = 2.7 mΩ)

| Quantity | Single FET per switch | 2× paralleled per switch |
|---|---|---|
| Per channel conduction | 80² × 2 × 2.7 m = **34.6 W** | **17.3 W** |
| Worst FET | 80² × 2.7 m / 3 = **5.76 W** | **1.44 W** (per die, I/2 each) |
| 4 channels simultaneously | **138 W** | **69 W** |

Add copper loss. For a 2 oz outer layer (≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq), about 8 squares in the phase path gives ≈ 2 mΩ (and at least 0.5 mΩ even with heavy stitching). At 80 A that is 0.5–2 mΩ × 6400 = **3–13 W per channel**. Inner-layer pours and via stitching are mandatory.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The sync-rectifier FET soft-switches through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r \approx t_f \approx 10\ \text{ns}$$

Real gate resistance and driver droop typically double this. Use **t_r + t_f = 20–40 ns**.

### 2.2 Loss components (per channel, I = 80 A, V = 25.2 V, f = 48 kHz)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r+t_f) f | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.97 W** (1.94 W at 40 ns) |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f | 50 n·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss V² f (×2 FETs) | 0.5·1.5n·635·48k·2 = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD I · 2t_dt · f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipates) | Q_g V_gs f × 2 | 40n·10·48k·2 = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.7 – 2.7 W/ch** |

**Key insight.** At 80 A, conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 15:1. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per channel. Lowering R_ds(on) or paralleling FETs is the lever that matters.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Cauer/Foster network per FET

```
P_FET ──[R_jc 1.5 K/W | C_j ~0.02 J/K]──[R_cs (pad/solder) 0.3 K/W]──
       ──[R_spread (PCB Cu) ~15–25 K/W per FET local]──[C_board]──[R_conv]── T_amb
```

The local spreading resistance only becomes reachable after about 0.5–1 s. Below that, the copper's thermal mass under the FET acts as the heatsink.

### 3.2 Board-level adiabatic burst check (2 s)

**Board heat capacity.** A 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board weighs about 4 g. With c ≈ 0.6 J/g·K (FR4 plus Cu mix):

$$C_{board} \approx 2.5\ \text{J/K}\ (20\times20),\qquad \approx 6\ \text{J/K}\ (30.5\times30.5,\ \sim10\ \text{g})$$
