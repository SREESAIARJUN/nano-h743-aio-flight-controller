**Scope:** Power-stage loss model, transient and steady thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and reference netlists.

**Feasibility finding:** The model shows that 80A × 4 simultaneously cannot be sustained on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm nano board for more than about 1–2 s. Section 4 quantifies this. Treat "80A" as a per-channel, short-burst rating, not a simultaneous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary (synchronous) commutation

In any 60° sector, two phases are active. For example, A is PWM'd high and B is held low.

- **PWM-on interval (duty D):** current flows through A_HS and B_LS.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** current freewheels through A_LS (synchronous, after dead time) and B_LS.

At every instant, exactly **two FETs carry the full I_m**. This gives:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

### 1.2 Distribution per FET over one electrical cycle

**Low-side FET of a phase:**
- It is the "return" FET for 120° (conducting 100%).
- It is the synchronous freewheel FET for 120° (conducting 1−D).

$$
P_{LS} = I_m^2 R \left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right]
$$

**High-side FET of a phase:**

$$
P_{HS} = I_m^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}D
$$

The low side runs hotter at low duty. Place low-side FETs closest to the thermal mass (GND pour).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

Solve self-consistently: T_j drives R, and R drives P.

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel = 2·80²·R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 16.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.40 mΩ | **17.9 W** |

### 1.4 Additional series resistances (often forgotten)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| PCB copper, ~0.3 mΩ per path at 2 oz | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt, if 0.5 mΩ on the DC bus | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W per channel |

The shunt loss is unacceptable here. Use a single 0.1–0.2 mΩ common-bus shunt or a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS772/TMCS1100-class) instead.

**Conduction total per channel @ 125 °C ≈ 17.9 + 1.9 ≈ 19.8 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; one hard-switching transition pair occurs per PWM period.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I_m·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge | ½·C_oss·V_bus²·f_sw = ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I_m·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.058 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.42 W** |

**Sensitivity:** Every 10 ns of extra t_r + t_f adds 0.48 W. Halving dead time to 25 ns saves 0.15 W.

At 48 kHz, switching is about 7% of the loss and conduction dominates. The higher PWM frequency is justified only for motor current ripple and acoustic reasons; it does not meaningfully affect the thermal budget.
