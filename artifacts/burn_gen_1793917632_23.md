## 0. Basis of Design and Critical Caveats

### 0.1 What "80A" can mean on a nano board

Read this before the numbers. A nano-format 4-in-1 ESC (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) cannot dissipate 80 A per motor continuously. The math in §3 shows that the steady-state limit is about **15–20 A per channel** at realistic prop-wash cooling. "80 A burst" is therefore a **time-limited rating**. This document derives that time limit; it does not assume the rating is valid.

### 0.3 Architectural clarification

The STM32H743 **does not generate the 48 kHz PWM**. The topology is as follows:

- The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- The ESC MCUs drive the bridges at 48 kHz. Suitable choices are:
  - AT32F421 running AM32, or
  - EFM8BB21 running Bluejay.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver, which drives the MOSFETs.

The two domains are electrically linked only by the signal line and ground.

### 0.4 System-level current

Four motors at 80 A means **320 A battery current**. This exceeds the XT60 rating (~60 A continuous / 180 A peak) and is marginal even for an XT90 with 10 AWG leads. On a nano airframe, a realistic simultaneous burst is far lower. Specify the per-channel burst rating separately from the aggregate.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous complementary PWM, the following holds at any instant:

- Two phases carry current.
- The non-PWM leg has one FET fully on.
- The PWM leg alternates between its HS and LS FET at duty D.
  - During dead time, the body diode conducts instead.

The current therefore always passes through **two channels in series**, regardless of D (dead time excluded):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\cdot(1 - 2t_{dead}f_{sw})
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.6 \text{ at } 125^\circ C
$$
