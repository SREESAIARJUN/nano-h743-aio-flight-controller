# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Loss Model, Thermal Model, Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET, gate driver or board stack-up has been specified for Job #44. Every number below therefore comes from **declared baseline parameters**. These are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts used on 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm ESCs. The equations are general: substitute datasheet values before sign-off.

The architecture is split in two:
- The **STM32H743 is the flight controller**. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- The **48 kHz switching is done by the ESC MCU**. The baseline is AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32 firmware, driving three half-bridges per motor.

The H743 never touches the gate drive.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Reduction

The model assumes six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous complementary PWM, which is the AM32 default. Take phase A as PWM'd and phase B as low-side-on within one 60° sector:

- **ON interval (duty D):** current path is HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** current path is LS_A (synchronous freewheel) → motor → LS_B.

In both intervals exactly **two FETs carry the full phase current**. Ignoring dead time:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle. It is spread across six FETs. Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, and the PWM'd high-side carries only the D fraction.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.40 = **1.68 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.68 mΩ | **21.5 W** | 86.0 W |

### 1.4 Electrothermal Fixed Point

Losses rise with temperature, and temperature rises with losses. The equilibrium is found by iteration:

$$
T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{\theta}(t)\, P\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)
$$

The loop is stable when the gain is below one:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, Z_\theta = 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_\theta < 1
$$

At 80 A this gives 0.0614 W/K per motor. The steady-state limit for Z_θ (per motor) is therefore about 16 K/W. A loaded board sits well below that, so there is **no runaway at the electrical level**. The real limit is energy, covered in §3.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The synchronous low-side switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS via body diode), apart from dead-time effects.

| Term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V_bus I (t_r+t_f) f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | **1.94 W** |
| C_oss charge | Q_oss V_bus f_sw = 40e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw = 50e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 Q_g V_drv f_sw | 0.058 W (dissipated mainly in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.72 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A. Moving to 96 kHz costs about 2.7 W. Dead-time loss is the second-largest term, so trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation skew first.

### Combined Burst Loss (T_j = 125 °C)

$$
P_{motor} = 21.5 + 2.72 \approx 24.2\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{ESC,total} = 4 \times 24.2 \approx \mathbf{97\ W}
$$

Additional loss terms:
- **Bus shunt:** with a single 0.1 mΩ bus shunt carrying 4 × 80 A = 320 A, P = 320² × 1e-4 = **10.2 W**. For this reason, use 2 × 0.2 mΩ in parallel, rated 3 W each or higher, as a burst-only rating.
- **Copper traces/planes:** budget 3 to 5 W at 320 A for a 2 oz 6-layer stack. This must be verified with a DC-IR simulation.

**Design figure: ≈ 110 W total burst dissipation.**
