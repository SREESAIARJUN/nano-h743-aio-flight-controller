# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): Power-Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

**Design-review position:** An 80 A per-channel burst on all four channels at once cannot be sustained on a nano-class board for more than a few seconds. The model in Part 2 puts the limit at about **4 s** under typical prop-wash cooling. The rating is defensible only as a short-burst, thermally-derated spec. Read Part 2.6 before committing to a datasheet number.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**ArduPilot does not commutate motors.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 that *receives* DShot and returns eRPM telemetry. The usual part is an STM32G071 or AT32F421, either one per motor or one MCU driving all four. An "ESC controlled directly by the H743" is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification
In each 60° commutation sector, two phase legs are active:
- During PWM **on**: the high-side FET (phase X) and the low-side FET (phase Y) conduct.
- During PWM **off**: the complementary low-side FET (phase X) and the low-side FET (phase Y) conduct.

So exactly **two FETs are always in series** with the phase current, independent of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:
$$R_{ds} = 1.0\,\text{mΩ} \times [1 + 0.006 \times 85] = 1.51\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 RMS current including PWM ripple
Peak-to-peak ripple current:
$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is D = 0.5:
$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\,\text{A}_{pp}$$

RMS current:
$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.1%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz.

### 1.4 Conduction loss per channel and per FET
Per channel:
$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 6406 \times 3.02\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

Each of the six FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period:
$$P_{cond,FET} = 19.3 / 6 = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ (average)}$$

During its 120° conduction window, a single FET's instantaneous loss is 9.7 W. This matters for transient junction heating at low eRPM, where the conduction window is long.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step operation, only one leg is PWM-ing at a time, so there is **one hard-switched high-side/low-side pair per channel**.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap loss (high-side FET)
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (low-side FET, after dead time)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

Most of the actual Q_rr energy is dissipated in the high-side FET during its turn-on.

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$
