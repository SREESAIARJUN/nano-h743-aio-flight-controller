# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Electro-Thermal Model, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Architecture Correction and Feasibility Gates

Two issues need settling before the math, because they change the design.

**Gate 1: The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.**
ArduPilot is a vehicle-control stack. It does not run six-step or FOC commutation. The workable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                          └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6 MOSFETs per motor (24 total)
```

The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. Everything in §3 applies to the H743 side only.

**Gate 2: 80 A burst on a "nano" footprint is thermally marginal.**
The model below shows a 20×20 mm board reaches its MOSFET limit in about 1–2 s at 80 A per channel with all four channels loaded. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s are typically rated about 35–45 A burst. My recommendation:
- Define "80 A burst" as **≤1 s, single-digit duty cycle**, or
- Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint.

**Assumptions.** All device parameters below are representative placeholders for a 40 V, about 1 mΩ class PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 MOSFET. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected part before sign-off. Assumptions are tagged **[A]**.

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model (per channel = 1 motor = 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Conduction loss (six-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM with synchronous rectification)

In each 60° sector, two legs are active:
- **Source leg (PWM'd):** the high-side conducts for D, and the low-side freewheels for (1 − D).
- **Sink leg:** the low-side is on 100%.

Summing the active devices:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification. Adding copper:

$$P_{cond,tot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,[R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}]$$

At 80 A and 100 °C:
- FET: 2 × 6400 × 1.74 mΩ = **22.3 W**
- Copper: 2 × 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **6.4 W**

### 2.3 Switching loss (one hard-switched leg per sector)

**Transition times.** These are dominated by the Miller charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{8\text{n}\cdot 6}{6.5} \approx 7.4\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{pl}} = \frac{48\text{n}}{3.5} \approx 13.7\text{ ns}$$

Budget 40 ns total to cover layout inductance and driver sink/source asymmetry.

**Loss terms at 48 kHz:**

| Term | Formula | Value at 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·40n·48k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·100n·48k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 6·Q_g·V_drv·f | 0.115 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.42 W** |

The overlap term is the worst case at mid-duty. At D → 1 (true full throttle), switching events vanish but conduction is unchanged.

### 2.4 Channel and board totals at 80 A burst

$$P_{ch} \approx 22.3 + 6.4 + 2.4 \approx 31\text{ W}$$

$$P_{board} = 4 \times 31 \approx \mathbf{124\text{ W}}$$

This excludes the shunt (§2.6) and bus-capacitor ESR.

### 2.5 Electro-thermal fixed point and runaway criterion

Iterate the following until T_j converges:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

The loop is stable (no thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} = 2I^2R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} < 1$$

Applying this per FET, with peak instantaneous loss I²R_ds ≈ 11.1 W and R_th,j-pcb ≈ 3 K/W **[A]**:
- Loop gain = 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.006 × 3 ≈ 0.14. This is stable.
- The average per-FET loss is about 31/6 ≈ 5.2 W (copper excluded: about 4.1 W).
- Junction rise above the PCB is therefore about **12–16 K**.
