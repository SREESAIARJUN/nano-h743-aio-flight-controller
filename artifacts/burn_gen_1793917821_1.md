# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Engineering Design Package

**Covers:** 80 A burst loss and thermal model, 48 kHz switching losses, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The spec mixes three domains that need clear boundaries:

| Function | Device | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 master, eRPM capture | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | Runs ArduPilot |
| Motor commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM reply | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) | Runs AM32. The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz PWM. |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 250 ns built-in dead time) | One per channel |
| Power stage | 24× 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 5×6, R_DS(on) ≈ 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, 10 V V_GS | 6 per channel |

**Feasibility flags (read before layout):**

1. **Form factor.** 80 A per channel cannot be done on a 20×20 "nano" board. Twenty-four 5×6 FETs plus the current-carrying copper require at least a **30.5×30.5 mm (~36×36 mm outline), 8-layer, 2 oz** board. On 20×20 with 3×3 FETs, a realistic rating is about 35–45 A burst.
2. **Burst rating is time-limited.** Section 2.6 shows that "80 A on all four channels" is about a **5-second** rating under prop wash, not a continuous one.
3. **Bus voltage.** The design targets 6S (25.2 V max). With 40 V FETs, a 6S system leaves about 37% margin for ringing and regenerative braking spikes. That margin is acceptable only with the snubbing and bulk-cap measures in §2.5.

## 1. Electrical Operating Model

The ESCs use trapezoidal 120° commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 does by default.

During any 60° commutation sector, current I (phase current) flows through:
- the high-side FET of leg A for duty D,
- the low-side FET of leg A (synchronous rectification) for 1−D,
- the low-side FET of leg B for 100% of the sector.

Variables:
- I = phase current (A), set to 80 A burst
- V_bus = 25.2 V
- f_sw = 48 kHz (T_sw = 20.83 µs)
- D = PWM duty; battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,D + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)(1-D) + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is **independent of duty**. This is why low-throttle, high-current conditions (prop stall, punch-outs from hover) are just as thermally severe as full throttle.

**Temperature dependence:**

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

**PCB copper path** (pads, vias, phase pours, about 0.3 mΩ effective per channel):

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only leg A's high-side/low-side pair switches. Assumed values: t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns (FD6288 at ~1.5 A source/sink with 10 Ω gate resistors), dead time t_dt ≈ 300 ns total per edge, V_f ≈ 0.8 V, Q_rr
