## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C with the max-spec part:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times 1.425 = 1.71\text{ mΩ}$$

### 2.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, current flows through two conducting legs:

- **Active (PWM) leg:**
  - The high-side switch conducts for fraction D.
  - The complementary low-side switch conducts for fraction (1 − D).
  - Either way, exactly one device carries I.
- **Return leg:** one low-side switch conducts continuously.

The instantaneous conduction path is therefore always two MOSFETs in series, independent of D (ignoring dead time).

### 2.3 Ripple correction

Phase current has a triangular ripple ΔI at f_sw:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Typical 5-inch motor values are L_ph ≈ 15 µH and V_bus − E ≈ 5 V at D = 0.9. This gives ΔI ≈ 6.25 A, which adds only about 0.05 % to I_rms². It is negligible at 80 A.

### 2.4 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.71\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

At 25 °C typical (1.0 mΩ) this falls to 12.8 W. Because loss rises with temperature, the system has positive thermal feedback (see §5.3).

### 2.5 Shunt and copper losses (frequently neglected)

**Shunt:**

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = 1.6\text{ W}$$

**Copper trace resistance:**

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(100\,°C) = 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot m$$
