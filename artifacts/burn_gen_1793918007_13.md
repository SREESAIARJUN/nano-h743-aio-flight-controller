# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package (Job #5)

This package covers the power-stage loss model, thermal model, DShot/DMA timer allocation, and reference netlists.

**Important finding:** At 6S, 80 A per motor (all four at once) dissipates about 130 W total. A nano-size board can handle this only as a time-limited burst of about 2 s, not as a sustained rating. Section 4 quantifies this, and Section 7 lists the design changes that would extend it.

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### System partitioning
The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors directly. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) that drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wire.

```
STM32H743 ──DShot600 (bidir, 1 wire/motor)──► ESC MCU ×4 ──► 3-ph gate driver ×4 ──► 6 MOSFETs ×4 (24 total)
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### Current paths
With complementary (synchronous) 6-step commutation, each 60° sector has:

- one high-side FET switching at duty D,
- its complementary low-side FET conducting for (1 − D),
- the return-leg low-side FET fully on for 100% of the sector.

### Loss per motor

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result does not depend on D, because synchronous rectification keeps one FET conducting in the switching leg at all times.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega = 2 \times 13.44 = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### Per-FET loss (rotation-averaged)
Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors:

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 26.9/6 \approx 4.5\text{ W}
$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is the fully-on return leg is 13.4 W.

### Copper loss

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

On nano boards this loss is often underestimated. Use at least 8 layers of 2 oz copper on power planes and stitch them heavily with vias.

### Self-heating iteration
R_hot depends on junction temperature T_j. Iterate until it converges:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005/\text{K}
$$

Starting at T_j = 125 °C gives k_T = 1.5, which is consistent with the burst result in Section 4.
