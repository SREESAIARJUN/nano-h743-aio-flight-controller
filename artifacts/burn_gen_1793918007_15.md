# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Issues

I don't have the Job #26 design files, BOM, or stack-up. Every component value below is a **stated assumption**. Replace these with your datasheet values; the equations stay the same. Three architectural points need attention before any numbers are trusted:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four (or one) dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 parts running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, and they do the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The model below assumes that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25×25) board is thermally marginal to unrealistic.** At 6S, 4 × 80 A is about 7.1 kW of electrical power. Section 4 shows that the realistic burst window is about 1 s, and only when the board starts cool.
3. **48 kHz is a high PWM frequency for an 80 A stage.** Switching and dead-time losses are modest in absolute terms, but they are not negligible. They are quantified in Section 2.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths
These equations assume trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side switch during the duty fraction D and through the low-side switch (synchronous rectification) during 1 − D.
- **Static leg:** the low-side switch is fully on.

There is always exactly one device per leg in the current path, so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2\left[D\,R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D)\,R_{LS}(T_j) + R_{LS}(T_j)\right] \approx 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (electro-thermal feedback)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Because P depends on R_DS(T_j) and T_j depends on P, the operating point must be solved iteratively (see Section 4.4).

### 1.3 Ripple correction
The worst-case peak-to-peak ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ A_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \Rightarrow +0.06\%$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one real benefit of the high switching frequency.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A
Two reference junction temperatures are evaluated:

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | P_cond for 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C (cold start) | 1.20 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 110 °C | 1.76 mΩ | **22.5 W** | 90.1 W |

### 1.5 Parasitic copper and interconnect
The FET losses are not the whole conduction budget:

- Phase pad plus internal planes: about 0.3 mΩ per phase path, giving 2 × 6400 × 0.3 m = 3.8 W per motor.
- Battery input path (pads, XT60 pigtail, 20 cm of 12 AWG ≈ 1.0 mΩ total, carrying 320 A): 320² × 1.0 m = **102 W**.

The battery input path is the single largest loss term at full burst. Most of it is dissipated in the cable rather than on the board, but the pads still take part of it. A 320 A input is not plausible for a nano-class build, which is a strong signal to re-examine the 80 A spec.
