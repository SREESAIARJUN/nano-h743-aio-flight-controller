## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points in the brief need correcting before any numbers make sense.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run the 48 kHz PWM and commutation, for example AM32 on STM32G071 or AT32F421. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCUs. The DMA allocation analysis applies to the H743.
2. **80 A per channel in a "nano" (20×20 or 30×30) footprint is a burst-only rating.** The math in §3 shows it is thermally sustainable for about 2 s. Treat any "80 A continuous" claim at this size as marketing.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C, k_T ≈ 1.47. Use the worst case:

$$R_{hot} = 1.3 \times 1.47 \approx 1.91\ \text{m}\Omega \;\;(\text{round to } 1.95\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ for margin})$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector, one leg is PWM-switched (HS for D, LS for 1−D) and one leg's LS is fully on. The phase current therefore always flows through two FETs in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.95\text{m} = \mathbf{24.96\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3} \approx 4.2\ \text{W (average)}, \qquad 12.5\ \text{W (instantaneous peak while conducting)}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

**Phase path** (per channel, 2 oz × 4 power layers, about 1.5 mm effective length):
- R_Cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ, giving P ≈ 0.96 W.

**Low-side shunt** (if used, 0.25 mΩ):
- P = I_bus² × R. At D ≈ 0.6, I_bus ≈ 48 A, so P ≈ 0.58 W.
