# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis and DShot Allocation

**Job #1, Rev A (engineering draft). All component values are reference picks that must be validated against the chosen vendors' datasheets before layout.**

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These points change the shape of the design, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs four independent motor-control MCUs, such as 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.
   - Bidirectional DShot is supported by AM32 and Bluejay.
   - 48 kHz PWM is a selectable AM32 setting.
2. **"Nano" and "80 A per channel" do not coexist thermally.** Section 3 shows that a 30×30 mm board cannot sustain 4×80 A, or even 1×80 A, for more than about 1 to 2 s. The spec is therefore restated:
   - **80 A = per-channel peak burst of ≤1 s.**
   - **Continuous rating ≈ 35 to 45 A per channel**, depending on airflow.
   - The math below is what forces this restatement.
3. **Power system:** 6S LiPo, 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal. MOSFETs must be rated 40 V because of regenerative and inductive overshoot.

## 1. System Block Definition

```
VBAT(6S) ──┬── TVS + 4×(470µF low-ESR bulk, off-board) + 8×10µF/50V X7R local
           ├── 4× ESC power stage (3 half-bridges each = 24 FETs)
           ├── 12× gate driver phases (3-phase drivers, e.g. FD6288Q-class, 1 per ESC)
           ├── Shunt (single low-side, 0.25 mΩ) → current amp → FC ADC
           └── Buck 25V→5V/3A (FC + peripherals) → LDO 3.3V (H743, sensors)

STM32H743VIT6 (FC) ── 4× DShot600-BIDIR ──> 4× AT32F421 (AM32, 48kHz PWM)
```

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (worst case) | V_DC | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V |
| R_DS temperature coefficient | α | 0.007 /K (R_DS ≈ 1.7× at 125 °C) |
| Gate charge | Q_g, Q_gd | 40 nC, 7 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 20 nC |
| Gate drive | V_GS | 10 V, ~1 A source/sink |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Board copper path (phase + bus share) | R_Cu | 0.5 mΩ |

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- **During t_on:** high-side of phase A and low-side of phase B conduct.
- **During t_off:** the low-side of phase A rectifies synchronously, and the low-side of phase B stays on.

Two R_DS(on) are therefore in series at every instant, independent of duty cycle:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.2 × 1.70 = 2.04 mΩ:

- P_FET,cond = 2 × 6400 × 2.04 mΩ = **26.1 W**
- P_Cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**

### 2.3 Switching Loss

Only one half-bridge per commutation state is PWM-switched.

**Transition times** are estimated from Miller charge and gate current:

$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} + \text{(V}_{th}\text{ rise share)} \approx 20\,\text{ns}
$$

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{DC} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{DC} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{DC} f_{sw} = 0.012\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs per switching leg), dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

**Total switching-related loss:**

$$
P_{sw,tot} \approx 2.64\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Total and Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

$$
P_{ch}(T_j) = 2I^2R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + I^2R_{Cu} + P_{sw}
$$

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{th}
$$

Solving in closed form for the FET-only portion:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th}\left(2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)+P_{sw}\right)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}
$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion.** The temperature feedback loop gain must stay below 1:

$$
\boxed{\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} < 1\,}
$$

At 80 A this evaluates to 2 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.007 = 0.1075 W/K. Therefore:

- R_th(j→ambient) for the channel's FET group must be **< 9.3 K/W**, or the system has no stable operating point.
- A nano board in still air is about 15 to 25 K/W per channel. **At 80 A the board has no continuous solution.** This is the formal proof that 80 A can only be a transient rating.

**Per-channel total at T_j = 125 °C:**

| Loss component | Value |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 26.1 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W |
| Switching / dead-time / gate | 2.64 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 31.9 W** |

The 6 FETs share this unevenly. The commutation-state average is about 1/3 duty per leg, but the instantaneous heat is in 2 to 3 dice.
