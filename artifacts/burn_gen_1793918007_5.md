## 0. Architecture Review Findings

Before the math, there are three issues that change the design. The calculations later in this document support each of them.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---------|--------|
| **F1** | **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** Of its timers, only TIM1 and TIM8 have full complementary 3-phase outputs with dead time and break. HRTIM is not a practical substitute for 4 sensorless BLDC channels, and neither ArduPilot nor AM32/BLHeli supports H7-driven commutation. | Use the **H743 as the flight controller** and **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) ESC MCUs running AM32**, driven by bi-directional DShot600. All DMA analysis below assumes this split. |
| **F2** | **"80A burst per motor" does not survive thermally on a 20×20 mm nano board.** The board heats at about 20 K/s under that load (§3.4). | Re-rate to **80A total / 20A continuous per motor**, with **≤1 s, 80A per-motor peaks**, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs. |
| **F3** | **48 kHz PWM roughly triples switching plus dead-time loss versus 24 kHz** for little benefit on 5"-and-smaller motors. | Keep 48 kHz only if low-inductance (<15 µH) micro motors need it. Otherwise use 24 kHz. |

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, synchronous 6-step)

With complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, at any instant exactly **two FET channels carry the phase current I**: one in the high-or-complementary-low leg, and one in the static low leg. Six FETs exist per motor. Over a commutation cycle, each FET carries current about one third of the time.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha = \frac{k_T - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Conduction loss per motor (two channels always conducting):

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

PCB copper adds loss. A 2 oz phase pour of 8 mm × 6 mm × 70 µm gives:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{L}{W t} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.006}{0.008\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.18\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The full current path is roughly 2× this, plus about 0.4 mΩ for the battery pour share.

$$P_{Cu} \approx I^2 (0.8\ \text{m}\Omega)$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg)

Gate currents through the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{7}{6} = 1.17\ \text{A},\qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.5\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 10.3\ \text{ns},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{0.5} = 24\ \text{ns}$$

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on} + t_{off}) f_{sw}$$

**Reverse recovery** (complementary FET body diode, after dead time):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_{SD}\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}$$

**Gate drive** (6 FETs per motor; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, but on-board):
$$P_{gate} = 6\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$$

### 2.3 Evaluated: 80 A per Motor, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Equation result | W/motor |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond | 2 × 6400 × 2.4 mΩ | **30.72** |
| P_Cu | 6400 × 0.8 mΩ | **5.12** |
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 34.3 ns × 48 k | **1.66** |
| P_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 |
| P_oss | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.04 |
| P_dt | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **1.23** |
| P_gate | 6 × 40 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.12 |
| **Total** | | **≈ 38.9 W/motor** |

**4 motors: ≈ 156 W into a 20×20 mm board.**

Per hottest FET: (30.72 / 2) + switching share ≈ **17.5 W peak**. Averaged over commutation, this is about 1/3 duty per FET, so ≈ 6.5 W average per FET.
