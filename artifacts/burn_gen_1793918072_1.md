## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

### System partition

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC section has its own MCU for each channel, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071 or AT32F421.

| Block | Function |
|---|---|
| H743 (FC) | ArduPilot, IMU, OSD, DShot600 bidirectional out ×4 |
| ESC MCU ×4 | Commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class ⚠) |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 6 per channel, 40 V N-channel |
| Bulk caps | Low-ESR polymer and MLCC on the DC bus |

### Feasibility flag

80 A burst per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is at or beyond the physical limit of that form factor. Section 3 shows the board survives only a few seconds at full burst. Use the model to set firmware current limits and burst timers. Do not use it to justify the "80 A" marketing figure as a continuous rating.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

The ESC uses 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM.

- At any instant, current flows through **two FET positions in series**: the PWM'd leg and the return leg.
- In the PWM'd leg, current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1−D). Exactly one of them conducts at a time.
- Therefore the instantaneous conducting path is always 2 × R_DS(on). This holds independent of duty cycle, ignoring dead time.

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot (1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.5) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per FET.** Each of the 6 FETs carries the path roughly one-third of each electrical cycle. Electrical frequency is about 1–4 kHz at flight RPM, which is far faster than the die thermal time constant. So the loss averages out:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} \approx 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Temperature coupling.** R_DS(on) rises roughly linearly with temperature:

$$
R(T) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}
$$

Section 3.3 iterates this.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched leg)

**V–I overlap loss**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 24\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 1.16\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (the synchronous FET's body diode conducts during dead time)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance**

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 12 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

The FET-side switching loss is ≈ 1.55 W per channel. It is concentrated in the two FETs of the currently PWM'd leg, rotating through all three legs.

### 2.3 Copper and passive losses

**Copper.** Assume 0.3 mΩ ⚠ of effective copper per channel (bus pours plus phase pads, 6-layer, 2 oz outer):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.3\text{ m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}
$$

**Bulk capacitor ripple.** Worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \cdot 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}
$$
