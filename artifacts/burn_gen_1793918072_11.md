## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU handles commutation, PWM generation and the bi-directional DShot reply. The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and captures the eRPM telemetry. This document models it that way.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano footprint is time-limited, not a rating.** The thermal model in §3 shows the following for a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board:

| Load case | Burst limit |
|---|---|
| One channel at 80A, starting from a 50 °C board | ≈ 8.6 s |
| All four channels at 80A simultaneously | ≈ 2.0 s |
| 320 A continuous | Impossible: the XT60, battery and PCB copper cannot carry it |

The spec should therefore be written as **"80A / 2 s (all channels), 80A / 8 s (single channel), 45A continuous with prop-wash airflow."** If the "nano" requirement means a 20 × 20 mm board, burst times scale down by roughly 2.3× because board thermal capacitance scales with area.
