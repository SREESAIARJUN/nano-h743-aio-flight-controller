# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #80): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Decision

Two points need settling before any math. They determine whether the design is buildable.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The defensible architecture is:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **ESC domain:** 4× dedicated ESC MCUs, such as STM32G071 or AT32F421, running AM32. Each drives one 3-phase bridge at **48 kHz complementary PWM**.

So the 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC bridges. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board is a short transient, not a rating.** The model below shows:

- **Continuous:** about 25 A per channel.
- **Burst:** 80 A on all four channels is survivable for about 2 s.
- **Single channel:** 80 A on one channel lasts considerably longer.

Marketing should not label this an "80 A ESC" without a defined burst duration and duty cycle.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current distribution in 6-step complementary PWM

Over one electrical cycle, each half-bridge leg spends time in three states:

- **Source leg (1/3 of cycle):** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) as synchronous freewheel.
- **Sink leg (1/3 of cycle):** the low-side FET is fully on.
- **Floating (1/3 of cycle):** off, with BEMF sensing.
