## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several points in the brief need correcting before the design can proceed.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 running AM32/BLHeli_32/Bluejay). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter, not an H743 timer setting. I model it that way below.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-format board is achievable only for a few seconds.** The math below shows that continuous capability on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board is roughly 35–40 A per channel. The 80 A figure must be specified with a duration (I recommend ≤2 s at 40 °C ambient), or the product will fail in the field.
3. **All MOSFET and component values are stated assumptions** for a typical 40 V, ~1 mΩ-class 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm DFN part. Before freeze, substitute the datasheet values of your chosen part. The equations remain valid.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With trapezoidal or 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, two phase legs conduct at any instant. In each conducting leg, exactly one FET carries the phase current I at all times (the HS during on-time, the LS during off-time). Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on),T}$$

The ripple contribution is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" to 7" motor (L_ph ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 6.6 A. This adds about 0.6 % to I², so it is negligible at 80 A. It is significant only at low load.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}$$

This gives 1.5 mΩ at 125 °C. I use the hot value as the worst-case design point.

### 1.3 At 80 A burst

| Loss | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2·80²·1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Copper path | 80²·0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shunt | 80²·0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Conduction subtotal** | | **22.7 W / channel** |

Per-FET distribution: each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Average dissipation is ≈ 19.2/6 ≈ 3.2 W, with a peak of 9.6 W while conducting. At commutation rates in the hundreds of Hz to kHz, the FET Z_th(j-c) smooths this, so average values dominate.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. With complementary PWM, there is one hard-switched HS turn-on/turn-off pair per period per channel.

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the LS body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance** (two FETs in the leg):
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\cdot 635\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

| Switching subtotal | **≈ 2.2 W / channel** |
|---|---|

**Design insight:** at 80 A, switching loss is only about 9 % of the total, so the board is conduction-limited. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W per channel but worsens current ripple and acoustic noise. Keep 48 kHz. Spend the effort on lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs instead.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Totals at 80 A

$$P_{ch} \approx 22.7 + 2.2 = \mathbf{24.9\ W}, \qquad P_{board,4ch} \approx \mathbf{100\ W}$$

### 3.2 Battery-side current

$$I_{bat} \approx \sum_{k=1}^{4} D_k\, I_k$$

At full throttle (D → 1), I_bat approaches **320 A**. An XT60 (≈60 A continuous) is inadequate for this rating. Specify XT90 or direct 10–12 AWG pads, and use ≥3×1 mm² of bus copper across the 2 oz or thicker layers.

### 3.3 Transient (burst) model: lumped RC

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt} = P - \frac{T - T_a}{R_{th,ba}}$$

$$T(t) = T_a + P R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/R_{th}C_{th}}\right)$$

**Estimates for a 25.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board:**
- C_th ≈ 3 J/K (PCB plus copper, FETs, and caps; FR4 ≈ 1.2 J/g·K, Cu ≈ 0.385 J/g·K)
- R_th,ba ≈ 4 K/W in prop-wash airflow (≈ 12 K/W in still air)

Because τ = R·C ≈ 12 s is much longer than the burst, the board is effectively adiabatic during a burst:

$$\frac{dT}{dt}\approx\frac{100}{3}\approx 33\ \text{K/s}$$

Starting at a 40 °C board temperature with a 125 °C board limit (leaving T_j headroom):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{85}{33} \approx \mathbf{2.5\ s}\ \text{(all four channels at 80 A)}$$

**Rated spec: 80 A for 2 s, with ≥10 s recovery at ≤30 A.**

### 3.4 Junction rise over board

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET}\,(R_{th,j-c} + R_{th,c-b})$$

With P ≈ 3.2 W average per FET and R_th(j-c) + R_th(c-b) ≈ 1 + 4 K/W, the rise is ≈ 16 K. The peak junction is therefore about 141 °C at a 125 °C board, inside a 150 °C or 175 °C T_j(max).
