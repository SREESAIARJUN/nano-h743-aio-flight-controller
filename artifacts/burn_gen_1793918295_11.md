| # | Assumption | Why it matters |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005 K⁻¹

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.0 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.75 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 3.0 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current path in six-step synchronous drive

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly two phase legs carry the current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D. The low-side FET conducts as a synchronous rectifier for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

So there are always two channel resistances in series, independent of D:

P_cond,motor = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds(T_j)

With 48 kHz ripple, I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. For typical motor inductance (~10–20 µH line-line), ΔI is small at 80 A, so I_rms ≈ I.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2·6400·0.0020 = **25.6 W** | 102 W |
| 125 °C | 2·6400·0.0030 = **38.4 W** | 154 W |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, and significant)

- **Phase copper:** 2 oz, about 0.3 mΩ effective per phase path.
  - P = 2·I²·R_Cu = 2·6400·0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per motor**.
- **Bus shunt:** 0.25 mΩ, shared by all four motors. At D = 0.8:
  - I_bus = 4 × 0.8 × 80 = 256 A
  - P_shunt = 256² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **16.4 W**
  - This is a hard limit. Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt or per-motor sensing.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

Per motor, only the PWM leg hard-switches. That gives one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

### 2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover) loss

t_sw ≈ Q_sw / I_g, where Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 8 nC.

With a typical ESC driver (FD6288-class, about 0.3–0.4 A effective through R_g), plus layout inductance:
- t_r ≈ 25 ns
- t_f ≈ 20 ns

P_ov = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 · 22.2 · 80 · 45 ns · 48 kHz
= **1.92 W**
