# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #14)

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules

Three facts constrain everything else. The tables later depend on them.

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel (or a shared dual-core part) has its own motor-control MCU running the 48 kHz PWM, commutation and dead-time. This design assumes an AT32F421-class MCU running AM32. The H743's job is DShot600 generation, eRPM telemetry capture and the flight stack. The switching-loss model applies to the ESC side; the DMA model applies to the H743 side.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano footprint is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math below shows roughly 28 W per channel at 80 A. A 20×20 / 25.5 mm board cannot reject about 115 W continuously. The deliverable is therefore a burst-duration model.
3. **All component values are design assumptions.** Part numbers are candidates. Verify R_DS(on)(T), Q_g, Q_rr and R_θJC against the current datasheets before layout freeze.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.4 × 1.45 ≈ **2.03 mΩ**. Use 2.1 mΩ to allow for V_GS sag at the bootstrap.

### 1.2 Current paths

In 6-step mode with synchronous rectification, two phases are active in each sector:
- During the ON portion of the PWM (duty D), current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the OFF portion (1−D), current freewheels through the synchronous low-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.

So **exactly two FET channels carry the full phase current at all times**, apart from dead-time:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. Peak dissipation while conducting is I²R = 13.4 W, so the average is:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.0021 \approx \mathbf{4.5\ W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 4.5 W ≈ 27 W, which matches the total.
