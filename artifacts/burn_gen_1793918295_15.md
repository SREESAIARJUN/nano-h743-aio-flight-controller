# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #3)

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Three points change the design before any math is done.

**1. The H743 cannot also be the ESC controller.** ArduPilot has no ESC commutation firmware, and running 4× sensorless 6-step or FOC at 48 kHz inside the flight stack is not viable. The correct partition is:

- **STM32H743 (FC):** runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines.
- **4× ESC MCUs:** AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (BLHeli_32 is discontinued), one per motor.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class), which drives 6 N-MOSFETs.

**2. "Nano" vs. 80A burst.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80A. The thermal model in §3 shows the board's heat capacity is the limiting factor. This package assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm board as the minimum and a 36×36 mm board as recommended. If "nano" means 20×20, derate to about 35A burst.

**3. No MOSFET part number was specified.** All loss figures use the parameterized device below. Substitute datasheet values into the equations. They are written to be re-evaluated, not copied.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value (40V, PDFN5×6 class) |
|---|---|---|
| On-resistance @25°C / @125°C | R_ds(on) | 1.0 mΩ / 1.6 mΩ |
| Gate charge total | Q_g | 60 nC @ 10V |
| Switching charge (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) | Q_sw | 15 nC |
| Plateau voltage | V_pl | 3.5 V |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC @ 25V |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 40 nC |

**Operating point:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case, full charge), f_sw = 48 kHz, I_phase = 80 A burst, T_j,max target = 125°C (abs. max 150°C).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Complementary/Synchronous PWM)

AM32 by default uses trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM. In each 60° sector, one high-side (HS) FET chops at duty D, its partner low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D), and the opposite phase's LS FET is held fully on.

Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg sees:

- an HS-active window: 1/3 of the period (chopping)
- an LS-static window: 1/3 of the period (fully on)
- a floating window: 1/3 of the period (BEMF sensing)

**Per-FET average conduction loss**

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence** (closes the loop with §3):

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Numbers at I = 80 A, T_j = 125°C (R = 1.6 mΩ)**

- I²R = 6400 × 0.0016 = **10.24 W** (instantaneous, one FET)

| Condition | P_HS | P_LS |
|---|---|---|
| D = 1.0 (full throttle) | 3.41 W | 3.41 W |
| D = 0.5 | 1.71 W | 5.12 W |
| D → 0 (high current, low duty; e.g. stall/punch-out start) | ≈0 | **6.83 W** ← worst |

**Per-leg conduction** = (2/3) I²R = 6.83 W, independent of D.

- Per motor (3 legs): **20.5 W**
- Per board (4 motors): **82 W** of conduction loss alone at simultaneous 80A burst

**Parallel-FET option.** With N FETs per switch, conduction scales by 1/N (each FET carries I/N, so its loss is (I/N)²R; the switch total is I²R/N). Two FETs per switch gives a 41 W board total, at the cost of 48 FETs, which is impossible below 36×36 mm.

**Copper/trace conduction** must be added. Use 2 oz copper, 6 layers, with phase/bus pours in parallel on 4 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}\,L}{w\,t\,n_{layers}} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.010}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.15\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That is about 1 W per motor path at 80A. Budget **~4 W** board-wide.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the chopping HS FET hard-switches. It is active 1/3 of the electrical period.

### 2.1 Gate-drive-limited transition time

Gate driver source/sink current is approximately:

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.5}{2.2+1.0+0.8}\approx 1.6\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{r}\approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.6\,\text{A}} \approx 9.4\ \text{ns}$$

Use **20 ns each** to cover layout inductance and the Miller tail.
