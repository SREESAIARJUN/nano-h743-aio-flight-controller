# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Architecture, Loss Model, Timer/DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Constraints to Resolve First

Three issues will affect the design review regardless of the math below.

1. **Who switches at 48 kHz.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. In every production 4-in-1 stack, each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The H743 sends DShot600 frames to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. This document models both domains separately.

2. **"Nano" and 80 A do not fit together.** 24 power MOSFETs plus bulk capacitance will not fit a 20×20 mm footprint at 80 A burst. All thermal numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board (≈36×36 mm outline)** stacked under the FC. If 20×20 mm is a hard requirement, the realistic ceiling is about 35–45 A burst. Section 2.6 shows why.

3. **Assumed operating point.** No component list was supplied, so I assumed the values below. Re-run the equations with datasheet values before committing.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 5×6 PDFN, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 50 nC, Q_gd ≈ 15 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal, 120° per phase, complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side |
| PCB | 8-layer, 2 oz copper on power layers |
| Burst rating | 80 A **phase** current per motor |

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model, ESC Power Stage (per motor)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**. Use the hot value for burst sizing. Section 2.7 closes the loop iteratively.

### 2.2 Conduction losses

With trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its synchronous low-side partner conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously for the 120° segment.

Instantaneous bridge conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\left[R_{HS}D + R_{LS}(1-D) + R_{LS,ret}\right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}\quad(\text{matched FETs})
$$

At 80 A with hot FETs:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

Copper and trace loss adds to this. Assume about 0.3 mΩ per current path, including the phase pad and battery plane:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.8\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Over one electrical revolution each of the 6 FETs carries current for one third of the time. At eRPM ≈ 3.5 kHz the electrical period is far shorter than the junction thermal time constant, so averaging is valid:

$$
\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 20.5/6 \approx 3.4\ \text{W}
$$

The worst case is the return low-side FET at 10.2 W instantaneous. This matters only at very low eRPM, such as stall or startup.
