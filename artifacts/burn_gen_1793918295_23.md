## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flag

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors. It generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.

**Form factor.** "Nano" is taken as a 20×20 mm mounting pattern on a board of about 26×26 mm.

**Operating point.** 6S pack, with switching losses evaluated at V_bus,max = 25.2 V. PWM frequency is 48 kHz. Commutation is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (read this first).** An 80 A per-channel burst means about 320 A total if all four channels burst together. That is roughly 8 kW from 6S, through a 26 mm board. The numbers below show that this is a **transient-only** rating, a few seconds at most. It is not a continuous rating. Section 2 quantifies this. Treat "80 A burst" as a marketing ceiling that must be backed by a firmware current/temperature limiter.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6 FETs)

### 1.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step synchronous PWM, two phase legs carry the motor current I at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and its low-side partner (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for that 60° sector.

At every instant, exactly two channel resistances are in series with the current, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

Phase inductance is finite, so a ripple term is added:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2\left(1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{avg}}\right)^2\right)$$

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" or 7" motor at high load, with L_ph ≈ 15 µH, f_sw = 48 kHz, and ΔI ≈ 10 A, the correction is about +0.13%. It is negligible at 80 A, but it matters at light load.

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent Resistance (closed-loop)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.28\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper path.** The PCB copper adds loss as well. Assume 0.15 mΩ for the bus-to-phase-pad path, through 2 oz inner planes:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.15\,\text{m}\Omega \times 2 \approx 1.9\ \text{W}$$

**Distribution across FETs.**

- **At speed:** the return-leg role rotates among the three legs. The average per FET is 17.28 / 6 = **2.9 W**.
- **At stall or very low eRPM (worst case):** one FET can hold the return role for the full burst. Its loss is I²R = **8.64 W in a single 3×3 package.** This hot-spot case is the one that sets the design.

### 1.4 Switching Losses (hard-switched HS FET of PWM leg)

**Gate currents during the plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_g} = \frac{5.5}{4} = 1.375\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink}+R_g} = \frac{4.5}{3} = 1.5\ \text{A}$$
