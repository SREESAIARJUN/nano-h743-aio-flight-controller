## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The correct partitioning is:

```
                    ┌──────────────────────── FC domain (3V3) ─────────────────────────┐
 VBAT ─► BEC 5V/3A ─► LDO 3V3 ─► STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz max, run at 400 MHz)
                                   │ TIM1 CH1..CH4 ── DShot600 bidirectional ──┐
                                   │ ADC ── VBAT / CURR                         │
                    └──────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────┘
                                   ▼                                            ▼
                    ┌────────── ESC domain (×4, one per motor) ────────────────────────┐
                    │ AT32F421K8U7 / STM32G071 running AM32  ─►  3-phase gate driver   │
                    │   48 kHz complementary PWM, six-step     ─►  6× N-MOSFET (40 V)  │
                    └───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency belongs to the ESC MCUs (AM32 `PWM_FREQ=48`). DShot600 and its DMA allocation belong to the H743 under ArduPilot.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant in a commutation sector, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for duty D, and through the synchronous low-side FET for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the full sector.

Therefore **two R_DS(on) are always in series**, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_J)
$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_J-25)\right],\qquad \alpha=\frac{k_T-1}{100}=0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_J = 125 °C, R = 1.20 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond,motor}=2(80)^2(1.20\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.36\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Distribution per FET

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries load for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, in either the PWM role or the static role. Averaged over the cycle:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{15.36}{6}= \mathbf{2.56\ W}
$$

The *instantaneous* dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 7.68 W. This matters only if the electrical period is long relative to the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), i.e. at very low eRPM or during stall/desync.

> **Stall/desync case:** a single LS FET can carry 80 A DC, giving 7.68 W. Over 3 ms this alone produces roughly a 9 K die rise, so AM32 current limiting and stall protection are mandatory.
