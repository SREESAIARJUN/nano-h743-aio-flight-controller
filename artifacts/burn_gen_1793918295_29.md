## 0. Architecture Corrections

These issues change the design, so they come first.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|------------|
| 1 | **ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors.** An STM32H743 running ArduPilot cannot drive the half-bridges directly. | The architecture is **H743 flight controller + 4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32-class). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. Everything in §4 assumes this. |
| 2 | **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst" conflict.** The thermal model (§3) shows ~85–100 W of total stage dissipation at 4×80 A. A 20×20 board heats at roughly 30–40 K/s under that load. | Two options: **(a)** keep 80 A on a 30.5×30.5 board with 5×6 mm MOSFETs, or **(b)** keep 20×20 and derate to ~40–45 A burst. The math below lets you choose; §3.5 gives the crossover. |
| 3 | **Define "80 A".** | Defined here as **per-motor phase current, 6S (25.2 V max), burst ≤ 5 s, plus a 1 s worst-case step**. |

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.6 × R25, which is **1.6 mΩ** for the baseline part and **2.88 mΩ** for the nano part.

### 2.2 Conduction losses: six-step with synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond}=I^2R\,[D+(1-D)]+I^2R = 2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$
