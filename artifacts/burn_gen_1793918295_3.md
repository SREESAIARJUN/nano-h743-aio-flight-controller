# STM32H743 Nano AIO FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #44)

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Corrections

These assumptions drive every number below. Validate each one before release.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal, 30 V transient clamp |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm nano (M2 mounting), 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes |
| Topology | 3-phase full bridge × 4 = 24 MOSFETs (2 per switch position is impossible at nano scale, so 1 per position) |
| MOSFET (placeholder) | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q<sub>g</sub> = 40 nC, Q<sub>gd</sub> = 8 nC, Q<sub>rr</sub> = 40 nC, C<sub>oss(er)</sub> = 1.5 nF, R<sub>θJC</sub> = 1.5 K/W |
| ESC control | 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32 with EDT. The H743 does **not** commutate motors. |
| PWM | 48 kHz complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Ambient | 40 °C, T<sub>j,max</sub> = 150 °C, design limit T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C |

**Architectural correction.** Bidirectional DShot600 is a protocol between the H743 (flight controller) and the ESC MCUs. The H743 cannot run DShot to its own gate drivers. A single-MCU design would require the H743 to run FOC for four motors, and ArduPilot does not support that. The architecture is therefore FC MCU + 4 ESC MCUs on the same PCB stack.

**Headline finding.** 80 A per motor is physically a **sub-second burst** on a 20×20 board. The math in §1 and §2 shows this. Any rating claim must be stated as a time-limited burst, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step, complementary PWM)

During any commutation sector, phase A high-side (HS) PWMs at duty D, phase B low-side (LS) is held ON, and phase C floats. With motor current I<sub>m</sub>:

$$
P_{HS,A} = D\, I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \quad
P_{LS,A} = (1-D)\, I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \quad
P_{LS,B} = I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)} \quad \text{(independent of } D\text{, excluding dead-time)}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.5 \times 1.40 = 2.10\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Result at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}
$$

The worst single device is the statically-on LS MOSFET:

$$
P_{LS,B} = 6400 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{13.4\ W}
$$

That device rotates every 60° electrical. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each device sees about 1/3 of the peak load. However, at low eRPM (stall or punch-out from hover), the commutation period exceeds the device's local thermal time constant (≈ms). Design to the **peak**.

### 1.4 Copper / interconnect loss

- Battery pad → bus plane: R ≈ 0.25 mΩ.
- Phase pours + motor pads: ≈ 0.4 mΩ per phase.

Per channel:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0004 \approx 5.1\ \text{W}
$$

The bus plane carries the battery current, summed over channels:

$$
I_{bat} = \sum D_k\, I_{m,k}
$$

At 4 × 80 A, D ≈ 0.85, I<sub>bat</sub> ≈ 272 A, so P<sub>Cu,bus</sub> ≈ 272² × 0.00025 ≈ **18.5 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only the PWMing leg switches. Its loss components are:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V<sub>bus</sub> I<sub>m</sub> (t<sub>r</sub>+t<sub>f</sub>) f<sub>sw</sub> | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q<sub>rr</sub> V<sub>bus</sub> f<sub>sw</sub> | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.05 W** |
| C<sub>oss</sub> | ½ C<sub>oss(er)</sub> V<sup>2</sup> f<sub>sw</sub> | ½·1.5n·635·48k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 t<sub>dt</sub> V<sub>F</sub> I<sub>m</sub> f<sub>sw</sub> | 2·50n·0.8·80·48k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 Q<sub>g</sub> V<sub>GS</sub> f<sub>sw</sub> | 2·40n·10·48k = **0.04 W** (in driver) |
| **Total P<sub>sw,ch</sub>** | | **≈ 1.87 W** |

The switching times come from the gate drive:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}, \quad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{4.7\ \Omega} = 1.4\ \text{A}
\;\Rightarrow\; t \approx 6\ \text{ns}
$$

15 ns is used to cover source-inductance (L<sub>s</sub>·di/dt) slowdown.

**Sensitivity.** At 48 kHz, switching is about 6.5 % of total loss, and conduction dominates. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only ~0.9 W per channel and costs current ripple and acoustic noise. 48 kHz is justified.

## 3. Total Dissipation Budget (all 4 motors at 80 A burst)

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 26.9 W | 107.6 W |
| Switching | 1.87 W | 7.5 W |
| Phase copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Bus copper | — | 18.5 W |
| Drivers + LDOs + MCUs | — | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 155 W** |

Electrical efficiency check: P<sub>out</sub> ≈ 272 A × 25.2 V ≈ 6.85 kW, so η ≈ 97.7 %. The efficiency is fine; the **heat density** is the problem.
