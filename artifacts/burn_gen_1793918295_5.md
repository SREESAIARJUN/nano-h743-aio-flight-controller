# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #37)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Risks

These assumptions drive every number below. Change one and you must re-run the dependent calculations.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Worst case for switching loss is 25.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel, ≤10 s | Continuous rating derived in §3.5 |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) | FOC changes the conduction math (see §2.4) |
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6, ArduPilot | |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), running AM32 | |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≈ 0.9–1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, PDFN5x6 class | Representative, not a specific part |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class), 1 per ESC | |

> **Architecture flag:** The H743 should **not** run the motor control loops. The H743 is the flight controller. It talks bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. That split is standard and is assumed throughout.

> **Form-factor flag:** "Nano" (20×20 mm mounting) **cannot** carry 4 × 80 A burst. §3 shows the board heat capacity is the limit. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** (≈36×42 mm outline) stacked under a 20×20 or 30×30 FC. If you need a true 20×20 board, de-rate to roughly 45 A burst.

## 1. Power Stage Topology (per channel)

```
V_BAT ──┬────────┬────────┐
        │        │        │
      Q_AH     Q_BH     Q_CH        (high-side N-FETs)
        │A       │B       │C  ──► motor phases
      Q_AL     Q_BL     Q_CL        (low-side N-FETs)
        │        │        │
        └────────┴────────┴── R_SHUNT ── GND
```

In 6-step commutation two phases are active at any instant. Take an example step where phase A is PWM'd high and phase B is held low:

- During **D·T<sub>s</sub>**, current flows Q<sub>AH</sub> → motor → Q<sub>BL</sub>.
- During **(1−D)·T<sub>s</sub>**, current freewheels Q<sub>AL</sub> (synchronous) → motor → Q<sub>BL</sub>.
- In both intervals, **exactly two MOSFETs conduct** the full phase current I. One is always Q<sub>BL</sub>. The other is either Q<sub>AH</sub> or Q<sub>AL</sub>.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C with R<sub>DS(on),25</sub> = 1.0 mΩ:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.45) = 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega \;(\text{use } 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega)$$

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A

Two devices are always in the path, so:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS(on)} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-device distribution (critical for hotspot analysis)

The loss is not shared equally between the FETs:

| Device | Duty | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Q<sub>BL</sub> (held-on low side) | 100% of the commutation step | I²R = **9.6 W** |
| Q<sub>AH</sub> | D | D · 9.6 W |
| Q<sub>AL</sub> (sync rectifier) | 1 − D | (1 − D) · 9.6 W |

At D = 0.5, the PWM'd phase splits to 4.8 W + 4.8 W. **The held low-side FET is the hotspot at 9.6 W.**

Commutation rotates roles every 60° electrical. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates 19.2 / 6 = 3.2 W. However, at low eRPM (e.g. hard punch-outs from low throttle) the thermal time constant of the die is shorter than one commutation step. Size for the 9.6 W instantaneous case.

### 2.4 Shunt and PCB copper (often forgotten)

- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ, 2512 or 3-terminal) at 80 A: **1.6 W per channel**.
  - Alternative: use 0.15 mΩ with a higher-gain amplifier, giving 0.96 W.
- **Copper trace/plane resistance**, battery pads to phase pads. Assume ≈0.3 mΩ effective with 2 oz outer layers, 4-layer paralleled power planes, and via stitching:
  - P = 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W per channel**.
  - Note that the battery plane is *shared*, so its loss scales with the *total* current (§3.4).

> **FOC note:** With sinusoidal commutation, three phases conduct with RMS current I<sub>pk</sub>/√2 per phase. Loss ≈ 3 · (I<sub>pk</sub>/√2)² · R ≈ 1.5 · I<sub>pk</sub>² · R. This is roughly equivalent for the same torque, but the distribution across FETs is smoother.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches; the held low-side FET does not. Per channel, per switching event pair:

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

Gate driver sources and sinks about 1.0–1.5 A into Q<sub>g</sub> ≈ 60 nC. The Miller-plateau-limited transition times come out around t<sub>r</sub> ≈ t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 15 ns:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

All of this lands in the PWM'd high-side FET.

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (in the sync-rectifier FET)

$$P_{DT} = V_F\, I\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times (2 \times 60\ \text{ns}) \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.37\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 60\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} \approx 50\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

The Q<sub>oss</sub> energy is charged from the bus and dissipated across the switch pair. This form is conservative.

### 3.4 Gate drive (dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_{G} = Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 60\ \text{nC} \times 10\ \text{V} \times 48\,000 \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Per-channel loss budget at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz, T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C

| Component | Loss |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W |
| V–I switching | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.37 W |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> + Q<sub>oss</sub> | 0.13 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W |
| Shunt | 1.60 W |
| Phase-path copper | ~1.0 W |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 23.8 W** |

At 4 × 80 A, add the shared battery-input copper: (320 A)² × 0.05 mΩ ≈ **5.1 W**.

**Total board dissipation at full 4×80 A burst: 4 × 23.8 + 5.1 ≈ 100 W.**

**Efficiency check:** Input power at 80 A × 22.2 V × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW. η ≈ 98.6%. Losses look small as a percentage, but 100 W into a ~6 g board is the entire design problem.

**Frequency sensitivity:** Switching loss scales linearly with f<sub>sw</sub>; conduction does not.

- Going 24 → 48 kHz adds ≈1 W per channel.
- Going 48 → 96 kHz adds another ≈2 W per channel.

48 kHz is a good compromise between motor current ripple (smoother, quieter) and switching loss.
