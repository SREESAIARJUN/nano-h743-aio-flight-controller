## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two problems in the brief need correcting before the analysis is trustworthy.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM with hardware dead-time. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bi-directional DShot600. The "48 kHz switching" analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCU's TIM1, not to the H743.
2. **"Nano" and "80A burst per channel" are not physically compatible.** As Section 3 shows, 80A on all four channels dissipates about 100W. A 20×20 mm board cannot carry that for more than a few seconds, and cannot fit MOSFETs with low enough Rds(on). I have modelled a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with PDFN5×6 FETs as the smallest credible platform. A true 20×20 nano realistically tops out around **40–45A burst**.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path Topology

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two legs are active in each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. Its complementary low-side (LS) conducts for (1 − D), minus dead time (synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** the LS conducts continuously for the whole sector.

So at every instant there are exactly **two FET channels in series** with the motor current. This is true regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

Typical figures: L_ph ≈ 15 µH for a 2306-class motor at 48 kHz, giving ΔI ≈ 5–10A at mid-duty. That is under 0.2% of I². **We take I_rms ≈ 80A.**

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At Tj = 125°C:

$$
R_{ds(on)} = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.4 Results Per Channel

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

Averaged over an electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about 19.2 / 6 = **3.2 W**. During its own 120° conduction window, the instantaneous figure is about 9.6W.

**Option:** paralleling two FETs per switch halves the FET conduction loss to 9.6W per channel. It needs 48 FETs, which forces a 30.5 mm board minimum and is only realistic with double-sided placement.
