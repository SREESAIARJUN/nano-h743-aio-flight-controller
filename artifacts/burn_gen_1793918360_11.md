## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the gates.** In every production ArduPilot stack, the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which runs commutation, the 48 kHz PWM and back-EMF sensing. The H743 sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry over the same wire (bidirectional DShot). ArduPilot has no native FOC/6-step motor driver for the H7. The design is partitioned accordingly:

```
[ H743 FC board ] --DShot600 bidir x4, Vbat/Curr ADC, ESC UART telem--> [ 4-in-1 ESC board: 4x AM32 MCU + 3-ph gate driver + 6 FETs each ]
```

**80 A burst on a "nano" form factor is thermally marginal.** The math in §3 shows it is a roughly 1–2 second rating, not a continuous one. I state this up front because the board will be judged on it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous PWM)

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. One leg is PWM'd complementarily (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D). The other leg's LS is held on. Each FET is in the conduction path for 1/3 of the electrical period.
