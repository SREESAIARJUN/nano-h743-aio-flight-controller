# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture.** In an ArduPilot system, the STM32H743 runs the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which handles the 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and back-EMF sensing. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models both halves:

- **Power stage:** the 4-in-1 ESC.
- **Control and telemetry link:** H743 timers and DMA.

## 1. Conduction Losses at 80 A Burst

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- the **commutated leg** switch is on 100 % of the time;
- the **PWM leg** alternates between its high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) FETs (synchronous rectification).

So the current always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

**At 80 A, hot:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = 19.2\ \mathrm{W\ per\ motor}$$

The same calculation at 25 °C gives 12.8 W.

**Positive feedback check.** P_cond(T_j) = 19.2 × [1 + 0.005(T_j − 125)]. The solution remains stable while

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\, R_{\theta} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha\, R_\theta < 1$$

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| 2 I² R₂₅ α | 2 × 6400 × 0.001 × 0.005 = 0.064 W/K |
| Constraint | R_θ < 15.6 K/W |

The per-FET path computed in Section 3 is well below this, so there is no thermal runaway.

**Additional copper and shunt losses**, assuming a 0.5 mΩ shunt and 0.3 mΩ of phase copper:

$$80^2 \times 0.8\ \mathrm{m\Omega} \approx 5.1\ \mathrm{W\ per\ motor}$$

This can be eliminated by moving to low-side or phase sensing with a 0.25 mΩ shunt. For the remainder of the model, assume **2.5 W per motor** with an optimized layout.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

These losses occur only in the PWM leg. The commutated leg switches at electrical frequency, which is negligible.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched HS edge)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 1.94\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery** (LS diode recovering at HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\mathrm{nC} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(c) Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = 0.07\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}$$

**Switching total:** P_sw ≈ 2.74 W per motor.

**Frequency scaling:** P_sw(f) ≈ 57 µJ × f (strictly, 2.68 W / 48 kHz ≈ 56 µJ/cycle on the FETs plus 0.06 W gate). Going to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per motor. Going to 96 kHz costs about 2.7 W.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss budget at full 80 A burst, all four motors

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.74 W | 11.0 W |
| Shunt and copper (optimized) | 2.5 W | 10.0 W |
| **Total** | **24.4 W** | **≈ 98 W** |

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts about one-third of the electrical cycle. At typical electrical frequencies (1–4 kHz), this is far faster than the junction-to-case time constant (milliseconds), so averaging is valid:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 21.9 / 6 \approx 3.7\ \mathrm{W}$$

The instantaneous peak is about 11.5 W on a FET that is simultaneously commutated-on and switching.
