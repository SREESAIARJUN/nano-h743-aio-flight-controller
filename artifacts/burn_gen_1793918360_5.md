# Job #65: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC, Nano Form Factor

## Design Analysis: 80 A Burst Losses, 48 kHz Switching, Bi-directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points about the brief need to be settled before any math.

**A. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware, not ESC firmware. On a 4-in-1 AIO board, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which handles commutation, the 48 kHz PWM and eRPM telemetry. The ESC MCU is typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071, either one per motor or one per pair. The architecture is therefore:

```
VBAT ─┬─► 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× MOSFET half-bridges] → Motors
      └─► Buck 5V/3V3 ─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► ESC MCUs
```

**B. "80 A burst" is a short-transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** The analysis below shows that a nano board cannot sink about 90 W continuously. The real deliverable is the **allowable burst duration**.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-step, Synchronous Rectification)

In any commutation sector, current flows through two legs. One leg is PWM-switched with a complementary high-side/low-side pair. The other leg's low side is held ON.

With synchronous rectification there are **always exactly two MOSFET channels in the current path**, regardless of duty D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds,on}(T_j)
$$

The PWM-switched leg adds ripple. The inductor ripple is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}},\qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

Example values for a 5" motor: L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 20 V. This gives ΔI ≈ (5.2 · 0.9)/(15e-6 · 48e3) ≈ 6.5 A. The ripple term is 6.5²/12 = 3.5 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

**Burst case (80 A, T_j ≈ 100 °C, R = 1.5 mΩ):**

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

**PCB copper loss** must be included. Assume a 15 mm phase path on 8 × 2 oz layers, 4 mm wide, about 70 % usable:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\text{e-}8 \cdot 0.015}{0.004 \cdot 70\text{e-}6 \cdot 8 \cdot 0.7} \approx 0.16\text{ m}\Omega
$$

Each path traverses this twice, giving 80² · 0.33 mΩ ≈ **2.1 W per motor**. This is not negligible, and it heats the board directly.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg switches hard. There is one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period.

**(a) V–I overlap:**
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery** (charge Q_rr is dumped into the opposing FET at turn-on):
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{e-}9 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance**, two devices per leg:
$$
P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{e-}9 \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.03\ \text{W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, two edges per period:
$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs switch; this power is dissipated in the gate resistors and driver, not the FET):
$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{e-}9 \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.04\ \text{W}
$$

### Loss summary per motor

| Loss term | 80 A burst | 20 A cruise |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 19.2 W | 0.80 W (R at 70 °C ≈ 1.25 mΩ… ≈1.0 W at 1.5×) |
| PCB copper | 2.1 W | 0.13 W |
| V–I switching | 1.94 W | 0.48 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.13 W |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 24.0 W** | **≈ 1.9 W** |
| **Board total (×4)** | **≈ 96 W** | **≈ 7.6 W** |

At 48 kHz, conduction is about 80 % of burst loss. Raising f_sw improves ripple and acoustics but costs about 0.05 W per kHz per motor at 80 A. **The single most effective lever is R_ds,on.** Paralleling two FETs per switch halves conduction loss but doubles the Q_g and C_oss terms, which are tiny here.
