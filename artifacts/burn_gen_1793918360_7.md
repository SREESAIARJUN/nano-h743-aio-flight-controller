## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 must **not** commutate the MOSFETs directly. ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC/6-step motor control, and putting 24 gate signals on the FC MCU creates an unacceptable single point of failure. The standard partitioning is used instead:

```
LiPo 6S ─┬─► 4× ESC power stage (3 half-bridges each, 24 FETs total)
         │       ▲ gate drive
         │   4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32 class)
         │       ▲ bi-dir DShot600 (1 wire per motor)
         └─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

All numbers below rest on stated assumptions (§1). Substitute your selected MOSFET's datasheet values into the equations; the models are written to make that a mechanical exercise.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

In 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification, exactly **two legs** conduct at any instant. In each conducting leg, exactly **one FET** carries the phase current: the HS or LS FET of the PWM leg (alternating with duty D), and the steady FET of the non-PWM leg. Therefore, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{pk}^2\, R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\,k_T = 0.9 \times 1.55 = 1.40\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.40\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 240° of each 360° electrical cycle (120° sourcing, 120° sinking).

- **Average per FET:** P_cond/6 ≈ 3.0 W
- **Instantaneous worst FET (non-PWM leg, 120° window):** I²R = 80² × 1.40 mΩ = **8.96 W**

At a typical electrical frequency (≥ 500 Hz, i.e. a 120° window ≤ 0.67 ms), this window is shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). The junction ripple is therefore only a few kelvin, and the per-FET *average* governs the thermal model.

### 2.3 Copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = I_{pk}^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

This heat is deposited directly into the board planes.

### 2.4 Sinusoidal/FOC variant

With sinusoidal current of peak $\hat I$, using RMS per phase $\hat I/\sqrt2$ across 3 phases:

$$
P_{cond} = 3\left(\tfrac{\hat I}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 R_{ds,hot} = 1.5\,\hat I^2 R
$$

For $\hat I$ = 80 A this gives 13.4 W. The 6-step result is used as the conservative case.
