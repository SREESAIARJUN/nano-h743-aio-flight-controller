# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #75): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points constrain the rest of this document.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO "FC + 4-in-1" design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/STM32 running BLHeli_32/Bluejay. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and drive the gate drivers. This split is assumed throughout because:
- the H743 lacks enough advanced-control timers for 12 complementary gate outputs plus BEMF/ADC sync, and
- ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC/6-step motor control.

**2. "80 A burst" must be bounded.** The math in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously cannot be supported on a 20 × 20 mm board, and only for about 3–4 s on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board. I have sized the design to a **30.5 × 30.5 mm** form factor and define 80 A as a **per-channel burst ≤ 2 s, ≥ 20 % duty cycle**. If "nano" means 20 × 20 mm, the honest rating is about 35–45 A burst per channel.

**3. All component parameters are representative assumptions.** Substitute datasheet values for your actual BOM. Every equation is written so you can re-plug them.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, the phase current always flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFET channels:
- the high-side of phase A (or its low-side complement during off-time), and
- the low-side of phase B.

Duty cycle therefore redistributes loss between devices but does not change the total:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Including inductor ripple (triangular, ΔI peak-to-peak):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For a typical 5-inch-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5, V = 25.2 V):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 8.75\,\text{A} \Rightarrow \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6.4\,\text{A}^2 \;(\text{negligible vs } 6400)$$

At T_j = 100 °C, R_ds = 1.3 × 1.34 = **1.74 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond} = 6406 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.74\text{m} = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

**Electro-thermal feedback.** R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate. The loop converges because α·R_θ·P/T < 1. Its gain is:

$$G = \alpha \cdot P_{cond} \cdot R_\theta^{(eff)}$$

With the transient board R_θ from §3 (≈ 1–2 K/W for the burst), G ≈ 0.0045 × 22 × 1.5 ≈ 0.15. That is stable, with roughly 18 % loss amplification over a cold-start estimate.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Edge, 48 kHz)

Edge time from gate-drive charge (Miller plateau V_pl ≈ 3.5 V, R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω):

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{6n \cdot 4}{10-3.5} \approx 3.7\,\text{ns (ideal)}$$

In practice, loop inductance and dV/dt limiting (gate resistors chosen for EMI) give **t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns**. Use 20 ns.

| Loss term | Formula | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20n·48k = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·300n·48k = **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30n·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| Coss charge/discharge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 25n·25.2·48k = **0.030 W** |
| Gate drive (2 active FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·30n·10·48k = **0.029 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.9 W** |

**Key finding.** At 48 kHz, dead time dominates switching loss, not the edges. Reducing t_dt from 300 ns to 100 ns saves about 1.2 W per channel, which is about 5 W across the board. This is the cheapest thermal improvement available and is set via `dead_time` in AM32 config. Verify shoot-through margin on the bench before relying on it.
