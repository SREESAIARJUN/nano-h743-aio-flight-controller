**Correction 1: Partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. **Correction 2: "Nano" plus 80A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows a 20–30 mm board sustains roughly 25–30 A per motor continuously. 80A is achievable only for a few seconds. Market it as "80A burst (≤3 s)" or it will fail qualification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

**Commutation pattern.** The ESC uses 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:
- One phase's low-side FET is fully on.
- The PWM phase alternates between the high side (fraction D) and the low side (fraction 1−D).
- The third phase floats.

Over one electrical cycle, each phase is "driven-fixed" for 1/3 and "PWM" for 1/3. Per phase:

$$P_{\phi}= \frac{I^2R}{3}\big[1 + D + (1-D)\big] = \frac{2}{3}I^2R$$

So **two R_ds are always in the current path, independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad R_{ds}(T_j)\approx R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.006/\text{K}$$

**At T_j = 125 °C:** R = 1.6 mΩ, so

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\cdot 80^2\cdot1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Parasitic resistances.** Add I²·(R_shunt + R_copper). For a 0.25 mΩ shunt plus 0.3 mΩ of copper, that is 80² × 0.55 mΩ ≈ **3.5 W**. This is often ignored and is not negligible.

**Instantaneous peak in the worst FET.** 80² × 1.6 mΩ = 10.2 W. With R_θjc ≈ 1.2 K/W, the junction sits about **12 K above the case** during conduction. This is acceptable, so the board, not the die, is the binding limit.
