# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #15)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture assumed.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or EFM8BB51 class, running AM32 or BLHeli) that produces the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 cannot directly commutate four motors at 48 kHz in ArduPilot. Treating the two as one control domain is the most common architectural error on this kind of design.

> **Reality check (read before the math).** 4 × 80 A = 320 A, which is about 8 kW at 6S. On a 30.5 mm or 20 mm AIO board this is **strictly a transient (≤ 1–2 s) rating**, not a continuous one. The derivation in Section 1.5 shows why. Battery leads, the connector (XT60 is inadequate; XT90 or direct solder is needed) and the bulk capacitance become first-order design items.

## 1. Loss and Thermal Model

### 1.1 Conduction loss (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two FETs:
- the PWM'd phase, where the high-side conducts during D and the synchronous low-side conducts during 1−D, and
- the static low-side of the return phase.

So the conduction path always contains two R_ds in series.

Temperature-corrected on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds(25)}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ**.

Including PWM current ripple (r = ΔI/I):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

For r ≤ 0.3, the correction factor is below 0.8 %, so it is neglected here.

Conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.71\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The low-side commutates through its body diode, so it switches near zero voltage but incurs dead-time diode loss.
