## 0. Architecture and Scope

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. In every production AIO design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot to **four independent ESC MCUs**, one per motor, and each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and drives its own gate driver and bridge.

```
             ┌────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──────────────┐
 Gyro SPI ── │ SPI1   TIM3 CH1..CH4 ── bidir DShot600 ─────────┐ │
 OSD/Flash ─ │ SPI2/3 UARTs, ADC, I2C, USB                     │ │
             └──────────────────────────────────────────────────┼─┘
                 ┌──────────────┬──────────────┬────────────────┘
              ESC1 MCU       ESC2 MCU       ESC3 MCU      ESC4 MCU   (AT32F421 / AM32)
                 │              │              │              │
             3-ph driver    3-ph driver    3-ph driver    3-ph driver  (FD6288Q class)
                 │              │              │              │
              6× N-FET       6× N-FET       6× N-FET       6× N-FET
```

**Feasibility warning (stated up front).** The analysis in §3 shows that **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a burst of roughly 1 second, not a 10-second burst.** Nano AIOs normally carry about 12–25 A continuous. These numbers are a design-check model. Validate them on hardware with thermocouples or an IR camera before quoting any rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**.

This equation is coupled to the thermal model in §3. Solve it iteratively: estimate T_j, compute losses, recompute T_j, and repeat until converged. Two or three passes are enough.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, the current path runs through **one high-side and one low-side leg**.

- **Active leg (PWM side):**
  - The high-side FET conducts for fraction D.
  - The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the time.

So two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current, regardless of D. Including the PWM ripple current ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\left(I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)}$$

The ripple current is:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor, L_ph-ph ≈ 15 µH. At D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A, which adds only about 1% to the loss.

**Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 0.00218 \times (6400 + 6.4) = \mathbf{27.9\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average is about 27.9 / 6 ≈ 4.7 W per FET. The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is 80² × 2.18 mΩ ≈ **14 W**.

At low electrical RPM, the commutation interval approaches the thermal time constant of the die (on the order of milliseconds). For startup and stall cases, use the 14 W peak value, not the average.
