## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.5\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.375 = 2.06\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the complementary low-side conducts for (1−D). Together they carry I for 100% of the time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on and carries I for 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of sync rectification.

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect term

Assume an effective 0.5 mΩ per current path through the battery pad, internal planes, phase pad and solder:

$$P_{Cu,motor} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{mΩ} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.06\times10^{-3} = 26.4\ \text{W}$$

- Per FET, averaged over an electrical cycle (6 FETs share the load): 26.4 / 6 ≈ **4.4 W**
- Instantaneous per conducting FET: I²R = **13.2 W**. This matters at low eRPM, where sector dwell becomes comparable to the FET's thermal time constant (a few ms).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high-side FET is hard-switched. The low-side commutates through its body diode, which causes dead-time loss and Q_rr loss.

### 2.1 Gate-drive currents

The plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 3 V. The total gate resistance is R_g,ext 5 Ω + R_g,int 1 Ω = 6 Ω.

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{6} = 1.17\,\text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.5\,\text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.17} = 8.6\,\text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.5} = 20\,\text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components (per motor)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on+t_off)·f = ½·25.2·80·28.6 ns·48 k | **1.38 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2·t_dead·f = 0.8·80·2·100 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Gate charge (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f = 2·40 nC·10·48 k | 0.038 W (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead time is the largest controllable term after overlap. Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.
- Moving to 24 kHz halves P_sw, but increases current ripple and motor iron/copper loss.

## 3. Board-Level Power Budget at 4×80 A

| Source | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 26.4 W | 105.6 W |
| Switching | 2.1 W | 8.4 W |
| Copper / interconnect | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Bulk cap ESR | I_rms,cap ≈ 0.5·I → 40 A, ESR 2 mΩ (shared) | ~3.2 W (one shared bank) |
| Driver, LDOs, MCUs, H743 (~0.6 W) | | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 131 W** |

Battery current at full throttle is about 4×80×D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is **≈ 290 A**. The XT60 connector, 12 AWG leads and battery pads are a co-equal bottleneck.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state: not viable

With prop wash, a 20×20 board achieves θ_BA ≈ 8–15 K/W.

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 131\,\text{W} \times 8\,\text{K/W} \approx 1050\ \text{K}$$

This is impossible, so 80 A on all four motors is strictly a transient rating. The sustainable total is roughly (110 − 40) K / 8 K/W ≈ 9 W. That corresponds to about **20–25 A continuous per motor**, depending on airflow.
