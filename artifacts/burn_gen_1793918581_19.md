## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **2.2 mΩ**. This hot value is the design number.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one phase leg is PWM-switching, one phase leg has its low side held on, and one phase floats (used for BEMF sensing).

- **Switching leg:** the high side conducts for D·T_sw and the low side (sync rectifier) for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Static leg:** the low side conducts for the full period.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$

Including the dead-time correction, the sync FET conducts for (1−D−2t_dt·f_sw):

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,(2 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

### 1.3 Worked number (one motor channel, 80 A, hot)

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET average

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 120° (as high or low side). For bursts much longer than the electrical period (at 30 krpm with 7 pole pairs, the electrical period is about 0.29 ms), the heat spreads across all 6 devices:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 4.7\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case single device at low eRPM, such as stall or startup where the commutation step dwells, can see the full I²R for milliseconds. That case is covered in §4.2.

### 1.5 PCB copper conduction loss (often forgotten)

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}(100°C) \approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

Example: a phase pour of 15 mm × 4 mm × 70 µm (2 oz) gives R = 1.18 mΩ, so P = 80² × 1.18 m ≈ **7.5 W per phase path**. Use paralleled inner planes and stitching vias. This loss is comparable to the FET loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate drive currents during the Miller plateau

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,on}}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv,off}}$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3.5 V, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, R_g,int = 1 Ω, R_drv ≈ 2 Ω:

- I_g,on ≈ 0.84 A
- I_g,off ≈ 0.45 A

### 2.2 Transition times

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,off}}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 10 nC: t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 22 ns.

### 2.3 Loss components (switching leg only)

**Hard-switching V–I overlap (high side):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 34\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.65\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery of the sync FET body diode:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$
(Q_rr scales with di/dt. Re-check it at your actual slew.)

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = E_{oss}\,f_{sw} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
