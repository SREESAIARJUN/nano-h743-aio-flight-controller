# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5): Electrothermal Model and DShot Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three issues govern every design decision below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no on-FC BLDC/FOC commutation. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s), typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The netlist reflects this two-tier architecture.
2. **80 A burst on a 20×20 mm nano board is not physically credible.** It needs 24 power MOSFETs (5×6 mm class) plus drivers and caps on 400 mm² per side. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards are typically rated 25–45 A burst. 80 A burst is usually found on 30.5×30.5 mm or larger boards. The model below quantifies this, and the result is roughly 98 W of loss on a board that can reject about 8 W steady-state.
3. **All component values are representative assumptions, not verified part data.** Substitute datasheet values for your selected FETs before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Topology.** The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phase legs carry I_ph. Each leg has exactly one FET on (high or low, by PWM state), except during dead time.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

- R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.383 = 1.38 mΩ
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 6400 × 1.38 mΩ = **17.7 W**

**Per-FET loss.** Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle. That conduction is split between the high-side and low-side FET according to duty and sync-rect state.

- Average per FET ≈ 17.7 / 6 ≈ 2.95 W.
- Instantaneous peak per FET while conducting: I²R = **8.8 W**. This value drives the junction transient.

**Copper and interconnect.** Assume 0.3 mΩ per current path (PCB pours, vias, solder pads):

- P_Cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per motor
