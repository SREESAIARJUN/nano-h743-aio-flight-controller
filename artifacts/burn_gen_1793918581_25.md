# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In this architecture:

- The **H743 runs ArduPilot**. It generates four bidirectional DShot600 streams and decodes eRPM telemetry from them.
- **Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071KBU6. That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-channel MOSFETs.

This document uses the following baseline. Every symbol is parameterized, so you can substitute your own datasheet values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_in | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current per motor | I_pk | 80 A (phase current, DC-equivalent during conduction) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (example class: 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN) | R_ds(on),25 | 0.8 mΩ at V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 0.0045 /K (≈1.5× at 125 °C) |
| Gate-drain charge | Q_gd | 10 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward drop | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive (source/sink) | I_g | ≈1.0 A / 1.5 A, V_drv = 10 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Board | — | 30×30 mm stack-equivalent, 8-layer, 2 oz copper |

> **Verify these against your actual MOSFET datasheet.** Q_gd and Q_rr dominate the switching result.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) rectification. At any instant two phase legs are active:

- One high-side FET is PWM'd at duty D.
- Its complement (the low side of the same leg) conducts during (1 − D).
- The low-side FET of the return leg is on continuously for the 60° interval.

Within the PWM period, current therefore always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.8 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **1.16 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.16\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.8\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for one-third of an electrical cycle. Thermally, the eRPM frequency is much faster than the junction time constant, so the per-FET average is:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 14.8 / 6 \approx 2.5\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET conducts is about 7.4 W.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses

Copper losses are not negligible. Per motor, estimate 0.3 mΩ for the phase pad path plus about 0.15 mΩ per motor for the shared input planes:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.45\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check (electro-thermal feedback)

R_ds rises with temperature, so the steady state must satisfy:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta,ja}\cdot P(T_j)$$

This is stable only if:

$$R_{\theta,ja}\cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = R_{\theta,ja}\cdot 2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha < 1$$

At 80 A this gives R_θ,ja × 0.046 W/K < 1, so **R_θ,ja,eff must be below ~22 K/W per conducting pair**. Without forced airflow, this criterion is easily violated on a nano board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The complementary low side switches near zero voltage, apart from its body-diode intervals.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (Miller-plateau limited)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}} \approx 6.7\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 16.7\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.81\ W}$$

> Gate drivers like the FD6288Q are weaker than 1 A. That increases t_r/t_f roughly 3–5×, which pushes P_VI to **2.5–4 W**. **The driver choice is the biggest lever on switching loss.**

### 2.2 Output capacitance (Coss)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} \approx 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

> Datasheet Q_rr is specified at low di/dt. At real di/dt it may be 2–3× higher.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 6\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.144\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor at 80 A

$$P_{sw} = P_{VI}+P_{oss}+P_{rr}+P_{dt} \approx \mathbf{1.5\ W}\ \text{(strong driver)},\quad \approx 3.5\text{–}4.5\ \text{W (weak driver)}$$
