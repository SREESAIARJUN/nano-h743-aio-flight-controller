# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timing, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

These points shape every calculation that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. It does not run ESC firmware. The correct partition is:
   - **H743 (ArduPilot):** sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
   - **Four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware):** run 6-step or sinusoidal commutation at 48 kHz PWM and drive three-phase gate drivers.
2. **80 A on a "nano" board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** At 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm, the board can only absorb 80 A per motor for roughly 1–3 seconds, and only with airflow. Section 3.5 shows this quantitatively. Continuous ratings will land at about 30–40 A per motor. Marketing should be told this explicitly.
3. **Component parameters are representative, not vendor-final.** All MOSFET and driver values below are typical for 40 V, 3×3 mm DFN parts. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; the equations remain valid.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path
In 6-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- **During the PWM on-time:** one high-side FET and one low-side FET.
- **During the off-time:** the switching leg's low-side FET freewheels, together with the conducting low-side FET of the other leg.

Because synchronous rectification removes the body-diode path, both intervals are resistive. Current therefore always passes through 2·R_ds, independent of duty cycle D.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.0059/\text{K}\Rightarrow k_T(110^\circ\text{C})\approx1.5$$

$$R_{ds,hot}=1.3\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.5=1.95\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Conduction loss per motor
$$P_{cond}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}=2(80)^2(1.95\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{24.96\ W}$$

### 2.4 PCB copper loss
Assume a phase-plus-bus path of 0.4 mΩ, using 2 oz copper on internal layers:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R=6400\times0.4\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{2.56\ W/motor}$$

This term is frequently omitted, and it is significant at 80 A. It is the main argument for a 6-layer stackup with 2 oz inner layers.

### 2.5 Electrothermal feedback
R_ds depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j=T_{board}+P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{th,j-b}$$

The loop converges if the following holds:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_{th}=I^2R_{25}\alpha\,R_{th}<1$$

Per FET, with an average of 1/3 of the conduction share (see §3.3) and R_th,j-b = 3 K/W:

$$\frac{6400}{3}\times1.3\times10^{-3}\times0.0059\times3 \approx 0.049 \ll 1$$

The loop is stable, so there is no runaway at the FET level. Runaway risk exists only at the board level, through heat capacity (§3.5).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg switches at a time. That leg makes one turn-on and one turn-off hard transition per PWM period.

### 3.1 Loss components (per motor)

**V-I overlap (hard switching):**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}=25.2\times50\text{ nC}\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance (C_oss) discharge:**
$$P_{oss}=C_{oss,er}V_{bus}^2f_{sw}=1.5\text{ nF}\times635\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs per switching leg; this loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET):
$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2\times60\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{ kHz}=\mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

**Total switching-related loss:** ≈ **2.71 W/motor**

### 3.2 Frequency sensitivity
$$\frac{dP_{sw,total}}{df}\approx56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.35 W per motor (about 5.4 W for the board) at 80 A. The benefit is lower current ripple and quieter motors. For 80 A burst operation, 48 kHz is acceptable. Consider allowing AM32 to fall back to 24 kHz under high load.

### 3.3 Per-FET distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time as the "steady" device and switches during a portion of it. Average loss per FET:

$$P_{FET,avg}\approx\frac{24.96+2.71}{6}=\mathbf{4.6\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak is about 12.5 W, but at electrical frequencies above 1 kHz the junction thermal time constant (≈ ms) averages it out.
