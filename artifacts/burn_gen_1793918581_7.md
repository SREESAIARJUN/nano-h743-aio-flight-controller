These are baseline assumptions. Change any of them and recompute using the equations in §1–§3.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|

| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) 6-step | AM32 or BLHeli_32 class firmware |
| ESC MCU | 4 × AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | The H743 is the **FC**, not the ESC MCU |
| FET | 40 V, R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V, PDFN 5×6 | Representative of the class |
| Gate driver | 3 × half-bridge per motor (FD6288Q class), I_g ≈ 1.5 A | |
| FC protocol | ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600 | |

**Design-review flags. Resolve these before layout:**

1. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A per motor is not physically credible.** A 20×20 mm board cannot place 24 FETs in 5×6 packages. It also lacks the thermal mass for an approximately 100 W burst (§2.4). I recommend one of two options:
   - **(a)** Keep 80 A per motor and move to 30.5×30.5 mm (M3) as a stacked FC + ESC.
   - **(b)** Keep 20×20 mm and derate to roughly 35–40 A burst per motor, using 3.3×3.3 FETs.
   
   The math below is written for (a). The scaling for (b) is given in §2.5.
2. **Firmware split.** The H743 never switches the bridge. It only emits DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-MCU firmware setting.

## 1. Loss Model per Motor (One 3-Phase Bridge)

### 1.1 Conduction Topology
In 6-step complementary PWM, two phases are active at any instant: phase A (PWM'd) and phase B (low side held on).

- During t_on, current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- During t_off, current flows through LS_A (synchronous rectification) and LS_B.

Current therefore always crosses **exactly two channel resistances**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{/K} \Rightarrow \times1.5 \text{ at } 125\,°C$$

The phase current includes the PWM ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Assume a low-inductance racing motor with L_ph = 15 µH, D = 0.5, and V = 25.2 V. Then:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (2 × 15 µH × 48 kHz) = **4.4 A p-p**.
- The ripple adds 1.6 A² to I², which is negligible at 80 A (0.03 %).

**At 80 A burst:**

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0012 = **15.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.8 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0018 = **23.0 W** (design value) |

### 1.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz
Only the PWM'd high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side commutates at roughly the body-diode voltage, so it switches at near-zero V_ds.

**(a) V–I overlap.**

Gate-charge-limited transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,nC + 7.5\,nC}{1.5\,A} \approx 15\,ns,\qquad t_f \approx 15\,ns$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,ns \times 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50n \times 25.2 \times 48k = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 50 ns, two edges per period, V_f = 0.8 V):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100n \times 48k = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

**(d) C_oss** (C_oss,eff ≈ 2 nF):

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f = 0.5 \times 2n \times 635 \times 48k = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (Q_g = 60 nC, two FETs switching per active phase):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{gs}f = 2 \times 60n \times 10 \times 48k = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Most of P_g dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 1.3 Shunt and Copper Losses
- **Per-motor current shunt** (0.5 mΩ, sees battery-side current I·D): at D = 0.8, I_shunt,rms ≈ 80√0.8 = 71.6 A, giving P = **2.56 W**. Consider a 0.25 mΩ shunt or a Hall sensor.
- **Phase pad and trace copper** (~0.3 mΩ for 2 oz × 4 layers × 6 mm × 15 mm, ×2 paths): 2 × 6400 × 0.0003 = **3.84 W**.

### 1.4 Per-Motor Total at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.04 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| Shunt | 2.56 |
| Copper | 3.84 |
| **Total** | **≈ 31.4 W** |

The four-motor board dissipates **≈ 126 W** at full burst. FETs account for ≈ 100 W of that.

Switching is only about 6 % of the loss at 48 kHz. **Conduction dominates**, so R_ds(on) and copper are the levers that matter.

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw-related terms scale linearly with f_sw:
- 24 kHz: ≈ 0.95 W per motor
- 96 kHz: ≈ 3.8
