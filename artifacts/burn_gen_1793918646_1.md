# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #50)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture assumption:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 does not drive the gates directly. This partitioning is standard for 4-in-1 stacks and keeps the H7 timer budget available for DShot.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
The drive is a 6-step trapezoidal scheme with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, phase current flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:
- one device of the PWM leg (HS during ON, LS during OFF/synchronous freewheel), and
- the static low-side of the return leg.

Synchronous rectification keeps the current in a channel (not a diode) for both PWM states. Conduction loss is therefore approximately **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.81\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Ripple contribution at 48 kHz
The worst-case ripple occurs at an effective D(1−D) of 0.25:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds less than 0.06% to conduction loss. This is a key benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where ripple would be about 13 A pk-pk and acoustic and iron losses would be higher.

### 1.4 Result per channel

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 2 × 6404 × 1.81 mΩ | **23.2 W** |
| Shunt | 6404 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Copper (est. 0.3 mΩ path, 2 oz) | I²R | ~1.9 W |
| **Subtotal conduction** | | **~26.7 W** |

### 1.5 Thermal-runaway fixed point
Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th,eff}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)$$

$$\Rightarrow T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th}\,k\,R_{ds,25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,k\,R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}, \quad k = \frac{2I^2}{6}$$

Stability requires R_th · k · R_ds,25 · α < 1. With k ≈ 2133 A² per FET-share, R_ds,25 = 1.2 mΩ, and α = 0.006, the product is 0.0154 · R_th. Electrical runaway therefore needs R_th > 65 K/W. That cannot happen with soldered DFNs, so the real constraint is **board heat capacity**, covered in §4.
