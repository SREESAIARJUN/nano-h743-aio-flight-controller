## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths
In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through **exactly two conducting channels in series**: one high-side and one low-side, or the complementary FET during the PWM off-time. So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase = I_burst.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (coupled, must iterate)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot Z_{th,ja}(t)$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration. Converges if $\alpha\, I^2 R_{25} Z_{th} < 1$. If this inequality fails, you have **thermal runaway**, and that is the formal stability criterion for burst rating.

### 1.3 Worked numbers (per motor, 80 A)

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond (2 FETs) | Per conducting FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 9.6 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 12.9 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 13.9 W |

Each FET conducts on average 1/3 of the electrical cycle at full current (plus complementary intervals). Averaged across the 6 FETs of one bridge, at 125 °C:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 27.9/6 \cdot 2 \approx 9.3\ \mathrm{W\ per\ FET}$$

The factor of 2 accounts for each instant having two conducting devices.

**Four motors at 80 A burst:** ≈ 112 W conduction alone at T_j = 125 °C.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, only **one leg per motor is PWM-switching** at any instant. The other active leg is static. Switching loss is therefore per motor, not per FET-count.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\,\mathrm{ns}\cdot48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.2\,\mathrm{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.018\ \mathrm{W}$$

Use the energy-equivalent C_o(er) if the datasheet gives it. It is higher than C_oss at V_bus.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\,\mathrm{ns}\cdot48\,\mathrm{k} = 1.23\ \mathrm{W}$$

This is the largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it but needs verified gate-driver propagation matching.

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\,\mathrm{k}\cdot2 = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 0.97+0.02+1.23+0.05 \approx 2.3\ \mathrm{W}$$

**48 kHz vs 24 kHz:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw (≈1.1 W saved per motor at 24 kHz). 48 kHz reduces current ripple and audible noise, but at 80 A it is a secondary loss term. Conduction dominates by about 12:1.

## 4. Transient Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network
Use a Foster RC ladder per FET into a shared board node:

```
P_FET ──[R_jc=1.2K/W]──┬──[R_cb≈2K/W*]──┬── T_board ──[R_ba]── T_amb
                    C_j≈0.02J/K      C_pad      C_board         (prop-wash convection)
```
\*R_cb includes solder, thermal vias and spreading. It is highly layout-dependent.

### 4.2 Board-node lumped capacity
A 4-in-1 nano ESC board (20×20 mm, 6–8 layers, 2 oz Cu, plus FETs) has:

$$C_{board} \approx m_{Cu}c_{Cu} + m_{FR4}c_{FR4} \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{g}\cdot0.385 + 2\,\mathrm{g}\cdot1.1 \approx 3.2\ \mathrm{J/K}$$

### 4.3 Adiabatic burst limit
For burst durations shorter than the board-to-air time constant (τ_ba ≈ R_ba·C_board ≈ 15 K/W × 3.2 J/K ≈ 48 s), treat the board as adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{board}} = \frac{140}{3.2} \approx 44\ \mathrm{K/s}$$

Set T_a = 40 °C, a junction limit of 150 °C, and a junction-over-board rise of ≈ 9.3 W × 3.2 K/W ≈ 30 K. Then:

$$t_{burst,max} \approx \frac{(150-30)-40}{44} \approx 1.8\ \mathrm{s}\quad\text{(all four motors at 80 A)}$$

### 4.4 Design verdict
- **80 A × 4 simultaneous on a nano board is only credible as a ≈1–2 s rating**, and only with the best-case 1.5 mΩ FETs, which likely don't physically fit.
- With realistic 3×3 mm FETs (≈3 mΩ), conduction roughly doubles and t_burst drops below 1 s.
- A defensible spec for a 20×20 nano 4-in-1 is typically **≈35–45 A continuous / 55–65 A burst (≤5 s)** per motor. Recommend validating with IR thermography plus a thermocouple on the FET tab during a stepped-load bench test before publishing any 80 A figure.
- Mitigations: 3 oz inner copper, a via-in-pad array under each FET drain (≥ 12 × 0.3 mm vias), an aluminium spreader, firmware current limiting (AM32 `current_limit`) and ArduPilot `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`.
