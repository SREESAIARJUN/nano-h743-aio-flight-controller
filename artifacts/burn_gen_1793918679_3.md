## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

Let θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset be the electrical angle.

**Clarke** (balanced, i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\,(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Plant in the dq frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The torque is

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p\lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q .
$$

### 1.3 PI design by pole-zero cancellation

Use the controller C(s) = K_p + K_i/s on the plant 1/(Ls+R). Choosing K_i/K_p = R/L cancels the electrical pole. The closed loop then becomes first order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p^{(i)} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i^{(i)} = R\,\omega_c
$$

**Bandwidth limit.** The controller sees a total delay of about 1.5·Ts (computation plus PWM hold). To keep roughly 60° phase margin:

$$
\omega_c \le \frac{1}{1.5 T_s}\cdot\frac{\pi}{6}\cdot 2 \approx \frac{1}{4.3\,T_s}
$$

At Ts = 50 µs this gives ω_c ≈ 2π·750 Hz. I use **ω_c = 2π·600 Hz**, which yields:

- K_p = 6.79 V/A
- K_i = 21 100 V/(A·s)

**Decoupling feedforward**, added to the PI outputs:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\hat\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \hat\omega_e L i_d + \hat\omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Discretization.** The integrator uses forward Euler with a clamp on its state. The output voltage is circularly limited:

$$
|v_{dq}| \le \frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}\cdot m_{max},\qquad m_{max}=0.95
$$

The d-axis gets priority on the voltage budget. Anti-windup uses back-calculation with gain K_aw = 1/K_p.

### 1.4 SVPWM by min/max injection

This is algebraically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM:

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta),\quad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big),\quad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

### 1.5 Speed and position estimation (encoder PLL)

Differentiating a 14-bit encoder directly is far too noisy at 20 kHz. Use a type-II tracking PLL instead:

$$
e_k = \text{wrap}_{\pm\pi}(\theta_{m,k} - \hat\theta_k)
$$

$$
\hat\omega_{k+1} = \hat\omega_k + K_{i}^{pll}T_s e_k,\qquad
\hat\theta_{k+1} = \hat\theta_k + T_s(\hat\omega_k + K_{p}^{pll}e_k)
$$

The gains come from the PLL's natural frequency and damping:

$$
K_p^{pll} = 2\zeta\omega_n,\qquad K_i^{pll} = \omega_n^2
$$

I use ζ = 1 and ω_n = 2π·250 Hz. This ω_n should be at least 3× the velocity loop bandwidth.

The PLL also provides a latency-compensated electrical angle. Because the PWM output acts about 1.5·Ts after the encoder read, the angle is advanced:

$$
\theta_e = p(\hat\theta + 1.5\,T_s\,\hat\omega)
$$
