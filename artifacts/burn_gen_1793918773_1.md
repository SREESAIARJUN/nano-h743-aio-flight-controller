## 0. Architecture Decisions and Sanity Flags

| Item | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Motor control | 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class running AM32), with the H743 as flight controller only | The H743 has only 2 advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8), so it can drive at most 2 complementary 3-phase bridges. Back-EMF zero-crossing comparators per motor also favor dedicated MCUs. |
| FC→ESC link | Bidirectional DShot600 from the H743 | Native ArduPilot support, with eRPM telemetry for harmonic notch filtering. |
| Switching | 48 kHz, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM | Matches the AM32 default range. |
| Bus | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max. Component ratings use 40 V FETs. | Regen and ringing spikes reach about 35 V. |

**Red flag: 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows the board survives about **1–2 s at 4×80 A** before hitting T_j limits. Specify "80 A" as a **≤1 s burst rating**, and set continuous current around 35–45 A depending on airflow. Use **2 paralleled FETs per switch position** if the footprint allows; otherwise move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses: 6-step synchronous model

In any commutation sector, exactly two phases are active:
- **Active (PWM) leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_s. Its complementary low-side FET freewheels for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the entire sector.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two FET positions in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{cu}$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Phase-current ripple adds a small RMS term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\text{ A} \Rightarrow \Delta I^2/12 = 3.6\text{ A}^2$$

This is negligible against 6400 A².

**Per-FET breakdown.** Each FET carries current for one-third of the electrical cycle in each conducting role:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R\,D \;+\; \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R \;\;(\text{as static low? no, HS is never static})$$

Correct form for the 6-step scheme where PWM is applied on the high side:

- High-side FET: $P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds} D$
- Low-side FET: $P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}$

Summing across all six FETs gives 2·I²·R_ds, which checks against the bridge equation above.

**At 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_ds = 1.5 × 1.4675 = **2.20 mΩ**
- Single FET per position: P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 2.20 mΩ = **28.2 W**
- Copper: 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**
- Two paralleled FETs per position: FET conduction loss drops to **14.1 W**

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The transition times follow from gate-drain charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx 8\text{–}15\text{ ns}$$

Use 15 ns for margin.
