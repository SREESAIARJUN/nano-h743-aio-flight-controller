# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Reference Architecture and Loss Model

> **Scope:** This is an engineering reference model. All device parameters are stated explicitly so you can substitute values from your chosen datasheets. Before any production release, verify every number against the actual MOSFET, driver, and PCB stack-up data and confirm it on the bench with thermocouples and IR.

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| **MCU partitioning** | The H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors itself in an ArduPilot build. The architecture must be **H743 (ArduPilot) plus 4× dedicated ESC MCUs** (STM32G071/AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware), linked by bidirectional DShot600. |
| **"Nano" (20×20 mm) at 80 A** | **Not physically viable.** The model below shows that a 20×20 board reaches its junction limit in about 1 s at 80 A per channel. A **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 5×6 mm PDFN FETs reaches about 3 s. The "80A" figure should be marketed as a ≤2 s burst on 30×30, not as a nano rating. |
| **System current** | 4 × 80 A phase current at about 90% duty means roughly **290 A of battery current**. This exceeds any XT60 (60 A continuous) or XT90 rating and stresses 6S packs below about 50 Ah·C. Size the connector and pads for the realistic 4 × 45 A continuous case. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme:** six-step trapezoidal control with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant the current flows through exactly **two** conducting channels:

- the PWM leg (high side for D, low side for 1−D), and
- the low side of the return phase, which is on continuously.

**Current with ripple**, where ΔI is the peak-to-peak phase ripple:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

**Channel conduction loss:**

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{pcb}
$$
