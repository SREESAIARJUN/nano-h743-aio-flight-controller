## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in trapezoidal synchronous drive

During each 60° commutation sector, exactly one phase leg PWMs and one low-side MOSFET conducts statically:

- **PWM on:** High-side (HS) of phase A and low-side (LS) of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off (synchronous):** LS of phase A and LS of phase B conduct.

So **two MOSFET channels carry the full phase current at all times**, apart from dead time.

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha_T \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2\, R_{Cu}$$

Per MOSFET, averaged over the electrical cycle at duty D. Each leg is active for 120° (1/3 of the cycle):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, D\, I^2 R_{DS} \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(1-D)\, I^2 R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{DS}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D = 0.9, Tj = 125 °C

- R_DS(hot) = 0.70 × 1.5 = **1.05 mΩ**
- MOSFET conduction: 2 × 80² × 1.05 mΩ = **13.44 W per channel**
- Copper (bus, phase pads, shunt; R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ incl. a 0.25 mΩ shunt): 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.20 W**
- Averaged per MOSFET:
  - P_HS = (1/3)(0.9)(6.72 W) = 2.02 W
  - P_LS = (1/3)(0.1)(6.72 W) + (1/3)(6.72 W) = 2.46 W

The low-side MOSFET is the hottest device for conduction.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWMing leg switches.

| Loss term | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k = **1.935 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw (t_dt = 100 ns) | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2 · 50 nC · 48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Output capacitance | V·Q_oss·f_sw | 25.2 · 60 nC · 48 k = **0.073 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2 · 60 nC · 10 V · 48 k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.74 W** |

**Design lever:** Halving t_dt to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz roughly halves P_sw but increases motor current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.1 Bus capacitor ripple

The RMS ripple current drawn from the bus capacitors is:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80\sqrt{0.09} = 24\ \text{A per channel}$$

Four channels are partially interleaved. Budget about 35 A RMS total using:

- 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R ceramics per channel, plus
- a 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail.

### 2.2 Totals

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per channel (MOSFETs + copper + switching) | 13.44 + 3.20 + 2.74 ≈ **19.4 W** |
| 4 channels at 80 A burst | ≈ **77.5 W** |
| Electrical efficiency (input power 4 × 22.2 V × 80 A × 0.9 ≈ 6.4 kW) | ≈ **98.8 %** |

The efficiency is excellent, but 77 W into a roughly 10 g board is the real constraint.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Lumped board model (first-order RC)

| Quantity | Derivation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Heat capacity C_th | 8-layer 2 oz, 36×36 mm, ~6 g Cu + 3 g FR4 + 3 g Al heat spreader: (6·0.385 + 3·1.1 + 3·0.9) | ≈ **8.3 J/K** |
| Convective R_θ | Prop-wash h ≈ 80 W/m²K, two-sided area 2.6e-3 m²: 1/(h·A) | ≈ **4.8 K/W** |
| Time constant τ | R_θ·C_th | ≈ **40 s** |

The board temperature rise over time is:

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_\theta\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

### 3.2 Burst duration limit

Allow a board rise from 40 °C ambient to 100 °C (ΔT = 60 K). At P = 77.5 W, the steady-state rise would be 372 K, which is impossible. The allowed burst time is therefore:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{60}{372}\right) = -40\ln(0.839) \approx \mathbf{7.0\ s}$$

The **initial adiabatic slope** is P/C ≈ 9.3 K/s.
