## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous)

### 2.1 Current Path

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-driven (high-side duty D, low-side complementary at 1−D) and one phase is held low.

Ignoring dead time, the motor current I always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:
- one switch in the PWM leg (either the high side or the synchronous low side), and
- the low side of the return leg.

The duty cycle therefore cancels out of the per-ESC conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ \times 1.5 \text{ at } 110\,°\text{C}
$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET breakdown.** Each leg conducts for 240° of 360° electrical.
- Hottest device: a low-side FET that carries the full current for 120° plus synchronous conduction during PWM.
- Instantaneous loss while conducting: $80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6$ W.
- Average loss: ≈ 3.2 W per FET (19.2 W ÷ 6), with up to ≈ 4 W on the worst low-side device at low duty.

### 2.3 Copper and Interconnect Loss

This term is often forgotten. Assume an effective series path of 0.3 mΩ per channel (vias, pads, phase pours, battery pads):

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The synchronous low side turns on after the body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

### 3.1 Loss Components

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f$ = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f$ = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | $Q_{oss} V f$ = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | $2 Q_g V_{GS} f$ = 2·50 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.05 W (dissipated in the driver and R_g) |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $2 V_F I\, t_{dt} f$ = 2·0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **1.23 W** |

$$
P_{sw,ESC} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}
$$

**Design levers:**
- Dead time is the largest secondary loss. Trimming it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin first.
- Dropping f_sw to 24 kHz halves P_sw. The cost is higher current ripple and audible noise.

### 3.2 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Term | W per channel | W for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 | 76.8 |
| Copper | 1.9 | 7.6 |
| Switching + dead time | 3.3 | 13.3 |
| Driver, LDO, MCU | 0.3 | 1.2 |
| **Total** | **≈24.7** | **≈99 W** |

ESC efficiency at this point: output power ≈ 25.2·0.9·80 ≈ 1.8 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98.6 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is the *heat density*.

### 3.3 Thermal Model (Lumped RC)

**Junction to board.** For PQFN 3.3×3.3:
- R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W
- R_θ(case→board) through solder and via array ≈ 1.0 K/W
- ΔT_J-board at 9.6 W instantaneous (time-averaged ≈ 4 W) ≈ **9–21 K above the PCB**. This is acceptable.
