## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. The PWM leg alternates between its high-side (HS) and synchronous low-side (LS) switch. The return leg's LS stays on continuously.

Therefore, **exactly two FETs carry the phase current at all times**, apart from the dead time.

Per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\, R_{Cu} + I_{rms}^2 R_{shunt}$$

where R_DS rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{2 L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

The factor of 2 appears because two phase windings are in series.

The RMS current then includes the ripple term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Worked case: L_ph = 8 µH (a typical 2207 motor), D = 0.85, V_bemf ≈ 18 V.

- ΔI = (25.2 − 18)(0.85) / (2 · 8e-6 · 48e3) = **8.0 A p-p**
- Ripple term: ΔI²/12 = 5.3 A², which is **< 0.1 %** of 6400 A²

At 48 kHz the ripple is negligible for conduction. This is one benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.3 Electrothermal fixed point

Because R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CS})$$

$$P_{FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot k$$

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). k ≈ 1 accounts for HS/LS sharing on the PWM leg.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | 2·I²·R_DS per motor (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.50 | 19.2 |
| 100 | 2.01 | 25.7 |
| 125 | 2.18 | **27.9** |

The remaining conduction terms per motor:

| Path | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Copper bus + phase pours (estimate) | 0.6 mΩ total | 3.8 W |
| Shunt (single bus shunt, shared across the ESC) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

**Per-motor conduction loss at T_j = 125 °C: ≈ 33 W.**

**Four motors at 80 A: ≈ 133 W.** This is the dominant term.

Per-FET average power is about 27.9/6 ≈ **4.7 W**. While a FET is conducting, its instantaneous dissipation is about 14 W.

At eRPM ≥ 1 kHz, the electrical period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms). Average power is therefore the correct input to the thermal network.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Analyze per motor, per switching period.

### 2.1 Voltage-current overlap

Gate-driver-limited transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}}$$

Gate current is set by driver capability and the gate resistor:

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

With R_g = 4.7 Ω, V_plateau ≈ 3.5 V, and R_g,int = 1 Ω:

- I_g ≈ 1.1 A
