**The 80A figure is ambiguous, and it changes the design completely.** It can mean two things:

| Interpretation | Per-channel loss | Total board loss | Verdict on a 20×20/25.5 mm board |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A: 80 A per motor channel (burst)** | ~25.6 W | ~102 W | Adiabatic burst only, about 2 s. Not sustainable. |

I model both. The PCB stackup, battery copper, and connector (XT60 vs. pads) depend on which one the customer actually means. **Get this confirmed in writing.**

**Architecture assumption.** The H743 is the flight controller MCU. It is not a good choice to run 4× sensorless FOC/6-step itself. The standard topology, used here, is:

- H743 → 4× DShot600 (bidirectional) → 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071-class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) → 4× 3-phase gate drivers → 24 N-MOSFETs.
- A single ESC MCU driving all four motors is possible, but it breaks standard firmware support.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, current at any instant flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET of two different phases. With complementary PWM:

- During the ON time, current flows through HS(phase a) and LS(phase b).
- During the OFF time, current freewheels through LS(phase a), which is synchronous, and LS(phase b).

In both states, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series. This holds after dead time, and only if the ESC firmware uses complementary PWM, as AM32 does.

$$P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

The current is split across six FETs as follows:

- Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical period.
- A given FET is in the current path about 1/3 of the time, split between ON-state and freewheel roles depending on duty D.

At electrical frequencies well above the thermal time constant of the junction (more than ~100 Hz, which is true above roughly 1,000 eRPM), use the averaged value:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times k_{imb}, \quad k_{imb}\approx1.2\text{ (low-side carries freewheel at low D)}$$

**Low-RPM caveat.** At startup or stall, one pair conducts for milliseconds at a time. In that regime, use the instantaneous loss 2·I²·R split over two FETs, not the 1/6 average. Locked-rotor desync is the real killer here, not cruise.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage after diode conduction.

|---|---|---|---|
