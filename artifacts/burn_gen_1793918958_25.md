## 0. Architectural Reality Check (Read First)

Three facts shape the rest of this document.

1. **The 48 kHz PWM is not generated by the H743.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole bridge set) is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 only emits DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The netlist follows that partition.

2. **"80A" on a nano form factor is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows that the board's thermal mass, not the MOSFET junction, limits 80A operation to roughly **5 s** with prop-wash cooling. Continuous capability is about **35 to 40 A per motor** unless a heatsink is bonded to the board.

3. **84 W of heat sits beside the IMU.** The thermal gradient and current loops are a gyro-bias and magnetometer problem as much as a reliability problem. Use a split stack (a separate ESC board) or an IMU heater with thermal isolation slots. I treat this as a design requirement, not an option.

### Commutation model

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM on the active phase leg. At any instant, exactly **two FETs carry the full phase current I**: one high-side or its complementary low-side, plus one low-side.

## 2. Loss Derivation (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

During either PWM state, two FETs conduct the full current I:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_J),\qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_J)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_J-25)\right],\ \alpha\approx0.0053\,/\mathrm{K}$$

At T_J = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.26\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{16.1\ W}$$

**PCB copper loss.** The path is battery → bus → leg → phase pad → leg → return. Assuming 0.25 mΩ per leg path (2 oz, 4 paralleled layers) gives R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R_{Cu}=6400\times0.5\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This is distributed through the board, not concentrated in the FETs, but it heats the same thermal mass.

**Thermal runaway check.** Because R_ds(on) rises with T_J, the loss is self-reinforcing:

$$T_J = T_{ref} + P(T_J)\,R_{\theta}$$

The loop is stable if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_\theta = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_\theta < 1$$

With the effective per-FET R_θ ≈ 1.5 K/W: 2·6400·0.9e-3·0.0053·1.5 ≈ 0.09, which is less than 1. The loop is stable, so there is no electrothermal runaway at the FET level.
