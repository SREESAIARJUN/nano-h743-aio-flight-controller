# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Architecture & Analysis

**Job #19: Design Basis Document, Rev A**

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

These points correct assumptions in the brief. They affect the entire model.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, commutation and the 48 kHz bridge PWM run on dedicated ESC MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 commands to them.
   - This design uses **4× AT32F421K8U7** (or STM32G071) running **AM32** firmware.
   - A single-MCU ESC with 4 motors is possible but has worse fault isolation. It is rejected.
2. **"80 A burst" in a nano form factor (~25.5×25.5 mm board, 20×20 mounting) is thermally limited to seconds.** Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a per-channel transient rating, not continuous.
3. **The voltage basis is 6S (25.2 V full charge).** This requires 40 V MOSFETs.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Reference MOSFET Parameters

The model is parameterised so you can substitute your selected device. The reference part is a 40 V N-ch in 3.3×3.3 mm DFN:

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ (max, V_GS = 10 V) | On-resistance |
| α_T | 1.55 (normalised at T_j = 125 °C) | R_DS temperature multiplier |
| Q_g | 30 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_gs2 + Q_gd | 9 nC | Switching charge |
| Q_rr | 30 nC (high di/dt) | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| Q_oss | 40 nC | Output charge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Junction-to-case |

Hot on-resistance: **R_DS(T) = R_DS,25 · [1 + k(T_j − 25)]**, with k ≈ 0.0055 /K. This gives R_DS(125 °C) = 2.33 mΩ.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss: 6-Step Synchronous BLDC

In trapezoidal 6-step operation, exactly two phases carry current at any instant. With synchronous (complementary) PWM, the current path always passes through exactly **two conducting MOSFET channels**:

- During on-time: HS(A) + LS(B)
- During off-time: LS(A) + LS(B)

So the conduction loss is independent of duty:

**P_cond,ch = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)**

Average per FET (each leg conducts for 240° of 360°, split across its HS/LS by duty):

**P_cond,FET,avg = P_cond,ch / 6** (balanced over a full electrical revolution, for f_e ≫ 1/τ_thermal of the die)

At I_ph = 80 A:

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond,ch | Per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.12 mΩ | 27.2 W | 4.5 W |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ | **29.8 W** | **5.0 W** |

PCB copper adds loss. Assume ~0.3 mΩ effective phase-path resistance (2 oz, multilayer, via stitched):

- P_Cu = 2 · 80² · 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W** per channel, deposited directly into the board.

**Electrothermal feedback.** Conduction loss rises with temperature, which raises loss further:

P(T) = P₀[1 + k(T − 25)]

With a thermal resistance R_θ, the steady state is:

**T_ss = T_a + P₀R_θ(1 − 25k) / (1 − k·P₀·R_θ)**

**Thermal runaway occurs when k·P₀·R_θ ≥ 1**, i.e. R_θ ≥ 1/(0.0055 × 19.2) ≈ 9.5 K/W per channel. A nano board's convective R_θ is well above this, which is why 80 A must remain a burst rating.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per channel, per PWM period there is one turn-on and one turn-off of the active HS FET. The LS FET switches near-ZVS after dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

Edge times: t_r ≈ t_f ≈ (Q_gs2 + Q_gd)/I_g. With a ~0.6 A effective driver current and gate resistor, t ≈ 15 ns.

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k
= **1.45 W**

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

AM32 dead time is set per hardware target. Assume t_dt = 300 ns per edge, two edges per period:

P_dt = V_SD · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw
= 0.8 × 80 × 600 ns × 48 k
= **1.84 W**

This is larger than the overlap loss. Minimise dead time to the driver's guaranteed shoot-through margin.

**(c) Reverse recovery**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W**
