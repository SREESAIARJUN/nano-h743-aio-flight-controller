# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal and Bidirectional DShot600 Design

**Status: Rev A model.** Several input parameters were not specified, so they are stated as assumptions in §0. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values. The most important finding comes first.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** An 80 A-per-channel burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally limited to roughly **0.6–0.8 s**, about **4 s** if derated to 50 A, and is not sustainable on any small multirotor PCB without a heatsink. §3.4 quantifies this. If "80 A" is the *total* battery current (20 A per channel), the design is comfortable. Please confirm which spec is intended before layout.

**Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and does **not** commutate the motors. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 (AT32F421-class), receive bidirectional DShot600 from the H743, and return eRPM telemetry. On an AIO board the ESC MCUs share the PCB but have their own clocks and firmware. This separation is the industry-standard partition and is what ArduPilot expects.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}\ (\text{typical 40 V trench FET})$$

At T_j = 110 °C (the iterated operating point, §3.3): R_ds ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one high-side (HS) FET of phase A and one low-side (LS) FET of phase B carry current. With complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **On-state (duty D):** current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- **Freewheel (1−D):** current flows through LS_A (synchronous) and LS_B.

So the motor current **always passes through exactly two channels**, regardless of D (dead time excepted):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot k_{ripple},\qquad k_{ripple} = 1+\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I}\right)^2$$

**Ripple.** The phase inductance of a typical 2207–2810 motor is L ≈ 15 µH line-to-line. Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\times0.25}{15\,\mu\mathrm{H}\times48\,\mathrm{kHz}}=8.75\ \mathrm{A}\Rightarrow k_{ripple}=1.001$$
