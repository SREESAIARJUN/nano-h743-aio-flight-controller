## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

Three issues determine everything downstream. Resolve them before layout.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to a separate ESC MCU. That ESC MCU is typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. Section 3 covers the H743 side. Sections 1–2 cover the ESC power stage.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as per motor channel, ≤5 s.** This matches 4-in-1 marketing convention. As shown below, 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board is thermally capacity-limited, not steady-state capable. Treat the result as a burst rating with a hard firmware current/temperature limit.
3. **Component values are representative, not datasheet-verified.** The MOSFET, driver and passive parameters are typical of 40 V DFN3×3 parts. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations. The structure of the model does not change.

## 1. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

At any instant in 6-step operation, two phase legs carry I_ph.

- **Switched leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for D, and the low side (LS) synchronously rectifies for 1−D. One FET always carries the current.
- **Return leg:** the LS is held on.

So exactly two R_ds(on) are in the current path at all times, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

The temperature dependence is approximately linear:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \beta (T_j - 25)], \quad \beta \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1} \;\Rightarrow\; 1.5\times \text{ at } 110\,°C$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

At a 25 °C junction, conduction loss is 12.8 W. The hot value is used for design.

**Thermal-runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with temperature, so the loop must be stable. The stability condition is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta,JA,eff} < 1$$

Here ∂P/∂T_j = I²·2·R_ds,25·β = 6400 × 2 × 1e-3 × 0.0059 = 0.076 W/K.

Steady state requires R_θ,eff < 13 K/W. A board-level R_θ of about 6.7 K/W (§2) gives margin. Stability is not the issue; heat capacity is.

### 1.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched HS Only)

With synchronous rectification and inductive load, the LS turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting. This is near zero-voltage switching (ZVS), so its V·I overlap loss is negligible. The HS hard-switches.

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}} = \frac{7}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} = \frac{3}{3} = 1.0\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 10.7\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{1.0\ \text{A}} = 15.0\ \text{ns}$$

**V·I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25.7\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.24\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery.** The LS body diode is recovered by the HS turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 30\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$
