## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

These constraints shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In this architecture the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32). The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and decode bidirectional DShot600. The H743 only emits DShot and captures eRPM telemetry.

2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm stack is a burst-only rating.** Section 3 shows the thermal mass of a nano board supports roughly **1–3 s** at 80 A × 4 before reaching T_j limits. A sustained rating of about 30–35 A per motor is realistic. The datasheet should say "80 A burst (≤2 s, 25 °C ambient, with airflow)".

3. **Package choice.** The H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) does not fit a 20×20 mm board with gyro, OSD and connectors. Use **STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm)**. This requires a 6-layer HDI stack with via-in-pad for escape routing.

4. **Voltage class.** The target is 6S (25.2 V max). Use 40 V MOSFETs only if layout keeps ringing below about 32 V. The conservative choice is 60 V parts at a higher R_DS(on). This spec assumes 40 V with a strict layout and a TVS on the bus.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The drive is six-step (trapezoidal) with synchronous rectification:

- At any instant two phases carry current I.
- One leg is PWM-switched: its high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw and its low-side (LS) for (1−D)·T_sw.
- The other leg's LS is held on.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two channel-resistances in series**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The temperature-dependent resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The channel conduction loss, which is independent of D because of synchronous rectification, is:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical period each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the time: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static-LS leg.

$$P_{HS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, D\, I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{DS}$$

At low D the **LS FETs are the hot spots**. Place them closest to the thermal pad and copper pour.

**Numbers at 80 A:**

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond (channel) | Worst LS FET (D = 0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W | 4.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 4.8 W |

### 2.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

Only one leg switches at a time.

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_F\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 200\text{n}\cdot 48\text{k} = 1.23\text{ W}$$

This term is larger than the V·I switching loss. Trimming dead time to about 100 ns, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows, saves around 0.6 W per channel.

### 2.5 Gate-Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs. Two FETs switch at f_sw.

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 35\text{n}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.034\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Additional Copper and Shunt Loss

| Element | Assumption | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (shared, 4-in-1) | 0.25 mΩ, carries bus current ≈ D·ΣI | Up to 1.6 W |
| PCB copper per channel | ≈0.3 mΩ path (2 oz, 6 layers paralleled) | ≈1.9 W |

### 2.7 Burst Loss Budget at T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (V·I + Q_oss + Q_rr) | 1.05 W | 4.2 W |
| Dead-time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Gate | 0.03 W | 0.14 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Shunt | — | 1.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈23.4 W** | **≈95 W** |

At ≈80 A × 4 × ~22 V effective (≈7 kW delivered), efficiency is about 98.6%. That looks efficient, but 95 W into a 20×20 mm board is the binding constraint.

**Switching-frequency sensitivity.** The frequency-dependent terms total about 2.3 W per channel at 48 kHz:

$$P_{f}(f) \approx 2.3\text{ W}\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.15 W per channel, at the cost of audible noise and higher current ripple. Conduction loss dominates, so **lowering R_DS(on) beats lowering f_sw**.
