# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #50): Design Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### Architecture
Two points about the architecture determine everything below.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In an ArduPilot stack, the H743 runs the vehicle code and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. So:
   - The loss model (§1–2) belongs to the ESC power stage.
   - The DMA model (§4) belongs to the H743.
2. **80A burst on a nano board is thermally marginal.** The calculation in §3 shows the problem: four channels at 80A dissipate about 100W into a board with about 6 J/K of heat capacity. That gives roughly 2 seconds of all-motor burst. Plan accordingly.

## 1. Conduction Losses at 80A

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.1 \times 1.45 \approx 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This hot-max value is used for all worst-case figures below.

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

During each 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd at duty D and phase B returns current through its low-side FET, which is held on:

| Device | Conduction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH (PWM high) | D | $I^2 R D$ |
| Q_AL (sync rect.) | 1−D | $I^2 R (1-D)$ |
| Q_BL (return) | 1 | $I^2 R$ |

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2I^2R$$

The result is independent of D. Synchronous rectification removes the duty dependency.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\text{m} = 20.5\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Copper losses
Assume 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner layers, with about 0.25 mΩ per current path (input, phase pad, return):

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition time
$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g},\quad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{drv}} \approx 0.5\text{–}0.7\ \text{A} \Rightarrow t_r \approx t_f \approx 25\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss terms (per channel; one PWM leg is active at a time)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | ½·25.2·80·50 ns·48 k = **2.42 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | $V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$ | 0.8·80·600 ns·48 k = **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss | $C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw}$ | 1 n·635·48 k = **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver IC) | $2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$ | 2·40 n·10·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 4.4 W** |

**Key finding:** dead-time diode loss is about 40% of switching loss. Cutting t_dt from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per channel. This is the cheapest efficiency gain available.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals at 80A

$$P_{ch} = 20.5 + 4.4 + 3.2 \approx 28\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{board} = 4 \times 28 \approx \mathbf{112\ W}\ \text{(plus ~1 W for FC, BEC, and ESC MCUs)}$$

Over an electrical cycle, the loss rotates through all 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx (20.5 + 4.4)/6 \approx 4.2\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous loss in a single FET (the return low-side) is about 10 W during its sector.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET → board → air)

```
Tj ──[Rth_jc 1.5]──[Rth_spread ≈ 8 K/W, 2oz + via array]── T_board ──[Rth_ba]── T_amb
                                                               │
                                                          C_board
```

**Board heat capacity** (36×36 mm, 8-layer, ~1.6 mm, heavy copper):
- FR4: ≈ 3.5 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 3.9 J/K
- Copper: ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/gK ≈ 1.7 J/K
- FETs, caps, and solder: ≈ 0.5 J/K
