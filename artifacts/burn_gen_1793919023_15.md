## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A production-viable AIO splits the work as follows:

```
            ┌────────────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──────────────────────┐
 IMU/Baro ──┤ SPI1/SPI4, I2C2      TIM3_CH3/CH4, TIM5_CH1/CH2 (Bidir DShot600)  │
 RX/GPS ────┤ USARTs                         │                                  │
            └────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────┘
                                             │ 4 × single-wire DShot (bidir)
            ┌─────────────┬──────────────────┼───────────────┬──────────────┐
         ESC MCU 1     ESC MCU 2        ESC MCU 3        ESC MCU 4
       (AM32/BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071, 48 kHz PWM)
            │ 6× PWM        │                │                │
         3-ph gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q-class, bootstrap)  × 4
            │
         6× N-MOSFET half-bridges × 4 = 24 FETs
```

**Rationale:**
- A 48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM across 4×3 phases needs 12 complementary channel pairs with dead-time insertion. That would consume TIM1, TIM8 and more, and it conflicts with the DShot/DMA budget.
- Running ArduPilot plus sensorless commutation on one core is not a supported configuration.
- The H743's job here is **DShot600 bidirectional output plus RPM telemetry ingest** for harmonic notch filtering.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Topology-level derivation

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. Within any 60° commutation sector, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg. During the PWM on-time (D), current flows through HS_a and LS_b. During the off-time (1−D), it flows through LS_a (synchronous freewheel) and LS_b. Two channels therefore always conduct the phase current, so the conduction loss is **independent of duty**:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \left[ D\,(R_{HS}+R_{LS}) + (1-D)(R_{LS}+R_{LS}) \right] \approx 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]
$$

Add the copper loop and the shunt:

$$
P_{cond,total} = I^2\left[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu} + R_{shunt}\right]
$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution (for junction-temperature calculation)

Over one electrical revolution, each FET is "active" for 120°:

| FET role | Conducting fraction | Loss expression |
|---|---|---|
| HS (PWM switching side) | (1/3)·D | I²R·D/3 |
| LS (freewheel, same leg) | (1/3)·(1−D) | I²R·(1−D)/3 |
| LS (return leg, static on) | 1/3 | I²R/3 |

The averaged worst-case FET (the LS that sees both the return and freewheel roles) carries:

$$
P_{FET,avg,max} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}(2-D)
$$

**Critical caveat (low eRPM / punch-out from hover).** The electrical period may be longer than the FET thermal time constant τ_JC (≈ 1–5 ms for PQFN 3.3×3.3). For example, at 2,000 eRPM the period is 30 ms. In that regime each FET sees the **full I²R pulse** for 10 ms rather than the averaged value, so you must use Z_θJC(t_p) in place of R_θJC/3. This is handled in §4.4.
