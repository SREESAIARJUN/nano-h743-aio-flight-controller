### 0.1 Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 AIO has:

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot, which outputs DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **4 × ESC MCUs:** AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These run 6-step commutation at the 48 kHz PWM.
- **4 × 3-phase bridges:** 24 MOSFETs total, plus gate drivers.

The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC MCUs. The DMA/timer analysis applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Synchronous Commutation

During each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side (LS) synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the LS is held ON for the entire sector.

Per-ESC conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[\underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{switching leg}} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{static leg}}\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Ripple correction:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

At 48 kHz with typical 5–15 µH phase inductance, ΔI/I_ph is below 10%. The correction is therefore under 0.1% and is neglected below.

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]
$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 0.90 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 85) = 1.28\ \text{mΩ}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W\ per\ ESC}
$$

Copper loss: 2 phases × 80² × 0.30 mΩ = **3.8 W per ESC**.

The loss is not evenly shared. Within one sector, the static-leg LS dissipates 80² × 1.28 m = **8.2 W**, which is the hottest device. Commutation rotates this role, so the time-averaged loss is 16.4 W / 6 FETs ≈ 2.7 W per FET. That averaging only holds if the electrical period is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). This is true when spinning (eRPM ≫ 1000), but **false for a stalled or locked rotor**. A locked rotor puts the full 8.2 W on one die and is the worst case for desync protection.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg incurs these losses.

| Term | Equation | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V_bus I_ph (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½·25.2·80·27 ns·48 k = **1.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw | 40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss | ½ C_oss V_bus² f_sw × 2 FETs | 2 × ½·1.5 n·635·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f I_ph (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive* | Q_g V_drv f_sw × 2 | 2·70 n·10·48 k = **0.07 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.8 W** |

\*Gate-drive power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the MOSFET.

Observations:

- At D → 100% (full throttle) switching stops, so P_sw → 0. **Worst-case total loss occurs at D ≈ 85–95%**, where current is high and switching is still active.
- Doubling f_sw to 96 kHz adds about 1.8 W per ESC. At 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise.
- Dead time is the second-largest term. Tuning AM32 dead time to the minimum safe value (verify shoot-through margin on a scope) is the cheapest efficiency gain.
