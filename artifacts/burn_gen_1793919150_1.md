# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): System Architecture & Derivations

## 0. Architectural Corrections Before Design Freeze

Three constraints shape everything below. Review these before accepting any numbers.

| Issue | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 cannot commutate the ESCs directly in ArduPilot** | ArduPilot runs the flight stack and emits DShot. Running 4× sensorless FOC/trapezoidal at 48 kHz on the same core is not supported. | Use a split architecture: the H743 is the FC and DShot master. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. |
| **"Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot sustain 80 A/motor** | The thermal model in §3 shows about 125 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. A 20×20 board has a heat capacity of about 3 J/K, which saturates in under 2 s. A 20×20 board also cannot fit 24 FETs rated for this current. | The 80 A burst figure requires at least a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board (stacked with a 20×20 or 30.5 FC), 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 DFN FETs, and 2 oz or thicker copper. Rate the board at **80 A for ≤2 s and ~45–50 A continuous with airflow**. |
| **The 48 kHz PWM and DShot600 are independent** | The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's PWM carrier. DShot600 is the FC→ESC command link. | They are modeled separately: §2–3 cover power, §4 covers timers and DMA. |

**Baseline assumptions** (change any of these and re-run the equations):
- Battery: 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max
- FET example: 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 DFN class
  - R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ at 25 °C, 10 V V_GS
  - Q_g = 40 nC; Q_rr = 40 nC; Q_oss = 30 nC
  - R_θJC = 1.2 K/W
- Six FETs per ESC, 24 FETs total.
- Gate driver: FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap), 10–12 V gate drive.
- Complementary (synchronous) PWM, dead time t_dt = 100 ns.

## 2. Loss Model: One ESC at 80 A Burst

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phase legs are active at any instant:
- **Leg A (PWM'd):** the high side (HS) conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low side (LS) for (1−D).
- **Leg B (static LS):** conducts 100% of the sector.

Ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

**Hot R_ds(on).** Use a temperature coefficient of about 0.4 %/K:

$$R_{ds,hot}=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1+0.004(125-25)] = 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Phase-current ripple.** A 48 kHz carrier with phase inductance around 15 µH gives a ripple of roughly 6 A p-p. This adds:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3 \approx 6403\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible.

**Result:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6403 \times 2.1\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

Per-FET worst case (static low side): 6403 × 2.1 mΩ = **13.4 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on one leg per sector.

**(a) V–I overlap.** Assume t_r = t_f = 20 ns, set by the FD6288 drive (about 1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink) and a 10 Ω gate resistor.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode** (conducting during dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction.** Take V_f = 0.8 V and two dead-time intervals per cycle:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2\times40\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** P_sw ≈ 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.04 + 0.61 + 0.04 = **2.68 W**

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz: P_VI ≈ 0.97 W, saving about 1.3 W per ESC.
- Retain 48 kHz only for acoustic/low-inductance motor reasons.

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Losses

**PCB trace.** Assume 2 oz copper (70 µm), a 6 mm wide × 15 mm phase trace, and ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m with ×1.4 hot correction:

$$R = \rho\frac{L}{wt}\times1.4=\frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.006\times70\times10^{-6}}\times1.4 \approx 0.86\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This trace carries current in two phases, so:

$$P_{Cu} = 2\times6400\times0.86\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{11\ W}$$

This is a critical finding. **Copper loss is comparable to FET loss.** Mitigation:
- Use 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, with stitched parallel planes.
- Add solder-pasted bus bars and the shortest possible phase pads.
- Target ≤0.3 mΩ per phase path, giving about 3.8 W.

**Battery shunt.** A 0.1 mΩ shunt (2512 or metal-strip, or 2× 0.2 mΩ in parallel) at a total of 320 A dissipates:

$$P = 320^2 \times 0.1\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{10.2\ W}$$

Burst only. Alternatively, use a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS772-class or TMCS1100 equivalent) to eliminate this loss.

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per ESC | ×4 ESC |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 26.9 W | 107.6 W |
| Switching (all) | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| Copper (optimized, 0.3 mΩ) | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| Shunt (system) | — | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | **~33.4 W** | **~144 W** |

At the input power of 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A ≈ 8.06 kW (at full duty), the conversion efficiency is about **98.2%**.
