**Architecture note.** The H743 does not commutate the motors directly. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Running sensorless FOC/6-step for 4 motors at 48 kHz *plus* ArduPilot on one H743 is possible in principle, but it is not supported by ArduPilot and creates unacceptable scheduling risk.

**Feasibility warning (summarized in §2.6).** 80 A per channel is a **transient** rating only on a nano-format board. The model below shows about 100 W dissipated board-wide at full burst. That gives roughly 2–3 s to thermal limit with no airflow, and only a bounded continuous capability with prop wash.

## 1. Power Stage Topology

Each ESC channel has the following structure:

- **Supply:** V_BAT feeds the bulk capacitors and three half-bridges (A, B, C). Each half-bridge has a high-side FET (HS) and a low-side FET (LS).
- **Drive:** FD6288Q gate driver with bootstrap. HIN/LIN come from the ESC MCU's TIM1 complementary outputs (with dead time).
- **Sensing:** BEMF via resistor dividers to the ESC MCU comparator/ADC. Current via a shared low-side shunt (0.25 mΩ) on the board ground return, read by an INA-class amplifier into the H743 ADC.

**Modulation:** 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant one phase is PWM-ing on the high side, one is held on the low side, and one is floating.

## 2. Thermal / Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}=1.2\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, during the PWM-on interval current flows HS(x) → motor → LS(y). During the off interval it flows LS(x) → motor → LS(y). Phase current I is essentially continuous at 48 kHz because L_motor ≫ the ripple time constant.

**Per-FET current in the active pair:**

- PWM leg: HS conducts with duty D, LS conducts with duty (1−D). The leg total is I²R regardless of D.
- Held-low leg: LS conducts 100 %, giving I²R.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{shunt} + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FETs | 2 × 6400 × 1.92 mΩ | 24.6 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, shared per channel path) | 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Copper (≈0.4 mΩ effective phase + bus path, 2 oz, 8-layer) | 6400 × 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| **P_cond,ESC** | | **≈ 28.8 W** |

**Per-FET average loss.** Each FET is in the conducting set about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (6-step).

$$\bar P_{FET}\approx \frac{24.6}{6}\approx 4.1\ \text{W (burst)}$$

The electrical frequency is 1–3 kHz, which is far faster than the FET thermal time constant (tens of ms), so averaging is valid.

### 2.3 Switching loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. Its HS turns on and off once per PWM period. Its LS commutates softly (ZVS through the body diode) but contributes Q_rr.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r = 14 ns and t_f = 10 ns (FD6288 at ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, R_g = 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times24\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz} = 1.16\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr}= Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

With Q_rr ≈ 45 nC at 80 A: P_rr = 0.054 W. The reverse-recovery current also adds to HS turn-on loss, roughly +Q_rr·V·f again, so about 0.05 W more.

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss}= \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\times 2$$

The factor of 2 accounts for both FETs in the leg. With Q_oss ≈ 40 nC: P_oss = 0.048 W.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt}= V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}$$

With t_dt = 100 ns and V_F = 0.8 V: P_dt = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = 0.61 W.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2$$

With Q_g = 55 nC and V_GS = 10 V: P_g = 0.053 W.

**Switching total per ESC at 80 A:**

$$P_{sw,ESC}\approx 1.16+0.10+0.05+0.61+0.05 \approx 1.97\ \text{W}$$

**Sensitivity:**

- P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, P_sw drops to about 1.0 W.
- Conduction dominates by about 14:1, so 48 kHz is justified for motor current ripple and acoustic reasons at a modest cost.
