## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 What the H743 does and what the ESC MCUs do

On a 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. Each motor bridge, or each pair of bridges, is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU. The usual choices are an AT32F421 running AM32, or an STM32G071/F051 running BLHeli_32/AM32.

The split of responsibilities is:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz | Attitude loop. Emits DShot600 on 4 lines and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (or equivalent) | 6-step or FOC commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead time, current limiting. |
| Gate drive | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) ×4 | Level-shifts the HS gates. Drives at ~V_DRV = 10–12 V. |
| Power stage | 6 × N-MOSFET per motor (24 total) | 3 half-bridges per motor. |

The loss model in Sections 1–4 therefore applies to the **ESC power stage**. Section 5 covers the **H743 timer/DMA side**.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

The MOSFET on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)\right],\qquad k_T \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 125 °C this gives R ≈ 1.6 mΩ.

### 1.2 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry the current I and the third leg floats.

- In the PWM leg, the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of the cycle and the low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1 − D, minus dead time.
- In the return leg, the low-side FET conducts continuously.

So at every moment two R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current. The duty ratio D only redistributes the loss between the HS and LS devices.

**Total per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2\left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times (3.2 + 0.3)\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

Of this, 20.5 W is in silicon and 1.9 W in copper.

**Per-FET average.** Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical period: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the return leg.

- Worst-case HS FET (D → 1):

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} D = 6400 \times 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega \times \tfrac{1}{3} = 3.41\ \text{W}$$

- Worst-case LS FET. It carries the return-leg interval (1/3) plus the sync-rectification share (1/3 × (1 − D)):

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R \left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right] \le 6.83\ \text{W at } D \to 0$$

The realistic burst condition is D ≈ 0.8–1.0, which gives P_LS ≈ 3.4–4.1 W.
