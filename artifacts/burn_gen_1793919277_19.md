## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Flag

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. The 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bi-directional DShot. Example MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.

```
6S LiPo ─┬─ Bulk caps ─┬─ ESC ch1: AT32F421 → 3-ph driver → 6× N-FET → M1
         │             ├─ ESC ch2 ...                               → M2
         │             ├─ ESC ch3 ...                               → M3
         │             └─ ESC ch4 ...                               → M4
         └─ 5V/10V BEC ── STM32H743 FC ── 4× DShot600 (bidir, single wire each)
```

### 0.2 Feasibility warning

The math below shows that **80 A per motor, simultaneously on all four channels, is about 100 W of heat** in the power stage.

- **20×20 "nano" footprint:** this cannot be sustained, and even a 5 s burst exceeds safe junction temperature without a heatsink.
- **Recommended interpretation:** either 80 A per channel as a ≤1–2 s burst on a 30.5×30.5 board with an aluminium heat spreader, or 80 A total-board burst on 20×20.

§3 quantifies both cases so you can pick the specification honestly.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM'd):** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T. The low-side (LS) then conducts in synchronous rectification for (1−D)·T.
- **Phase B (sink):** the LS is held fully on.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two R_DS(on), independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.3 \times 1.4675 = 1.91\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 RMS current including ripple

The ripple during the on-interval is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Worst case is a low-inductance racing motor with L_ph-ph = 15 µH, V_bus − V_bemf = 5 V, and D = 0.9:

- ΔI = 5 × 0.9 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = 6.25 A
- ΔI²/12 = 3.3 A
