# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #36)

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz), board-level transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Three architectural facts shape everything that follows:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Sensorless BLDC commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection needs a dedicated MCU per motor, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and speaks DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. A single-MCU design driving 12 half-bridges is not supported by ArduPilot and is not proposed.
2. **"80 A burst" means 80 A per channel, not 4 × 80 A simultaneously.** 320 A at 25 V is 8 kW. No nano-format connector, battery lead, or input plane survives that. The design therefore:
   - rates **80 A per channel for ≤ 5 s** (single-channel transients such as yaw punch or flips), and
   - caps **total pack current at 160 A burst / 100 A continuous** through the current sensor and `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`.
3. **"Nano" form factor.** I assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting pattern and a 36 × 36 mm outline. A 20 × 20 mm board cannot be honestly rated at 80 A/channel, and the thermal model in §3 shows why.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In any 6-step sector, current flows through exactly **two conduction paths**: the PWM-switched leg (HS during D, sync-rectifying LS during 1−D) and the static low-side return leg. Each path has one R_DS(on) in series at any instant, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)$$
