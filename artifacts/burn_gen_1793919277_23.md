## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points change the design, so they come first.

### 0.1 The H743 is the flight-controller MCU, not the ESC MCU
The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and *generates* DShot. Each of the 4 ESC channels still needs its own commutation controller. The usual choice is 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or one MCU per pair with BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation for 4 motors while also running the EKF. The DShot link is an on-board trace from the FC to the ESC MCUs.

### 0.2 "Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict
On a 20×20 mm board, 24 MOSFETs plus drivers plus the H743 (LQFP100, 14×14 mm) do not fit. The 80 A thermal budget also fails (see §3).

- **Baseline in this document:** 30.5×30.5 mm stack-style AIO, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes.
- **Burst definition:** 80 A per ESC for ≤1.5 s at 6S. Continuous rating is ≈35–40 A per channel with propwash airflow.
- **If 20×20 is mandatory:** realistic ratings are ≈30 A continuous and 40 A burst.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification
In any 60° commutation sector, two phase legs are active.

- One leg is PWM'd. Its high-side FET conducts for D·T. Its low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier.
- The other leg's low-side FET is held on.

So the motor current always passes through exactly **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D. Ignoring ripple:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction
Phase inductance L_ph is assumed to be 8 µH (typical 5" 1800–2000 KV motor). The worst case is D = 0.5.

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} \approx \frac{12.6 \cdot 0.5}{8\mu H \cdot 48 kHz} = 16.4\,A$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 22.4 = 6422\ A^2$$

The ripple adds 0.35%, so it is negligible at 80 A. It matters more at low current.
