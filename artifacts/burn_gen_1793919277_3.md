## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6-step trapezoidal)

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through one high-side FET of leg A and one low-side FET of leg B. One leg is PWM'd (hard-switched). With synchronous rectification, the complementary FET carries current during the off-time, so **two FET channels are always in the current path**.

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Use α ≈ 0.005 /K, which gives a factor of about 1.5 at 125 °C.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Copper/interconnect loss.** Assume 0.5 mΩ total for the bus planes, phase pads and solder joints in the current path:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg, linear V-I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The rise and fall times come from the driver capability and the gate charge:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G}}, \quad I_G=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 14 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 4.5 V and R_g,total = 8 Ω, the gate current is I_G ≈ 0.69 A. That gives t ≈ 20 ns each, and:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 1.3 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48k | 0.06 W |
| Body-diode dead-time conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k | 0.61 W |
| C_oss energy (2 FETs in leg) | 2 · ½ C_oss,eq · V² · f = 1.5 nF · 635 · 48k | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (driver + R_g, not die) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f = 2 · 40 nC · 10 · 48k | 0.04 W |

### 1.4 Totals

| | Per ESC | ×4 (board) |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor (excluding gate) | 21.8 W | 87.2 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

At 80 A × 22 V nominal, this corresponds to an efficiency of about 98.6 %. Conduction loss is 88 % of the semiconductor loss.

**Design leverage: conduction dominates, not switching.**
- Halving R_DS(on), e.g. by paralleling FETs, saves about 10 W per ESC.
- Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (current ripple, acoustic), not for efficiency
