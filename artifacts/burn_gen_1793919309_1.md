## 0. Architecture Clarification

In a standard ArduPilot stack, the **STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** It is the flight controller, and it emits DShot600 frames. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, typically running AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU runs 6-step commutation at 48 kHz.

This document models the system that way:

```
[H743 FC] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU] --48 kHz PWM--> [FD6288Q gate driver] --> [3x half-bridge] --> motor
```

If you intended the H743 to drive the FETs directly (a single-MCU FOC design), the loss model in §1–2 still applies. The DMA plan in §4 would not, because it would be replaced by TIM1/TIM8 complementary PWM with dead-time.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Relationships

In 6-step commutation, the conduction interval is 120° electrical. During each 60° sector:

- One leg switches at duty D, alternating high-side and low-side through synchronous rectification.
- One leg is held on. For example, the low side stays on continuously.

The phase current relates to battery current by:

$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{batt}}{D}$$

At burst conditions (full throttle, D → 1): I_ph ≈ I_batt = 80 A.

**Key result:** exactly **two FETs conduct the phase current at every instant**, independent of D. Total bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

The motor inductance produces a triangular ripple Δi on top of the phase current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}$$

Take L_ph = 15 µH, V = 25.2 V, D = 0.5 (worst ripple case), and f = 48 kHz:

$$\Delta i = \frac{V\,D(1-D)}{L f} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\mu \cdot 48k} = 8.75\text{ A}$$
