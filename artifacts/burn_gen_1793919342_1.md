# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Power, Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

I don't have the Job #58 BOM, stackup, or enclosure data. Every number below comes from a **declared reference design** that you can replace with your actual parts. All equations are parametric, so you can substitute values directly.

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU runs the 48 kHz complementary PWM and commutation using AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The two halves are modeled separately below.

**Feasibility warning.** 80 A per channel on a nano-class (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) 4-in-1 is a **burst-only** rating of roughly 1–3 s, set by board thermal mass. It is not a continuous rating. The math in §3 shows why.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant one phase is PWM'd and another is held low. With complementary PWM at duty D, the device losses are:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of the period, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{hs}(T_j)\,D + I^2 R_{ls,sync}(T_j)(1-D) + I^2 R_{ls,ret}(T_j)$$

With identical FETs this simplifies to:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}, \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Use the RMS phase current. At burst, current ripple is small compared with 80 A, so I_rms ≈ I_dc.

### 1.2 Numbers at I = 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 102.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.8 W | 111.4 W |

The loss per conducting FET at 125 °C is about 14 W.

### 1.3 Additional conduction paths (do not omit)

- **Copper traces and planes.** For 8 layers of 2 oz copper, R_sheet ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer. With 4 layers paralleled and about 3 squares of path, R_cu ≈ 0.19 mΩ per leg. That adds about 2.4 W per motor at 80 A.
- **Shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt carries the summed current. At 4 × 60 A average, P = (240)² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ 14 W.
  - This is a serious problem. Use a 0.1–0.15 mΩ shunt, or a Hall-effect current sensor.
- **Battery pads and wires.** These are outside the board budget but affect V_bus sag.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the actively PWM'd leg switches. The terms are:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} + Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} + Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} + 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} + 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$$

The terms are, in order: V–I overlap, reverse recovery, output capacitance, dead-time body-diode conduction, and gate drive (for the HS + LS pair).

| Term | Expression | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Q_rr | 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Q_oss | 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead time | 2·0.8·80·50 ns·48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive | 2·50 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.048 W (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |
| **Total per motor** | — | **≈ 1.41 W** |
| **4 motors** | — | **≈ 5.6 W** |

**Interpretation.** At 48 kHz, switching losses are only about 5% of conduction losses at burst. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz costs about 1.4 W more per motor. Lowering it barely helps thermally. Choose f_sw for acoustic and ripple reasons, not for burst thermals.

The dead-time term is the largest one you can reduce. Cutting t_dt from 50 ns to 25 ns saves about 0.15 W per motor.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation (T_j ≈ 125 °C, all four motors at 80 A)

$$P_{tot} \approx 4(27.8 + 1.41 + 2.4) + P_{shunt} + P_{reg} \approx 126 + 14 + 1.5 \approx 142\text{ W}$$

### 3.2 Thermal network (Foster/Cauer, per FET)

```
Tj ──[Rjc 1.0 K/W]──┬──[Rspread ~4 K/W local]──┬──[Rboard→air ~10 K/W whole board, still air]── Ta
                 [Cfet ~0.05 J/K]           [Cboard,local]                     [Cboard ~6.7 J/K]
```

Board heat capacity:

- Copper: 36 × 36 mm × 70 µm × 8 layers = 0.726 cm³ → 6.5 g → C_cu = 2.5 J/K
- FR4: 2.07 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 3.8 g → C_FR4 = 4.2 J/K
- **C_board ≈ 6.7 J/K.** Add about 0.1 J/K per FET, and about 1–3 J/K if an aluminum heat-spreader plate is used.
