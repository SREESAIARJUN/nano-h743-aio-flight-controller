**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, which run the commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288Q. This split is the only practical topology, and it is how ArduPilot expects to talk to an ESC.

**Feasibility warning.** As §3 shows, 4 × 80 A on a nano-format board is a burst of about 2 to 4 seconds, not a continuous rating. Any spec sheet claiming more needs a heatsink or much larger copper area.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the T_j = 110 °C design point:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85) = 2.07\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Six-step bridge conduction

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two phase legs. One leg is the PWM leg, where HS conducts for D and the synchronous LS for 1 − D. The other is the static leg, a continuously-on low side. Because synchronous rectification is used, the PWM leg always presents one R_DS regardless of D.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.07\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.5\ W}$$

Per-FET behaviour:
- **Instantaneous loss during its conduction interval:** 80² × 2.07 mΩ = 13.2 W.
- **Average per FET:** 26.5 / 6 ≈ 4.4 W. Each FET carries current about one-third of the electrical cycle. Electrical frequency (≈1–3 kHz) is far above the die thermal corner, so averaging is valid.

### 1.3 Thermal runaway check

Positive feedback through α gives a closed-loop steady state:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\,I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

The loop is stable while the gain term R_th · I² · 2R₂₅ · α stays below 1. At board level, R_th ≈ 3 K/W in prop wash and the loop gain is about 0.26 per motor quadrant. That is stable, but it inflates losses by about 1/(1 − 0.26) ≈ 1.35×. This feedback is why sustained 80 A is not survivable.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (non-negligible)

Assume a phase path of 15 mm, 4 mm wide, using 4 paralleled 2 oz layers:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004 \cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} = 0.23\ \text{mΩ}$$

This gives about 1.5 W per phase at 80 A, or about 3 W per motor, since two phases are active.
