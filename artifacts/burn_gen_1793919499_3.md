The loops are separated by bandwidth: ω_c,i ≈ 2π·2 kHz, ω_c,v ≈ 2π·150 Hz, ω_c,θ ≈ 2π·20 Hz. That gives roughly 10× spacing between each loop and the next, so each inner loop looks like unity gain to the loop around it.

## 2. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 2.1 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

The electrical angle is computed from the mechanical angle, a calibrated offset, and a latency correction:

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{0}) + \omega_e T_d$$

- θ₀ is the electrical zero offset found at calibration.
- T_d ≈ 1.5 T_s compensates for computation plus PWM-update delay.

Clarke transform (phase currents to stationary frame):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform (stationary to rotating frame):

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of the matrix above.

### 2.2 Plant and PI design

For a surface-mount PMSM:

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

**Pole-zero cancellation.** Choose the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole (R/L):

$$K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{c,i},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{c,i}$$

The closed-loop current response is then a first-order lag, i_q/i_q* = ω_c/(s+ω_c).

**Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward:**

$$v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(e_d) - \omega_e L\,i_q,\qquad v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(e_q) + \omega_e L\,i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

### 2.3 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

1. **Circular voltage limit.** The SVPWM linear region allows |v| ≤ V_dc/√3. Give d-axis priority:

$$v_d^{sat} = \text{clip}(v_d^*, \pm V_{max}),\qquad v_q^{sat} = \text{clip}\!\left(v_q^*, \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - (v_d^{sat})^2}\right)$$

2. **Back-calculation anti-windup.** Bleed the integrator by the saturation excess:

$$x_{I}[k+1] = x_I[k] + T_s\left(K_i e[k] + K_{aw}(v^{sat}-v^*)\right),\qquad K_{aw}=1/K_p\cdot K_i$$

### 2.4 SVPWM via min-max zero-sequence injection

$$v_{abc} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha,v_\beta),\quad v_{0} = -\tfrac12(\max v_{abc} + \min v_{abc}),\quad d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}$$

This produces the same duty cycles as sector-based SVPWM at a fraction of the cost.
