# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack: Design Model (Job #45)

## 0. Scope, Architecture Correction, and Assumptions

**Architecture correction.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC. The ESC has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This document models both sides and the interface between them.

**No BOM was specified.** Every number below comes from the reference parameters in this table. Swap in your datasheet values and the equations still hold.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V | Worst-case switching |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A | Requirement |

> ⚠️ **Headline finding:** a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 **cannot sustain 80 A/motor**. It can only survive a time-limited burst. The model below quantifies that limit (§3).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The motor is driven by 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) rectification, as AM32 and BLHeli_32 do.

- At any instant, current flows through one "PWM leg" (high-side or low-side, alternating with duty D) and one "static leg" FET.
- With synchronous rectification, the current always sees exactly two R_DS(on) in series, regardless of duty.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ** (use 1.8 mΩ with margin).

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

With trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_DC-link-equivalent. At burst, take I_rms = 80 A.

- FET term: 6400 × 3.6 mΩ = **23.0 W**
- Copper term, assuming ~0.3 mΩ phase-plus-supply trace share: 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**

$$P_{cond,motor} \approx 24.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts as the static switch for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. During its PWM-leg third, it conducts a fraction D (high side) or 1−D (low side).

The average per FET is about P_FET ≈ 23.0 / 6 ≈ **3.8 W**, symmetric to first order.

### 1.5 Electrothermal runaway criterion

Losses rise with temperature, so check loop gain:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta,total} = I^2\, 2R_{25}\alpha\, R_{\theta} < 1$$

- Per motor: 6400 × 2.4 mΩ × 0.0045 = 0.069 W/K.
- All four motors on a shared board: 0.276 W/K.
- With R_θBA = 8 K/W, gain = **2.2 > 1 → thermal runaway in still air.**
- With prop-wash at 3 K/W, gain = 0.83, which is stable but marginal.

**Conclusion:** sustained 4×80 A has no stable operating point. It is burst-only by physics.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor:

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_d·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.038 W |

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 2.14\ \text{W (FETs)}$$

**Design levers:**
- Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor.
- The overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to 0.73 W, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.

### 2.1 Totals at burst

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu) | 24.9 W | 99.6 W |
| Switching | 2.1 W | 8.6 W |
| Driver, LDO, MCU, cap ESR (≈ I_ripple² ESR) | ~1 W | ~4 W |
| **Total** | **≈28 W** | **≈112 W** |

At the burst operating point, the drive delivers about 80 A × ~22 V × 4 ≈ 7 kW to the motors, so efficiency is ≈98.4%. Even so, 112 W is far more than a 4 cm² board can reject.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (lumped Cauer, per FET → board → ambient)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5]── T_case ──[R_θCB ~2]── T_board(C_th=3 J/K) ──[R_θBA]── T_amb
```

### 3.2 Junction rise above board (steady within the burst)

The thermal time constant of the junction-to-case path is milliseconds. The electrical frequency is about 3.5 kHz (7 pole pairs, 30 krpm), so the per-FET loss is effectively averaged:

$$\Delta T_{JB} = P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB}) = (3.8+0.36)\times 3.5 \approx 15\ \text{K}$$

### 3.3 Board transient (dominant)

$$T_B(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_{th}$$

With prop-wash: τ = 3 × 3 = 9 s, and the asymptote is 112 × 3 = 336 K rise, which is never reached safely.

The early-time slope is dT/dt ≈ P/C = 112/3 ≈ **37 K/s**.

### 3.4 Allowed burst duration

Limits: T_j,max = 150 °C, derated to 135 °C. The junction sits 15 K above the board, so T_B,max = 120 °C. Starting board temperature is 45 °C (hover-warm).

$$t_{burst} = -\tau\ln\!\left(1-\frac{T_{B,max}-T_0}{P R_{\theta BA}}\right) = -9\ln(1-75/336) \approx \mathbf{2.3\ s}$$

| Scenario | Burst limit |
|---|---|
| All 4 motors at 80 A | ≈2 s |
| Single motor at 80 A, others at hover (~10 A) | ≈7–8 s |
| Continuous rating, gain <1 and T_B ≤ 100 °C | ≈35–40 A/motor |

**Recommendation:** label the board "40 A continuous / 80 A ≤2 s burst." Enable ESC temperature protection (AM32 temp limit about 110 °C). Have ArduPilot log ESC temperature via bidirectional DShot EDT.
