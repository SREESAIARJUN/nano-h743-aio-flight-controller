| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |

| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg | |
| Dead time | 100 ns (an aggressive-but-common choice) | Sensitivity shown below |

**Key architectural point:** the STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.
- The H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- Each ESC MCU drives its own 6-FET bridge at 48 kHz.
- This split governs both the netlist and the timer/DMA allocation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 0.9\ \text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055\times85) = 0.9 \times 1.47 \approx \mathbf{1.32\ m\Omega}$$

Use 1.4 mΩ to include package/bond resistance.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, two legs conduct:
- **Static leg:** its low-side FET is fully on for the entire sector and carries I.
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET is on for D·T_sw, and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly two channels, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\left[R_{hot}\cdot D + R_{hot}(1-D)\right] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Dead-time (body-diode) conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2\,t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$$

| t_dt | P_dt per channel |
|---|---|
| 100 ns | 0.8 × 80 × 2(100 ns) × 48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| 300 ns | **1.84 W** |

Dead-time tuning is worth roughly 1.2 W per channel at this operating point.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

Assumed values:
- Phase pad + trace + solder: ≈ 0.3 mΩ
- Shared bus path (battery pads → bulk caps → bridges): ≈ 0.2 mΩ

$$P_{Cu,phase} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W per channel (two phase wires conduct)}$$

With 4× channels at full burst, battery current approaches 4·I_ph·D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is ~290 A, giving:

$$P_{bus} = 290^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 16.8\ \text{W}$$
