## 0. Executive Summary

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each motor gets a dedicated AT32F421 ESC MCU running AM32 at 48 kHz. The H743 cannot run the commutation loops itself (see §1). |
| **Loss per motor @ 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz** | ≈ **20.9 W**. Conduction is 19.2 W of that. |
| **Board loss @ 4 × 80 A** | ≈ **84 W** in silicon, plus ≈ 6–10 W in copper. |
| **80 A burst survivability** | About **1.5–2.5 s**, starting from a 50 °C board with a 110 °C junction limit. The limit is set by board heat capacity. |
| **Continuous rating (honest)** | About **25–30 A per motor** on a 25.5 mm board in propwash. 80 A is a burst-only rating. |
| **Bi-directional DShot600** | All four motors on TIM1 CH1–CH4. Uses 1 TIM1_UP DMA stream plus 2 capture streams (CH1, CH3), shared. |
| **Max practical ArduPilot loop with bidir DShot** | 1–2 kHz is comfortable. 4 kHz is marginal. 8 kHz is not supported. |

**Design stance.** An "80 A" rating on a nano board is a marketing burst number. The mathematics below shows that copper heat capacity, not the MOSFETs, is what limits it. I state this explicitly because it determines the firmware current limits and the product rating.

## 1. System Architecture

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──┬── TVS + 4×470 µF low-ESR bulk (off-board) + 8×10 µF X7R/phase-bank
                        │
          ┌─────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │  POWER BOARD (25.5 × 25.5 mm, 8L, 2 oz inner power)     │
          │  4 × [AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) → FD6288Q → 6 × 40 V PQFN3.3]  │
          │  1 × total-current shunt + amp, VBAT divider            │
          └─────────────▲──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                        │ 4 × DShot600 bidir (single-wire, 3.3 V, open-drain-tolerant)
          ┌─────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┐
          │  FC: STM32H743VIH6 @ 400 MHz (ArduPilot)                │
          │  IMU on SPI1, baro/mag on I2C, OSD SPI, 6–8 UARTs, SDMMC │
          └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Why the H743 does not drive the bridges directly:**
- Four simultaneous sensorless six-step or FOC loops at 48 kHz PWM would need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time. The H743 has only TIM1, TIM8 and HRTIM with true complementary outputs.
- Each loop also needs BEMF zero-cross comparators. The H743 has only 2 COMP units.
- On top of that it would have to run ArduPilot's EKF3.

A per-motor ESC MCU also isolates faults: one desynchronising motor does not stall the autopilot.

**Recommendation.** At 25.5 mm the realistic build is a **two-board stack** (FC on top, ESC below, 8-pin board-to-board connector). A single-board AIO cannot fit 24 FETs, 4 ESC MCUs, 4 drivers and an H743 BGA while keeping the copper area the thermal model needs. The math below assumes the ESC board has its full area to itself.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model

### 3.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any of the six sectors, exactly one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats. With complementary PWM:

- **High-side FET of the PWM leg** conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- **Low-side FET of the same leg** conducts for (1 − D) (synchronous rectification).
- **Low-side FET of the return leg** conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So, at any instant, the motor current flows through exactly **two FETs in series**, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}
$$

### 3.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{2.3}
$$

At T_j
