# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #9): Systems Architecture Package

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Assumptions

**Correction to the brief's implied topology.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 commands. Each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 firmware, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture separates into three layers:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 bidir x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7, AM32)
                                               --> 4x FD6288Q gate driver
                                               --> 4x 3-phase bridge (24 MOSFETs)
```

**Assumed parameters.** Replace these with datasheet values once parts are locked.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I_b | 80 A per motor |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds temperature coefficient (110 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Rise / fall time | t_r / t_f | 20 / 15 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |

| Shunt | R_sh | 0.25 mΩ |

| Board outline | — | 27 × 27 mm (20 × 20 mm mounting) |

**Feasibility warning.** As the following sections show, "80A" on a nano-format board is a **burst rating of a few seconds**, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation model.** The bridge runs six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, the current flows through two conducting MOSFET positions in series: the high-side path of one phase and the low-side path of another. Synchronous rectification means the freewheel path is also a MOSFET, so the number of series devices stays at two.

**Hot on-resistance:**

R_ds,hot = k_T · R_ds,25 = 1.5 · 1.0 mΩ = 1.5 mΩ

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

P_cond = I² · (2R_ds,hot + R_sh + R_cu)

| Term | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 80² × 3.0 mΩ | 19.2 W |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **P_cond (per channel)** | | **24.0 W** |

**Per-FET average.** The MOSFET share (19.2 W) is distributed across 6 FETs over one electrical revolution, giving an average of 3.2 W per FET. The instantaneous peak while a FET conducts is 80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

**Hard-switching transitions** (one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period on the switching leg):

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 35 ns × 48 k = **1.69 W**

**Body-diode reverse recovery:**

P_rr = V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 k = **0.048 W**

**Output capacitance:**

P_oss = ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = **0.030 W**

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

P_dt = V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**

**Gate drive** (complementary pair; this loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k = **0.058 W**

**Total:** P_sw,total ≈ **2.44 W per channel**.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching loss scales roughly linearly with f_sw (dP/df ≈ 50 µW/Hz):
- At 24 kHz, the switching loss is about 1.2 W.
- At 96 kHz, it is about 4.9 W.

Conduction loss dominates at 80 A. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower current ripple and acoustic noise.
