## 0. Feasibility Findings

These items determine whether the design is viable. Read them before the derivations.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors under ArduPilot, because ArduPilot has no ESC commutation firmware. The architecture is therefore an **H743 FC plus four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32). The H743 drives them over bi-directional DShot600. | Sets the overall architecture. |
| F2 | **80 A burst on all four channels simultaneously is thermally infeasible on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board.** The board heats at about 28 K/s (§3.4). | Re-scope the spec to one of: "80 A single-channel burst ≤ 2 s", or a 30.5×30.5 board with a heatsink, or a 50–55 A burst rating. |
| F3 | A 6S supply (25.2 V) requires 40 V-rated MOSFETs at minimum. Layout-induced ringing must be kept below 8 V overshoot. | Drives FET selection. |
| F4 | Bulk-capacitor RMS ripple current reaches about 40 A per ESC at 80 A. On-board MLCCs alone cannot absorb this, so an external low-ESR electrolytic is mandatory. | Affects the BOM and the user manual. |

The rest of this document gives the full model, so the team can re-run it with final datasheet numbers.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, every instant has exactly **two conducting MOSFET channels in series** with the motor current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET (fraction D of the time) or its synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, held fully on for its 120° sector.

Let R_hot be the hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{DS(on),25} = 1.5 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

The total conduction loss for one ESC is then:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 2.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Each FET is active in a sector one-third of the time. Over a full electrical cycle, the per-FET averages are:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}\,D,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}\,\big[(1-D) + 1\big]$$

The low-side FETs run hotter, by a ratio of (2 − D)/D. At D = 0.8:

- P_HS = 3.84 W
- P_LS = 5.76 W
- **The low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot.**

Note on the coupling: R_hot depends on T_j, and T_j depends on R_hot. Iterate until the two converge (§3.5).

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The loss terms are:

**V–I overlap (high-side FET, hard-switched):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$
