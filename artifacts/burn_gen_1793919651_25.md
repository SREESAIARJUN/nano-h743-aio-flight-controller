## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

Add package and PCB parasitics in series. For a DFN3.3 with 2 oz copper, typically 0.2–0.4 mΩ per switch path:

$$R_{path} = R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{pkg} + R_{Cu} \approx 1.92 + 0.3 = 2.22\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Commutation-mode conduction

**Trapezoidal (6-step, 120° conduction, synchronous rectification).** At any instant, two legs carry the phase current I.

- The PWM'd leg alternates HS (fraction D) and LS (fraction 1−D). Because its current is always flowing through one switch, it dissipates I²R regardless of D.
- The return leg's LS is held on, so it also dissipates I²R.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{path}$$

$$P_{cond,ch}(80\text{A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.22\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.4\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts during one third of the electrical cycle on its own interval:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{1}{3}\left[D + (1-D)\right]\frac{I^2R}{1}\cdot\frac{\ldots}{} \;\approx\; \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot\frac{6}{6} \approx 4.7\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is I²R = **14.2 W**. This peak drives die-level transient heating, covered in §3.3.

**Sinusoidal/FOC (for reference).** With peak current Î and all three legs active:

$$P_{cond,ch} = \frac{3}{2}\hat I^2 R_{path}$$

If 80 A is taken as I_rms per phase, then P = 3 I_rms² R = 42.6 W. Trapezoidal AM32 is the realistic case here.

### 1.3 Board copper and shunt losses

These are not negligible at 80 A:

- **Bus plane.** 2 oz copper is 0.25 mΩ/sq at 20 °C. A battery-pad-to-bridge run of about 3 squares with 2 layers paralleled gives ≈ 0.4 mΩ. Shared bus current for 4 × 80 A at D ≈ 0.85 is about 270 A avg, which is **~29 W** in the bus planes. This is why full 4-channel 80 A is physically a seconds-long event.
- **Current shunt** (if used): 0.25 mΩ × (4·I_avg)² is the same order of magnitude. Prefer a 0.1–0.2 mΩ, 2512, 3–5 W part, or Hall-effect sensing.
