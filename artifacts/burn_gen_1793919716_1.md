# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55)
## Design Package Rev A: Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot DMA Allocation, Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Feasibility Summary

The math in Sections 2–4 leads to three conclusions:

1. **80 A per motor × 4 on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable beyond about 1 second.** Total dissipation at that operating point is about 125 W. The board's thermal capacitance is about 1.7 J/K, which gives a heating rate of roughly 75 K/s. Treat "80 A burst" as a ~1 s single-event rating. Realistic continuous capacity is about 12–18 A per motor with airflow.
2. **The STM32H743 does not fit on a 20×20 board in LQFP100.** You must use the **TFBGA100 (8×8 mm)** package, which requires 6+ layers and via-in-pad or HDI.
3. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A standard AIO architecture is used: the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 running AM32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

If the 80 A figure is a hard requirement, Section 4.5 lists the board sizes and FET classes that can support it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx \frac{0.5}{75} = 0.00667\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R_DS = 2.25 mΩ.

### 2.2 Current path

In 6-step commutation one phase is driven high and one phase is driven low. The high-side (HS) FET carries current for a fraction D of the PWM period. During the remaining (1−D) fraction, the synchronous low-side (LS) FET of the same leg carries it. The return-leg LS FET carries current for the full period. The effective conduction is therefore always **two FETs in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

If the phase current has ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak), replace I² with $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$. At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~20 µH) the ripple term contributes less than 1 %, so it is neglected here.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per ESC | P_cond × 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** | **115.2 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.50 mΩ | 32.0 W | 128.0 W |

### 2.4 Electrothermal feedback

R_DS rises with temperature, and temperature rises with loss, so the conduction loss is a coupled fixed point:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big]$$

$$\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches zero, i.e. when $R_\theta \ge 1/(2I^2R_{25}\alpha)$. At 80 A that threshold is **R_θ ≥ 7.8 K/W**.

The board's steady-state R_θ is about 12 K/W (Section 4.3). **There is therefore no steady-state operating point at 80 A.** The burst can only be bounded by time, not by temperature equilibrium.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per ESC, only one leg is hard-switching at any instant (the HS FET of the PWM'd phase). Its complementary LS FET soft-switches through its body diode.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.2\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and current. Allow ×2 margin, giving about 0.1 W.

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_{g} = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{(HS+LS)} = 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W per ESC}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal per ESC

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr (with margin) | 0.10 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W |
| **Subtotal** | **≈ 2.67 W** |

### 3.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 55.6\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Each step of 24 kHz therefore adds or removes about 1.3 W per ESC at 80 A. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per ESC, which is negligible against 28.8 W of conduction loss. **At 80 A the design is conduction-dominated.** f_sw is a secondary lever.
