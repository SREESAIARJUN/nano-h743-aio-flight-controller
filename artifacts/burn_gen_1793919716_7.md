## 0. Assumptions and Red Flags

No MOSFET part number, layer stackup, or mounting pattern was specified, so the numbers below rest on stated assumptions. Every equation is parametric. Substitute your datasheet values and the model still holds.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge) | Worst case for switching loss |
| Burst current | 80 A **per channel** (320 A total) | Typical "80A" ESC marketing convention |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | Class-typical for 80 A parts |
| R_ds tempco | α ≈ 0.0045 /K | Typical for trench MOSFETs |
| Switching times | t_r = t_f ≈ 15 ns | Driver at ~1 A, Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 11 nC |
| Q_rr, dead time | Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, t_dt = 100 ns | Typical values |
| Thermal resistance | R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W (DFN5×6) | Typical package value |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, synchronous complementary | AM32-class ESC firmware |
| Architecture | H743 runs ArduPilot; 4× ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421/STM32G071 running AM32) drive the bridges | H743 does **not** commutate motors directly |

**Physical feasibility warnings. Resolve these before layout:**

1. **The FETs do not fit on a 20×20 board.** 24 FETs × 30 mm² (DFN5×6) = 720 mm² of footprint, versus 400 mm² per side on a 20×20 board.
   - "Nano" at 80 A requires either a 30.5×30.5 mounting pattern or DFN3.3×3.3 parts.
   - DFN3.3×3.3 parts are ≈1.8–2.5 mΩ, which roughly doubles conduction loss.
2. **The burst is seconds, not tens of seconds.** At 80 A on all four channels, the board heats at ≈20+ K/s (§3). The realistic rating is "80 A, ~2–3 s".
3. **A single total-current shunt is impractical.** At 320 A, a 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates 25.6 W. Use per-channel phase sensing or a Hall sensor.
