# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80 A ESC: System Design Package (Job #60)

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (conduction and switching at 48 kHz), transient thermal model for an 80 A burst, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. MOSFET parameters are representative 40 V / ~1 mΩ PDFN5×6 values. Substitute your chosen part's datasheet values before release. The equations are written so you can do that directly.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Two points should be settled before detailed design. Otherwise the requirements are internally inconsistent.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and *emits* DShot. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s): 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The "bi-directional DShot" requirement presupposes this split. The H743 is the flight controller; the ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM.

2. **80 A per channel does not fit a 20×20 mm "nano" board.**
   - The math in §3 gives about 26 W per channel at 80 A and Tj = 125 °C, or about 105 W total.
   - A 20×20 board has roughly 2 J/K of thermal mass. That means an adiabatic rise of about 50 K/s.
   - **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm (M3) form factor, with 6-layer, 2 oz outer and inner copper. Define 80 A as a ≤3–5 s burst rating with about 45–50 A continuous.

   The rest of this document assumes the 30.5 mm board.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths

In any six-step sector, one phase's high-side switch is PWM'd and a second phase's low-side switch is held on.

- **During on-time D:** current flows through HS(a) → motor → LS(b).
- **During off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(a), which is the synchronous rectifier, and LS(b).

The phase current is approximately flat at I_ph, because motor inductance is much greater than what is needed at 48 kHz.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,\text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{ds}(125^\circ\text{C}) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\Big[\underbrace{D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS,a}}_{\text{PWM'd phase}} + \underbrace{R_{LS,b}}_{\text{held phase}}\Big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$

With matched FETs (R_HS = R_LS = R), the D-dependence cancels:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 (2R + R_{cu})$$

The dead-time diode term is handled separately in §3. Evaluating at 80 A:

| Condition | 2R | P_FET | P_cu | P_cond |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tj = 25 °C (start of burst) | 2.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.2 W | **16.0 W** |
| Tj = 125 °C (end of burst) | 3.2 mΩ | 20.5 W | 3.2 W | **23.7 W** |

### 2.4 Per-device distribution (worst device)

The held low-side, LS(b), carries I² R for the full 1/3 sector. Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time as a "held" or "PWM" device. The *instantaneous* sector loss, however, is what drives Tj, because the electrical period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant at low eRPM:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R = 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 10.2\ \text{W}$$

At high eRPM, where the electrical period is much shorter than τ_die (about 1–5 ms), the loss averages:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,FET}}{6}\cdot 2 \approx 6.8\ \text{W}$$

**Design guidance:** use 10.2 W per device for the stall or low-speed burst case, which is the worst case for desync and punch-out from a hover.
