**Loop rates (one axis; the other axes are identical):**

```
                20 kHz ISR (PWM-synchronous)
 ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ ADC ia,ib → Clarke → Park → PI(id,iq) → invPark → SVPWM         │
 └──────────────────────────────▲─────────────────────────────────┘
                                │ iq_ref = iq_ctrl + iq_cog(θm)
                 4 kHz          │
 ┌──────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Rate loop: ω_ref − ω_fb (fused gyro, filtered) → PI → iq_ctrl  │
 └──────────────────────────────▲─────────────────────────────────┘
                 1 kHz          │ ω_ref
 ┌──────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Angle loop: q_ref ⊗ q̂⁻¹ → axis error → P + FF → ω_ref           │
 │ Attitude estimator (Mahony) on fused IMU                       │
 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
 8 kHz IMU pipeline: 2× ICM-42688-P → rotate → lever-arm comp →
   adaptive notches → health/weighting → fusion → LPF
```

The **angle and rate loops close on the camera IMU**, not on the encoder. That is what makes a gimbal stabilize in inertial space. The encoder is used for commutation, for indexing the cogging map, and as a fallback.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Transforms
Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

Park transform, with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 Plant
The surface-PMSM voltage equations in the rotor frame are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

where λ_m = (2/3)·K_t/p for amplitude-invariant scaling (K_t = 1.5·p·λ_m).

### 1.3 PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

After decoupling, each axis is the first-order plant G(s) = 1/(Ls + R). Pole-zero cancellation with a target bandwidth ω_ci gives:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{i}{i^*}=\frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}
$$

Rule of thumb: ω_ci ≤ 2π·f_pwm/20. At 20 kHz PWM, f_ci ≈ 1 kHz, so ω_ci = 6283 rad/s, K_p = 13.2 V/A, and K_i = 35 186 V/(A·s).

**Gimbal note:** high-resistance gimbal motors are often run in voltage mode (v_q = R·i_q* + ω_eλ_m) when current sensing is poor. The code supports both modes.

### 1.4 Voltage limiting and anti-windup
Limit the voltage vector to the linear SVPWM hexagon's inscribed circle:

$$
|v| \le V_{max}=\frac{V_{dc}}{\sqrt 3}
$$

Prioritize d (keep i_d = 0), then saturate q with the remaining headroom:

$$
v_{q,max}=\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}
$$

Use back-calculation anti-windup:

$$
\dot x_I = K_i e + K_{aw}(v_{sat}-v_{unsat}),\qquad K_{aw}\approx \frac{K_i}{K_p}
$$

### 1.5 SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_a,v_b,v_c = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha,v_\beta),\quad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})\big)
$$

$$
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM and has no branches.

### 1.6 Angle latency compensation
The voltage computed at sample k is applied over [k+1, k+2], so on average it is 1.5·T_s late. Advance the angle used in the inverse Park transform accordingly:

$$
\theta_{e,inv} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s
$$

## 2. Cascaded Rate and Angle Control

### 2.1 Mechanical plant (one axis)

$$
J\dot\omega = K_t i_q - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - B\omega - \tau_{fric}\,\text{sgn}(\omega) - \tau_{d}
$$

Here τ_d is the disturbance: cable stiffness, base motion coupling, and imbalance m·g·r·sin(·). With the current loop much faster than the rate loop, the plant seen by the rate loop is:

$$
G_\omega(s)=\frac{K_t}{Js}\cdot\frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}\cdot e^{-sT_d}\cdot H_{filt}(s)
$$
