# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no record of "Job #31," so I've stated every assumption below. If your BOM differs, replace the parameters in the tables. The equations are written to be reused.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. On every production 4-in-1 stack (BLHeli_32, AM32, Bluejay), each motor channel has its own ESC MCU. The FC's job ends at the DShot600 line. So the 48 kHz switching analysis belongs to the ESC board, and the DMA analysis belongs to the FC.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.8 mΩ × 1.55 = **2.79 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in six-step synchronous commutation

During each 60° sector, phase A carries current in through the high side and phase B returns it through the low side.

- **PWM leg (A):** the high side conducts for D·T_sw. The complementary low side conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time. Current flows continuously through one FET of the pair.
- **Return leg (B):** the low side stays on for the whole sector.

So two FETs carry I_phase at all times:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D under synchronous rectification)}$$

Per-FET RMS currents (each high side or low side is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle):
- High side: $I_{HS,rms}=I\sqrt{D/3}$
- PWM-leg low side: $I\sqrt{(1-D)/3}$
- Return low side: adds $I\sqrt{1/3}$ in its own sector. At D → 1 this collapses to the classic $I/\sqrt{3}$ per FET.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.79\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{35.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The worst single FET is the high side at D ≈ 1, carrying 80 A for its 120° sector:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 2.79\text{m} \times \tfrac{1}{3} = 5.95\text{ W average},\quad 17.9\text{ W instantaneous in-sector}$$

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction (often forgotten)

Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) trace, 4 mm wide, 15 mm long, with 2 layers paralleled:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times2} = 0.46\text{ m}\Omega$$

Phase out and back gives ~0.9 mΩ, so P_Cu = 80² × 0.9 m ≈ **5.9 W per motor** (rising ~1.4× when hot).

On nano boards the copper is a significant fraction of FET loss. Use 8-layer, 2 oz inner layers, and via-stitched pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition time

Only one leg switches (the PWM leg). The high side hard-switches the inductive load. Gate current at the Miller plateau (V_pl ≈ 3.5 V):

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.5}{2.2+1+2} \approx 1.25\text{ A}$$

$$t_{r}\approx\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g}=\frac{6\text{ nC}}{1.25\text{ A}}=4.8\text{ ns (ideal)}$$

Layout inductance and ringing typically make it **≈ 15 ns** in practice. I use t_r = t_f = 15 ns.

### 2.2 Loss terms per motor

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | $C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw}$ (both FETs of leg) | 600 p × 635 × 48 k = **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode | $V_f I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw}$ | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | $2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$ | 2 × 20 n × 10 × 48 k = **0.019 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.13 W** |

### 2.3 Sensitivity

- P_sw ∝ f_sw. Going to 24 kHz saves ~1 W per motor at 80 A but adds current ripple and audible noise.
- Ripple check: with L_phase ≈ 15 µH (typical 2207 motor, line-line ~30 µH):

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL} f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\times0.25}{30\mu\times48k}\approx 4.4\text{ A}$$

  This is small relative to 80 A, so the I_rms ≈ I_dc assumption holds.
