# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Several points in the brief conflict with physics or with standard ESC architecture. Each needs a decision before layout.

| # | Issue | Consequence | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **80 A per channel on a 20×20 "nano" footprint** | 24 power FETs plus driver, MCU, and bulk capacitance do not fit in 400 mm². Phase copper cannot carry 80 A without heavy copper or bus bars. | Use a **30.5×30.5 ESC** (or 36×36) stacked under a 20×20 or 30×30 H743 FC. Alternatively, derate the nano to about 35–40 A burst. |
| F2 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors** | In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel (or channel pair) runs its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8) on AM32 or BLHeli firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. | Use a two-board architecture (FC plus ESC). The H743 only generates DShot and captures eRPM. |
| F3 | **4 × 80 A = 320 A pack current** | This exceeds XT60 (~60 A continuous / ~180 A short burst) and XT90 ratings. Pack sag will be severe. | Specify "80 A burst" as **≤ 2 s per channel, not simultaneous across all four**. Size the total system for about 160–200 A peak. |
| F4 | **48 kHz PWM** | This is valid in AM32 (configurable 24–96 kHz), but switching loss scales linearly with frequency (see §2). | Keep 48 kHz for low-inductance motors. 24 kHz saves about 1 W/channel at burst. |

The rest of this document models the **30.5×30.5 ESC at 6S (25.2 V max)** with an H743 FC on top. Where the nano footprint changes a result, I give the derated number.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current path
During each 60° commutation sector, current flows through:
- **Phase X high-side (HS):** conducting for duty D
- **Phase X low-side (LS):** synchronous rectification for (1 − D), with active freewheeling (AM32 "complementary PWM" on)
- **Phase Y low-side:** held on for the whole sector (the return path)

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

This loss is **independent of duty** when synchronous rectification is used. Without it, the (1 − D) term becomes body-diode loss $V_F I (1-D)$, which is far worse.

### 1.2 Temperature-adjusted R<sub>DS(on)</sub>
$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ /\text{K}$$

Reference FET: 40 V, PDFN 5×6, R<sub>DS(on),25</sub> ≈ 0.7 mΩ @ V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V (BSC007N04LS6-class; confirm against the datasheet curve).
At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R ≈ 0.7 × 1.5 = **1.05 mΩ**.

Add PCB and solder path resistance (phase pad, via fields, bus) of about **0.3 mΩ per conducting leg** on a 4-layer, 2 oz board:

$$R_{path} = 1.05 + 0.3 = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Results per channel

| I<sub>phase</sub> | P<sub>FET</sub> (2·I²·1.05 mΩ) | P<sub>copper</sub> (2·I²·0.3 mΩ) | **P<sub>cond</sub> total** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 A (cruise) | 1.89 W | 0.54 W | **2.43 W** |
| 50 A | 5.25 W | 1.50 W | **6.75 W** |
| **80 A (burst)** | **13.44 W** | **3.84 W** | **17.28 W** |

The I² scaling is brutal: 80 A dissipates 7× the loss of 30 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

With V<sub>bus</sub> = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and f = 48 kHz, there is one hard-switched HS/LS pair per PWM period in the active phase.

### 2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover) loss
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
FD6288-class driver (~1.0 A source / ~1.5 A sink) with 10 Ω gate resistors and Q<sub>g</sub> ≈ 60 nC gives t<sub>r</sub> ≈ 18 ns and t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 12 ns:
$$P_{ov} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
This assumes 50 ns dead time per edge (AM32 typical register value; tune it to measured t<sub>f</sub> plus margin).

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 60\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$
(Q<sub>oss</sub> is charge-equivalent at 25 V.)

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)
$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.6 Summary per channel @ 80 A, 48 kHz

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> | 0.06 |
| Q<sub>oss</sub> | 0.07 |
| **P<sub>sw</sub>** | **1.89** |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 |

At 24 kHz, P<sub>sw</sub> ≈ 0.95 W. At 96 kHz, it rises to ≈ 3.8 W.

### 2.7 Total loss budget

| Condition | Per channel | 4 channels | + Logic/LDO/caps ESR | **Board total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 A cruise | 2.43 + 0.71 = 3.14 W | 12.6 W | ~1.5 W | **~14 W** |
| **80 A burst** | 17.28 + 1.89 = **19.2 W** | **76.7 W** | ~3 W | **~80 W** |

The bulk capacitor ripple contribution is included in the "logic/caps" term. With 4 × 470 µF low-ESR (~25 mΩ each, paralleled to ~6 mΩ), ripple current is about 0.5·I<sub>phase</sub> ≈ 40 A RMS per active channel. That adds about 1 W/channel at burst, so **cap self-heating is a real failure mode**. Spread the capacitors and add MLCC bulk.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state network (per FET, worst case: the always-on LS)
The always-on LS FET carries I² × R<sub>DS</sub> = 80² × 1.05 mΩ = **6.7 W** for one third of the electrical cycle. HS and LS share the other two-thirds. Averaged over commutation sectors, each of the six FETs sees about P<sub>cond,FET</sub> + P<sub>sw,FET</sub> ≈ (13.44 + 1.89)/6 × 2 ≈ **3.2 W** average at an 80 A burst, with peaks up to 6.7 W within a sector. The sector period is ~0.1–1 ms, far shorter than the FET thermal time
