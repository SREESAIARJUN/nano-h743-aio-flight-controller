# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Constraints

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max | 40 V FETs required (~1.6× margin for ringing) |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel | Burst only. Section 2.4 shows this is time-limited. |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32 default range |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm ("nano") or 25.5×25.5 mm | Sets the thermal mass |
| Firmware | ArduPilot (FC) and AM32 (ESC MCUs) | |

**Architecture correction.** The H743 should not commutate the motors. It needs 24 complementary PWM outputs plus 12 BEMF comparators, and it already carries the ArduPilot scheduler. The standard and correct partition is:

- **H743**: flight control, sending bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6**: running AM32, one per motor, each driving an FD6288Q gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

DShot is only meaningful with this split. The rest of this document assumes it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct during each 60° sector:

- **PWM phase**: the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D, and its complementary low-side (LS) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Return phase**: the LS conducts 100% of the sector.

At every instant, exactly two FET channels carry the phase current I. Dead time is handled separately.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Per FET, averaged over a full electrical cycle (each FET is active for 1/3 of the time):

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I^2 R(T_j)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}$$

The LS loss is weighted because the LS serves as the return path in alternating sectors. Summed over the six FETs, the total reduces exactly to 2I²R.

### 2.3 Temperature-coupled solution

R depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Solve the fixed point:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot 2I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}R_\theta\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway condition.** The denominator must stay positive: 2I²R₂₅R_θα < 1. With I = 80 A, R₂₅ = 1.5 mΩ, and α = 0.0045, this requires **R_θ < 11.6 K/W for that channel's heat path**. Steady-state 80 A is therefore only stable with heavy forced airflow. The design treats 80 A as a transient condition.

### 2.4 Numerical evaluation at 80 A

| Tj | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | All 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | **25.7 W** | 102.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** | 111.6 W |

The instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET at 125 °C is 6400 × 2.18 mΩ ≈ **14 W**.

Add copper trace and pad resistance. A 2 oz, 3 mm wide, 8 mm long phase trace is about 0.25 mΩ per layer; with 4 paralleled layers that is ~0.06 mΩ. This adds 2·6400·0.06 mΩ ≈ 0.8 W per channel to the board heat budget, not to the junction.

### 2.5 Adiabatic burst-duration limit (board thermal mass)

Thermal capacitance of a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 1.6 mm board with 6 effective 2 oz copper planes:

| Item | Volume | Mass | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | 4 cm² × 70 µm × 6 = 0.168 cm³ | 1.51 g | 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4 | 0.64 cm³ | 1.18 g | 1.30 J/K |
| 24 FETs + components | — | ~0.8 g | 0.35 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 2.2 J/K** |

Total burst dissipation at 80 A on all four channels is P ≈ 4 × (27.9 + 2.2 switching, see §3) ≈ **120 W**. The resulting temperature slope is:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{120}{2.2} \approx 55\ \text{K/s}$$

Starting from 45 °C and limiting the board to 110 °C (Tj ≈ 125 °C, given the R_θJC offset of 14 W × 1.2 K/W ≈ 17 K), the allowed burst is:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{110 - 45}{55} \approx \mathbf{1.2\ s}\ \text{(4 motors simultaneously)}$$

On a 25.5×25.5 mm board, C_th ≈ 3.6 J/K, which gives ≈ 2 s.

**Spec statement:** "80 A burst ≤ 1 s (20×20) / ≤ 2 s (25.5×25.5), 40 A continuous with airflow." Any rating above this is marketing, not engineering.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase hard-switches. The HS turns on hard; the LS commutates softly through its body diode.

### 3.1 Loss components, per channel (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz)

**(a) V-I overlap (HS).** Transition times from the gate driver (~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink, R_g = 4.7 Ω): t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 100 ns, twice per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 35\text{ nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 FETs switching, dissipated in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(30\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$
