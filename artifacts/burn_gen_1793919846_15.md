## 0. Architecture Note and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs. The model below follows that partitioning.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase A's high-side switch (HS_A) is PWM'd and phase B's low-side switch (LS_B) is held on:

- **On-time (duty D):** I flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- **Off-time (1 − D):** I freewheels through LS_A (sync-rect) and LS_B.

Two R_DS(on) are always in the current path, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 (80)^2 (1.6\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Duty-dependent split between the two FETs of a commutating leg:

$$P_{HS} = D\,I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS,A} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

LS_B carries the full current for the whole sector, so it is the hottest device.

### 1.3 Per-FET average over one electrical revolution

Each phase is driven (high or low) for 240° of every 360°. Averaged over a revolution, each of the six FETs dissipates:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 3.4\ \mathrm{W}$$

However, the **instantaneous** peak during a sector is I²R_DS = **10.2 W** in LS_B. The FET's die thermal time constant (roughly 1–10 ms) is comparable to a sector at low eRPM, so thermal analysis must use the peak value at low speed and high current. The worst case is stall or startup.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

**Trace and plane loss:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \frac{\rho L}{W t}$$

With 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm), ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, a 10 mm × 5 mm path gives 0.49 mΩ, so P_Cu ≈ 3.1 W per channel at 80 A.

- Use parallel planes on L1, L6 and inner layers.
- Stitch with vias at roughly 10 A per 0.3 mm via (plated).

**Shunt loss:**

$$P_{shunt} = I_{bus}^2 R_{sh}$$

A common 0.2 mΩ shunt at **4 × 80 A = 320 A** dissipates 20.5 W. This is not survivable on a nano board. In practice the "80 A burst" rating is per-channel and non-simultaneous; realistic total bus burst is about 120–160 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The switched leg is HS_A versus LS_A at the bus voltage.

| Term | Equation | Value (per channel, 80 A, 25.2 V) |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.023 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2·40 nC·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Body diode during dead time | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·60 nC·10 V·48 k = **0.058 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

### 2.1 Edge-time estimate

The Miller plateau dominates the edge time:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 4 V and R_g,total = 4 Ω:

- I_g = 1.5 A
- t ≈ 6.7 ns (ideal)

Use 15 ns to account for driver source/sink limits.
