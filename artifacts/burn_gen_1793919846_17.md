## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

Three assumptions shape everything that follows.

**Assumption 1: Split-MCU topology.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU: 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32 firmware. The 48 kHz PWM, commutation and gate drive all live on the ESC side. The H743 and ESCs communicate over bi-directional DShot600. A single H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops while also running the ArduPilot EKF3 at acceptable latency.

**Assumption 2: Form factor.** A true "nano" (20×20 mm, M2) board cannot place 24 power MOSFETs that survive 80 A per channel. The analysis therefore uses a 30.5×30.5 mm board (M3 or M2 grommet holes) and quantifies how long the 80 A burst can last. A 20×20 layout is noted as a degraded case.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At the design junction temperature T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 2.20\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-Step Trapezoidal Drive with Synchronous Rectification

AM32 uses complementary PWM, so for each commutation sector:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously for the full 120° sector.

Exactly two channels carry the full phase current I at every instant, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With a flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.20\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} \cdot (1 + \text{split factor}) \approx \frac{2 \cdot I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = 4.7\ \text{W average}$$

Instantaneous dissipation during the active sector is 14.1 W.

The electrical frequency at 100,000 eRPM is 1.67 kHz, so each sector lasts about 200 µs. That is comparable to the die thermal time constant (≈100–500 µs). Junction ripple of roughly +5–8 K above the averaged value is therefore expected.
