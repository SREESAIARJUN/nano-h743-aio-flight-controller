**Architectural note.** In an integrated "FC + 4-in-1" design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each ESC channel, or a 4-in-1 group, has its **own ESC MCU** (for example, an AT32F421 running AM32) that runs the 48 kHz commutation PWM. The H743 does not drive the gates directly. The design is partitioned this way below.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** Four channels at 80 A sustained is not thermally possible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. Section 2.4 shows the board heats at roughly **40 K/s** at full four-motor burst. On a 20×20 board, treat 80 A as a **~1–2 s peak rating**. A 30.5×30.5 board extends this to about 4 s. A genuine 10 s, 4×80 A rating requires a heatsink or forced-air coupling. This finding should drive the spec negotiation.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs carry the DC-link current at any instant:
- During the PWM on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: the current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Either way, **two FET channels are in series** with the phase current I_ph ≈ I_ESC (at high throttle, duty approaches 1).

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.50 mΩ**.

Conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \left(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 (2 \times 1.50 + 0.5)\,\text{mΩ} = 6400 \times 3.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

This splits into 19.2 W in the FETs and 3.2 W in copper and the shunt.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS, which raises loss again. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, R_{\theta,j\text{-}b}$$

The loop converges when the loop gain is less than 1:

$$\alpha\, P_{FET}\, R_{\theta} = 0.0055 \times 11 \times 1.5 \approx 0.09 \ll 1$$

So there is no thermal runaway at the device level. Runaway, if it occurs, is a board-level heat-capacity problem (Section 2).

### 1.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz (hard-switched leg)

**Overlap (V–I crossover) loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the complementary FET):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two complementary switching FETs; this loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | W / channel | Lands in |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (2 × R_DS) | 19.2 | Two FETs, about 9.6 W each |
| Copper + shunt | 3.2 | PCB |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 | PWM-ing high-side FET |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | Low-side FET |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 | Distributed |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 103 W** | |

Efficiency at 80 A × 25.2 V = 2016 W per channel is about **98.7%**. Electrically this is excellent. The problem is purely heat density.

**Design lever: 48 kHz vs 24 kHz.** Halving f_sw saves about 1.6 W per channel (overlap plus dead-time terms). That is only about 6% of the burst loss, because conduction dominates. At burst current, 48 kHz is therefore justified for its smoothness and lower motor ripple loss. Conduction, not switching, is the term to attack, through R_DS selection or paralleled FETs.

### 1.4 Hottest Device

The hottest device is the high-side FET of the PWM-ing phase:

$$P_{HS} = 9.6 + 1.94 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{11.6\ W}$$

The low-side FET dissipates about 9.6 + 1.23 ≈ 10.8 W.
