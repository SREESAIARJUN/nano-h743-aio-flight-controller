# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #60): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

This is an engineering baseline to start design reviews, not a fab-ready release. I derived the numbers from stated assumptions; replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts. Before the math, there are two problems you should know about.

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Issues

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four separate ESC MCUs, which run AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. Letting ArduPilot also run four 6-step or FOC loops is not a supported architecture. The model below assumes:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [4x 3-phase gate driver] --> [24x N-MOSFET]
```

**2. Four motors at 80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The math in Section 3 shows roughly 1 s of adiabatic headroom at 4×80 A, and only about 20–25 A per motor continuous. Market the board as "80 A burst (≤1 s), 25 A continuous", or move to a 30×30 mm form factor.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phases:

- The high-side FET in the PWM leg conducts for duty D.
- The complementary low-side FET in the same leg conducts for (1−D), because of synchronous rectification.
- The low-side FET in the return leg conducts 100% of the time.

The PWM leg therefore always carries current through exactly one FET, so the result is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ** (typical) or 1.89 mΩ (max).

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}\ \text{(typ)},\quad 24.2\ \text{W (max)}$$

$$P_{pcb} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·35 ns·48 k | **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.14 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.5 W** |

Design levers:
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.85 W, at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple.
- Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 2.3 Totals at the 80 A burst point (T_j = 125 °C, typical)

| | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.5 W | 10.0 W |
| **Total** | **24.3 W** | **≈ 97 W** |

Electro-thermal coupling is a fixed point, because loss depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss. Iterate until it converges:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + R_{th,jb}\cdot\left[\tfrac{2I^2}{6} R_{ds25}\big(1+\alpha(T_j^{(k)}-25)\big) + \tfrac{P_{sw}}{2}\right]$$

The 1/6 factor gives the average share per FET, since each FET conducts about 1/3 of an electrical cycle. The worst-case switching FET takes the P_sw/2 term.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET junction rise

- Average FET dissipation is about (18.6/6) + (2.5/2) ≈ **4.3 W** (hottest FET).
- R_th,jb (DFN3.3 junction to board, with a via array) is about 3 K/W.
- **ΔT_jb ≈ 13 K.** This is short-term and quasi-static, since the FET thermal time constant is around 10 ms.

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz)

| Material | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 (0.64 cm³) | 1.18 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| Copper (6 layers × 70 µm × 4 cm²) | 1.50 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.58 J/K |
| 24 FETs, caps, ICs, solder | ~1.0 g | ~0.6 | 0.60 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | | **≈ 2.5 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst duration (adiabatic, which is valid for times under about 5 s)

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{97}{2.5} \approx 39\ \text{K/s}$$

Allowed board rise: T_j,max 125 °C − 13 K (ΔT_jb) − 10 K margin = 102 °C board. From a 40 °C start, that leaves 62 K.

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{62}{39} \approx \mathbf{1.6\ s}\ \text{(typ)},\quad \approx 1.2\ \text{s (max } R_{ds})$$

### 3.4 Continuous rating (convective)

- Prop wash gives h ≈ 100 W/m²·K over 8 cm² (two sides), so G_conv ≈ 0.08 W/K.
- Conduction into the battery leads and motor wires adds about 0.12 W/K. This is highly build-dependent; measure it.
- With G ≈ 0.2 W/K and a 62 K rise, P_cont ≈ 12–15 W total, or about 3.5 W per motor.
- Solving 2I²R_ds(100 °C) + I²R_pcb + P_sw(I) = 3.5 W gives **I_cont ≈ 22–25 A per motor**.

### 3.5 Firmware protection (required)

- Enable the AM32 temperature limit at 110 °C, and current limiting via the shunt.
- In ArduPilot, monitor ESC temperature through the telemetry carried in EDT (Extended DShot Telemetry), and set `SERVO_DSHOT_ESC` and `SERVO_BLH_*` accordingly.
