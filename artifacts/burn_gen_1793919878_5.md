## 0. Architectural Corrections (Read First)

Two items in the brief need correcting before the math is useful.

1. **The H743 does not run the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller and sends bi‑directional DShot600 to the ESC. Motor commutation runs on four separate ESC MCUs using AM32 firmware, one MCU per motor. The H743 cannot run ArduPilot plus four sensorless field‑oriented or six‑step loops at 48 kHz.
   - Recommended ESC MCU: AT32F421 or STM32G071.
   - Topology: stacked or single‑board "AIO" design, with the FC section and power section on separate copper zones.

2. **"Nano" and "80A burst" are in tension.** A 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board cannot sink 4×80A for long. Section 3 quantifies this.
   - The defensible rating is **80A per channel for a burst of seconds**, not 320A total sustained.
   - The marketing spec must state the burst duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In six‑step operation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct in every 60° sector:
- The **PWM phase** alternates between its high‑side switch (duty D) and its low‑side switch (1−D).
- The **return phase** holds its low‑side switch on for 100% of the sector.

So at any instant the motor current I flows through exactly **two** FET channels in series. This holds regardless of D.

### 1.2 Total conduction loss per ESC channel

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad k \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow \times 1.6\ @\ 125\,°C$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per‑FET distribution
Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle, either as the hold device or in PWM.

| Measure | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Average per FET | P_cond,ch / 6 | ≈ **3.4 W** |
| Instantaneous on a conducting FET | I²·R | **10.2 W** |
| Phase RMS current (120° conduction) | I·√(2/3) | 65.3 A |

The instantaneous peak matters when the electrical period is long relative to the junction time constant. This occurs at low eRPM or stall: below ~50 Hz electrical, design to the 10.2 W figure.

### 1.4 Electrothermal coupling
R_ds rises with T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\,\big(P_{cond,FET}(T_j) + P_{sw,FET}\big)$$

Thermal runaway is possible when $2I^2R_{25}k\,R_\theta/6 \to 1$. At 80 A this ratio is ≈ 0.02, so the solution is stable and converges in 2–3 iterations.

### 1.5 Other per‑channel conduction elements

| Element | Value | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if per‑phase or per‑channel sense) | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Copper, battery pad to FET, per channel | ~0.4 mΩ effective | 2.6 W |

**Recommendation:** Use one shared 0.25 mΩ shunt on the FC current sensor instead of per‑channel shunts. This saves ≈ 10 W at full burst.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM half‑bridge switches. Per channel:

### 2.1 Hard‑switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low‑side body diode, hard turn‑on of high side)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead‑time body‑diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is the one to attack. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel, provided the gate driver's propagation matching allows it.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel @ 80 A

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.05 + 0.05 + 1.23 = \mathbf{3.3\ W}$$

The two PWM FETs share this loss. The hold FET switches only at commutation, which is negligible.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 68\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$
