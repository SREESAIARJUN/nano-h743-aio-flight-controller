## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**How the system splits.** On a 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU drives the gate driver at 48 kHz. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back.

```
 6S LiPo ──► VBAT bus ──► 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6 (or 12) MOSFETs → motor]
                │                       ▲
                │                       │ BDShot600 (1-wire, bidirectional)
                └──► 5V/3V3 BEC ──► STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
```

**Key findings:**

1. **The 80 A burst rating is only credible in a limited form.** It holds for at most about 1–2 s on a 20×20 mm board, or about 5 s on 30.5×30.5 mm, and only with paralleled FETs. The derivation is in §3.
2. **Dead-time body-diode loss is comparable to hard-switching loss at 48 kHz.** This is often overlooked.
3. **BDShot on the H743 is DMA-feasible on a single advanced timer (TIM1).** It requires per-channel CCx DMA requests through the DMAMUX.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation model

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

In any 60° sector:
- One phase high-side is PWM'd with duty D.
- Its low-side is the synchronous rectifier, conducting for (1 − D).
- A second phase low-side is held ON continuously.
- The third phase is floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The current relationship is **I_batt = D · I_ph**. This is important because "80 A" can mean battery current or phase current, and they are equal only at D = 1.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Effective switch resistance at junction temperature:

$$R_{sw}(T_j) = \frac{R_{ds(on),25}\cdot k_T}{N_p}$$

Summing the conduction intervals in one sector:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{sw}\left[D + (1-D) + 1\right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{sw}$$

This loss is independent of D under synchronous rectification. Adding PCB copper and shunt resistance gives:

$$P_{cond,tot} = I_{ph}^2\left(2R_{sw} + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right)$$

Two copper reference values:
- 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ per square.
- With 3 parallel power layers over about 2 squares of path, R_Cu ≈ 0.17 mΩ.

| Case | R_sw | 2·I²·R_sw | Copper + 0.2 mΩ shunt | **P_cond** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1 | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 2.4 W | **21.6 W** |
| N_p = 2 | 0.75 mΩ | 9.6 W | 2.4 W | **12.0 W** |

### 2.3 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the PWM'd leg switches.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Rise-time sanity check.** The Miller plateau sets the transition time:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv})}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

Using Q_gd = 8 nC, total gate resistance R_g,tot ≈ 11 Ω, V_drv = 12 V and V_pl ≈ 3 V:

$$t_r \approx \frac{8\,\text{nC} \times 11\,\Omega}{9\,\text{V}} \approx 9.8\ \text{ns}$$
