# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Power-Stage Model and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Ground Truths

Three architectural facts shape everything below:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC runs one dedicated MCU per channel (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 is the flight controller and talks to each ESC MCU over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"Nano" plus "80 A" is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal section shows that a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board cannot sink about 100 W continuously. 80 A is only physically meaningful for about 1–3 s bursts against the board's thermal capacitance.
3. **All component parameters are representative placeholders.** Re-run the model with the actual datasheet values for the parts you select. Every equation is written so you can substitute directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths

In 6-step (or FOC with synchronous rectification), at any instant the motor current flows through **exactly two MOSFETs** in series:

- one high-side (HS) FET in the PWM phase during D·T, or its complementary low-side (LS) FET during (1−D)·T, plus
- the always-on LS FET in the return phase.

Synchronous rectification is assumed, so the LS conducts through its channel rather than the body diode, except during dead time.

Per channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_m^2 \, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D, to first order)}$$

The phase-current ripple adds a correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5):

- ΔI_pp = 25.2 · 0.25 / (15e-6 · 48e3) ≈ 8.75 A
- The ripple term adds 6.4 A², which is under 0.1 % of 6400 A². It is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one justification for the high f_sw.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Self-Consistent Solution)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, Z_{\theta}(t)$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration, which converges in 3–4 steps. Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C:

- R_ds = 1.2 · (1 + 0.004 · 100) = **1.68 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 · 80² · 1.68e-3 = **21.5 W per channel**
- Per conducting FET: 80² · 1.68e-3 = **10.75 W**

The loss is unevenly distributed. Time-averaged over an electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the time, so the average is P_cond/3 ≈ 7.2 W per FET. The instantaneous peak is 10.75 W. For electrical frequencies above about 100 Hz (true for any spinning motor), the die time-constant averages these. Use 7.2 W average and verify the peak against Z_θ at about 3 ms.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches. That is one HS/LS pair per channel at any time.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (HS FET)

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 8\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 16\text{e-9} \cdot 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{0.77\,W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_m \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 600\text{e-9} \cdot 48\text{e3} = \mathbf{1.84\,W}$$

This is the dominant switching term. Cutting dead time to 150 ns saves 0.92 W per channel, provided the gate driver's propagation-delay matching (typically ±20 ns on FD6288-class drivers) allows it.

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery, Output Charge, and Gate Charge

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{e-9}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e3} = 0.060\,W$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-9}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{e3} = 0.048\,W$$

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 30\text{e-9}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{e3} = 0.029\,W \;\text{(dissipated in driver and } R_g\text{, not the FET)}$$
