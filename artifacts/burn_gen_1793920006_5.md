# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It is the flight controller (FC). It generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed) that performs 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. This document covers both sides.

**Representative parameters.** Replace these with your BOM datasheet values. All equations are parametric.

| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz all layers | — |

**Feasibility flags (read before layout).**
1. 4 × 80 A means up to **320 A drawn from the battery** during a simultaneous burst. A single XT60 and a nano-size power plane cannot carry that except for milliseconds. In practice the burst budget must be per-motor and asynchronous, or the pack and connector class must change.
2. As section 3 shows, the 80 A rating is **thermally a 1–3 s burst**, not a continuous rating. The datasheet should say so.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths
With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, exactly two FETs carry the phase current I at every instant:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** the high-side of leg A and the low-side of leg B conduct.
- **PWM-off interval (1–D):** the low-side of leg A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side of leg B conduct.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (electrothermal feedback)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j and T_j depends on R_ds, the equations are coupled. Solve them by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + \left[\frac{2I^2 R_{ds,25}(1+k_T(T_j^{(n)}-25)) + P_{sw}}{6}\right] R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}$$

The system has a stable solution only if:

$$\frac{2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,k_T\,R_{\theta}}{6} < 1$$
