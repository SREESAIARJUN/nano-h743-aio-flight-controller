# STM32H743 Nano Stack (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Job #37: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

**Revision:** A (preliminary). All numerical parameters are design assumptions and must be replaced with values from the selected datasheets before layout freeze.

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three decisions shape every calculation below.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.**
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600.
   - Each motor's 48 kHz commutation PWM comes from the ESC-side MCU, assumed here to be AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32.
   - The two domains are therefore analyzed separately.

2. **The 80 A rating is a per-motor burst figure, and only for a few seconds.**
   - On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm nano footprint, 80 A is thermally possible only as a short burst, and only with paralleled FETs. Section 3 quantifies the limit.
   - Four motors × 80 A gives 320 A from the pack. That exceeds XT60/XT90 continuous ratings and typical 14 AWG battery leads.
   - **Recommendation:** specify the product as "80 A burst (≤2 s), 45–55 A continuous per channel" and validate on a thermal bench.

3. **Baseline operating point:** 6S LiPo, so V_bus = 25.2 V maximum (22.2 V nominal).

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any time, with current entering through one leg and leaving through another.

- The PWM'd leg uses synchronous rectification, so current flows through either the high-side FET (duty D) or the low-side FET (duty 1 − D), excluding dead time.
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.
- The current path therefore always passes through approximately two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D.

The general expression is:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \frac{2\,R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]}{N_p} \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}
$$

where R_Cu is the PCB and solder-joint copper resistance per path (estimated in §2.3).

With ripple included (I_rms² = I_dc² + ΔI²/12) and ΔI ≈ 10 % at 48 kHz on a typical 5-inch to 7-inch motor (L ≈ 15–40 µH), the correction is below 0.1 % and is neglected.

**Worked case at 80 A:**

| Case | R_eff (hot) | P_cond (FET) |
|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1, Tⱼ = 125 °C, R_max = 1.3 mΩ | 2 × 1.3 × 1.55 = 4.03 mΩ | 80² × 4.03 m = **25.8 W** |
| N_p = 1, Tⱼ = 100 °C, R_typ | 2 × 1.0 × 1.41 = 2.83 mΩ | **18.1 W** |
| N_p = 2, Tⱼ = 100 °C, R_typ | 1.41 mΩ | **9.0 W** |

Because R_DS(on) depends on Tⱼ, the model must be solved iteratively (§3.3).

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each PWM period has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off of the high-side FET, while the low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage after dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched FET):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \cdot N_p = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.023\ \text{W per FET}
$$

For N_p = 2 this is ≈ 0.05 W.

**(c) Body-diode conduction during dead time** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(d) Reverse recovery** (this loss lands in the opposing FET):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_G, not the FET):

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times N_{sw\,FETs} = 60\text{ n} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

**Total switching loss ≈ 1.7 W per motor at 80 A.**

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw, so changing frequency moves the total as follows:

- 24 kHz: ~0.85 W
- 96 kHz: ~3.4 W

At this current level, conduction loss dominates by about 10×. **Parallel FETs and copper matter more than switching speed.**

### 2.3 PCB Copper Loss

Assumptions:
- 2 oz (70 µm) copper on 4 layers, with phase and bus planes stitched across 3 layers.
- Path length ≈ 15 mm, effective width ≈ 6 mm.
- Copper resistivity ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, with a +39 % correction at 125 °C.

Per-layer resistance:

$$
R = \rho L / (w t) = 1.72\text{e-}8 \times 0.015 / (0.006 \times 70\text{e-}6) = 0.61\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Three layers in parallel give ≈ 0.20 mΩ. With two paths (in and out) plus solder pads and the motor pad, R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ, so:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

This loss is not negligible and heats the same board.
