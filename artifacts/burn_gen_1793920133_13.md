# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Check

The model below uses these baseline assumptions. Change them and re-run the equations; every result is parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | Worst case for switching loss |
| Burst phase current per channel | I_ph | 80 A | Motor-side, per ESC channel |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | AM32/BLHeli_32-class setting |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_25 | 1.5 mΩ | 40 V, DFN5x6-class |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor @ T_j ≈ 125 °C | k_T | 1.5 | Typical 40 V trench FET |
| Rise / fall time | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns | ~1–2 A gate driver plus gate resistor |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns | |
| Body-diode V_F | V_F | 0.8 V | |
| Q_rr / Q_g / C_oss | | 50 nC / 50 nC / 1.5 nF | |
| Commutation | | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM | |

**Architecture correction.** In an ArduPilot system the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to these MCUs. ArduPilot has no firmware path for an H743 to drive the bridges directly.

**Feasibility.** "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 / 25×25 mm) 4-in-1 is a marketing figure, not a sustainable rating. Section 3 shows that 4 × 80 A dissipates roughly 125 W into a board of only a few grams. Design to the numbers, not the label.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Hot R_DS(on)
$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path
With 6-step synchronous rectification, two FETs are always in series with the motor current: one high-side and one low-side. Their identities depend on the PWM state.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{hot}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution
Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle:
- 1/3 of the cycle as the PWM'd source leg.
- 1/3 of the cycle as the sink leg.

For PWM duty D:

| FET | Conduction fraction | P at D = 1.0 (full throttle) | P at D = 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| High-side (source leg) | D/3 | 4.8 W | 2.4 W |
| Low-side (sync rect, source leg) | (1−D)/3 | 0 | 2.4 W |
| Low-side (sink leg) | 1/3 | 4.8 W | 4.8 W |

The general per-FET expression is:

$$P_{FET} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} \cdot \delta_{FET}$$

where δ_FET is the fraction from the table. The worst-case device is the **sink-leg low-side FET, at 4.8 W**, regardless of duty.

### 1.4 Channel totals at 80 A
$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.00225 = \mathbf{28.8\,W}$$

### 1.5 Copper losses (often forgotten)
The bus path includes the pad, the planes to the half-bridges, and the phase pads. Assume 0.5 mΩ effective per channel with 2 oz copper on a 6–8 layer stack:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.2\,\text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time in 6-step. Losses therefore count once per channel.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched high-side:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\,W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\,W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\,\text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.02\,\text{W}$$

**(e) Gate charge.** This dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\,\text{W}$$

**Switching total:** ≈ **2.7 W per channel**, roughly 2.6 W of which lands in the FETs.

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz, switching loss drops to ~1.35 W.
- At 96 kHz, it rises to ~5.4 W.

At 80 A, conduction dominates (~90%), so lowering f_sw buys little. Lowering R_DS(on) and using thicker copper buys a lot.
