# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Your brief does not include several parameters, so I assumed the values below. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute datasheet values for your actual parts.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Rationale |

**Important architectural point.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. Each motor (or the 4-in-1 as a whole) needs its own ESC MCU that runs AM32 or similar firmware and drives the gates. Typical choices are 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. The models below cover the ESC power stage (§1–3) and the FC timer/DMA plan (§4–5).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. This holds in both the drive and freewheel intervals, because the complementary FET conducts instead of the body diode. Including PWM ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2\left(1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2\right)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

At 80 A with ripple ΔI/I ≈ 0.2 (I_rms² ≈ 6421 A²):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6421 \times 1.74\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The electrical period is milliseconds, which is much shorter than the FET thermal time constants (≥10 ms), so averaging is valid:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\times 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 3.7\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Copper losses (often neglected, but significant)

For 2 oz (70 µm) copper, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. A 6-layer stack with 4 paralleled power layers gives about 0.07 mΩ/□. A 10-square path from pad to FET to phase gives ≈ 0.7 mΩ for the round trip:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.7\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 4.5\ \text{W per motor}$$

Add the shunt for current sensing (0.25 mΩ → 1.6 W, if per-motor) and connector/solder resistance.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time, so these losses apply once per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Switching times from gate charge and drive current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}, \qquad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int} + R_{drv}}$$

With 12 V drive, a 4.5 V plateau, and 5 Ω total resistance, I_g ≈ 1.5 A. This gives t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary FET body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt. At 80 A and fast edges, derate by 2–3× and use **0.15 W**.

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5\times1.5\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$
