## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Corrections

I don't have prior Job #44 documentation, so every assumption below is explicit and parameterized. Substitute your actual BOM values into the equations.

### 0.1 Architecture Correction (Important)

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production-viable 4-in-1 AIO uses:

| Function | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot, generates 4× bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCUs (×4, or ×1 quad-capable) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class | AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, six-step commutation, BEMF ZC, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q-class 3-phase half-bridge driver | 6 outputs per motor, bootstrap high-side |
| Power FETs | 24× N-ch, 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | 6 per motor |

Trying to run four 6-step FOC/BLDC loops plus the ArduPilot scheduler on the H743 is technically possible but is **not supported by ArduPilot** and would break DShot telemetry semantics. The DShot link between the FC and ESC MCUs stays.

### 0.2 Feasibility Warning (Read Before Layout)

**80 A burst per channel on a nano-class PCB (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal to infeasible** for sustained use. Section 3 quantifies this:

- Burst duration is measured in **seconds**, not tens of seconds.
- With all four channels at 80 A simultaneously, you get roughly **2–3 s** before the junctions reach their limit.

I will model the requirement honestly rather than certify it. Recommended remediations are in §3.6.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in Six-Step Commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct. Take phase A high and phase B low:

- **On-time (D):** Q_AH and Q_BL conduct.
- **Off-time (1−D), synchronous rectification:** Q_AL and Q_BL conduct, with A's freewheel through the low-side channel.

During both intervals, exactly two channel resistances are in series with the phase current. Therefore:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 \cdot k_T \cdot R_{DS(on),25}}{N_{par}} \cdot \big[D + (1-D)\big] + \frac{I_{ph}^2 \cdot k_T R_{DS(on),25}}{N_{par}} = \frac{2\,I_{ph}^2\, k_T R_{DS(on),25}}{N_{par}}
$$

**Per-FET loss** (rotating over 120° electrical conduction per FET, so a duty factor of 1/3 averaged over a revolution):

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{1}{3}\cdot I_{ph}^2 k_T R_{DS(on)} \quad\text{(averaged)}, \qquad P_{cond,FET,inst} = I_{ph}^2 k_T R_{DS(on)} \quad\text{(during its sector)}
$$

The electrical frequency is far above the junction thermal time constant at speed (f_e ≈ 1–3 kHz versus τ_j of roughly ms). The averaged value therefore governs T_j, plus a small ripple term.
