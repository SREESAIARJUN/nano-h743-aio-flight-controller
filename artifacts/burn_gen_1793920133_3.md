## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors in this design. Each ESC channel needs its own sensorless-commutation MCU. This is how AM32 and BLHeli_32 ESCs work, and it is the only practical way to run bi-directional DShot.

```
6S LiPo ─┬─> 4-in-1 ESC board (power stage)
         │     ├─ ESC MCU ×4 (STM32G071 / AT32F421, AM32 firmware)
         │     ├─ 3-phase gate driver ×4
         │     └─ 24 MOSFETs (6 per channel)
         └─> BEC ─> FC board: STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
                     └─ M1..M4 bi-dir DShot600 ─> ESC MCUs
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step complementary PWM topology

During each 60° commutation sector, current flows through:
- the **high-side FET** of phase A for a fraction D of each PWM period,
- the **low-side synchronous FET** of phase A for the remaining fraction (1 − D),
- the **low-side return FET** of phase B, which is on continuously for the whole sector.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The result does not depend on duty cycle because synchronous rectification makes the switching leg always conduct through one FET.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The average loss per FET is:

- High-side FET: (1/3) · I²R · D ≈ 1.6 W
- Low-side FET: (1/3) · I²R · [1 + (1 − D)] ≈ 4.8 W

Low-side FETs run hotter. **Place them on the thickest copper and closest to the via thermal arrays.**

### 1.4 Copper loss (do not ignore it)

For a 2 oz (70 µm) trace, 4 mm wide and 15 mm long, at about 100 °C:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 1.18\ \text{m}\Omega$$

With two such traces in series per path, that adds **≈ 15 W per channel at 80 A**. This is comparable to the FET loss. Use 4-layer 2 oz copper with paralleled power planes and aim for R_Cu,path below 0.5 mΩ.

### Burst totals

| | Per channel | Four channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| **FET subtotal** | **21.9 W** | **87.6 W** |

**Sensitivity:**
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel but increases current ripple.
- Dead time is the cheapest lever. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to board (steady state within a burst)

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

For the hottest low-side FET (4.8 W + about 0.5 W switching share), with R_θJB ≈ 2 K/W for a 5×6 mm or 3×3 mm clip package on a via array:

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 5.3 \times 2 \approx 11\ \text{K}$$

### 3.2 Board lumped-RC transient model

**Thermal capacitance:** about 3 g of copper plus FR4 plus FETs gives

$$C_{th} \approx 3\ \text{J/K}$$

**Convection (with prop wash):** h ≈ 100 W/m²K over both faces of a 30×30 mm board, A = 1.8×10⁻³ m², so

$$R_{\theta,amb} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 5.5\ \text{K/W}, \qquad \tau = R_{\theta,amb}\,C_{th} \approx 16.7\ \text{s}$$
