## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM-on interval (duty D):** current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** current flows through LS(A) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(B).

So the current always passes through **exactly two channel resistances**:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS,A} + R_{LS,B} \right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This holds when all FETs are identical, and it is independent of D (body-diode intervals are excluded here and handled in §2).

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor (80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.70 mΩ | 21.8 W |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | 23.8 W |
| 150 °C | 2.03 mΩ | 25.9 W |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 120° high-side plus 120° low-side per electrical cycle. Averaged over a cycle, a motor's 6 FETs share the loss, so the average per FET is P_cond/6 ≈ **4.0 W at 125 °C**.

The **instantaneous** dissipation in an active low-side FET, however, reaches I²R = 80² × 1.86 mΩ ≈ **11.9 W**. At low eRPM, where the electrical period exceeds roughly 10 ms, the die's thermal time constant is short enough that T_j tracks this instantaneous value. For that reason, **stall or low-RPM burst is the worst case**, not cruise.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal self-consistency and the runaway criterion

With effective thermal resistance R_θ from junction to ambient for the relevant path:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_\theta\,P_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j - 25 = \frac{(T_a - 25) + R_\theta P_{25}}{1 - \alpha R_\theta P_{25}}
$$

- **Stability condition:** α·R_θ·P₂₅ < 1.
- With P₂₅ = 15.4 W per motor and α = 0.0055, runaway occurs if R_θ > 11.8 K/W per motor.
- A nano 4-in-1 in still air is typically 15–25 K/W **for the whole board**. Steady-state 80 A on all four motors is therefore **not thermally stable**. 80 A must be specified as a time-limited burst (§3).

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. The other conducting phase is static.

| Loss term | Formula | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (HS hard switching) | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.229 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | **0.038 W** (dissipated mainly in driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching per motor** | | **≈ 2.30 W** |

**Key insight:** At 48 kHz, **dead-time diode loss exceeds overlap loss**. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, which is more than halving the edge times would. AM32 lets you configure dead time ⚠VERIFY minimum safe value against the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

**Frequency scaling:**

$$
P_{sw}(f) = 2.30\,\text{W} \times \frac{f}{48\,\text{kHz}}
$$

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.15 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.30 W |
| 96 kHz | 4.60 W |
