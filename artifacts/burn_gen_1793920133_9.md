## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Red Flags

| Item | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Design for 25.2 V, with transients to ~30 V |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor phase**, 10 s | Worst-case interpretation |
| Continuous current | Derived below | Not assumed |
| f_PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) | |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal | One high-side and one low-side FET active, a third phase floating |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421 (AM32 firmware) **[A]** | The H743 does **not** drive the gates. It sends DShot to the ESC MCUs. |
| FETs | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.65 mΩ at 25 °C, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 **[A]** | 24 FETs total |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, 1 per motor **[A]** | |
| Board | 36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz inner and outer copper **[A]** | |

**Red flag 1: form factor.** A "nano" (20×20 mm) board **cannot** sustain 4 × 80 A. Section 3 shows the total burst dissipation is about 60 W. This design assumes at least a 30.5×30.5 mm mount on a 36×36 mm board. Even then, 80 A is a **time-limited burst rating**, not a continuous one.

**Red flag 2: MCU heating.** The STM32H743 is rated at T_A ≤ 85 °C, or 125 °C for the "3"-suffix temperature grade. A stacked or integrated layout requires a thermal break between the power stage and the MCU. See §3.5.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \textbf{[A]}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.65 \times 1.5 = 0.975\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step complementary PWM

Within one commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is low-side "on":

- **Active interval (duty D):** current flows through Q_AH and Q_BL. Two R_DS are in series.
- **Freewheel interval (1 − D):** current flows through Q_AL (synchronous) and Q_BL. Two R_DS are in series.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}\cdot D + I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}\cdot(1-D) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}$$

This loss is **independent of duty cycle**. That is the key result for synchronous 6-step drive.

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.975\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{12.48\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over a full electrical cycle, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time in the "static" low-side role and 1/3 in the PWM role. Two consequences follow:

- Low-side FETs carry the freewheel current, so they dissipate more at low D.
- At D → 1, losses balance across the bridge, at roughly 12.48 / 6 ≈ **2.1 W average per FET**.
- At D = 0.5, the hottest low-side FET reaches about **2.8 W**.

### 1.3 Copper (PCB) loss

Assume each phase has an effective path of 15 mm length and 6 mm width, using 2 oz copper on 3 paralleled layers.

$$R_{sheet,2oz} \approx 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega/\square\ (25°C)$$

$$R_{path} = \frac{0.25\cdot(15/6)}{3} \approx 0.21\ \text{m}\Omega \;\xrightarrow{\times1.39\ @125°C}\; 0.29\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.29\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{3.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

This term is frequently ignored. Here it is **30% of FET conduction loss**, and it directly drives the board-level ΔT.

### 1.4 Battery-side current relationship

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph} \quad (\text{lossless approx.})$$

At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95), 80 A of phase current corresponds to about 76 A of battery current per motor. **Total pack current is ~300 A.** The power entry pads, XT90-class connector, and leads must be sized accordingly.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, **only one leg switches per sector**: one hard-switched high-side transition, plus the complementary low-side transition.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

Assumptions:

- Gate-charge plateau charges: Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 14 nC **[A]**
- Driver source/sink current at the Miller plateau: ≈ 1.0 A / 1.5 A **[A]**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,src}} = 14\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{14\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} \approx 9.3\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 23.3\times10^{-9}\times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.13\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (Coss)

Take C_oss,eff ≈ 2.0 nF **[A]**. The loss is per switching leg, counting one FET's charge plus the complementary FET's discharge:

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\,V^2\,f_{sw} = 2.0\times10^{-9}\times 25.2^2\times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.061\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

Take Q_rr ≈ 60 nC at 80 A and high di/dt **[A]**:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 60\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The recovery charge also generates a ringing and EMI burden. This sets the snubber and layout requirements in §5.4.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

Assumptions: two dead-time windows per PWM period, t_dt = 100 ns (AM32 typical), V_F ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A.

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
