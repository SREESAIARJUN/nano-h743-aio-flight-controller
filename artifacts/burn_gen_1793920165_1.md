# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #47): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Timer Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Corrections

I don't have access to any prior Job #47 documentation, so this model is built from first principles. Every assumed parameter is listed in §1. Replace the placeholders with your actual BOM datasheet values and the equations will recompute directly.

Several architectural corrections need to be stated before the math:

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The architecture below is an AIO board with one H743 FC MCU and four G071 ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an ESC firmware setting, not an FC setting.
2. **80 A per channel is only achievable as a short burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** The math in §4 shows the realistic sustained rating is roughly 15–20 A per channel without a heatsink. The 80 A figure must be specified with a duration (≈1–4 s, depending on how many motors are loaded simultaneously).
3. **Four motors at 80 A means roughly 320 A of battery current.** No nano-class connector, battery lead, or bus plane can carry that. Bus design should assume about 120–150 A total peak.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series. During the PWM on-time this is the high-side of one leg plus the low-side of another. During off-time, the high-side is replaced by the synchronous low-side of the same leg.
