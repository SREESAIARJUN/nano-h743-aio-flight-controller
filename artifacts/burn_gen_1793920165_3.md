## 0. Scope, Architecture and Assumptions

No prior Job #2 design package is available to me, so this document states every assumption explicitly. Replace the parameters marked **[A]** with your actual datasheet values. The equations hold; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

### Architecture decision

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to a stacked 4-in-1 ESC that has one small MCU per channel running AM32 or BLHeli_32.

I recommend against having the H743 commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no FOC/6-step motor-control path, and bi-directional DShot is meaningless if the H7 is the ESC.

### Headline result

80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is thermally sustainable for about **2 seconds**. The defensible continuous rating is about **20 A per channel**. The analysis below shows why. Any "80 A burst" label on a nano ESC must be specified with a duration.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:

- **PWM on-time:** high side of leg A, then the motor, then low side of leg B.
- **PWM off-time:** low side of leg A (sync-rectifying), then the motor, then low side of leg B.

This gives the channel conduction loss, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \text{[A]}$$

At T_j = 110 °C with R_ds,25 at its maximum of 1.9 mΩ:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.9 \times 1.38 = 2.63\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but it matters)

The phase and battery paths add roughly 0.3 mΩ per channel (2 oz, short pours, vias) [A]:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.63\,\text{m}\Omega = 33.7\ \text{W}$$

Adding copper loss gives about **35.6 W per channel**.

There is a self-heating feedback loop: higher loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises loss. Solve it by iteration. It converges in 2–3 passes for any board that is not already in thermal runaway.
