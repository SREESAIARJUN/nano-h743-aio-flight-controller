## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three points in the brief need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** In production 4-in-1 designs, the H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each of the four motor bridges has its own small commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). One H743 doing four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz alongside a 1 kHz+ ArduPilot loop is not a supportable design. The netlists below therefore use **H743 FC + 4× AT32F421 (AM32)**.

2. **"80A burst" must be defined.** I model **80 A per motor channel** as the worst case. I also give the per-board figure, because the conclusions differ sharply.

3. **"Nano" form factor.** A 20×20 mm board cannot thermally support 4×80 A. I use a **30.5×30.5 mm mount (≈37×37 mm board)** and show the 20×20 derating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, current I flows through:

- the **PWM phase**: high-side FET for duty D, low-side FET (synchronous freewheel) for 1−D;
- the **return phase**: low-side FET for 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

It is **independent of duty**, which is a useful simplification. With $R_{hot} = k_T R_{ds,25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$:

$$
P_{cond,ch}(80\text{ A}) = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 1.2 PCB Copper Loss

The current path is: battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad, with roughly 25 mm of 4-layer × 2 oz paralleled copper, 3 mm wide.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.025}{4 \cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 0.003} \approx 0.51\ \text{m}\Omega \quad (\times 1.3 \text{ hot} = 0.67\ \text{m}\Omega)
$$

$$
P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \cdot I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 2\cdot6400\cdot0.00067 \approx \mathbf{8.6\ W}\ \text{(worst case, two phase legs)}
$$

This is frequently ignored and is a major reason small boards fail. Mitigations are discussed in §7.

### 1.3 Shunt Loss

A single bus shunt of 0.25 mΩ, shared by all four channels, carries the board total current:

$$
P_{shunt} = (4\cdot 80)^2\cdot0.00025 = 25.6\ \text{W}
$$

This is unacceptable. **Use per-channel shunts or a Hall sensor (e.g., TMCS1100 / ACS772) on the bus.** The netlist uses one 0.1 mΩ shunt per channel:

$$
P = 80^2 \cdot 0.0001 = 0.64\ \text{W per channel}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM-phase pair hard-switches. The loss terms are:

| Term | Expression | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.060 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss(er)·V²·f_sw ×2 FETs | 0.046 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_d·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw ×2 = 45 nC·10·48 k·2 | 0.043 W |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 36\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.87 W per channel. That is small next to conduction loss: at this current, **switching frequency is not the thermal problem; I²R is.**
