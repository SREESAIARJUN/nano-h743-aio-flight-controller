# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4‑in‑1 ESC (Job #64): System Architecture Package

**Rev A: Analytical baseline. All values must be validated against selected‑part datasheets and bench thermal testing before release.**

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

Two architectural points need to be settled before the math.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** DShot is an FC‑to‑ESC protocol. If you are asking for ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600, the H743 is the *flight controller*, and each motor channel (or the whole 4‑in‑1) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32‑class firmware. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Typical choices are the STM32G071 or AT32F421.

- The alternative is to have the H743 drive 12 gate signals directly with an FOC stack.
- That removes DShot entirely and is not compatible with stock ArduPilot motor output.

This document assumes the conventional split: **H743 FC + 4 × G071 ESC MCUs on one stacked or AIO PCB.**

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** The analysis below quantifies exactly how short that burst must be.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature‑dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | Multiplier | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.41 | 1.41 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.55 | 1.55 mΩ |

Use **1.55 mΩ** for worst‑case burst.

### 1.2 Current paths in trapezoidal synchronous drive

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct. Within a PWM period of duty D:

- **On‑time (D):** high‑side FET of phase A and low‑side FET of phase B conduct I.
- **Off‑time (1−D), synchronous rectification:** low‑side FET of phase A freewheels with the low‑side FET of phase B.

RMS‑squared current per position:

$$I^2_{HS,A} = D\,I^2,\quad I^2_{LS,A} = (1-D)\,I^2,\quad I^2_{LS,B} = I^2$$

The sum is always **2I²**, independent of duty. That gives the clean per‑channel result:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

Add the series parasitics in the current path:

$$P_{cond,total,ch} = I^2\left[2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{shunt} + R_{Cu,path}\right]$$

The values used are:

- R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ (single low‑side bus shunt)
- R_Cu,path ≈ 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, plus vias, plus solder pads to motor wire)

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Loss per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FETs at 125 °C | 6400 × 2 × 1.55 mΩ | **19.84 W** |
| Shunt | 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Copper | 6400 × 0.40 mΩ | 2.56 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **24.0 W** |

The worst single device is the low‑side FET of the "sinking" phase, which carries 100 % of the time:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 1.55\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{9.92\ W}$$

### 1.4 Battery‑side current (sanity check)

$$I_{bat} \approx D\cdot I_{phase}$$

At D = 0.9 that is 72 A per channel, or **≈ 288 A total battery current** for all four at burst. This tells you two things:

- The XT60 and the 12 AWG pigtail are already well beyond rating.
- The bulk capacitor ripple must be checked (see §3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **active (high‑side) FET hard‑switches**. The synchronous low‑side FET turns on after its body diode has already clamped, so it switches near‑ZVS.

### 2.1 Hard‑switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

The rise and fall times are set by gate drive current:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}$$

With Q_gd = 10 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 4.5 V, and R_g = 10 Ω + 1 Ω, I_g ≈ 0.5 A, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
