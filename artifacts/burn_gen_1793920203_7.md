# Job #61: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC
## Systems Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Allocation, Netlists

The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. In any viable 4-in-1 design, each bridge has its own small ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). These MCUs commutate the motors and produce the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The model below is split along that boundary.

All device parameters are representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class and an FD6288Q-class gate driver. Replace them with your actual BOM datasheet values before signing off. Part 1.7 shows that this choice decides whether the "80A burst" claim survives.

### 1.2 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation two phase legs carry current at any instant:
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1−D). With synchronous rectification, this leg always has one FET in the path.
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET is on for the full 60° sector.

The total conducting resistance in the path is therefore always 2·R_DS(on), independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Ripple contribution.** Ripple peaks at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ A$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ A^2$$

The ripple term is negligible (+0.06%), so I²_rms ≈ 6400 A².

**Results:**

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond (per channel) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 17.9 W |
| 100 °C | 2.03 mΩ | 26.0 W |
| 125 °C | 2.24 mΩ | **28.7 W** |

Per FET, the low side of the fixed leg sees the worst case: I²R ≈ 14.3 W at 125 °C while it conducts. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time. That is ≈4.8 W average, but the peak dissipation during its sector is what drives the junction ripple.

### 1.3 Switching (V·I Overlap) Losses

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{6.5}{7} = 0.93\ A$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3.5}{5} = 0.70\ A$$

Ideal transition times:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\,nC}{0.93} \approx 11\ ns, \qquad t_f = \frac{10\,nC}{0.70} \approx 14\ ns$$

Common-source inductance, layout loop inductance, and the driver's own rise time typically stretch these. Use **t_r ≈ 20 ns and t_f ≈ 25 ns** for design.

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 45\,ns \times 48\,kHz = 2.18\ W$$
