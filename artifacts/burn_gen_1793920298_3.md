**Architectural clarification (important).** The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz phase PWM. In every practical 4-in-1 AIO, each motor bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **bi-directional DShot600** to those ESC MCUs. The model is therefore split into:

- **Power stage (per ESC channel):** conduction, switching and thermal losses.
- **Command link (H743 → ESC MCUs):** timer/DMA allocation and DShot timing.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two FETs conduct at every instant: one in the PWM'd leg (high side during D, low side during 1–D) and the low side of the return leg. Phase current therefore flows through exactly **two R_DS(on) in series** at all times. Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time (see §2.3).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.5\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.55 = 2.33\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Loss per motor channel

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I_DC within the conduction interval. At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.33\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{29.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Per-FET (worst conducting device): **≈ 14.9 W**.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, not negligible)

- **Board copper, bus to phase path:** about 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, about 15 mm effective, multilayer paralleled). P_Cu = 80² × 0.4 mΩ = **2.6 W/motor**.
- **Shunt:** a 0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt carries the sum of currents (≈ 320 A peak, all 4 motors). P_shunt = 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**.
  - Recommendation: use per-motor shunts or a Hall sensor, or accept that the bus shunt is a burst-only device.

### 1.5 Self-heating feedback (convergence)

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on P. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P\big(R(T_j^{(n)})\big)$$

This converges for the burst durations in §4. **Thermal runaway** occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot Z_{th} \ge 1$$

That means I²·2R₂₅·α·Z_th ≥ 1, i.e. **Z_th ≥ 9.5 K/W at 80 A**. This is safe for the junction-to-board path, but it is approached in steady state on a small board. That is one reason 80 A cannot be continuous.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per commutation interval, one leg is hard-switched at f_sw. The loss terms below are per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

With a realistic Miller plateau, if the driver is weaker than assumed:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

Size R_g so that t_sw ≤ 25 ns. Doubling t_sw doubles this term.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and temperature. Budget **0.15 W**.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss,eq}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.4\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.02\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\,N_{switching} = 45\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

For 6-step, only one leg pair switches at a time. Multiply by about 3 if gates are refreshed on all legs. Budget **0.13 W/driver**.
