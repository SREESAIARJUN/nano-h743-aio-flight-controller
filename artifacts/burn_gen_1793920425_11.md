# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #39)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** It is the flight controller and generates DShot. Each motor (or all four) needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. Bidirectional DShot is the protocol between the two domains.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible beyond about 1–2 s.** The math in §3 shows why. Treat this as a 1–2 s per-motor burst rating, with a much lower combined continuous rating. The alternative is moving to 30.5×30.5 mm.
3. **All device parameters below are assumed representative values** for a 40 V, roughly 1 mΩ-class PDFN MOSFET and a 6S pack. The structure of the model is exact. The numbers must be re-run against the datasheets of the parts you actually select.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

With 6-step trapezoidal drive and synchronous (complementary) PWM, two legs conduct at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for duty D, and its complementary low side (LS) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the LS is held on at 100%.

The instantaneous current therefore always passes through exactly two R_ds(on) channels in series, excluding dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of duty. Per FET, rotation over six sectors gives each switch one third of the electrical period:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,D,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,[(1-D)+1]$$

The low side runs hotter at low duty. For PCB layout, LS copper should get thermal priority.

**Ripple correction.** Use I_rms² = I_dc² + ΔI²/12, with

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, line-to-line 2L), the worst case at D = 0.5 gives ΔI ≈ 4.4 A. This adds less than 0.1% and is negligible at 80 A. It matters for low-inductance motors (below about 5 µH) at 48 kHz.

### 1.2 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve by iteration:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad T_j = T_{board}(t) + P_{FET}\,Z_{th,j\text{-}b}(t)$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond per motor (W) | Worst LS FET at D = 0.5 (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 | 3.2 |
| 100 | 1.41 | 18.1 | 4.5 |
| 125 | 1.55 | 19.8 | 5.0 |

### 1.3 Copper and Interconnect Losses

These are frequently ignored but are first-order at 80 A.

- Sheet resistance is R_□ = ρ/t. For 2 oz (70 µm) copper this is 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, with a +0.39 %/K coefficient.
- A realistic nano-board path is about 3 squares of 2 oz copper on a 4-layer board with paralleled planes: battery pad → HS → phase → motor pad, plus LS → GND. This gives roughly R_cu ≈ 0.35 mΩ per motor current path.
- Copper loss is P_cu = I²·R_cu ≈ 6400 × 0.35 mΩ ≈ **2.2 W per motor**.
- The shared battery bus carries ΣI. At 4 × 80 A = 320 A, even 0.1 mΩ dissipates 10 W. This is the strongest argument against a 4×80 A rating on 20×20 mm.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The return-leg LS is static within a sector.

### 2.1 Transition Times from Gate Charge

The gate drive current during the Miller plateau is:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{drv,on}+R_g+R_{g,int}},\qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,off}+R_g+R_{g,int}}$$

With V_pl = 3.5 V, R_g = 2.2 Ω, R_g,int = 1 Ω and R_drv ≈ 2 Ω:

- I_g,on ≈ 1.25 A
- I_g,off ≈ 0.67 A

The transition times are then:

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 11\text{ ns},\qquad t_{off}\approx\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 21\text{ ns}$$
