# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #16)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor channel is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step drive

In any commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the full sector.

At any instant, the phase current therefore flows through exactly two channels in series. This makes conduction loss essentially independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This expression gives 1.5 × R_25 at 125 °C.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond (FETs) per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | **17.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** |

**Copper path:** assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for the phase pad, the planes and the solder. That adds 6400 · 0.3e-3 = **1.9 W**.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each low-side FET is the "return" device for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Each phase is the PWM leg for 1/3 of the cycle.

The averaged dissipation is 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**.

At low throttle (D → 0), the low-side FETs carry most of the stress. This is the worst case for punch-out recovery.

### 1.5 Dead-time body-diode loss

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_F\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}$$

With V_F = 0.8 V and t_dt = 100 ns:

2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100e-9 · 48e3 = **0.61 W** per motor.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the PWM leg switches:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

The edge times follow from the gate drive:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}}, \quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 18 nC, V_DRV = 10 V, V_plateau = 4.5 V and R_g = 10 Ω + 1 Ω:

- I_g ≈ 0.5 A, so t_r ≈ 36 ns.
- Using t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns as the design target, P_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40e-9 · 48e3 = **1.94 W**.

Reaching the 40 ns target requires stronger sink current on turn-off. Use a diode in parallel with a 4.7 Ω resistor in the gate path.

### 2.2 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Gate-drive loss

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{DRV}\, f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$
