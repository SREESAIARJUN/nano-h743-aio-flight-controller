# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Electro-Thermal and DShot System Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No datasheets or prior design files for Job #44 were provided, so every numeric parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your actual MOSFET, driver and stack-up values. The equations are general and stay valid.

**Two architectural points shape the whole model:**

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The loss model therefore belongs to the ESC board, and the DMA model belongs to the FC.
2. **80 A burst on a 20×20 mm board is a transient-only rating.** As §2.6 shows, it is limited by thermal capacitance, not steady-state resistance. Shipping 20×20 products typically sit at 40–55 A burst. Treat 80 A as a design-review red flag unless the board is 30.5×30.5 mm or uses paralleled FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Synchronous Commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, exactly two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** HS conducts for D·T_sw, LS (synchronous rectifier) for (1−D)·T_sw. The phase current flows through one of these two devices continuously.
- **Return leg:** LS fully ON.

As a result, two R_ds(on) are always in series with I_ph, whatever the duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical period, so per-leg and per-FET average losses are:

$$P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{2}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

The per-FET split skews toward LS at low D and toward HS at high D.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence: Positive Electro-Thermal Feedback

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta\,P(T_j) \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\,(2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw})}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{\theta,crit} = \frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

At 80 A this is 1 / (2·6400·1.4e-3·0.0051) ≈ **10.9 K/W**. Per-channel R_θ to ambient on a 20×20 board is well above this unless the board is heavily cooled, so **no stable steady-state operating point exists at 80 A**. Burst operation is mandatory, not optional.
