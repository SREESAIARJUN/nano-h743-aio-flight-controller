# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #33): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Several items in the brief need clarifying before the math will hold up.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each ESC channel, or a pair of channels, is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. The model below follows that split.
2. **"Nano" and "80A burst" are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot sink 80A×4 for more than a few seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80A as a per-channel, phase-current burst lasting 5 s or less, not a continuous rating.
3. **Representative component values are used throughout.** These are 40V N-MOSFETs in a PDFN 3.3×3.3 package with R_DS(on),25°C = 1.0 mΩ, a 6S pack (V_bus = 25.2 V maximum), and a 3-phase gate driver of the FD6288Q class. Substitute your BOM datasheet values into the equations. The structure of the model does not change.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In every sector, phase current *I* flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- the PWM'd leg, where the high-side conducts for D and the low-side synchronous FET for (1−D), and
- the static low-side of the return leg.

There is therefore always a series path of two R_DS(on) values, independent of duty cycle.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

Include ripple in the RMS current. With phase inductance L_ph and switching frequency f_sw:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance (α ≈ 0.0045 /K for modern trench FETs):

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Channel conduction loss, adding the bridge copper/pad resistance R_cu:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2\,\big(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big)
$$

**Numbers at 80A burst.** Take L_ph = 15 µH (a typical 2207-class motor), D = 0.5 (worst-case ripple), and f_sw = 48 kHz.

- ΔI_pp = 25.2·0.25 / (15e-6·48e3) = **8.75 A**, so the ripple term is 6.4 A². This is negligible against 6400 A². I_rms ≈ 80.04 A.
- At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0·(1+0.45) = **1.45 mΩ**.
- Take R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner pours, about 1.6 squares at 0.25 mΩ/sq).

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 6406\,(2.90 + 0.40)\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET is active in roughly two of six sectors, so P_FET,cond ≈ I²·R_DS / 3 ≈ **3.1 W** averaged over an electrical cycle. Instantaneous peaks within a sector reach about 9.3 W, which matters for Z_th at low eRPM.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Hard-switched inductive transitions occur on the PWM'd leg only.

**(a) V–I overlap.** The transition time is set by the Miller charge and the driver current:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{g,int}}
$$

With Q_gd = 8 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 4 V, and R_g,total = 6 Ω: I_g = 1 A, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 8 ns.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·16\text{ns}·48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}
$$

**(b) Body-diode dead-time conduction.** With t_dt = 250 ns per edge and V_F = 0.8 V:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8·80·500\text{ns}·48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.54\ W}
$$

This is the largest switching term. AM32 dead-time tuning is the cheapest thermal win available.

**(c) Reverse recovery.** With Q_rr = 40 nC:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}·25.2·48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(d) Output capacitance.** With Q_oss = 50 nC:

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive.** Six FETs per channel, two of which switch at f_sw, with Q_g = 45 nC:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2·45\text{n}·10·48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}
$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FETs.
