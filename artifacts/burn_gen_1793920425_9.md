## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With complementary 6-step commutation, exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current at every instant:

- **PWM on-time (D):** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** LS(phase A) (sync-rectifying) → motor → LS(phase B)

The duty cycle therefore cancels out of total conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

Add the PCB copper and solder path, R_trace ≈ 0.3–0.6 mΩ per current loop:

$$P_{cond,total} = I^2\left[2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{trace}\right]$$

### 1.2 Phase ripple correction

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{avg}^2 + \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

At 48 kHz with a typical 5″ motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH), ΔI is about 10 A. The ripple term adds roughly 0.1 % and can be neglected for 80 A.

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point

Let R_th,j-amb,eff be the effective thermal resistance seen by the conducting pair. Define:

$$k = 2I^2R_{25}$$

Solving T_j = T_ref + k[1+α(T_j−25)]·R_th gives:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + kR_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\,\alpha\,R_{th}}}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion:** k·α·R_th < 1. With k = 19.2 W and α = 0.0055, R_th must stay below 9.5 K/W. This is a hard layout requirement.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

At T_j = 110 °C, R_DS = 1.5 × 1.4675 = 2.20 mΩ.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond, 2 FETs | 2 × 6400 × 0.00220 = **28.2 W per motor** |

| ×4 motors | **≈ 123 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step commutation only one half-bridge is hard-switching at a time. Per motor:

**(a) V-I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of the HS FET):**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = 15\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery.** The LS body diode conducts during dead time and is recovered by HS turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$
