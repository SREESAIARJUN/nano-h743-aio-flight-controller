## 0. Architectural Ruling (Read First)

These three findings change the design before any maths is done.

**1. The H743 must not drive the gates directly.**
- Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time.
- The H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8), giving 2 bridges.
- HRTIM adds 10 outputs, but ArduPilot has no motor-commutation stack on it.
- **Decision:** the H743 is the flight controller. Each of the 4 ESC channels gets its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). DShot600 bidirectional is the link between them.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" are in direct physical tension.**
- The model below shows about 27 W per channel at 80 A.
- With all four channels at 80 A, a 25×25 mm board with no heatsink heats at roughly 35 K/s.
- 80 A is therefore a **sub-2-second burst rating per channel**. It is not a continuous or 10-second rating. Continuous capacity lands near **20–25 A per channel** with propwash cooling.
- Market it honestly. Inflated burst ratings are the leading cause of field ESC failures.

**3. 48 kHz is on the high side for 80 A at 6S.**
- Dead-time body-diode loss and switching loss scale linearly with f_sw.
- They contribute about 2.9 W per channel at 80 A, 48 kHz.
- Consider 24 kHz as a high-current fallback (AM32 supports variable PWM frequency).
