# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These four points change the design, so they come first.

1. **The H743 cannot also be the ESC.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The architecture must be:
   - an **STM32H743 flight controller** running ArduPilot, and
   - **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which do the 48 kHz PWM.
   - Bidirectional DShot600 links the two.
   
   Everything below assumes this split.

2. **An 80A burst on a nano board (20×20 mm) is physically limited to under about 1 s.** The math in §3 shows this. Treat "80A" as a **sub-second transient rating**. Realistic continuous current on this footprint is about 25–35A per motor.

3. **All device parameters below are representative placeholders.** They match typical 40V DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFETs and FD6288-class gate drivers. Replace them with the datasheet values of the parts you actually procure before signing off the design.

4. **Verify the ArduPilot timer/DMA mapping against your ArduPilot version.** Build it and check `hwdef.h` and the `@SYS/dma.txt` output on the target.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, one 3-phase bridge)

### 2.1 Assumptions (placeholders)

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S max | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | burst phase current | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | per FET, Vgs = 10 V | 1.5 mΩ |
| k_T | Rds(on) multiplier at Tj ≈ 125 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r, t_f | effective V–I overlap times | 20 ns each |
| Q_rr | body-diode reverse recovery | 40 nC |
| C_oss | effective output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | total gate charge | 40 nC |
| V_gs | gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| t_dt | dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| V_f | body-diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.8 V |

**Commutation model.** The bridge uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary, synchronous PWM on the active leg. At any instant, **two switch positions** carry I_ph:
- one fixed-on low-side, and
- one PWM'd leg whose high-side and low-side share the current as D and (1−D).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

The hot on-resistance is:

R_ds(T) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)] ≈ R_ds,25 · k_T

The bridge conduction loss is:

P_cond = I_ph² · R_ds(T) · [1 (fixed leg) + D + (1−D) (PWM leg)] = **2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T)**

Substituting:

P_cond = 2 · 80² · (1.5 mΩ · 1.5) = 2 · 6400 · 2.25 mΩ = **28.8 W**

That is **14.4 W per conducting FET**. Over an electrical cycle, each FET conducts about one third of the time, so the time-averaged loss is ≈ 9.6 W per FET. Because the burst is shorter than the thermal time constant, the **instantaneous** 14.4 W governs junction temperature.

**PCB copper loss.** Phase and bus copper add P_Cu = I² · R_trace. For example, 0.3 mΩ of 2-oz copper path adds 80² · 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W** per motor. Use 3–4 oz inner planes.

### 2.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each loss term is computed below.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48k | **0.05 W** |
| C_oss (HS + LS) | 2 · ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.04 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A, at the cost of more current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A burst

| | Per motor | Four motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.8 W | 115 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 11 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.4 W** | **≈ 134 W** |

Bridge efficiency at this operating point is roughly 92–95%, but the absolute heat is what kills the design.
