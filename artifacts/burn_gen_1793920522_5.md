## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC, e.g. GBM4108 class: 12N14P → pole pairs `p = 7`, wye, R ≈ 5–10 Ω, L ≈ 2–4 mH |
| Driver | 3-phase half-bridge, low-side or inline current shunts, center-aligned PWM at 20 kHz |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701 class) per axis |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, mounted at symmetric lever arms (r₁ ≈ −r₂), SPI at 24 MHz |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7 class) with single-precision FPU |

**Loop rates:**

```
 20 kHz  ── Current loop (FOC): ADC → Clarke/Park → PI_d, PI_q → SVPWM
  8 kHz  ── IMU sampling (both ICM-42688-P, gyro+accel, FIFO or DRDY)
  2 kHz  ── IMU filtering + fusion → attitude; velocity loop; angle loop
           (velocity loop runs at ≥ 5× its bandwidth; angle loop co-scheduled)
 50 Hz   ── Cogging-table LMS adaptation (background), diagnostics
```

**Cascade structure (per axis):**

```
θ_ref ─►(+)─► P/PD angle ─► ω_ref ─►(+)─► PI velocity ─► iq_fb ─►(+)─► PI current ─► SVPWM ─► Motor
        ▲−                          ▲−                             ▲   ▲−
        θ_cam (IMU attitude)        ω_cam (fused gyro, axis-proj.) │   iq (measured)
                                                                   │
                                         iq_cog(θm) feed-forward ──┘  (+ inertia FF)
θ_e for commutation ← encoder
```

Stabilization feedback (θ, ω) comes from the camera-side IMUs because they measure what you actually want to hold steady. The encoder is used for commutation, the cogging-table index, and the joint-angle limits.

## 1. FOC Current Loop (Inner, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform with balanced currents (i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

Park transform with electrical angle θₑ = p·(θₘ − θ_offset):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform maps v_d, v_q back to v_α, v_β.

### 1.2 PMSM Model and Decoupled PI

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

Electromagnetic torque (surface PM, L_d ≈ L_q):

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q \equiv K_t\, i_q
$$

The control law adds decoupling and back-EMF feed-forward:

$$
v_d^* = \mathrm{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \mathrm{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

**Gain selection by pole-zero cancellation.** The PI zero K_i/K_p is placed on the electrical pole R/L. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c \le \frac{2\pi f_{PWM}}{10\text{–}20}
$$

At 20 kHz this gives f_c ≈ 1–2 kHz.

Set i_d* = 0 (no field weakening is needed at gimbal speeds).

**Voltage limit.** Circularly limit to v_max = V_bus/√3 (the linear SVPWM range). Prioritize d, then give q the remaining headroom:

$$
v_q^{max}=\sqrt{v_{max}^2 - v_d^2}
$$

**Anti-windup:** clamp the integrator whenever the output saturates.

### 1.3 SVPWM (Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max + \min),\qquad d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is equivalent to conventional 7-segment SVPWM and is cheaper to compute.
