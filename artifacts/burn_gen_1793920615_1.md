# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture

## 0. Scope, Corrections, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The H743 should not commutate the motors directly. Sensorless control of 4 motors (12 half-bridges, 4 BEMF zero-cross comparators, ADC at 48 kHz) alongside an ArduPilot loop at 1–2 kHz is not a viable partition.

The standard partition, used here, is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot)  ──Bidir DShot600 ×4──►  4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                   └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 24× MOSFET
```

The rest of this document follows that partition.

### 0.2 Physical reality check

The thermal model in §3 shows that a nano-class board (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) cannot sustain 80 A per motor. 80 A is a burst rating of a few seconds, set by board thermal capacitance. Steady-state capability is set by convective dissipation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM-switched and phase B is held low:

- **On-state (duty D):** the high-side FET of A and the low-side FET of B conduct.
- **Off-state (1−D):** the low-side FET of A (synchronous rectifier) and the low-side FET of B conduct.

Total conduction loss per ESC:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Two consequences follow:

- With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**.
- Without synchronous rectification (diode freewheeling), the (1−D) term becomes $V_{SD}\,I\,(1-D)$, which is roughly 50 W at low duty and 80 A. Synchronous rectification is therefore mandatory.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At an assumed T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 90) = 1.686\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.686\times10^{-3} = 21.6\ \text{W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 21.6 / 6 = 3.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Assume a path resistance (bus, phase pours, pads, and solder joints) of R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per conduction path. Copper has α ≈ 0.0039 /K, so at about 70 °C R_cu ≈ 0.35 mΩ:

$$P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.35\times10^{-3} = 2.2\ \text{W per ESC}$$

This loss is significant. On nano boards, copper resistance often rivals FET resistance. Use 2 oz outer layers and stack inner-layer pours in parallel on the bus.

### 1.4 Shunt loss

A 0.5 mΩ battery shunt carrying 4×80 A × D_avg dissipates 51 W at D = 1. This is excessive. Either:

- use a 0.2 mΩ shunt (about 20 W at full burst, applies only during the burst), or
- sense per ESC through per-phase low-side sensing.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches; one is active per sector at a time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched turn-on and turn-off)

Gate current at the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10 - 3}{7} = 1.0\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.43\ \text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 12\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 28\ \text{ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

Turn-off dominates. A dedicated low-impedance sink path (an anti-parallel diode across R_g, or a split R_g,on/R_g,off) cuts t_f roughly in half and saves about 0.7 W per ESC.
