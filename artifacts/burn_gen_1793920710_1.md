# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

## 0. Framing and Assumptions

I don't have any prior project data for "Job #27," so every parameter below is an explicit assumption. Substitute your real BOM values into the equations. The math is structured so that works cleanly.

Three architectural findings come first, because they change the deliverable:

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** It has only two advanced-control timers (TIM1, TIM8) with complementary outputs and hardware dead-time. Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary pairs. The standard and correct architecture is:
   - H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
   - Four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) drive the bridges.
   - Each ESC MCU receives bidirectional DShot600 from the H743.
2. **80 A per channel on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating, measured in seconds.** On a 20×20 mm board it is not credible at all. Section 3 shows the derivation. I assume a 30.5×30.5 mm mount pattern on a ~36×36 mm PCB. If the board is truly 20×20 mm, divide the burst duration by roughly 2.5.
3. The netlists in Section 5 are **reference topology netlists**. They are not a verified layout. Pin choices must be checked against the H743 datasheet alternate-function table and your package (LQFP100 vs. TFBGA, etc.).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Hot on-resistance

R_ds,hot = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0045 /K.

At T_j = 125 °C:

R_ds,hot = 1.4 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.03 mΩ** (use 2.0 mΩ)

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

With synchronous rectification in 6-step, current at every instant flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:
- the PWM leg (high side during t_on, low side during t_off), and
- the static low-side leg.

Dead-time intervals are handled separately in §2.4.

P_cond,ch = 2 · I² · R_ds,hot · (1 − 2·t_dead·f_sw)

The dead-time correction factor is 1 − 2·200 ns·48 kHz = 0.981.

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 0.002 × 0.981 = **25.1 W**

### 1.3 Copper loss

P_Cu,ch = I² · R_Cu = 6400 × 0.0005 = **3.2 W**

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, i.e., 1/3 duty. At a typical burst speed the electrical frequency is several kHz (e.g., 30 k rpm with 14 poles gives 3.5 kHz). That is far above the FET's thermal time constant, so average power applies:

P_FET,avg ≈ P_cond,ch / 6 ≈ **4.2 W per FET**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step, only one leg switches at any time. Its hard-switched FET sees the full V_bus × I overlap.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

P_ov = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz
= **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The complementary body diode recovers on each turn-on:

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.060 W**

This is small in absolute terms. However, Q_rr rises steeply with di/dt and temperature, so get the value from the datasheet at your operating di/dt.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 0.5 × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.024 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

P_dt = V_f · I · 2·t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W**

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, provided the driver propagation-delay matching allows it.

### 2.5 Gate drive

P_g = N_FET · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 6 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k = **0.17 W**

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

P_sw,ch = 1.94 + 0.06 + 0.02 + 1.23 + 0.17 ≈ **3.4 W**
