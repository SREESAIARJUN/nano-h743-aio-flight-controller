# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Design Analysis

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

Some points in the brief need correcting before the analysis:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any 4-in-1 design, each motor bridge is driven by its own ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **bidirectional DShot600** to those MCUs. ArduPilot does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. That frequency is a setting in the ESC firmware.
2. **80 A on a nano/AIO form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) can only be a short burst.** The math in §2 shows that 80 A is thermally limited to a few seconds. It is not a continuous rating.
3. **All device parameters below are stated assumptions.** Replace them with values from your actual MOSFET and gate-driver datasheets. The netlist in §4 is a reference topology, not a fab-ready release. Run it through your ECAD ERC/DRC and a bench thermal validation.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The analysis assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification. In every 60° sector, current I flows through one high-side and one low-side path.

The active phase leg has duty D:
- The high-side FET conducts for D.
- Its synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).

That leg's sum is therefore I²R. The return leg's low-side FET conducts continuously. Hence:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}\,k_T = 2(80)^2(1.0\times10^{-3})(1.5) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The result is independent of D, to first order.

PCB copper adds:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses (48 kHz)

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(24\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\text{ nF})(635)(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(600\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

**Gate drive (one switching pair active):**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{ nC})(10)(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

Gate-drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

> **Insight:** At 48 kHz, dead-time loss exceeds overlap switching loss. Trimming dead time from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per ESC, or 3.7 W per board. That is the cheapest thermal win available.

### 2.3 Totals

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 (all motors at burst) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + Q_rr + C_oss | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Dead time | 1.84 W | 7.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.5 W** | **≈ 102 W** |

Efficiency check: input power is about 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At full throttle (≈ 2 kW per motor), ~25 W of loss gives ≈ 98.7 % bridge efficiency. Efficiency is not the problem. Heat density is: ~100 W on roughly 6–10 cm² of board.

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above Board (quasi-static)

Average loss per FET ≈ (19.2 + 1.2 + 1.8) / 6 ≈ 3.7 W, but the load is shared unevenly. The peak FET is the continuously-on return low-side FET in a sector:

$$P_{peak} = I^2 R k_T = 9.6\text{ W}, \qquad \Delta T_{JC} = 9.6 \times 1.5 = 14.4\text{ K}$$

This rotates among FETs every 60° electrical, so the time-average is lower. However, at low eRPM (stall or punch-out from hover) a single FET sees near-DC loading.

### 3.2 Board Heating (adiabatic burst, which dominates)

Board thermal mass:
- 25.5 mm AIO, 8-layer, 2 oz: ~9 g PCB plus ~2 g components.
- Effective specific heat c ≈ 0.6 J/(g·K), mixing FR4 at ~1.1 and Cu at 0.385.
- C_th ≈ 11 × 0.6 ≈ **6.6 J/K**.

Heating rate at full 4-motor burst:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{102\text{ W}}{6.6\text{ J/K}} \approx \mathbf{15\ K/s}$$

Convective relief:
- Prop-wash h ≈ 80 W/(m²·K), area A ≈ 2 × 6.5 cm² → R_θ,amb ≈ 9.6 K/W.
- Steady-state ΔT at 102 W would be ~980 K, so there is no steady state at 80 A.
- Time constant τ = R·C ≈ 63 s, which is much longer than the burst, so the adiabatic estimate holds.

**Burst duration limit:**

$$t_{burst} = \frac{T_{lim} - T_0 - \Delta T_{JC}}{dT/dt} = \frac{125 - 45 - 14}{15} \approx \mathbf{4.4\ s}$$

Assumptions: start at 45 °C, T_j limit 125 °C for derating margin.

**Sustainable continuous current.** Setting P_loss = ΔT / R_θ with ΔT = 60 K:
- Allowed total loss ≈ 6.25 W board-wide, about 1.6 W per ESC.
- Using P ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻³ · I² (hot R + copper, small switching term), I ≈ **21 A continuous per motor** with prop-wash cooling.

**Recommended rating:** 25 A continuous / 80 A burst (≤ 3 s). Implement both in firmware:
- ESC-side temperature limiting (AM32 temperature limit).
- ArduPilot-side monitoring via the bidirectional DShot/EDT temperature telemetry.
