### Architectural clarification

In a 4-in-1 ESC, the 48 kHz PWM and commutation are generated by the ESC MCUs (AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay targets), not by the H743. The H743 only sources bidirectional DShot600 and reads eRPM telemetry.

The two halves are therefore modeled separately:
- **Sections 1–3**: the ESC power stage.
- **Section 4**: the FC timer and DMA allocation.

If Job #38 is a true single-MCU AIO where the H743 drives the bridges directly, tell me. The DMA budget changes substantially in that case: you would need 6 complementary PWM channels per motor, so TIM1/TIM8 advanced timers, and only 2 motors could be driven natively.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **1.89 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-active and one is held low:
- The current path is HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- During PWM off-time, the A-leg low-side FET freewheels in place of the high-side FET.

Losses per sector:
- PWM leg: I²R·D on the high-side FET plus I²R·(1−D) on the low-side FET, which sums to **I²R regardless of duty cycle**.
- Return leg, low-side FET held on: **I²R**.

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2\,I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.89e-3 = **24.2 W** |
| P_cond per FET (average) | **4.0 W** |
| All 4 motors simultaneously | **96.8 W** |

### 1.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}(t)\cdot P\!\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\right)$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. Thermal runaway occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot Z_{th} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot Z_{th} \ge 1$$

For one FET at 80 A with Z_th = 2.5 K/W, this gives 0.03, so the stage is stable at the FET level. Runaway is a board-level concern, covered in Section 3.

### 1.5 Copper loss

Copper losses are often forgotten. A 2 oz outer layer (70 µm) has a sheet resistance of about 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. Assume roughly 3 squares of phase and bus path per motor in parallel across 2 layers, about 0.37 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.37\text{e-}3 \approx \mathbf{2.4\ W/motor}$$

This is significant, and it is why the bus, GND, and phase pours need to span all layers.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Switching losses accrue on the PWM-active leg only (2 FETs at any instant). The terms are as follows.

**(a) Hard-switching V–I overlap** on the high-side FET:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1\text{nF}\times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

Q_rr itself scales with di/dt and current, so treat this as a lower bound.

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** on the low-side FET:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive**, dissipated mainly in the driver and R_g:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### Switching summary per motor at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.69 |
| C_oss | 0.03 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **P_sw total** | **≈ 2.42 W** |

**Design insight:** at 48 kHz, switching is about 9% of conduction loss. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 2.4 W per motor, which is acceptable if you want quieter, smoother motors. Dead time is the second-largest term, so trim t_dt to the minimum safe value. The AM32 dead-time setting is the relevant knob.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst power

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 24.2 W | 96.8 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.7 W |
| Copper | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Gate driver, LDO, MCU | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **29.3 W** | **≈ 117 W** |

The hottest FET is a high-side FET on the PWM leg:

$$P_{HS,peak} \approx \tfrac{I^2R}{3} + P_{VI} + P_{rr} \approx 4.0 + 1.7 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{5.8\ W}$$

Use this as the time-averaged value across the electrical cycle.
