# Job #5: STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 80 A ESC

## Engineering Model and Reference Design (Rev A, draft for review)

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Before any numbers, three problems with the brief as stated:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 only emits bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
   - The **48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter**, not an H743 timer.
   - The math below is split along this boundary.

2. **80 A burst in a "nano" form factor is not thermally sustainable.**
   - Section 2 shows roughly 85 W of dissipation across four channels at 80 A.
   - On a 30×30 mm board, that is a few seconds of burst, not a continuous rating.
   - A 20×20 mm "nano" cannot physically fit 24 FETs rated for this current with adequate copper.
   - Recommendation: **30.5×30.5 mm mounting, 80 A ≤ 5 s burst, ~40–45 A continuous.** I've modeled it that way.

3. **The netlist below is a reference architecture, not a release netlist.**
   - Every pin assignment must be checked against the STM32H743 datasheet (DS12110) alternate-function table and your exact package.
   - Every component must be checked against its own datasheet.

## 2. ESC Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

In 6-step operation, two half-bridges carry I_ph at any instant. With synchronous rectification, each leg's current flows through either the high-side FET (fraction D) or the low-side FET (fraction 1−D), so the leg loss is independent of D:

P_cond = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |

Worst single FET (high side at D→1, leg active 2/3 of the electrical cycle):

P_FET,max ≈ (2/3) · I² · R_DS ≈ 6.2 W at 100 °C

### 2.2 Switching Losses

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM period.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k | 0.97 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 0.07 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, mostly in driver) | 6 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 6 · 40 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.12 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 1.8 W** |

Scaling notes:
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.9 W.
- 48 kHz buys smoother low-throttle current and acoustic benefit for under 10% of total loss at burst current.
- Dead-time loss is the second-largest term. Tune dead time to the minimum safe value for the chosen driver.

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Loss

- Shunt (0.5 mΩ, low-side, single): I² · R · duty ≈ 80² · 0.5 m · ~0.67 ≈ 2.1 W if placed in the main path. Consider **0.25 mΩ** (≈1.1 W) or a per-phase sensing scheme.
- Board copper, phase pad to FET at about 0.3 mΩ effective: about 1.9 W.

### 2.4 Totals at T_j = 100 °C

| Scope | Power |
|---|---|
| Per channel: 18.6 + 1.8 + 1.1 (shunt) + 1.9 (copper) | ≈ 23.4 W |
| 4-in-1, all motors at 80 A | **≈ 94 W** |
| Efficiency: 94 W / (4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × ~0.9 effective duty ≈ 7.3 kW) | ≈ 98.7% |

The efficiency is good. **The heat density is the problem.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

```
P_FET → R_θJC (1.0) → R_θCS (via field + 2 oz Cu, ~2 K/W per FET footprint)
       → board node [C_board] → R_θBA (convection, prop wash) → T_amb
```

### 3.2 Junction Rise Above Board

ΔT_JB ≈ P_FET · (R_θJC + R_θCS) ≈ 6.2 · 3 ≈ **19 K**

### 3.3 Board Thermal Mass

For a 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer board with 24 FETs:
- FR4 ≈ 6 g × 1.1 J/gK = 6.6 J/K
- Cu + FETs ≈ 4 g × 0.39 J/gK = 1.6 J/K
- **C_b ≈ 8 J/K**

### 3.4 Steady State Is Impossible

Board area (both sides) A = 2 × (0.0305)² = 1.86×10⁻³ m². Under prop wash, h ≈ 75 W/m²K:

- R_θBA = 1 / (hA) ≈ 7.2 K/W
- Steady-state rise = 94 × 7.2 ≈ **680 K**

That is impossible, so 80 A is **burst only**.

### 3.5 Burst Duration (Transient)

The board temperature follows a first-order rise:

T_b(t) = T_amb + P·R_θBA · (1 − e^(−t/τ)), with τ = R_θBA · C_b ≈ 58 s

For t ≪ τ, this is nearly adiabatic: dT/dt ≈ P/C_b ≈ 11.7 K/s.

**Limit:** T_j,max = 125 °C, so T_b ≤ 125 − 19 = 106 °C.
- From T_amb = 40 °C, ΔT = 66 K.
- **t_burst ≈ 66 / 11.7 ≈ 5.6 s.**

Electrothermal feedback (R_DS rises as T rises) shortens this. Solve numerically:

C_b · dT_b/dt = 4[2I² R_25 (1 + α(T_b + 19 − 25)) + 4.8] − (T_b − T_amb)/R_θBA

**Result: ≈ 5.0 s to the T_j limit.** This supports a rating of **"80 A / 5 s burst."**

### 3.6 Continuous Rating

With a 25 K board rise, 25 / 7.2 ≈ 3.5 W per channel is allowed:

I_cont ≈ √(3.5 / (2 · 1.3 m)) ≈ **37 A per motor**

A conformal-coated or heat-spread design with more airflow reaches about 45 A.

### 3.7 Required Firmware Protection

- ESC temperature sensor (NTC per bank).
- AM32 current limit plus thermal derate starting at 100 °C board temperature.
- ArduPilot: `BATT_*` current limiting, and ESC telemetry temperature logged and in the OSD.
