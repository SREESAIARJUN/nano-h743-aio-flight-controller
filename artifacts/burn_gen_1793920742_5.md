# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Preliminary Design Model

I don't have prior project files for "Job #3," so this design starts from the explicit assumptions below. Every number traces back to a stated parameter, so you can substitute your actual BOM values and recompute.

**Feasibility flags.** These come first because they drive everything else:

1. **80A ×4 simultaneous on a 20×20 mm board cannot be sustained thermally.** It is a ~2-second burst at most (see §3).
2. **Battery-side current at full throttle approaches 320A.** No XT60 or nano power path survives this. "80A burst" must be specified per channel and non-simultaneous.
3. **A 40V MOSFET on 6S at 80A turn-off is at avalanche risk** unless di/dt is deliberately slowed (see §2.3).
4. **Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own 4 motor MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 does not commutate motors directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**

### 1.2 Bridge conduction (per ESC channel)
With synchronous rectification, at every instant exactly two FETs (one high-side, one low-side path) carry phase current:
$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2.9\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution
Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (120° high-side, 120° low-side). The worst device is the low-side FET held fully on for its 120° interval:
$$P_{FET,max} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.45\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{3.1\ W}$$
The average across 6 FETs is also ≈ 3.1 W, so the distribution is nearly uniform at high duty.

### 1.4 Copper path
Assume 0.5 mΩ of effective trace, via, and pad resistance per channel (2 oz, 6-layer):
$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{m} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The hard-switched device is the high-side FET in each PWM leg. The low-side FET commutates softly after dead time.

### 2.1 Transition times
Gate currents through the Miller region:
$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{dr}-V_{pl}}{R_{on}} = 1.375\text{ A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{off}} = 2.25\text{ A}$$

Transition charge Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 4 + 6 = 10 nC:
$$t_{on} = 7.3\text{ ns}, \qquad t_{off} = 4.4\text{ ns}$$

### 2.2 Energy per cycle
$$E_{on} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I\, t_{on} = 7.33\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$E_{off} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I\, t_{off} = 4.48\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$E_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{in}^2 = 0.29\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$E_{rr} \approx Q_{rr} V_{in} = 0.76\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$E_{sw} = 12.86\ \mu\text{J} \;\Rightarrow\; P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.62\ W}\ \text{per channel}$$

### 2.3 Turn-off overshoot (critical)
$$\frac{di}{dt} = \frac{80\text{ A}}{4.4\text{ ns}} = 18\ \text{A/ns} \;\Rightarrow\; V_{spike} = L_{loop}\frac{di}{dt} = 18\ \text{V per nH}$$

A realistic 1 nH commutation loop gives 25.2 + 18 = **43 V, which exceeds the 40V rating.**

**Fix:** Raise R_off so that t_off ≈ 12 ns (di/dt ≈ 6.7 A/ns, ~6.7 V/nH), and keep the loop below 1 nH with a 0402/0603 ceramic directly across each half-bridge. Alternatively, use 60V FETs and accept a higher R_ds.
