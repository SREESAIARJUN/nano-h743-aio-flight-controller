## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Design Assumptions

Two architectural points constrain the rest of the analysis:

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU runs the 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and back-EMF commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The design below follows this split. Driving 24 FETs with sensorless commutation directly from the H743 alongside the ArduPilot scheduler is not viable.

2. **"80 A burst" is a feasibility edge on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** The math below shows roughly 110 W of dissipation when all four channels are at 80 A. That is survivable only as a short transient of about 1–2 s. Treat 80 A as a per-channel, non-concurrent burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Complementary PWM
In each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current. One is PWM'd and the other is held on.

- **PWM on (duty D):** current flows through HS(A), the motor, then LS(B).
- **PWM off (1−D):** with synchronous rectification, current flows through LS(A), the motor, then LS(B).

At every instant, **exactly two channel-conducting MOSFETs** carry I. This holds outside dead time, which Section 2 covers.

### 1.2 Instantaneous and Average Conduction Loss per Channel

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

The cases below show the effect of junction temperature.

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel @80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 2·6400·1.30e-3 = **16.6 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | **22.3 W** |
| 135 °C | 1.95 mΩ | **25.0 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the time. Each active leg spends half of that as the PWM'd leg and half as the held-on leg.

- **Low-side FET:** conducts during the held-on 1/3 and during the (1−D) portion of the PWM 1/3.
  $P_{LS} = I^2R\left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right]$
- **High-side FET:** conducts during D of its 1/3.
  $P_{HS} = I^2R\,\tfrac{D}{3}$

At burst, D is close to 1, so P_HS ≈ I²R/3 and P_LS ≈ I²R/3. Summed over 6 FETs, this equals 2I²R.

**At 100 °C:** I²R = 6400 × 1.74 mΩ = 11.1 W, so **≈ 3.7 W average per FET**.

The FET's instantaneous peak while conducting is 11.1 W. Its thermal time constant (a few ms) is much longer than the electrical period at 80 A (about 0.3–1 ms at typical eRPM), so ripple in T_j is small.

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Conduction
- **Current shunt:** 0.25 mΩ per channel (or one shared shunt).
  $P = I^2 R = 6400 × 0.25\text{e-}3 = 1.6$ W per channel if per-channel.
- **PCB copper:** phase and bus paths of about 15 mm, 2 oz, 4 layers paralleled, roughly 0.3 mΩ effective.
  $P ≈ 6400 × 0.3\text{e-}3 ≈$ **1.9 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches, which is one leg per channel at any time.

### 2.1 Gate Current During Transition

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,int} + R_{drv,src}} \approx \frac{10 - 3}{1.5 + 4.5} ≈ 1.17\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,int} + R_{drv,snk}} \approx \frac{3}{1.5 + 1.5} = 1.0\,\text{A}$$
