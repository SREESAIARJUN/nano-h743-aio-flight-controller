## 0. Architecture Decisions and Critical Findings

**Topology.** ArduPilot on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, here AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32 firmware. The H743 drives them over bi-directional DShot600, and each AM32 runs its 48 kHz complementary PWM locally. This document models both halves: the H743 flight controller and the ESC power stage.

**Finding 1: 80 A on all four motors is not continuously sustainable on a 20×20 mm board.**
- The loss model in §1–3 gives about **26 W per motor at 80 A**, or about **105 W for the board**.
- Without a heatsink, a 20×20 board in prop wash has a lumped thermal resistance of about 12 K/W to air.
- The resulting board time constant is about 25 s.
- **Simultaneous 80 A on all four motors is therefore viable for about 1 s.** A single-motor 80 A transient is viable for about 5 s.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry

Six-step trapezoidal control, which AM32 uses, conducts through exactly one high-side and one low-side leg at any instant. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the current path during PWM-off transfers from the active high-side FET to the same-leg low-side FET, which has the same R_ds(on). The series resistance in the loop is therefore always 2·R_ds(on), **independent of duty cycle** (dead time excluded, see §2.4).
