**Two architectural corrections before the math:**

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC runs four independent commutation loops on its own MCU(s), e.g. 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. This is a two-board stack (FC + ESC), which is how the netlists in §5 are partitioned.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano 4-in-1 is thermally the binding constraint of the entire design.** As §3 shows, 4×80A is survivable for about 2–3 seconds. Specify the burst duration explicitly in the product requirements.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Battery Current vs. Phase Current

With battery current I_bat and effective duty D:

$$I_{phase} = \frac{I_{bat}}{D}$$

An "80A ESC" rating is usually a phase-current rating at high duty. At D = 0.5, an 80 A phase current is only 40 A from the battery. All losses below scale with **phase current**.

### 1.2 R_DS(on) Temperature Dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Six-Step Commutation with Complementary PWM

At any instant two phase legs carry current I. With synchronous rectification, each active leg always has exactly one FET conducting, either the HS FET (for duty D) or the LS FET (for 1−D). The conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.6\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The thermal time constant of the die and board (ms to s) is much longer than the electrical period (about 0.2 ms at 30k eRPM), so the average applies:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 3.4\ \mathrm{W}$$

The worst-case FET is the PWM-leg HS FET at high D, because it carries the switching losses as well (§2).

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper and Shunt Losses

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase-path copper (2 oz, ~4 mm wide, ~15 mm, multilayer parallel) | ~0.15 mΩ effective | 2 × I²R = 1.9 W |
| Low-side shunt, 0.5 mΩ (2512) | 40 mV signal | **3.2 W ✗ unacceptable** |
| Low-side shunt, 0.2 mΩ (2512 metal strip) | 16 mV signal, needs gain ≈ 100 | **1.28 W ✓** |

**Recommendation:** Use 0.2 mΩ shunts with an INA186 or an on-chip op-amp (gain 50–100), or move to a single common DC-link shunt for telemetry only.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg PWMs at a time in six-step (the "PWM-high, LS-on" scheme). V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 35 ns (gate drive ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink, R_g = 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f = 50\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.060\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

$$P_g = n\, Q_g V_{GS} f = 2 \times 50\,\mathrm{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Loss Summary (per ESC channel, 80 A, T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss | W | % |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 20.48 | 82.3 |
| Copper + 0.2 mΩ shunt | 3.18 | 12.8 |
| V–I switching | 1.69 | 6.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.5 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr + gate | 0.16 | 0.6 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 24.9 W** | |
| **4-in-1 total (all at 80 A)** | **≈ 99.6 W** | |

Conduction dominates (≈ 82%).

**Is 48 kHz justified?** Switching-related losses are about 2.5 W of 25 W. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.2 W per channel but increases current ripple and audible noise. **48 kHz is acceptable, but R_DS(on) is the lever.** Paralleling two FETs per position halves conduction loss (−10 W per channel) at the cost of board area.
