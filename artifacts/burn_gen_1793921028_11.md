# Job #45: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The STM32H743 is a flight-controller MCU. It is not a practical motor-commutation MCU for this design. I am therefore assuming the following partition:

| Block | Device (reference choice) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) @ 400 MHz | ArduPilot, bi-dir DShot600 master |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3× half-bridge, integrated bootstrap diodes) | 6 gates per channel |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≈ 1.0 mΩ typ @ 25 °C, 10 V V<sub>GS</sub> | 3-phase bridges |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V<sub>bus,max</sub> = 25.2 V | |

If your parameters differ, substitute them. Every equation below is kept parametric.

**Engineering flag:** an "80 A burst" rating on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board is only physically meaningful as a short, time-limited transient. Section 3 quantifies the limit. You should not publish an 80 A rating without a duration and duty specification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
Commutation is six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At every instant, phase current flows through exactly two MOSFET positions:
- one high-side FET, or its low-side partner during the off-time;
- plus one low-side FET.

With synchronous rectification, the resistive path is always 2 × R<sub>DS(on)</sub> (ignoring dead-time, which is handled in §2).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}\ \text{(typical for 40 V trench)}$$

| T<sub>j</sub> | Multiplier | R<sub>DS</sub> |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.45 | 1.45 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.60 | 1.60 mΩ |

### 1.3 Loss per ESC channel

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

For six-step drive, I<sub>ph,rms</sub> ≈ I<sub>DC,motor</sub> during the 120° conduction windows. Averaged, the stress on the two conducting legs equals I² · 2R.

**Copper and solder path.** For 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers in parallel, a phase path of about 8 mm and the bus pours, R<sub>Cu</sub> ≈ 0.4–0.6 mΩ per channel. Use 0.5 mΩ.

At I = 80 A and T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C:

$$P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.60\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 20.5\ \mathrm{W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 3.2\ \mathrm{W}$$

**Per-device split:** about 10.2 W per conducting FET, which is the hot-spot driver.

### 1.4 Iterative T<sub>j</sub> convergence
Because R<sub>DS</sub> depends on T<sub>j</sub> and T<sub>j</sub> depends on P, solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + I^2 R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\,Z_{th}$$
$$\Rightarrow T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2R_{25}Z_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\alpha Z_{th}}$$

**Thermal-runaway condition:** the denominator goes to zero when

$$I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} \ge 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I_{crit} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{R_{25}\alpha Z_{th}}}$$

With per-FET Z<sub>th</sub> = 15 K/W (DFN on a cramped board, steady state):
- I<sub>crit</sub> = √(1 / (0.001 × 0.006 × 15)) ≈ **105 A**.

80 A is therefore below runaway, but with thin margin in steady state. This is the mathematical reason the rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Losses at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated FET of the active pair hard-switches. Its complement soft-switches after dead-time.

### 2.1 Reference gate parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Q<sub>g</sub> (10 V) | 60 nC |
| Q<sub>gd</sub> | 12 nC |
| Q<sub>gs2</sub> | 6 nC |
| V<sub>plateau</sub> | 3.0 V |
| R<sub>g,int</sub> | 1.0 Ω |
| R<sub>g,ext</sub> | 2.2 Ω |
| V<sub>DRV</sub> | 10 V |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> | 50 nC |
| Q<sub>oss</sub> @ 25 V | 60 nC |
| t<sub>dead</sub> | 100 ns |
| V<sub>SD</sub> | 0.8 V |

### 2.2 Transition times
Gate current is limited by resistance. The FD6288 output resistance is small relative to R<sub>g</sub>, so it is lumped into R<sub>g,tot</sub> = 3.2 Ω.

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 2.19\ \mathrm{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 0.94\ \mathrm{A}$$

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.2\ \mathrm{ns}, \qquad t_{off} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 19.2\ \mathrm{ns}$$

**Caveat:** the FD6288 has a rated peak drive of about +1.5 A / –1.8 A. The turn-on current is therefore actually clamped to about 1.5 A
