## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Four points change the shape of the design.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and has no sensorless BLDC commutation. The correct partition is:

   ```
   STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                    └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6 MOSFETs per motor
   ```

   The 48 kHz PWM is an **ESC-firmware parameter** (AM32 supports 24/48 kHz). It is not an H743 timer function.

2. **"80 A burst" is treated as 80 A RMS phase current per motor, for ≤ 2 s.** On a nano footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) this is thermally a transient-only rating. Section 3 shows the board cannot sustain it.

3. **320 A total burst is beyond any nano power entry.** An XT30 is rated about 30 A continuous, and a 14 AWG lead about 50–60 A burst. Realistically the per-motor 80 A figure only occurs briefly and non-simultaneously. The bus is sized for **≥ 150 A burst via soldered pads, not a connector**.

4. **Device parameters are representative 40 V PDFN3.3×3.3 class values.** The model is parametric. Re-run it with the datasheet values of the part you actually select.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary PWM, two legs conduct at any instant. In the PWM'd leg, current alternates between the HS FET (duty D) and the LS FET (1−D, synchronous freewheel). In the static leg, one FET conducts 100 %.

Instantaneous conduction resistance is therefore always **2·R_DS(on)**, independent of D, except during dead time (Section 2.4).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad k \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.6\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.55 = 2.48\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Per-motor bridge conduction loss

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.48\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{31.7\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window:

- **Static (non-PWM) 60° sector:** one FET carries the full current.
- **PWM sector:** current is split by D between HS and LS.

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, with commutation rotating among all six FETs:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6}\times\text{(weighting)} \approx 5.3\ \text{W average}$$

The worst-case FET sees **I²R = 15.9 W** instantaneously, but at the eRPM associated with 80 A (> 1 kHz electrical) the thermal time constant of the die (~ms) averages this out.

**Use 5.3 W average per FET, with a peak-ripple correction of +10 % for T_j.**

### 1.5 Bulk copper and shunt losses

**Phase shunt or low-side sensing** (0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt per ESC):

$$P_{shunt} = I_{DC}^2 R_{sh}, \qquad I_{DC} \approx D\cdot I_{ph} \approx 76\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; 1.44\ \text{W}$$

**Copper planes:** sheet resistance of 2 oz Cu (70 µm) is

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{mΩ/□}$$

- **Per-motor path:** about 4 squares effective per path across 2 paralleled 2 oz layers gives about 0.5 mΩ, so P ≈ 80² × 0.5 m ≈ **3.2 W/motor**.
- **Bus plane from battery pads:** carries up to 320 A. Use 3 paralleled 2 oz layers, about 1.5 □ gives about 0.12 mΩ, so P ≈ **12 W at full 4×80 A**.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per motor, one HS/LS pair commutates hard each PWM cycle.

### 2.1 Gate-current-limited transition times

The plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 4.5 V and drive is V_DRV = 10 V.

Turn-on:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{drv,on}+R_g+R_{g,int}} = \frac{5.5}{3+4.7+1} = 0.63\ \text{A}$$

Turn-off:

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,off}+R_g+R_{g,int}} = \frac{4.5}{2+4.7+1} = 0.58\ \text{A}$$

Voltage transition times (Miller-dominated):

$$t_{vr}=\frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=14.3\ \text{ns},\quad t_{vf}=\frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}}=15.5\ \text{ns}$$

Adding about 5 ns for current commutation each edge gives t_on ≈ 19 ns and t_off ≈ 21 ns.

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitive, reverse-recovery and gate losses

**C_oss (energy-equivalent):**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.5 \times 900\text{p} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.027\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode → HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Ringing from Q_rr also adds about 10–20 % to the HS turn-on loss, so take P_VI × 1.15 → **2.23 W**.
