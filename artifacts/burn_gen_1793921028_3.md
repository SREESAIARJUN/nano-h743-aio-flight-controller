### Engineering flag

80 A *continuous* is not achievable on a 36 mm board. The physics below shows this is a **transient-limited** design: the sustainable burst is a few seconds with all four motors loaded. The firmware current limit and burst timer are part of the thermal design, not optional extras.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any commutation step, phase X is PWM-switched (duty D) and phase Y has its low-side FET held fully on.

- PWM leg: current flows in the HS FET for time D and in the LS FET (synchronous rectification) for time 1−D.
- Return leg: LS FET conducts for 100 % of the step.

The PWM leg's conduction loss is independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,PWM} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ret} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu} + I^2 R_{shunt}}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (electro-thermal feedback)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{th}}{1 - I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

**Stability condition (no thermal runaway):** I² · R₂₅ · α · R_th < 1.

For the worst-case FET (the return-leg LS, 100 % on): 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0055 × R_th < 1, which requires R_th < 28 K/W. This passes easily, so the system is stable but strongly gain-amplified.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| R_ds(125 °C) | 1.0 mΩ × 1.55 | 1.55 mΩ |
| FET conduction | 2 × 6400 × 1.55 mΩ | **19.84 W** |
| Phase copper (≈0.15 mΩ effective path) **[A]** | 6400 × 0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| Shunt, low-side DC bus, 0.25 mΩ **[A]** | (I_bus²) see note | ≈0.8 W |
| **P_cond per channel** | | **≈21.6 W** |

**Shunt note:** bus current ≈ D·I_phase. At D ≈ 0.7 (typical at 80 A), the RMS bus current through the shunt is √D · 80 ≈ 67 A, giving ≈1.1 W. A value of 0.8 W is used as the mid-duty estimate.

Per-FET distribution, rotated by commutation, so each FET carries this load for 1/3 of the electrical period:

- Return-leg LS: 9.9 W during its active step.
- PWM leg: split D : (1−D) between HS and LS.

**Average per FET:** ≈19.84 W / 6 = 3.3 W.
**Peak instantaneous per FET:** 9.9 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS FET hard-switches; its LS FET soft-switches (ZVS via body-diode freewheel).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode → dissipated in HS)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up with di/dt and I_F. Budget 3×, giving **0.18 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the dominant secondary loss. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. A shoot-through margin must be validated on the bench.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET die)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw,ch} = 1.94 + 0.18 + 0.05 + 1.23 + 0.06 \approx \mathbf{3.46\ W}$$

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

The frequency-dependent portion is about 0.072 W/kHz, so going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about +1.7 W per channel. 48 kHz is justified only by lower current ripple in low-inductance motors and acoustic and sync benefits.
