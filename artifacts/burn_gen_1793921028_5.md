# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 80 A ESC Nano Stack: Electro-Thermal Model and DShot Architecture

This is a parametric engineering model, not a fab-ready release. Part numbers are representative examples chosen to make the math concrete. Every parameter is tabulated so you can substitute vendor datasheet values. Pin and timer mappings must be verified against the STM32H743 datasheet (DS12110) alternate-function tables and the current ArduPilot `hwdef` documentation before layout freeze.

---

## 0. Architectural Decision (read first)

ArduPilot does **not** run ESC commutation firmware. A credible "H743 + 4-in-1" design is therefore a **two-domain system**:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Estimation, control, DShot600 master, eRPM capture |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 | 6-step/sinusoidal commutation at 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot slave |

**Recommended mechanical form factor:** a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm two-board stack. Use a separate FC board on soft-mount grommets, connected by an 8-pin harness: VBAT, GND, M1–M4, CURR, TLM.

The reason is thermal. The model below shows the ESC board reaching 100 °C+ during bursts. That would wreck IMU bias stability and H743 timing margins on a single PCB.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths

Assume trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through two phase legs:
- In the **PWM leg**, the high-side switch (HS) conducts for a fraction D and the low-side switch (LS, synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- In the **static leg**, the LS is fully on.

At any instant there are therefore **exactly two R_ds(on) in series** with the phase current, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = \alpha(T_j)\,R_{ds(on),25}
$$

**At 80 A and T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_ds(on) = 1.6 × 0.75 mΩ = 1.2 mΩ
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.2×10⁻³ = **15.4 W per ESC**

**Thermal runaway check.** R_ds rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. Solve self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

This converges because R_θJC is small (see §4.3). The board temperature T_pcb is the real constraint.

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical period: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static leg. Per-FET losses are:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R
$$

**At D = 0.9:**
- P_HS ≈ 2.3 W
- P_LS ≈ 2.8 W

LS FETs run hotter, so place them nearest the thermal vias and pad.

### 2.3 Shunt and copper

- **Shunt:** P_shunt = I² R_sh = 6400 × 0.2 mΩ = **1.28 W**
- **Copper:** a phase trace of 1 mΩ effective (2 oz, ~5 mm wide × 15 mm, across 4 layers) adds about 2×6.4 W per ESC if poorly designed. Target total trace resistance < 0.3 mΩ → about **2 W per ESC**.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r+t_f) f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss V f_sw | 0.5 × 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f I (2 t_dead) f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 300 ns × 48 k = **0.92 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g V_gs f_sw × 2 | 60 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.06 W** |

**Total switching ≈ 2.0 W per ESC.**

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.0 W, at the cost of audible noise and ripple current.

Dead-time loss is as large as overlap loss. Reducing AM32 dead time from 150 ns to 80 ns saves about 0.45 W per ESC. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Total loss budget (80 A burst, 6S)

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| Switching | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Shunt | 1.3 W | 5.1 W |
| Copper | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Driver + ESC MCU + LDO | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **21 W** | **≈ 84 W** |

**Efficiency check:**
- Output power ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D ≈ 7.3 kW at D = 0.9
- η ≈ 98.9%

The efficiency is excellent, but 84 W in a 9 cm² board is the binding constraint.

### 4.2 Lumped board model (first-order RC)

**Heat capacity (30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 6 × 2 oz copper):**
- Copper: 9.3×10⁻⁴ m² × 420 µm × 8960 kg/m³ = 3.5 g → × 385 J/kg·K = **1.35 J/K**
- FR-4 (partially coupled, use 50%): 2.75 g × 1100 J/kg·K × 0.5 = **1.5 J/K**
- 24 FETs (~0.12 g each) plus solder: **≈ 1.2 J/K**
- **C_th ≈ 4.0 J/K**
