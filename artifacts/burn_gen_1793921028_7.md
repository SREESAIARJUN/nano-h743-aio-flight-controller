# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #18): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. The ESC side uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32), each with a 3-phase gate driver and a 6-FET bridge. The H743 does not commutate motors directly. ArduPilot has no FOC/commutation path for the H7, so this split is mandatory, not a design choice.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | 40 V FETs required |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel | Duration is derived in §3 |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz | Center-aligned, complementary (sync-rect) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class | R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V; Q_g = 50 nC; Q_gd = 8 nC; Q_rr = 40 nC; C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF; R_th,jc = 1.5 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class | ~1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink, 12 V gate supply |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer 2 oz | §3 explains why a 20×20 nano cannot sustain 80 A |

**Important constraint:** "80 A burst" on a nano AIO is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state. The math below quantifies exactly how long it lasts.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (six-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)
At any commutation step, one phase is PWM'd (HS on for D, LS synchronous-rectifying for 1−D), one phase is held low (LS on), and one phase floats.

Two FETs are therefore always in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \big[ D \cdot R_{HS} + (1-D)\cdot R_{LS,sr} + R_{LS,ret} \big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This assumes matched FETs, and the expression is independent of D.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (must be solved iteratively)
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction
Phase ripple adds to conduction loss:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \quad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph,LL}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: a 2207 motor with L_LL ≈ 20 µH, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 6 V at high load, and D ≈ 0.85 gives ΔI_pp ≈ 5.3 A. The ripple correction is then +2.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². Ripple can be ignored at 80 A, but not at light load with low-inductance motors.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A
| T_j | R_ds | P per conducting FET | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 6.4 W | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.36 mΩ | 8.7 W | 17.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.48 mΩ | 9.5 W | **19.0 W** |

The design point is T_j = 125 °C, giving **P_cond ≈ 19 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase switches. Per channel, one HS/LS pair hard-switches the full I_ph.

### 2.1 V–I overlap
Approximate the transition times from the gate charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} \approx \frac{8\text{ nC}}{0.65\text{ A}} \approx 12\text{ ns},\qquad t_f \approx 12\text{ ns}$$

(The effective drive current is reduced by R_g and the Miller plateau.)

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 24\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
With t_dt = 50 ns per edge and V_f = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 40\text{nC} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

The recovery also adds turn-on overlap current of roughly Q_rr/t_r, which is already inside the margin of 2.1.

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Totals per channel at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C
| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.0 |
| Switching overlap | 1.16 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 |
| Gate (in driver) | 0.05 |
| Copper and shunt (≈0.25 mΩ path: 80² · 0.25 m) | 1.6 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 22.2 W** |
| **All 4 channels at 80 A** | **≈ 89 W** |

At 80 A, switching is only ~7% of loss. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs ~0.8 W per channel. The 48 kHz choice is justified for acoustic and current-ripple reasons and is not the thermal driver. Conduction dominates, so the lever is R_ds(on) (parallel FETs) and copper, not slew rate.

Worst single FET is the PWM high side: 9.5 + 1.16 + 0.31 ≈ **11.0 W**.
