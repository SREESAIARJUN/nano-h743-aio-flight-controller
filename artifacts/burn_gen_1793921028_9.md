# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Models and DShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** In a production 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel, or one MCU per pair, uses a dedicated ESC MCU such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to these MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. All models below follow this split.

**80A burst on a nano frame is the governing risk.** A 20×20 mm stack cannot sustain 80A per channel for more than about 1–2 s, and the thermal model in §3 shows why. The "80A" figure should be marketed and firmware-limited as a ≤1 s burst rating.

**Design assumptions** (replace these with your BOM values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full), 40 V FETs |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_25 | 1.3 mΩ (40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class) |
| R_DS(on) tempco factor @ T_j ≈ 110 °C | k_T | 1.5 → R_hot = 1.95 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (per ESC channel)

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs are active in each 60° sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):**
  - The high-side conducts for a fraction D of the period.
  - The low-side conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}
$$

Body-diode conduction during dead time is accounted for separately in §2.

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.95\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.96\ W}
$$

Copper and solder-joint losses add to this:

$$
P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical revolution, each leg is active in 4 of 6 sectors, so the loss rotates among the 6 FETs.

- Average loss per FET ≈ 25 W / 6 ≈ **4.2 W**.
- Instantaneous worst-case FET loss (the low-side in the return leg) = I²R = **12.5 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load):**
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery (low-side diode into high-side turn-on):**
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$
This term grows sharply with temperature and di/dt. It is negligible if dead time is tuned short.

**(d) C_oss charge/discharge:**
$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = (1\text{nF})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive (2 FETs switching per period).** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.
$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{nC})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 3.25 W per channel.**

**Frequency sensitivity.** Moving to 96 kHz roughly doubles the switching loss (~6.5 W) but gives smoother low-throttle current. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for this current class.

## 3. Thermal Budget

### 3.1 Total dissipation at the 80A burst

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 24.96 W | 99.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 3.25 W | 13.0 W |
| Gate drive + logic | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~31.7 W** | **~127 W** |

Efficiency at 80A, D ≈ 0.95:
- Output power ≈ 25.2 × 0.95 × 80 ≈ 1915 W per channel.
- Efficiency η ≈ 98.4%.

The percentage is high, but the absolute heat is far more than a 20×20 board can handle.

### 3.2 Junction rise (short time scale, < 100 ms)

Use the datasheet transient impedance Z_th(j-pcb). For a 3.3×3.3 DFN, a typical value is Z_th ≈ 1.5 K/W at 100 ms to 1 s.

$$
\Delta T_{j-pcb} = P_{FET,peak} \cdot Z_{th} = 12.5 \times 1.5 \approx \mathbf{19\ K}
$$

The junction-to-board path is therefore not the limit. The board's heat capacity is.

### 3.3 Board lumped heat capacity (1–10 s scale)

Assume a 20×20 mm board, 8 layers of 2 oz (70 µm) copper, 1.6 mm FR-4, plus the components.

- **Copper:** 4×10⁻⁴ m² × 8 × 70 µm × 8960 kg/m³ ≈ 2.0 g → C_cu = 2.0 × 0.385 = 0.77 J/K
- **FR-4:** 6.4×10⁻⁷ m³ × 1850 kg/m³ ≈ 1.18 g → C_fr4 ≈ 1.3 J/K
- **FETs, caps, connectors:** ≈ 0.8 J/K
- **Total:** C_th ≈ **2.9 J/K**

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{127}{2.9} \approx \mathbf{44\ K/s}
