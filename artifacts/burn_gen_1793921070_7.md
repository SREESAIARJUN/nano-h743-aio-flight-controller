## 0. System Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC uses its own per-channel (or dual) ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per channel)

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step synchronous commutation, two phases conduct at any time. In each PWM period:

- During D·T: the high-side (HS) FET of phase A and the low-side (LS) FET of phase B conduct.
- During (1−D)·T: the LS FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the LS FET of phase B conduct.

So **exactly two FETs carry the phase current I at all times**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The R_DS(on) temperature dependence is modelled as:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}
$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 110 °C:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.35\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.00135 = \mathbf{17.28\ W}
$$

This works out to **8.64 W in each of the two conducting FETs**. Over one electrical revolution, the loss is shared among 6 FETs, so the average is 17.28 / 6 ≈ **2.9 W per FET**. At low eRPM, however, a single FET sits in one commutation state long enough to see the full 8.64 W. This is the startup/stall hazard.

**Copper and trace loss.** Assume a total path resistance of 0.6 mΩ (battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad, on 2 oz × 8 layers):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.6\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

The hard-switched edge (the HS turn-on and turn-off) uses a linear V–I overlap model:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

A better estimate derives the transition time from the gate charge. With a driver source/sink of about 1 A through R_g = 4.7 Ω, the Miller plateau current is approximately (V_drv − V_plateau)/R_g ≈ (12 − 4.5)/4.7 ≈ 1.6 A, limited to about 1 A by the driver:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\ \mathrm{nC}}{1\ \mathrm{A}} = 12\ \mathrm{ns\ per\ edge}
$$

This gives about 24 ns combined, which confirms that the 20 ns assumption is optimistic. **Use P_sw ≈ 1.16 W at 24 ns.**

**Reverse recovery** of the LS body diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,000 = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, C_{oss(er)}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 1.5\,\mathrm{nF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\,000 = 0.046\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (2 actively switching FETs per channel, dissipated in the driver and R_g):

$$
P_{gate} = 2 \cdot Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\mathrm{nC}\cdot 12 \cdot 48\,000 = 0.058\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 200 ns, two dead-times per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\mathrm{ns} \cdot 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

Dead-time loss is now larger than the switching loss. **Recommendation:** trim dead time to 100 ns. This saves 0.6 W per channel and is the cheapest efficiency gain available.
