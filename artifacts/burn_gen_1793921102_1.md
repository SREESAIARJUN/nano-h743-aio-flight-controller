# Job #62: STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC
## Systems Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues need to be settled before any math, because they change the results.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware. It does not run 6-step or FOC commutation. A buildable architecture is:

- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It emits 4× bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each drives one 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

I model it this way. Fusing the ESC into the H743 would be a custom-firmware project outside ArduPilot.

**2. "80 A" on a nano footprint (20×20 or 30×30) is a burst rating, not continuous.** The math below shows it is thermally capacitance-limited to roughly 1–3 s. Continuous 80 A per motor (about 100 W of dissipation on a 9 cm² board) is not physically achievable without a heatsink and airflow.

**3. No MOSFET part number was specified.** I use a parameterized "representative 40 V, 5×6 mm" device. Replace the values in §1 with your selected part's datasheet values and re-run the equations.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology
I assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM on the active leg).

- At any instant, current flows through exactly **two legs**.
- In each leg exactly one device conducts: the high-side (HS) during on-time, or the low-side (LS) during the synchronous freewheel.
- The conduction path therefore always contains **two R_DS(on) in series**.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + k_T(T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.3 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{pk}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, so the per-device RMS current is:

$$I_{rms,FET} = I_{pk}\sqrt{1/3} = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

The 6-FET sum reproduces the expression above:

$$6 \cdot I_{rms,FET}^2 \cdot R_{ds} = 2 I_{pk}^2 R_{ds}$$

### 2.4 Numeric evaluation at T_j = 110 °C

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.81\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.81\times10^{-3} = 23.2\ \text{W per ESC}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step with complementary PWM, one leg hard-switches per commutation sector.

**(a) V-I overlap loss (hard-switched HS)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery loss.** The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance loss**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{pk} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive loss.** This is dissipated in the driver, not the FETs. Both FETs in the PWM leg switch:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 3.1 Per-ESC loss summary (80 A burst, 25.2 V, T_j = 110 °C)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.06 |
| **FET total** | **≈ 25.0** |
| Gate driver | 0.04 |

**Board total (4 ESCs): ≈ 100 W.**

**Conclusion:** at 80 A, conduction dominates (about 93%). Reducing f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.9 W per ESC. **R_DS(on) and paralleled FETs are the lever, not switching frequency.** 48 kHz remains justified for current ripple and acoustic reasons on low-inductance motors.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Per-FET dissipation
Average over an electrical revolution, which is sub-millisecond at burst RPM and much shorter than the thermal time constants:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 25.0 / 6 \approx 4.2\ \text{W}$$

The PWM-leg HS carries the extra switching loss, so the worst-case device is about 4.4 W.

### 4.2 Thermal network (per FET → board → ambient)

```
Tj ──[Rth_jc 1.2 K/W]── Tc ──[Rth_cb ~0.3 K/W (pad+vias, 2oz, ≥12 vias 0.3mm)]── T_board
T_board ──[C_board]── node,  ──[Rth_ba]── T_amb
```

**Board heat capacity.** A 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with components weighs about 7 g:

$$C_{board} \approx 4\ \text{J/K}$$

(Copper fraction: c_p = 0.385 J/g·K. FR4: about 1.1 J/g·K.)

**Board-to-ambient resistance**, using h ≈ 50 W/m²K under prop wash with both faces exposed:

$$R_{th,ba} \approx \frac{1}{h A} = \frac{1}{50 \times 2 \times 9.3\times10^{-4}} \approx 10.8\ \text{K/W}$$

### 4.3 Steady state (proves 80 A cannot be continuous)

$$\Delta T_{board,ss} = 100\ \text{W} \times 10.8\ \text{K/W} \approx 1080\ \text{K}$$

This is physically impossible, so 80 A is strictly a burst rating.
