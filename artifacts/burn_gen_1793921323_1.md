# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #8)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those MCUs drive the gate drivers and MOSFETs. Commutating four BLDC motors with sensorless BEMF timing from the H743 while it also runs EKF3 is not supported by ArduPilot and is not advisable. The model below assumes this split.

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your actual BOM values and rerun the equations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Derivation

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. At any instant two phases carry current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T. The low-side conducts the freewheel current for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts for the full period.

Summing the conduction intervals gives:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

**Duty cycle does not appear in the result.** Synchronous rectification makes conduction loss duty-independent.

R_DS(on) depends on junction temperature, which couples the loss and temperature equations:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0058\,/\text{K}$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Each MOSFET carries current for one third of the electrical cycle, so the loss is shared across all six devices:

$$P_{FET,avg} = 30.7/6 = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Copper Loss (Usually Ignored, Not Negligible)

Assume 15 mm of 2 oz copper, 3 mm wide, on two parallel layers, so R ≈ 0.6 mΩ per phase path:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0006 \approx 7.7\ \text{W per motor}$$

The board itself is a significant heater.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. There are five loss terms.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss charge (2 FETs) | C_oss·V²·f_sw = 800 pF·635·48k | 0.024 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈1.38 W per motor** |

**Total bridge loss per motor at 80 A:** 30.7 + 1.4 + 7.7 ≈ **40 W**.

**Total for four motors:** ≈ **160 W**.

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Raising to 96 kHz adds only about 1.4 W per motor, because conduction loss dominates at this current. Lower R_DS(on) is the lever that matters; f_sw is not.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady State: Not Achievable

A 20×20 mm board with prop-wash airflow has Rθ_board-ambient ≈ 4–6 K/W. At 160 W the predicted rise is ΔT ≈ 640 K, so **continuous 80 A is physically impossible on a nano board.**

Working backwards from a 70 K rise at 5 K/W, the board can dissipate about 14 W total. Since loss ≈ 2·I²·R_total per motor (≈ 3.0 mΩ hot, FET plus copper) across four motors:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\tfrac{14}{4 \times 2 \times 0.0030}} \approx \mathbf{24\ A\ per\ motor\ continuous}$$

### 3.2 Burst: Lumped Thermal Capacitance

| Component | Thermal capacitance |
|---|---|
| PCB (FR4 + copper, ~2.5 g) | ≈ 2.5 J/K |
| 24 FETs + capacitors + connectors | ≈ 0.8 J/K |
| **C_th total** | **≈ 3.3 J/K** |

The first-order model is:

$$T_{board}(t) = T_0 + P R_\theta \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_\theta C_{th} \approx 16\ \text{s}$$

For short bursts the response is nearly adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{160}{3.3} \approx 48\ \text{K/s}$$

With T_0 = 50 °C and an allowed board maximum of 110 °C, the board tolerates **all four motors at 80 A for ≈ 1.2 s**. One motor at 80 A with the others at 20 A gives roughly 3–4 s.

### 3.3 Junction Over Board

$$T_j = T_{board} + (P_{FET,avg} + P_{sw,share})\,R\theta_{jb} \approx T_{board} + 5.3 \times 2 \approx T_{board} + 11\ \text{K}$$

The worst-case transient Z_th(10 ms) adds roughly 5 K on top during commutation peaks.

### 3.4 Bus Capacitor Ripple (A Second Hard Limit)

$$I_{rms,cap} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5\,I = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor at } D = 0.5$$

Unsynchronized PWM across four motors partially cancels, but expect ≈ 30–50 A_rms total. A nano board cannot host enough MLCC/polymer capacitance for this. **Require an external low-ESR capacitor (≥1000 µF, 35 V) at the battery pads.**
