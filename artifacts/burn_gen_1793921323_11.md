### Architectural correction (important)

The STM32H743 cannot be the commutation MCU for four motors:
- Only TIM1 and TIM8 have complementary outputs with dead-time insertion. That is 2 motors' worth of 6-step or FOC drive.
- Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between a flight controller and an ESC MCU. It only makes sense if separate ESC MCUs exist.

The correct architecture, and the one modeled here:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× BiDir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                                     └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 24 MOSFETs
```

### Feasibility finding (stated before the math)

On a 20×20 mm "nano" stack, **80 A per motor is not thermally sustainable**. The model below shows about 27 W dissipation per motor (about 108 W total) at 80 A. That yields an adiabatic board heating rate of about 70 K/s, so the burst limit is **about 0.5–0.8 s**. If "80 A burst" means 80 A total across four motors (20 A/motor), the design is comfortable. Both cases are computed. **Clarify which one the spec means.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in complementary 6-step

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct and the third floats:
- The **PWM leg** alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and low-side FET (for 1−D).
- The **return leg** low-side FET carries current continuously.

So at any instant, **exactly two R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current**, independent of duty. That gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5. For a typical 5" motor (L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\text{ A} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 3.6\ \text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against 80² = 6400 A². So I_rms ≈ I_DC.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on), solved self-consistently

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

T_j depends on loss, and loss depends on T_j, so the system needs iteration. Start at T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.2\,m\Omega\,(1 + 0.0065 \times 100) = 1.98\ m\Omega$$

### 1.4 Results

| Case | I/motor | P per conducting FET | P_cond/motor | P_cond × 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 80 A per motor | 80 A | 80² × 1.98 mΩ = 12.7 W | **25.3 W** | **101 W** |
| B: 80 A total | 20 A | 20² × 1.6 mΩ* = 0.64 W | 1.28 W | 5.1 W |

\*Case B converges near T_j ≈ 80 °C, giving R ≈ 1.63 mΩ.

**Hot-spot note:** the return-leg low-side FET conducts for a full 120° of each electrical cycle. The two FETs of the PWM leg share their 120° window. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time, so:
- Per-FET average: 12.7 W / 3 ≈ **4.2 W** (Case A)
- Per-FET instantaneous peak: 12.7 W, lasting one sector

At high eRPM (sector ≪ thermal time constant), the per-FET average governs. At low eRPM or stall, it does not: a stalled rotor parks the full 12.7 W on single devices.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.
- The high-side FET turns on against the conducting low-side body diode: a hard edge.
- The low-side FET turns on after dead time, with its body diode already conducting: ZVS, so its V·I overlap loss is ≈ 0.
