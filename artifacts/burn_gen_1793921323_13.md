# Job #22: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst)
## Systems Architecture, Loss Model, DMA Plan, Netlists, Thermal Budget

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (one per motor) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 does not drive gates directly; ArduPilot has no motor-commutation code.

**Key findings:**

1. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** On a 30×30 mm 4-in-1 board, the model below gives about 3 s at 80 A per channel (all four channels loaded) before T_j reaches 125 °C. Sustainable continuous current is about 40 A per channel with prop-wash cooling.
2. **Conduction loss dominates.** It accounts for about 90% of loss. Paralleling FETs or lowering R_DS(on) is the only lever that meaningfully moves the 80 A number.
3. **The bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan is feasible** on a single timer (TIM3, CH1–CH4) with H7 DMAMUX.
4. **"Nano" form factor conflict.** If "nano" means 20×20 mm, 80 A burst is not credible: copper cross-section and thermal mass are roughly halved. The model scales accordingly (Section 3.6).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

In any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd, phase B is held low, and phase C floats. The current path depends on the PWM state:

- **ON state:** current flows through Q_AH, then the motor, then Q_BL. Two FETs conduct.
- **OFF state (synchronous freewheel):** current flows through Q_AL, then the motor, then Q_BL. Two FETs conduct.

The motor inductance holds current approximately constant over one PWM period (20.8 µs). Therefore, **exactly two FETs carry I at every instant, independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Here α ≈ 0.0065 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.55 at 110 °C.

### 1.2 Numbers

$$R_{hot} = 0.70 \times 1.55 = 1.085\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.085\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and Shunt Loss
These figures are per channel, for the current path from the bus to the motor pad and back.

| Element | R | P = I²R |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace and pads (2 oz, 4 parallel layers) | 0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| Bus planes (share per channel) | 0.10 mΩ | 0.64 W |
| Low-side shunt (0.25 mΩ, conducts in low-side path) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W × duty-weighted ≈ 1.0 W |
| **Copper/shunt subtotal** | | **≈ 2.6 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each FET carries current for roughly one-third of the time. At burst eRPM (more than 1 kHz electrical), the conduction is thermally averaged:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\times 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}... \approx \frac{13.9}{6} \approx 2.3\ \text{W average},\quad 6.95\ \text{W instantaneous}$$

**Low-RPM caveat.** At very low eRPM (stall or desync), a single FET carries the full 6.95 W for milliseconds. This is the true worst case for local junction temperature (see Section 3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the held-low leg is static. Per channel, one hard-switched FET pair (high side plus its synchronous partner) commutates twice per period.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap)

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = 8\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}$$
