# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #20): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

Two architectural points must be fixed before any numbers mean anything.

**The H743 does not drive the gates.** In this class of stack:
- The **STM32H743** is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC.
- Each **ESC channel** has its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

So the loss model below belongs to the ESC side, and the DMA/timer allocation belongs to the FC side.

**No MOSFET part number was specified.** Every result below is parameterised. The worked numbers use a representative 40 V-class N-channel FET suited to 6S:

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.005 /°C |
| Switching charge (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) | Q_sw | 10 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF |
| Reverse-recovery charge at 80 A | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Effective gate drive current | I_g | ≈ 1 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Burst current per channel | I | 80 A |

> **Design-review flag:** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is not thermally sustainable. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a **time-limited burst rating** and derive the allowable burst duration from it, rather than claiming it as continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
The motor is driven by 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In any 60° sector:
- Phase A high-side (Q_AH) switches at duty D.
- Its complement Q_AL conducts during (1 − D), carrying the freewheel current synchronously.
- Phase B low-side (Q_BL) is held fully ON for the whole sector.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly **two** channel resistances.

### 1.2 Instantaneous conduction power per ESC channel

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{on}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{on}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{on}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle because synchronous rectification is used.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

$$R_{on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.4 Parasitic series losses

$$P_{series} = I^2\,(R_{shunt} + R_{Cu,trace} + R_{conn})$$

### 1.5 Worked numbers at 80 A

- R_on(110 °C) = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **2.14 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 2.14 mΩ = **27.4 W per channel**
- Shunt at 0.25 mΩ: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**
- 2 oz copper trace, about 0.3 mΩ effective: about **1.9 W**

**Per-channel conduction plus series loss ≈ 30.9 W.**

### 1.6 Per-FET distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each of the six FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the time (high-side or low-side role). The average per FET is about 27.4 / 6 × (2 roles per sector, normalised) ≈ **4.6 W**.

The **peak** instantaneous dissipation, about 13.7 W, is what drives junction ripple at low eRPM. At very low eRPM the sector dwell exceeds the FET's thermal time constant, so design to the peak in that regime.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With synchronous rectification, only the PWM-ing high-side hard-switches. The low-side commutates softly through the dead-time diode, but its body-diode reverse recovery is charged to the high-side.

### 2.1 Voltage–current overlap

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = 10\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\text{ W}$$
