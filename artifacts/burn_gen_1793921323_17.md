# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #40): Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Findings

### Architectural clarification
The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses its own commutation MCU. This design specifies 4× AT32F421 running AM32, which supports native bidirectional DShot, a 48 kHz PWM option, and EDT telemetry. The H743 drives the ESCs over DShot600 and reads eRPM back on the same wire.

### Findings that must be resolved at architecture review
1. **Current rating basis.** "80 A burst" must be defined as phase current per motor. At 100% duty, four motors at 80 A draws 320 A from the battery. No XT60 or nano-class wiring survives that. Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four channels at once is a ~1–2 s event at most.
2. **Board size.** A 20×20 mm ESC cannot sustain this. The physics is thermal-capacity limited, not R_DS(on) limited. A 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 mm layout raises the burst time roughly in proportion to copper mass.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous rectification topology
At any instant in trapezoidal (6-step) commutation, two phases conduct:
- The high-side phase is PWM-switched at duty D.
- The low-side phase is held on.

With complementary (synchronous) PWM, current flows during the PWM-off time through the low-side FET of the switching leg rather than its body diode. The current path therefore always passes through exactly two MOSFET channels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(100^\circ\text{C}) = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Copper path resistance
The current path is battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, \ell}{w\, t\, n_{layers}}$$

Inputs: ℓ ≈ 12 mm effective, w = 4 mm, t = 70 µm, 4 parallel layers, ρ(100 °C) = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.012}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} = 0.24\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.24 mΩ | 1.5 W |
| **Conduction per channel** | | **20.7 W** |

Per-FET average: each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so its average is approximately 19.2 / 6 × 2 ≈ 6.4 W peak-weighted, or about 3.2 W true average when PWM sharing is included. At high eRPM (1–3 kHz electrical), the FET thermal time constant (~ms) averages this out.
