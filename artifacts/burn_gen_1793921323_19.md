# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

**Flag 1: The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any shipping "H743 + 4-in-1" design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421). Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs. This document models the system that way.

**Flag 2: "80A burst" on a nano-format board is a short-duration rating, not a continuous one.**
- 4 × 80A = **320A from the battery**. That exceeds the connector (XT60 ≈ 60A continuous), typical 6S packs, and a 20×20/25×25 board's copper.
- The thermal model below shows the stage survives about **1–2 s** at 80A per motor from a warm start, and not much longer.
- I model "80A burst" as **per-motor phase-current capability**. The realistic all-motor simultaneous case is much lower.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any sector, one phase is PWM'd (high side, HS, with complementary low side, LS), one phase is held LS-on, and one floats.

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS_a, then the winding, then LS_b. That is 2 FETs in series.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current flows through LS_a (sync rectification), then the winding, then LS_b. That is again 2 FETs in series.

Phase current therefore always flows through exactly two channel resistances, independent of D (excluding dead time, covered in Section 2):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

### 1.2 Worst-case numbers at 80A

R_ds(on) at 125°C, using the maximum datasheet value: 1.5mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Using typical R_ds(on) (1.2mΩ × 1.6 = 1.92mΩ) gives 24.6W per motor.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle sourcing, 1/3 sinking, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | Conduction share of electrical cycle |
|---|---|
| HS_x | (1/3)·D |
| LS_x | (1/3)·(1−D) from sync rectification + (1/3) as the return leg |

At full throttle (D → 1), the conduction losses are about equal across all six FETs:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 2.4\text{m}}{3} = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

At mid-throttle (D = 0.5), each LS FET carries 1/2 of the cycle and each HS FET carries 1/6. The LS FET is the hot device: 6400 × 2.4m × 0.5 = **7.7W worst-case per LS FET**. In practice, 80A does not occur at D = 0.5 except during hard acceleration from low RPM, which is a real transient case.
