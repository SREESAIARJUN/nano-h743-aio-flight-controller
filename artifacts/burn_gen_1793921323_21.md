# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no access to the Job #31 BOM, stackup, or mechanical drawings. Every number below comes from the stated assumptions. **Swap in your datasheet values and the model recomputes directly.** Treat the netlist as a reference topology for schematic capture. It is not a release-ready file.

One architectural point first. In a 4-in-1 stack, the **STM32H743 runs ArduPilot (FC side)**. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 handles only the DShot link and telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, current flows from the positive rail, through the motor, to ground.

- The **PWM'd leg** carries current in its high-side FET for fraction D. It carries current in its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for fraction (1−D).
- The **sinking leg** low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**. That makes it a convenient worst-case bound.

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.007\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.6 \text{ at } 110^\circ\text{C}
$$

**Numbers per ESC channel:**

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot (0.70\text{ m}\Omega \cdot 1.6) = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.12\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.3\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,PCB} = I^2 R_{pcb}\cdot k_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.6\text{ m}\Omega \cdot 1.33 \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W}
$$

Here k_Cu = 1 + 0.0039·(110−25) ≈ 1.33.

### 1.2 FOC / sinusoidal variant (if firmware supports it)

$$
P_{cond} = 3\,I_{rms,ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms,ph} = I_{pk}/\sqrt{2}
$$

### 1.3 Thermal–electrical coupling

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Iterate until the values stop changing:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\,P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})
$$

The iteration usually converges in 3–4 steps. If it diverges, you have **thermal runaway**, and the design point is invalid.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The sinking leg is static within the sector.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_F·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver IC, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·60 nC·10·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching (per channel)** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Key observations:**

- At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by roughly 6:1. Going from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.1 W per channel. That is a reasonable trade for lower current ripple and quieter motors.
- Dead-time loss is about 30% of switching loss. Trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns (if driver propagation-delay matching allows) recovers about 0.3 W per channel.
- Edge rate is limited by gate resistance and loop inductance, not by the FET. Keep the hot loop (bulk cap → HS → LS → GND) below about 1.5 nH, or overshoot will exceed the 40 V rating at 80 A di/dt.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 14.3 W | 57.2 W |
| PCB copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Switching (FET) | 2.1 W | 8.4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if present) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **~23 W** | **~92 W** |

At 80 A × 25.2 V × ~0.85 duty, each channel delivers about 1.7 kW. Efficiency is about 98.7%. Electrically the design is fine; **thermally it is not continuous-capable.**
