# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture (Job #1)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Upfront Caution

**Architecture.** ArduPilot on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. The correct architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 on AT32F421 / STM32G071)
                                              └─► 3-phase driver ─► 6× MOSFET per channel
```

All derivations below follow this split.

**80A burst on a nano board.** The math below shows that 80A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is only achievable as a short burst on one or two channels at a time. Four channels at 80A simultaneously on 6S is about 290A of bus current and about 7 kW, which no nano-format PCB or connector can carry. I keep the 80A figure as a burst specification and derive the realistic continuous rating from it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant: one high-side position and one low-side position.

- During the PWM on-time (D), current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the off-time (1−D), it flows through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Either way, the current crosses two conducting R_ds(on) positions:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \frac{R_{ds(on),25}\,k_T}{n_p}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 \frac{R_{ds(on),25}\,k_T}{n_p}
= 2\,I_{ph}^2\,\frac{R_{ds(on),25}\,k_T}{n_p}
$$

During dead time, the R_ds(on) term is replaced by the body diode. That loss is accounted for in Section 2.

### 1.2 Temperature coupling
R_ds(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/\text{K}
$$

This creates a feedback loop: T_j = T_board + P·Z_θ. Solve it iteratively. Thermal runaway occurs if the loop gain 2I²R₂₅·α·R_θ ≥ 1. At 80A with a 4 K/W effective resistance, the loop gain is 0.34 per channel. That is stable but strongly amplifying.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A, k_T = 1.5 (R = 1.65 mΩ)

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.65\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

This is about 10.6 W per conducting FET.

### 1.4 Copper loss
Assume a phase path of 0.4 mΩ (2 oz, short pours) and a shared bus path of 0.3 mΩ.

- Phase copper: 80² × 0.4 mΩ × 2 = 5.1 W per channel. Copper matters as much as the FETs on nano boards.
- Bus current: I_bus ≈ D·I_ph ≈ 72 A per channel at D = 0.9.
