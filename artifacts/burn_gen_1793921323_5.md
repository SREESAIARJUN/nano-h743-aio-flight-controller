## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own motor MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 and GD32E230. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600.

This document models both halves:

- **ESC side:** power stage and thermal model (Sections 1–3).
- **FC side:** DShot timing, timer and DMA allocation (Section 4).

### 0.2 Form-Factor Warning

80 A per motor on a true 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible. Section 3 shows why. I size the power stage for a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern (~36×36 mm board)** and treat 80 A as a **≤5 s burst**. If the mechanical envelope really is 20×20, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

Six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM is used, as in AM32 and BLHeli_32. At any instant two phases carry current. During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B. During the off-time it freewheels through the low side of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low side of phase B.

Therefore exactly **two switch positions conduct I_ph at all times**, independent of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Equations

Effective resistance per switch position:

$$R_{sw} = \frac{k_T \, R_{ds(on),25}}{N_p} = \frac{1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega}{2} = 0.75\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Copper path resistance (2 oz inner layers plus phase pad) is estimated at R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per motor current loop.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{sw} + R_{cu}) = 80^2\,(1.5 + 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{11.5\,W}$$

Of this, 9.6 W is in the MOSFETs and 1.9 W is in copper. Each of the 4 active devices dissipates about 2.4 W of conduction loss.

### 1.3 Temperature Coupling

R_ds(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\text{/K}$$

At 110 °C this gives k_T ≈ 1.51, which matches the assumption. Section 3.4 checks that the converged T_j is consistent.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. The worst case is D ≈ 0.9 at full current, because at D = 1 there are no switching events.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap (V–I crossover)

Transition time per edge, with gate charge doubled by paralleling:

$$t_{tr} \approx \frac{N_p\,Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{2 \times 10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 20\,\text{ns} \quad (t_r \approx t_f)$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\,W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time. It recovers when the high side turns on.

$$P_{rr} = N_p\,Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 100\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.12\,W}$$

The induced extra turn-on loss adds about 30–50 % of P_rr to the high side.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx N_p\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 120\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.15\,W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time windows per PWM period.

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\,W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing t_dead to about 100 ns, which the driver propagation matching must allow, halves it.
