# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Verdict

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC contains four (or one quad-core-equivalent) dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to these MCUs. The model below follows that split.

**Feasibility verdict.** "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is physically limited by heat capacity, not by MOSFET ratings. Section 3 shows that four channels at 80A cause the whole board to rise about 40 K per second, even with ideal heat spreading. 80A is only defensible as:
- a single-channel, burst of 1 s or less, at 25 °C ambient with prop airflow, or
- a move to 30×30 mm.

I'll give the math so you can choose.

All component values are **assumed design parameters**. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values before release.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In any commutation sector, current flows through two legs:
- the **PWM leg**, where current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D), and
- the **return leg**, where one FET conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Duty cycle cancels out with synchronous rectification. Worst-case single-FET loss is the return-leg FET:
- 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W** while it conducts.
- Each FET serves as the return leg for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average is 9.6/3 ≈ 3.2 W, plus its PWM-leg share.

**Copper and connection losses** (add separately; they are often overlooked):
- Each pad, via, and trace path is roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ.
- At 0.4 mΩ: 2 × 80² × 0.4 mΩ ≈ **5.1 W per channel**.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

**V-I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver, not the FETs), two FETs switching:
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.33\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.2 W, but current ripple rises. With low-inductance racing motors (L ≈ 15–30 µH), 48 kHz is a reasonable choice.

### 2.3 Bus-Capacitor and Shunt Losses

**Bulk capacitor RMS current** (single channel, D = 0.5):
$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\ \text{A}$$

- With effective ESR of 5 mΩ: ≈ 8 W, split between electrolytic and ceramic capacitors.
- This is why low-ESR polymer plus ≥10 × 10 µF X7R ceramics at the bridge are mandatory.
- Phase-interleaving the four channels reduces this, but DShot cannot enforce it, because each ESC runs its own PWM clock.

**Shunt** (0.25 mΩ, shared): at 4 × 80 A = 320 A, P = 25.6 W. This is unrealistic for a nano board. Use the total-current rating, not 4× channel current.

### 2.4 Totals

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper/via | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Switching | 2.3 W | 9.3 W |
| **Board total** | **~26.6 W** | **~106 W** |

**Efficiency check, one channel.** Output power ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D = 0.8 that is about 1.6 kW, so 26.6 W of loss gives roughly 98.4% bridge efficiency. Efficiency is fine; heat *density* is the problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Lumped Board Heat Capacity (20×20 mm, 1.6 mm, 8-Layer, 2 oz)

| Component | Volume / mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 0.64 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 1.18 g, × 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| Copper (8 layers × 70 µm × 4 cm²) | 0.224 cm³ → 2.0 g, × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.77 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | — | ≈ 0.3 J/K |
| **C_th,board** | | **≈ 2.4 J/K** |

### 3.2 Adiabatic Burst Rise (Upper Bound for Short Bursts)

$$\Delta T = \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}}$$

| Scenario | P | 1 s | 2 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A | 106 W | +44 K | +88 K |
| 1 × 80 A, others at 20 A | ≈ 33 W | +14 K | +28 K |

Board-level temperature is the *floor*. FET junctions sit higher, as shown next.

### 3.3 Junction Rise Above Board (Transient Thermal Impedance)

For a PDFN3.3×3 FET at a 1 s pulse, Z_th(j-pcb) is roughly 1.5 K/W (read yours from the datasheet Z_th curve). For the return-leg FET:

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} = 9.6\ \text{W} \times 1.5 \approx 14\ \text{K}$$

Hot-spot spreading resistance in the local copper adds another 10–20 K/W·cm² effect, so a realistic local adder is about **+25 K**.

### 3.4 Steady State (Continuous Rating)

Assume R_th(board→ambient) ≈ 8 K/W with prop wash (about 4 m/s over 8 cm² of wetted area, h ≈ 60 W/m²K, plus the frame conduction path).

For a T_j,max target of 125 °C at 35 °C ambient, with a 25 K junction adder:

$$P_{cont} \le \frac{125 - 35 - 25}{8} \approx 8\ \text{W}$$

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{8/4}{2 \times 1.9\,\text{m}\Omega}} \approx \mathbf{23\ A\ per\ channel\ (all\ four)}$$

The 1.9 mΩ is FET plus copper resistance.
