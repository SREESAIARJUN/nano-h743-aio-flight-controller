# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack: Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlist

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

No MOSFET, driver, or board outline was specified, so I used representative parameters. Every number below is parametric: substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations. Treat this as an architecture baseline, not a released design.

**Critical warnings:**

1. **80 A × 4 cannot be sustained on a nano board.** About 87 W of steady dissipation on a ~45 × 45 mm PCB gives a steady-state rise above 200 K (see §3). 80 A is only viable as a **time-limited burst** of a few seconds, or on a single motor. The rating must be enforced in ESC firmware with a thermal estimator, not just printed on the box.
2. **The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.** ArduPilot on the H743 only emits DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. The partitioning is:
   `H743 (ArduPilot) → bidirectional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) → 3-phase gate driver → 6 MOSFETs per motor (24 total)`

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through **exactly two MOSFETs**:

- **PWM on-interval:** HS of the PWM leg plus LS of the return leg.
- **PWM off-interval:** LS of the PWM leg (synchronous freewheel) plus LS of the return leg.

So the conduction loss per ESC is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp,max} = \frac{V_{bus}}{4\,L_{LL}\,f_{sw}}$$
