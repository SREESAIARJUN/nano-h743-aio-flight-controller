# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #61): Power-Stage and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Several requirements are ambiguous or physically aggressive, so I'm stating them before the math.

**Topology.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors: a 480 MHz H7 is the wrong device for a 48 kHz six-step loop × 4. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. All analysis below assumes this AIO stack.

**Electrical operating point.** 6S (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal). The 80 A is a burst *phase/motor* current per channel, for ≤ 5 s.

**Feasibility flags:**
1. Four channels at 80 A means up to **~320 A of battery current** at high duty. A "nano" board with a single XT60 cannot carry this. Even XT90 plus 10 AWG leads is marginal. The burst rating is only credible as a *per-channel* rating with a non-simultaneous total.
2. Steady-state dissipation at 4 × 80 A is ~85 W. That is a **transient, thermally-capacity-limited rating**, not a continuous one. Section 2.5 quantifies the burst window.
3. MOSFET parameters below are representative values for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm trench FET. **Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values.** The equations are parameterized for that purpose.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active:
- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- During the PWM off-time, it freewheels through LS(phase A), the synchronous rectifier, → LS(phase B).

So the motor current **always flows through exactly two FETs in series**, regardless of duty D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Including phase-current ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–7" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, so 2L ≈ 30 µH), at D = 0.9, V_bus = 25.2 V, and V_bemf ≈ 20 V:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{5.2 \times 0.9}{30\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} \approx 3.25\,A \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\approx 0.9\,A^2 \;(\text{negligible vs. } 6400\,A^2)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on): coupled solution

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta J\text{-}PCB}$$

Solve iteratively. At a target T_j ≈ 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0 \times (1+0.0045\times100) = 1.45\,m\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\,W}$$

### 1.3 Distribution per FET

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The two series slots are shared among 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{18.6}{6} \approx 3.1\,W$$

The instantaneous conduction loss while a FET is on is I²R ≈ 9.3 W. At burst eRPM (typically > 1 kHz electrical), the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) averages this out, so the average value is the right one to use.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 × 0.15 mΩ, 2 oz, 8-layer stitched) | 0.30 mΩ | 80 A | 1.9 W/ch |
| Bus-plane spreading (per channel share) | 0.10 mΩ | ~72 A (D·I) | 0.5 W/ch |
| Battery current shunt (0.1 mΩ, shared) | 0.10 mΩ | up to 320 A | **10.2 W total** |

**Recommendation:** Replace the shunt with a Hall-effect sensor (e.g., a TMCS/ACS-class part) or lower it to 0.05 mΩ. A 10 W shunt on a nano board is a hotspot.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching phase leg (HS + complementary LS) switches at 48 kHz. The other active leg is static for the 60° sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS FET)

The transition time is governed by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

With V_plateau ≈ 3.5 V and R_g total = 3.3 Ω:

$$I_g \approx 2.0\,A, \qquad t_r \approx t_f \approx 6\,ns$$
