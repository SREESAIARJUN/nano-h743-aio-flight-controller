## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, for example:

- AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or
- a BLHeli_32 / BlueJay-class target.

The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This split is assumed throughout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 135 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.1 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 110) = 1.64\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phase legs carry the phase current I. The two conduction states alternate:

- **PWM-on interval (fraction D):** high-side FET (HS) of leg A plus low-side FET (LS) of leg B.
- **PWM-off interval (fraction 1−D):** the freewheel path is LS_A (synchronous) plus LS_B.

In both states, current flows through exactly two channels in series. The total conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, this loss is shared across six FETs. The per-FET average is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

The per-FET split is duty-dependent:

- HS: $P = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R$
- LS: $P = \tfrac{1}{3}(2 - D)\, I^2 R$ (the low-side conducts both its own sector and the freewheel)

The low-side FETs are therefore the thermal hot spots at partial throttle.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | P per FET (avg) | Worst LS at D = 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.10 mΩ | 14.1 W | 2.35 W | 3.5 W |
| 135 °C | 1.64 mΩ | **21.0 W** | 3.5 W | **5.25 W** |

The worst-LS figure at 135 °C is 0.5 × 6400 × 1.64 mΩ = 5.25 W.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

These are added in series with the FET path:

- Shunt: 0.5 mΩ at 80 A gives 3.2 W per channel. This is significant; consider 0.25 mΩ or low-side R_DS sensing.
- Board and phase-pad copper: about 0.3 mΩ total at 80 A gives 1.9 W per channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. One hard turn-on and one turn-off occur per PWM period.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss charge/discharge | ½·C_oss,tr·V²·f_sw | ½·2 nF·635·48 k = **0.030 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·40 nC·10·48 k = 0.038 W (mostly in the driver) |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 1.87 W** |

**Interpretation:** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8% of the total. Conduction dominates. Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 0.9 W per channel but increases current ripple and acoustic noise. The better levers for burst capability are lower R_DS(on) and lower shunt resistance.

**Ripple check:** Motor L ≈ 15 µH (typical 5" class motor, line-to-line). The worst-case ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 8.75\ \text{A}$$

This is acceptable.
