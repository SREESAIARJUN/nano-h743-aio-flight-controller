# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #56)

**Assumptions:** I have no prior project files for Job #56. All component parameters below are representative values for the device class and must be checked against the selected datasheets. The equations are written so you can substitute real numbers.

---

## 0. Architectural Reality Check

Four problems need resolving before layout. They change the thermal model more than any equation does.

| Issue | Consequence | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| The H743 cannot run ArduPilot and also commutate four motors at 48 kHz | Missed commutation means desync and FET shoot-through risk | Use 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them. |
| 80 A × 4 = **320 A** of battery current on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) board | No battery connector or pad survives this. The board heats at about 19 K/s (see §3). | Treat 80 A as a **per-channel, single-motor, ≤1–2 s** burst rating. Specify around 45 A continuous per channel. Use a 30.5×30.5 board if the 80 A spec is firm. |
| 48 kHz PWM | Switching loss is about 1.6× that at 30 kHz | Acceptable. The model below quantifies it. |
| The FC and the ESC share ground at high di/dt | IMU noise, DShot corruption | Use a star ground, a dedicated FC-ESC ground pin, and an RC filter on DShot lines (§5). |

## 2. Loss Model (per channel, I = 80 A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The motor is driven trapezoidally with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phases carry I_ph. On the PWM leg, the high side conducts for D and the complementary low side for (1−D). Summed over the period, current always flows through exactly two channel resistances:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on),25}\,\alpha \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu} \;+\; I^2 R_{shunt}$$

**MOSFET term:**
2 × 80² × 0.80 mΩ × 1.6 = 2 × 6400 × 1.28 mΩ = **16.4 W**

**Copper term:** use 2 oz copper on 4 parallel layers (outer 2 oz, inner 1 oz).
- Effective sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ ÷ 3 ≈ 0.083 mΩ/□.
- The battery → FET → phase pad → FET → GND path is about 4 squares, so R_Cu ≈ 0.33 mΩ at 25 °C, or 0.42 mΩ at 100 °C.
- 6400 × 0.42 mΩ = **2.7 W**

**Shunt term:** 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. This is why many ESCs at this class use a bus shunt with a ×20 amplifier, or sense across R_ds.

**P_cond ≈ 20.7 W**

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg)

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

Edge-time derivation: t ≈ Q_gd·R_total / (V_drv − V_plateau) = 10 nC × 6 Ω / (10 − 4.5) V ≈ 11 ns. Add Q_gs2 and ringing and you get about 15 ns.

### 2.3 Body-Diode, Reverse Recovery, Coss, and Gate Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2 t_dead · f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| Coss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver and R_g) | 6 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw / 6 commutation share ≈ 2 FETs switching | ≈ 0.06 W in FETs, 0.12 W in driver |

**P_sw,total ≈ 2.2 W.** Switching is about 10% of the loss at 80 A. Conduction dominates. The 48 kHz choice is thermally cheap.

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals

| Condition | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst, hot | **≈ 22.9 W** | **≈ 92 W** |
| 45 A continuous, T_j ≈ 100 °C (α=1.45) | 2·2025·1.16 mΩ + Cu/shunt 1.3 + sw 1.3 ≈ **7.3 W** | ≈ 29 W |

Efficiency at the 80 A burst: 22.9 / (25.2 × 80 × D_eff). At full throttle that is about 1.1% of 2 kW. Electrically fine; the problem is purely thermal.

### 2.5 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; 40\,A_{rms}\ \text{per channel at } D=0.5$$

- Channels are not phase-interleaved, so the worst case is about 80 A_rms.
- Per channel, specify ≥ 8× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 (about 3 A each after DC-bias derating), placed within 3 mm of the high-side drains.
- Add one shared 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic on the battery leads, off-board.
