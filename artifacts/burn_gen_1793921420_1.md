## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs $p=7$), $R_s \approx 5\,\Omega$, $L_s \approx 2\,\text{mH}$ |
| Rotor encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A/MT6701 class), per axis |
| Driver | 3-phase, center-aligned PWM at 20 kHz, 2- or 3-shunt current sensing |
| IMU A | ICM-42688-P on the camera plate (primary) |
| IMU B | ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, mounted with a known lever arm $r_B$ |
| Optional third IMU | On the base, used for feedforward only |
| Axis order | Yaw (outer) → Roll → Pitch (inner, carries camera) |

The design uses five nested loops. Each runs at a rate set by its bandwidth, with a separation of at least 5× between adjacent loops.

```
             1 kHz                    4 kHz                     20 kHz
 θ_ref ──►[Angle P/PI]──ω_ref──►[Velocity PI + FF]──iq_ref──►[Current PI dq]──►SVPWM──►Motor
             ▲                        ▲          ▲                 ▲
     attitude (Mahony,       gyro rate (fused,   cogging FF      ia,ib,ic + θe
     dual IMU, 1 kHz)        notched, 8 kHz)     τ_cog(θm)       (encoder)
```

Two design choices drive this structure:

- **Velocity feedback comes from the gyro, not the encoder.** Gyro rate is an inertial measurement, so the gimbal stabilizes against base motion. The encoder is used for commutation, cogging lookup, and joint-limit handling.
- **The angle error is computed in the inertial frame**, then mapped to joint space through the gimbal Jacobian (§4.3).

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Current Loop, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle, with $\theta_{m}$ the mechanical angle, $\theta_{0}$ the encoder offset found at calibration, and $p$ the number of pole pairs:

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_0) \bmod 2\pi$$

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, using $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\,(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

**Park transform:**

$$\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e$$

### 1.2 Plant Model and Decoupling

For a surface PMSM, where $\omega_e = p\,\omega_m$ and $\lambda$ is the PM flux linkage:

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q$$

$$v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda$$

Torque is given by:

$$\tau = \tfrac32 p\lambda\, i_q = K_t\, i_q$$

The decoupling feedforward makes each axis a first-order $RL$ plant:

$$v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda$$

### 1.3 PI Tuning by Pole-Zero Cancellation

The plant is $G(s)=1/(Ls+R)$. Choose the PI zero to cancel the plant pole:

$$C(s)=K_p+\frac{K_i}{s},\qquad K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c$$

The closed loop then becomes $\dfrac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}$. Set the bandwidth with:

$$\omega_c \le \frac{2\pi f_{PWM}}{10\text{–}20}$$

At 20 kHz this gives $\omega_c \approx 2\pi\cdot 1.5\,\text{kHz}$.

### 1.4 Discrete PI with Back-Calculation Anti-Windup

The sample period is $T_s$. The integrator update is:

$$u_k^{*} = K_p e_k + I_k$$

$$u_k = \text{sat}(u_k^{*})$$

$$I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw}(u_k - u_k^{*})$$

A common choice is $K_{aw} = 1/K_p \cdot K_i T_s$ scaled to 1, or simply $K_{aw}=K_i T_s/K_p$.
