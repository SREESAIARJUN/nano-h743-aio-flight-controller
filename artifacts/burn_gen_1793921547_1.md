## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A/MT6701 class) |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, FPU, center-aligned PWM |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI, ODR 8 kHz, placed symmetrically about the plate's center |

**Loop rates.** Each loop runs well inside the bandwidth of the loop beneath it (≥ 4–10× separation).

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | f_c = 20 kHz (PWM ISR) | ω_ci ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz |
| Velocity | f_v = 4 kHz (every 5 PWM cycles) | ω_v ≈ 2π·150 Hz |
| Attitude/angle | f_θ = 1 kHz | ω_θ ≈ 2π·30 Hz |
| IMU acquire + filtering | 8 kHz (data-ready) | — |
| Fusion (AHRS) | 1 kHz | — |

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms
Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform, with electrical angle θ_e = p·(θ_m − θ_offset):

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

**Delay compensation.** PWM and computation add roughly 1.5 sample periods of delay. Compensate by advancing the angle used for the inverse Park:

$$
\theta_e^{out} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_c
$$

### 1.2 Plant model and PI design
The dq-frame motor equations (SPM motor, L_d = L_q = L) are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

Design the PI by pole-zero cancellation. This makes the closed loop first order with bandwidth ω_ci:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci},\qquad \omega_{ci} \le \frac{2\pi f_c}{10}
$$

Add decoupling and back-EMF feedforward:

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(e_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = PI_q(e_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Voltage limiting.** Limit to the linear SVPWM range, $|v_{dq}| \le V_{bus}/\sqrt3$. Prioritize d-axis voltage:

$$
v_q^{max}=\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}
$$

### 1.3 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup
At each sample k:

$$
u_k = K_p e_k + I_k,\qquad \bar u_k = \text{sat}(u_k)
$$

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T e_k + K_{aw}(\bar u_k - u_k),\qquad K_{aw}\approx \frac{K_i}{K_p}T
$$

### 1.4 SVPWM by min-max (zero-sequence) injection
1. Compute the phase voltages $v_a, v_b, v_c$ from $v_\alpha, v_\beta$.
2. Compute the offset $v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max + \min)$.
3. Compute duties $d_x = \tfrac12 + (v_x + v_0)/V_{bus}$.

This is mathematically equivalent to sector-based SVPWM.

### 1.5 Speed and angle observer (PLL)
Differentiating the encoder is too noisy, so use a PLL. With the wrapped angle error $\epsilon = \text{wrap}(\theta_m - \hat\theta)$:

$$
\hat\omega \leftarrow \hat\omega + K_{i}T\epsilon,\qquad
\hat\theta \leftarrow \hat\theta + T(\hat\omega + K_p\epsilon)
$$

$$
K_p = 2\zeta\omega_n,\quad K_i=\omega_n^2,\quad \zeta = 0.8,\ \omega_n \approx 2\pi\cdot 300\text{ Hz}
$$
