## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC) MCU**. It is not the commutation controller. Each of the four ESC channels uses its own Cortex-M4/M0+ class ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document assumes the AT32F421K8U7, which has 6-step or sinusoidal capability and a hardware comparator for BEMF sensing. The H743 drives four bidirectional DShot600 lines and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wires.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_DS(on),25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0055 /K for modern trench 40 V silicon.

- At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ** (use 1.5 mΩ)
- At T_j = 125 °C: R = **1.55 mΩ**

Add parasitic PCB and package resistance for each switch path:

- Leadframe/bond: ~0.15 mΩ
- Copper spreading, 2 oz × 3 layers paralleled: ~0.2 mΩ per path

So the effective resistance is R_eff ≈ 1.5 + 0.35 = **1.85 mΩ** per switch position.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) with complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases carry the full phase current I_ph:

- **Active leg (PWM'd):** high side conducts for D·T_s; low side (synchronous freewheel) conducts for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Return leg:** low side is on for the entire 60° sector.

Including inductor ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak), the RMS of a triangular-on-DC waveform is:

I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI²/12

The conduction loss per channel is:

P_cond = I_rms² · R_eff · [D + (1−D)] + I_rms² · R_eff = **2 · I_rms² · R_eff**

The total is independent of D because synchronous rectification shares the loss between the active leg's two switches.

### 1.3 Ripple current at 48 kHz

ΔI = V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)

Assume a typical 2207-class motor with L_line-line ≈ 25 µH. The worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (25e-6 × 48e3) = **5.25 A p-p**

The ripple term is ΔI²/12 = 2.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.4 Result at 80 A burst

P_cond = 2 × (6400 + 2.3) × 1.85 mΩ = **23.7 W per channel**

Using die-only resistance (1.5 mΩ), the result is 19.2 W in the FETs, with 4.5 W in copper.

| Board load | Conduction loss |
|---|---|
| **4 channels simultaneous burst** | **94.8 W** (≈ 77 W in silicon) |
| 55 A continuous, T_j ≈ 95 °C (R ≈ 1.38 + 0.35 mΩ) | 2 × 3025 × 1.73 mΩ = 10.5 W/ch → **42 W/board** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the **active (PWM'd) leg** hard-switches. The low side commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Overlap (V-I crossover) loss: high-side FET

The transition times are set by the Miller plateau charge and the gate current:

- Miller plateau: V_pl ≈ 3.2 V
- R_g,total = R_drv + R_ext + R_g,int ≈ 3 + 4.7 + 1.0 = 8.7 Ω
- Turn-on gate current: I_g,on = (V_DRV − V_pl)/R_g = (10 − 3.2)/8.7 = 0.78 A
- Turn-off gate current: I_g,off = V_pl/R_g' = 3.2/(1.5 + 1.0), using a turn-off diode bypass with R_ext,off ≈ 0, so ≈ 1.28 A
- Rise time: t_r ≈ Q_gd/I_g,on + Q_gs2/I_g,on ≈ (9 + 6) nC / 0.78 = 19 ns
- Fall time: t_f ≈ 15 nC / 1.28 = 12 ns

P_sw,VI = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 31e-9 × 48e3 = **1.50 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 45e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.054 W**

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, derate it ×1.5, which gives **0.08 W**.

Recovery also adds I·V overlap at the high-side turn-on:

P_rr,HS ≈ ½ · I_rr · V · t_rr · f ≈ 0.5 × 6 A × 25.2 × 20 ns × 48k ≈ **0.07 W**

### 2.3 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

Using the energy-equivalent capacitance, E_oss ≈ ½ · C_o(er) · V². With C_o(er) ≈ 2.5 nF:

P_oss = ½ × 2.5e-9 × 25.2² × 48e3 × 2 (both FETs in the leg) = **0.076 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

The body diode conducts during dead time, twice per period:

P_dt = V_SD · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw

With t_dt = 300 ns (a typical AM32 setting at 48 kHz) and V_SD ≈ 0.85 V at 80 A:

P_dt = 0.85 × 80 × 2 × 300e-9 × 48e3 = **1.96 W**

> **Optimization lever:** Reducing dead time to 150 ns (verify with scope; shoot-through margin must be greater than 50 ns at T_j,max) halves this to 0.98 W. It is the single largest switching-related loss.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET die.

P_gate = Q_g · V_DRV · f_sw · N_switching_FETs = 55e-9 × 10 × 48e3 × 2 = **0.053 W per channel**

Commutating legs add negligible loss because they switch at the electrical commutation rate of a few kHz.

### 2.6 Switching loss summary per channel at 80 A, 48 kHz

| Mechanism | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.50 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.96 |
| Q_rr (diode + HS overlap) | 0.15 |
| C_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |
| **P_sw total** | **3.74 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw, at roughly 0.078 W/kHz per channel. Moving from 48 to 24 kHz saves about 1.9 W per channel, but it doubles ripple to 10.5 A p-p and increases motor copper/iron loss and acoustic noise.

## 3. Total Loss and Efficiency

| Quantity | Per channel @ 80 A | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (die) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Conduction (PCB copper) | 4.5 W | 18.0 W |
| Switching | 3.7 W | 15.0 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-channel low-side shunt) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 29.0 W** | **≈ 116 W** |

**Efficiency at 80 A:**

- Input power at D ≈ 0.9: P_in ≈ 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 1814 W per channel
- Efficiency: η ≈ 1 − 29/1814 = **98.4%**

The efficiency is excellent. The problem is **power density**: 116 W must be dissipated in a 9.3 cm² board.
