# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Loss, Thermal and Timer/DMA Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071), a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.

## 1. Conduction Losses

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds ≈ 1.45 mΩ. Use 1.5 mΩ for design margin.

### 1.2 Channel conduction loss

In 6-step commutation, current always flows through two series switch positions:
- the high side of one phase, and
- the low side of another.

With synchronous rectification, the PWM-switched leg alternates between its high-side and low-side FET. The series resistance is still always two FETs, except during dead time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot(1 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

The dead-time correction factor is 1 − 0.0192 ≈ 0.98:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 0.98 \approx \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET average

Each FET conducts for about 120° of each 360° electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W (average)}$$

The peak during its conduction window is 9.6 W. This peak matters for the transient Z_th check in Section 4.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching loss. Terms per channel:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·400 ns·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.04 W** |
| Gate drive (driver-side loss) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·30 nC·10·48k = **0.03 W** |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 2.8 W** |

Two observations:
- **Dead-time loss is nearly as large as overlap loss.** Tightening dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns recovers about 0.6 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.
- **Switching frequency scaling.** Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles P_sw (+1.4 W per channel). In exchange, you get lower current ripple and quieter motors.

### Totals at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Level | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per channel | 18.8 + 2.8 ≈ **21.6 W** |
| Four channels simultaneously | ≈ **86 W** |
| Plus copper/pad losses (Section 3) | ≈ **90–95 W** total heat into the board |

## 3. Board Copper Losses

### 3.1 Power-plane resistance

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}\,[1+0.00393(T-25)]$$

- ρ = 1.72e-8 Ω·m
- 2 oz copper: t = 70 µm

Example: one 3 mm wide × 10 mm long outer-layer path gives R ≈ 0.82 mΩ, so 80² × 0.82 m ≈ **5.2 W**.

Mitigations:
- Stack **4× 2 oz layers in parallel** for VBAT, GND and phase paths.
- Stitch with ≥ 20 vias (0.3 mm) per transition. Each via is ≈ 0.5 mΩ, so 20 in parallel ≈ 25 µΩ.
- Target ≤ 0.2 mΩ total per phase path, which gives ≤ 1.3 W per channel at burst.

### 3.2 Bulk capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5I = 40\ \text{A (worst case at } D = 0.5)$$

This is shared between the on-board MLCCs and an external low-ESR electrolytic. A ≥ 35 V, ≥ 1000 µF electrolytic at the battery pads is **mandatory**.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network

```
Tj ──R_jc(1.5 K/W)── Tcase ──R_cs(0.5)── Tpad/vias ──R_spread(~6 K/W per FET footprint)── T_board ──R_ba── T_air
                                                                                           │
                                                                                        C_board
```

### 4.2 Board-level transient (lumped)

**Heat capacity.** A 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer heavy-copper board plus components has m ≈ 6 g and an effective c_p ≈ 0.75 J/(g·K), so C_board ≈ 4.5 J/K.

**Thermal resistance.** In prop-wash airflow (h ≈ 50–100 W/m²K, A ≈ 2×9.3 cm²), R_ba ≈ 6–10 K/W.

**Time constant.** τ = R_ba·C ≈ 27–45 s.

**Steady state is impossible at 80 A:** 90 W × 8 K/W = 720 K rise. **80 A is strictly a burst rating.** The burst is limited by the board heat capacity:

$$T_{board}(t) = T_0 + P R_{ba}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right) \;\approx\; T_0 + \frac{P}{C}t \quad (t \ll \tau)$$

At P = 90 W and C = 4.5 J/K, the heating rate is **≈ 20 K/s**.

Starting from T_0 = 40 °C with a limit of T_board ≤ 100 °C, the burst budget is **≈ 3 s with all four motors at 80 A**.
