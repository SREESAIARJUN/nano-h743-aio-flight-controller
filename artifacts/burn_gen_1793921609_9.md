## 0. Executive Summary

The model shows that **80 A per motor is a burst rating, not a continuous one, on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5) footprint.**

| Metric (6S, 80 A per channel, Tj-hot) | Value |
|---|---|
| Conduction loss per channel | ≈ 30.7 W |
| Switching + dead-time + gate loss per channel | ≈ 2.3 W |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 33 W** |
| **Total board (4 channels simultaneously)** | **≈ 132 W** |
| Allowable 4-motor 80 A burst (Tamb 40 °C, PCB starting at 80 °C) | **≈ 0.5–0.8 s** |
| Realistic continuous rating (prop-wash cooled) | ≈ 25–35 A per channel |

The 80 A figure should be marketed as a burst rating of ≤ 1 s, gated by firmware current limiting and temperature telemetry. Section 2.6 explains why.

**Architectural clarification.** On a production AIO, the H743 is the flight controller only. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class running AM32, or equivalent). The H743 commands them over bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry back. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency, configured in AM32, not an H743 timer.

## 2. Thermal Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step complementary PWM, current flows through exactly two FETs at all times:

- **PWM on-time:** high-side of phase A and low-side of phase B.
- **PWM off-time:** low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and low-side of phase B.

Current therefore always sees 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle (dead time is handled separately in 2.3):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = k_T \cdot R_{25}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on),hot} = 1.5 \times 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega = 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution (hot spot).** The low-side FET of the non-PWM phase conducts for the entire 60° commutation interval:

$$
P_{LS,hot} = I^2 R_{hot} = 6400 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{15.4\ W}\ \text{(instantaneous, during its active sector)}
$$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each FET is active roughly 1/3 of the time. The thermal time constant of a DFN die is short (≈ 1–10 ms) compared with the electrical period at low eRPM. **Use the instantaneous 15.4 W figure for Tj checks at low speed and high torque**, which is the punch-out-from-hover case.

Using datasheet max R_DS(on) (2.0 mΩ × 1.5 = 3.0 mΩ) gives P_cond,ch = 38.4 W. That is the conservative design corner.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase leg hard-switches. Its high-side FET sees the V·I overlap.

**Hard-switching (V–I overlap):**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (small, because synchronous rectification means the body diode conducts only during dead time):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.036\ W
$$

**C_oss charge/discharge:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.015\ W
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (two complementary FETs switching; this loss lands mostly in the driver, not the FETs):

$$
P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 25\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.024\ W
$$

**Switching subtotal per channel:**

$$
P_{sw,total} = 0.968 + 0.036 + 0.015 + 1.23 + 0.024 \approx \mathbf{2.27\ W}
$$

**Design observation.** At 80 A, dead-time diode loss exceeds hard-switching loss. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Validate shoot-through margin on a scope before doing so.

**Sensitivity to f_sw.** All switching terms scale linearly with frequency:

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw,total}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 47\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel. At 80 A, though, conduction loss dominates (≈ 93 % of total), so lowering PWM frequency is not an effective thermal lever. FET R_DS(on) and copper are.
