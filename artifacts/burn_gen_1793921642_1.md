# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Up-Front Warnings

No MOSFET, board size, or burst duration was specified, so I've fixed a reference parameter set. Every equation is parametric. Substitute your datasheet values and the numbers update.

**Architecture assumption.** The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each 4-in-1 ESC channel is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Dual-MCU is the only practical topology, and the DShot section assumes it.

**Design conclusions you need to see before the math:**
1. 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 board is not thermally sustainable. The model below shows about 21 W per channel and about 85 W per board. The board heats at roughly 30–35 K/s, so a burst lasts about 2 s, not the 5–10 s implied by typical marketing ratings.
2. If "80 A" means total board current (20 A per motor), losses fall by about 16× in conduction. That design is comfortable. Both cases are computed.
3. The netlist is a reference design for review. It is not production-verified. It needs SI/PI simulation, DRC, and a thermal prototype before fab.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current I. Exactly one FET in each of the two active legs conducts:
- In the PWM leg, current alternates between the HS FET (duty D) and the LS FET (1−D, sync rect).
- In the other leg, the FET is statically on.

Therefore, at every instant, current flows through 2 × R_DS(on) regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical period (1/3 duty, split HS/LS). Averaged over a full rotation, all 6 FETs share equally:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Results

| | Case A: 80 A/motor | Case B: 20 A/motor |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per motor | 2·6400·1.45 m = **18.56 W** | **1.16 W** |
| P_cond per FET (avg) | 3.09 W | 0.19 W |
| P_cond board (×4) | **74.2 W** | 4.64 W |

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect loss (often omitted, significant here)

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{path}$$

A realistic phase path is pad → 2 oz pour → motor pad, with about 0.3 mΩ round trip per conducting pair at 80 °C copper:
- Case A: 6400 × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per motor** (7.7 W board).
- Battery input: total 320 A × 0.2 mΩ (pads plus planes) adds another 20 W.

The 320 A DC-link figure alone shows Case A is non-physical for a nano stack. XT60/XT30 leads and 14 AWG wire cannot carry it either.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The HS FET switches hard, and the LS FET commutates through its body diode during dead time (soft switching for the LS FET).

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_G}, \quad t_{off} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{V_{pl}/R_G}$$

With Q_gs2 ≈ 4 nC:
- t_on ≈ 12 nC / 2.17 A = 5.5 ns. This is the ideal value. Common-source and loop inductance (~1–2 nH) degrade it, so the design value is **t_on = 10 ns**.
- t_off ≈ 12 nC / 1.17 A = 10.3 ns. The design value is **t_off = 15 ns**.

### 2.2 Energy per PWM cycle

$$E_{on} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} t_{on}, \quad E_{off} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} t_{off}$$
$$E_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}, \quad E_{oss} = \text{from datasheet}$$

| Term | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| E_on | ½·25.2·80·10 n = 10.08 µJ | 2.52 µJ |
| E_off | ½·25.2·80·15 n = 15.12 µJ | 3.78 µJ |
| E_rr (Q_rr scales roughly with I_F; held constant here, conservative for B) | 1.01 µJ | 1.01 µJ |
| E_oss | 0.50 µJ | 0.50 µJ |
| **E_total** | **26.7 µJ** | **7.8 µJ** |

$$P_{sw} = E_{total}\cdot f_{sw}$$

- Case A: 26.7 µJ × 48 k = **1.28 W per motor**, concentrated in the PWM leg's HS FET.
- Case B: **0.37 W per motor**.
