# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Engineering Model (Job #24)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

No MOSFET, stack-up or form factor has been fixed for this job, so every number below follows from the stated assumptions. Swap in datasheet values and the equations still hold.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V | worst case used for switching loss |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor channel** (320 A total) | job spec |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | job spec |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 dual-cool) | class representative |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ | |
| Q_g / Q_rr / Q_oss | 50 nC / 40 nC / 50 nC | |
| t_r + t_f (driver-limited) | 30 ns total | ~1–2 A gate drive |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge | |
| Body-diode V_F | 0.8 V | |
| Temperature coefficient α | 0.006 /K, so R(125 °C) ≈ 1.6 × R25 | typical trench FET |

These assumptions surface four architectural issues before any calculation:

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (48 kHz PWM is supported). The DShot link runs from the H743 to the four ESC MCUs.
2. **A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot hold 80 A/channel.** 24 FETs in 5×6 packages need about 720 mm² of footprint against 800 mm² of total two-sided area, which leaves no room for drivers, MCUs or the FC. **Recommendation: 30.5×30.5 mm (900 mm²/side)** or a two-board stack. The thermal model below assumes 30×30 mm.
3. **The 80 A rating is thermally a burst of about 2–3 s** (derived in §3). The continuous rating is about 25 A/channel with prop-wash cooling. Any "80 A, 10 s" marketing claim must be validated with a thermocouple test.
4. **Board copper, not FETs, may dominate.** Budget the power plane resistance explicitly (§1.4).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
$$R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1+0.006\times100) = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous PWM

In any 60° sector, current I flows through exactly two legs:

- **Switching leg (phase A):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier. One FET always carries I, apart from the dead time.
- **Return leg (phase B):** the low-side FET carries I continuously.

The total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:
$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active for 120° of each 360°. The average per FET is:
$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2I^2R}{6} = \frac{I^2R}{3}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C
$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\,W}$$
$$P_{cond,4\text{-in-}1} = \mathbf{82\,W}$$
$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 3.4\,\text{W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss (must be included)

A 2 oz copper layer (70 µm) has a sheet resistance of about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.

- A 30 mm by 8 mm battery bus is about 3.75 □, or 0.94 mΩ per layer.
- With 4 parallel power layers this falls to about 0.23 mΩ.
- Shared across 320 A total: P ≈ 320² × 0.23 mΩ ≈ **24 W**.

**Design rule:** use ≥ 4 power layers of 2 oz, stitch vias at ≥ 1 via/A (0.3 mm drill, roughly 1–1.5 A each in parallel arrays), and place the battery pads at the board centroid.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg's HS/LS pair commutates. Evaluate at V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f = 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48 k | 0.05 W* |
