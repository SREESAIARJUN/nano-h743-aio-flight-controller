## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Before the math, three points change the design. Each is backed by numbers later in the document.

| # | Issue | Finding | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Can the H743 commutate the motors itself? | The H743 has two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) with complementary outputs and dead-time. HRTIM adds more channels, but four sensorless motors need 12 half-bridges plus 4 BEMF comparator chains. This also puts the commutation ISR on the same core as the EKF and rate loop. | Use the standard topology. The H743 is the FC and sends DShot to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32). |
| F2 | 80 A burst *per motor* on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board | About 160 W of dissipation goes into roughly 2.2 J/K of board thermal mass, so temperature rises at about 70 K/s (§3.3). Pads, vias and battery leads cannot carry 320 A total. | Not credible on 20×20 mm. The model is parameterized below. Two consistent configurations are shown: **(A) 80 A/motor on a 30.5×30.5 board with 5×6 mm FETs** and **(B) 80 A total (20 A/motor) on a 20×20 nano board**. |
| F3 | Bi-directional DShot pins | Input capture is impossible on complementary `CHxN` outputs. | All four motor outputs must be on non-N channels: **TIM1_CH1–CH4**. |

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step drive, at any instant current flows through **two conduction paths in series**: the PWM leg and the opposite low-side leg.

With complementary PWM, the PWM leg's current alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its low-side FET (for 1−D). Both have the same R_DS, so the path resistance is independent of D, apart from dead-time diode conduction, which is treated separately.

For a flat-top phase current I (ripple neglected; ripple correction follows):

```
P_cond,motor = 2 · I_rms² · R_DS(T_j)
```

**Ripple correction.** With phase inductance L_ph (line-to-line inductance 2·L_ph), the peak-to-peak ripple is:

```
ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D) / (2·L_ph·f_sw)
```
