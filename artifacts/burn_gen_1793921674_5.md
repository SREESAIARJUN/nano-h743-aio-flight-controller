## 0. Architecture Decision (Read First)

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot does not run sensorless FOC or 6-step commutation for four 80A motors on the flight controller MCU, and the timer/ADC budget would collide with IMU, logging and DShot. The industry-standard and ArduPilot-supported partition is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight Controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, sensors, bi-dir DShot600 master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 / BLHeli_32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250V/bootstrap) | High-side/low-side gate drive |
| Power stage | 24× 40V N-MOSFETs (2 parallel per switch on burst variant, 48 total) | Inverter bridges |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an **ESC firmware parameter** (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`), not a flight controller setting.

**Form-factor warning:** "Nano" (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) combined with 80A burst per channel is at the physical edge. Section 3 shows that 80A is a **time-limited burst** rating, not a continuous one, on this footprint. Recommended board: a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC with a 20×20 FC stack, or a 25.5 mm AIO with ≥2 oz copper on 8 layers.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through exactly **two switch positions** in series: one high-side and one low-side. During the PWM off-time, the opposing low-side FET of the same leg conducts synchronously instead of its body diode. So the conduction path resistance is 2·R_switch regardless of duty cycle:

$$
R_{switch} = \frac{R_{DS(on),25}\cdot k_T}{N_p}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{switch}
$$

**At 80A burst:**

| Configuration | R_switch | P_cond per motor | P_cond ×4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| N_p = 1 | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·1.5m = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| N_p = 2 | 0.75 mΩ | **9.6 W** | 38.4 W |

**At 45A continuous (N_p=2):** 2·2025·0.75m = **3.04 W** per motor.

Add copper and trace losses. At 2 oz copper with roughly 0.3 mΩ effective phase and bus path per motor:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} = 6400 \cdot 0.3m = 1.92\,W \text{ per motor at 80A}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Hard-Switched Edges)

Only the PWM-ing high-side switch hard-switches. The synchronous low-side turns on after dead time at near-zero V_DS.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\,W}
$$

With paralleled FETs the total overlap energy is unchanged. It is shared between the two devices, but slightly worsened by current sharing mismatch. Use 1.0 W.

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} \cdot N_p = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.046\,W
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery (diode in the opposing FET recovers at turn-on):**

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} \cdot N_p = 25.2 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.073\,W
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time windows per PWM period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\,W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g, not in the FET):**

The two FET positions that switch at f_sw each have N_p FETs:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot 2 N_p = 50\text{n}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 4 = 0.096\,W
$$

### 2.3 Loss Summary per Motor at 80A Burst (N_p=2)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 9.60 |
| Copper/trace | 1.92 |
| V–I switching | 1.00 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.12 |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.10 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈13.35 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | **≈53.4 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2·80·(duty-weighted, ~0.85) ≈ 1.7 kW per motor, giving an inverter efficiency of about 99.2%. The losses are small relative to throughput, but large relative to the thermal mass of a nano board.

**f_sw sensitivity:** P_sw,total ∝ f_sw. Going from 48 to 24 kHz saves ~0.87 W per motor at 80A, but increases current ripple and acoustic noise. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance racing/cinewhoop motors.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-Level (Single FET, Steady-State Within Burst)

Use the hottest device, a high-side FET in the commutation sector, which carries conduction plus switching losses. Over a full electrical cycle each position conducts 1/3 of the time. During its active sector the instantaneous dissipation is:

$$
P_{FET,inst} = I_{FET}^2 R_{DS(on),hot} + \frac{P_{sw}}{N_p} = 40^2 \cdot 1.5\text{m} + \frac{1.6}{2} = 2.4 + 0.8 = 3.2\,W
$$

Here I_FET = 80/2 = 40A, assuming perfect sharing. Derate 15% for mismatch, giving about 3.7 W instantaneous.

The commutation sector at 80A and typical eRPM is ~100 µs. That is far shorter than the junction thermal time constant (~ms), so use the **cycle-averaged** value:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx 3.7 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot(1 + \text{sync share}) \approx 1.9\,W
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + \bar P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta JC} \approx T_{board} + 1.9\cdot 1.2 = T_{board} + 2.3\,°C
$$

**Conclusion:** the junction-to-case rise is small. **The board thermal mass and the board-to-air path are the limiting factors.**
