## 0. Architecture Clarification

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The partition is:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor)--> [ESC MCU ×4, e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32]
                                                         └─> 3-ph gate driver ─> 6× N-FET ─> motor
```

Everything below follows this partition. The 48 kHz switching frequency is the ESC MCU PWM frequency, set by AM32's PWM frequency parameter.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, current I_ph flows through:

- one high-side (HS) FET, and
- one low-side (LS) FET.

When complementary PWM is active, the switching leg alternates HS↔LS, but **one channel of conduction is always present** in each of the two active legs. In the worst case, therefore, two FET R_DS(on) values are in series with the full phase current at all times.

### 1.2 Equations

R_DS(on) as a function of junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0 \cdot \left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K} \Rightarrow k_T(125^\circ C) = 1.6$$

Conduction loss per motor channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,path}$$

### 1.3 Numbers

- R_DS(125 °C) = 0.70 × 1.6 = **1.12 mΩ**
- FET conduction loss: 2 × 80² × 1.12 × 10⁻³ = **14.3 W per motor**
- Copper path (phase pad → FET → bus, estimated 0.15 mΩ): 80² × 0.15 × 10⁻³ ≈ **0.96 W**
- **Total conduction ≈ 15.3 W per motor**

The loss splits across 6 FETs over one electrical revolution. Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the period, so the average loss is ≈ 14.3 / 3 ≈ **4.8 W per FET-pair position**. Peak instantaneous loss per conducting FET is 7.2 W.

**Battery-side (DC) current** follows from duty cycle D: I_bus ≈ D · I_ph. At D = 0.9, I_bus ≈ 72 A per motor. Size the bulk capacitor ripple current and battery-pad copper accordingly.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition Times

The Miller plateau dominates the transition:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss Components (per motor, one switching leg active)

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw | 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | Q_g · V_gs · f_sw × 2 FETs | 60 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.34 W** |

### 2.3 Key Insight

At 48 kHz, **dead-time diode conduction exceeds the V-I overlap loss**. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Do this only if driver propagation-delay matching (e.g., FD6288Q: ±20 ns typical) allows it without risking shoot-through.

### 2.4 Total Losses per Motor and per Board

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 15.3 W | 61.2 W |
| Switching | 2.3 W | 9.4 W |
| **Total** | **17.6 W** | **≈ 70.6 W** |

Add ≈ 1 W for the H743 and regulators. **Design load: ≈ 72 W during simultaneous 4×80 A burst.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-Board (Per FET, Steady During Burst)

Use P_FET,peak = 7.2 W (conducting) + ~1.2 W (switching share on the PWM leg) ≈ 8.4 W:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET} \cdot (R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,TIM/solder}) = 8.4 \times (1.0 + 0.5) \approx 12.6\ \text{K}$$

The junction therefore runs about 13 K above local board copper. **The board, not the FET, is the bottleneck.**
