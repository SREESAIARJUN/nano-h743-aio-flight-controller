# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #69)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Critical Findings

I have no prior record of Job #69, so this document defines its baseline assumptions explicitly. Every number below is parametric: substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max | Design to 30 V transients and 40 V FETs |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor | See Finding 1 |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary | AM32 supports 24/48/96 kHz |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal | Two FETs conduct in every interval |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, 1 FET per position | 24 FETs total |
| Board | Nano format, 20×20 mm mounting (~26×26 mm outline), 8-layer, 2 oz outer copper | |
| Ambient | 35 °C | Board limit 105 °C, T_j limit 150 °C |

### Critical Findings

1. **80 A per motor is a burst rating of about 4 s on a nano board, not a sustainable one.** Section 3 shows continuous capability of about 25–30 A per motor. Either the spec means 80 A per motor for under 5 s, or the board must grow to 30×30 mm with paralleled FETs. This needs a decision before layout.

2. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** ArduPilot does not run motor commutation. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 commands. Four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) perform commutation at 48 kHz. Switching losses therefore depend on the ESC firmware and gate driver, not on ArduPilot.

3. **The H743 package is tight on a nano board.** LQFP100 measures 14×14 mm on a ~26 mm board that also carries 24 FETs. Use the TFBGA100 or UFBGA package, or use a stacked FC/ESC design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ /^\circ\text{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.45–1.5 × R_DS,25, giving **R_DS,hot ≈ 1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D of the time) and the low-side FET (fraction 1 − D).
- The **return leg** low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.

The current therefore always flows through exactly two FETs, independent of duty cycle. Ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple (L_phase ≈ 10 µH, worst case at D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} \approx 13.1\ A$$

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}} = \sqrt{6400 + 14.3} \approx 80.09\ A$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (+0.2%) at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + pours, 2 oz, ~6 mm path | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Current shunt (if fitted per ESC) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Bus pour share | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |

Recommendation: delete the per-channel shunts. Use a single bus shunt, or rely on AM32 back-EMF and the FC battery monitor. This saves about 6.4 W across four channels.

$$P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(1.5\,m\Omega) + 80^2(0.4\,m\Omega) = 19.2 + 2.56 = \mathbf{21.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one high-side turn-on and turn-off per period. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like), because the body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The rise and fall times come from the Miller plateau and gate drive current:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})/(R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int})}$$

With Q_gd = 12 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, and R_g,total = 6 Ω: I_g ≈ 1.08 A, so t_r ≈ 11 ns. Including the turn-off asymmetry, use **t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns**.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\,ns \times 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Secondary switching terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f, with Q_rr = 50 nC | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ C_oss,er · V² · f, with C_oss = 1.5 nF | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f, with t_dt = 100 ns, V_F = 0.8 V | **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge (2 active FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f, with Q_g = 60 nC | 0.06 W (dissipated in driver and R_g) |

**Total switching-related loss ≈ 2.69 W per motor.**
