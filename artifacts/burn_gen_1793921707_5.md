# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #63): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, BDShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Design Assumptions and Three Problems to Resolve First

There is no source data for Job #63, so every number below comes from stated assumptions. The equations hold for any parameter set. Replace the parameters with the datasheet values for your chosen parts and the results update directly.

Three findings change the design and should be settled first:

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU is what switches at 48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. The netlist below follows this split.
2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 nano board can only be a burst rating.** The board's thermal mass and copper area limit it to roughly 1–3 s at 80 A (Section 3). The continuous capability is about 15–25 A per channel without heavy airflow.
3. **48 kHz is a high PWM frequency at 80 A.** Switching loss alone is about 2.6 W per channel (Section 2). Using 24 kHz for high-current builds is a legitimate trade, and the ESC firmware exposes it as a setting.
