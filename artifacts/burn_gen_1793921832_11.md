# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #42)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel runs on its own small MCU (assumed AT32F421 running AM32) driving an FD6288Q three-phase gate driver and six N-MOSFETs. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes trapezoidal (six-step) commutation with complementary, synchronous-rectified PWM.

- At any instant, one leg is in PWM and one leg is held low-side ON.
- In the PWM leg, current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1−D).
- So exactly two channel resistances carry I_ph at every instant, regardless of D (dead time is ignored here and handled in §2).

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 68.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74.2 W |

Conduction loss rises with temperature, which in turn raises temperature. This is the dominant term.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper losses

These are often ignored and should not be on a nano board. The phase-pad to FET path is assumed at about 0.3 mΩ per conducting path, giving 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W per motor**. Use 4 inner layers of 2 oz copper on the power nets and stitch vias densely, at least 1 A per 0.3 mm via as a conservative rule.

### 1.4 Bus capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; \max = 40\text{ A at } D = 0.5$$

- **Required capacitance per ESC:** ≥ 4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V (1210) near each half-bridge, plus a shared low-ESR polymer or electrolytic of 470 µF or more at the battery pads.
- **Derating:** X7R parts lose about 60 % of their capacitance at 25 V DC bias. Size for the derated value.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor, all terms are computed at 25.2 V and 80 A:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V_bus I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | 0.968 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.614 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw | 0.060 W |
| Output-capacitance charge | Q_oss V_bus f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 0.058 W (dissipated in driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching, P_sw** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead time costs nearly as much as V–I overlap. Reducing it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, but the shoot-through margin must be verified across temperature.
- Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw, at the cost of more acoustic noise and current ripple.
