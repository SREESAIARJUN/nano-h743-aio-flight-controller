## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 0.95 × 1.47 ≈ **1.40 mΩ** per FET.

Two FETs in parallel per switch position, with a 10 % current-sharing mismatch factor k_s = 1.1:

$$R_{sw} = \frac{R_{DS}(T_j)}{2}\,k_s = 0.77\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the HS and LS FET. HS conducts for D·T_sw, LS for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Static leg:** the opposite-side FET conducts continuously for the full 60° sector.

So at every instant exactly two switch positions are in series in the current path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{sw} + P_{trace}$$

Phase current RMS includes the PWM ripple:

$$I_{ph,rms} = \sqrt{I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With a typical L_ph = 15 µH, worst case at D = 0.5, V_bus = 25.2 V:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{2\times 15\mu \times 48k} = 4.4\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{rms}=\sqrt{80^2 + 1.6}=80.01\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible at 80 A, but it matters for low-inductance (low-kV, large-stator) motors.
