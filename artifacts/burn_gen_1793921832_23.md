## 0. Architecture Flags

These items affect the validity of everything that follows.

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs AM32/BLHeli_32 firmware and commutates the bridge. The H743 sends only DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. | Sections 1–2 apply to the ESC board. Section 3 applies to the FC. |
| F2 | **80A on a "nano" (20×20) format is not thermally credible.** Each FET at 80A dissipates about 10 W. Section 2 shows that even a 30.5×30.5 board with 8 layers of 2 oz copper only sustains about 3 s of simultaneous 4×80A. | The design basis is a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** ESC. The FC can be 20×20 or 30.5. |
| F3 | "80A burst" needs a defined duration. | Design basis: **80A for up to 2 s per motor, ~10% duty**, 6S. |

Each channel has 6 FETs (3 half-bridges), so the board has 24.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

During a 6-step commutation interval:
- Leg A is PWM'd with synchronous rectification. The high side conducts for D·T and the low side for (1−D)·T.
- Leg B's low side is on continuously.

Current flows through exactly **two** channels of R_ds at all times, regardless of D. The total is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

The per-device distribution matters for hotspots. The leg-B low-side FET carries I²R for the whole 60° interval. Over a full electrical cycle, each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the time in either role.

**Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C:**

$$R(125) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.005\times100) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

| Term | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Instantaneous loss per conducting FET | 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **9.6 W** |
| P_cond, FETs (per channel) | 2 × 9.6 W | **19.2 W** |
| P_cu, copper | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | **1.9 W** |

The worst-case device is the static low-side FET over a 60° step, at 9.6 W instantaneous. Averaged over the electrical cycle, each FET sees about 19.2/6 × 2 ≈ **6.4 W**. Use 9.6 W for the junction transient (Section 2.3) and the average for board heating.
