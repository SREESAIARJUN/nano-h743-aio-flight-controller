# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #45): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Reference Design

## 0. Feasibility Constraint: Read This First

**80 A burst per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally or electrically viable.**

- **Battery current:** four motors at 80 A each, at high duty, is roughly 250–320 A of battery current. That exceeds what 20×20 power pads, solder joints, and the XT30/XT60-class leads used at this size can carry.
- **Heat:** the model below shows about 95 W of dissipation at that operating point. The board's thermal mass supports it for under 1 second.

I have therefore built the model **parametrically**. You can evaluate it at 80 A per motor (worst case, shown below) or at a realistic nano rating. The recommendations in §3.4 give two viable options: re-rate the board, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor.

**Architecture assumption.** The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421) plus a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs.
