> **Architectural correction:** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does not commutate the motors. It generates DShot frames and captures eRPM telemetry. Each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC stage. The DMA/timer analysis applies to the H743.

> **Feasibility warning:** "80 A burst" on a nano-format board is a **seconds-scale transient rating, not a continuous rating**. Section 3 shows it is thermally limited to roughly 2–5 s with all four motors at 80 A. A 20×20 footprint cannot reach 80 A even as a burst using these FETs. Market it as "80 A burst (≤2 s, single motor or diagonal pair)" unless you validate otherwise on the bench.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.4 mΩ × 1.55 = **2.17 mΩ**. Use this for burst calculations.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):**
  - the high-side FET conducts for duty D;
  - the complementary low-side FET conducts for (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Phase B (sink phase):** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**. That is a key property of synchronous 6-step drive.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}},\qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

Example values for a typical 2207-class motor:

- L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH
- At 80 A, V_bemf ≈ V_bus·D − I·R_motor
- Worst case, ΔI_pp ≈ 12 A

The ripple term adds 12 A²/12 = 12 A² against 6400 A², a 0.2 % correction. It is negligible at this operating point.

### 1.4 Copper loss

Assume 3 phase-pad-to-FET paths plus the shared bus planes, with an effective conducted-path resistance of R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per active current loop at 2 oz:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0004 = 2.6\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 2.17 mΩ | **27.8 W** |
| Copper | 80² × 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **30.4 W** |

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries ≈ 27.8/6 ≈ **4.6 W**. During its own conduction sector, a single FET peaks at ≈ 13.9 W. The electrical period at 80 A (tens of kHz eRPM) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so the average figure governs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition (PWM high-side FET)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g}} = \frac{10-3}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A},\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6\ \text{A}
$$

Transition times:

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{7\,\text{nC}}{1.4} = 5.0\ \text{ns},\qquad
t_f = \frac{7\,\text{nC}}{0.6} = 11.7\ \text{ns}
$$

V-I overlap loss:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 16.7\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.81\ \text{W}
$$
