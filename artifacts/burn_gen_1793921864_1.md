## 0. Feasibility Statement

80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is a **transient (≤ 1–2 s) rating, not a continuous one**. The math below supports that conclusion:

- At 80 A with four motors bursting, total stage dissipation is about 110 W.
- The board's thermal mass is only about 5 J/K.
- The design is therefore governed by **burst duration and duty cycle**, not by steady-state Rθ.

The model below is written to be re-run as parameters change. Treat the numbers as a sizing baseline, not a validated rating.

Architecture note: the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel still needs its own commutation MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32 class: STM32G071 / AT32F421). The H743 talks to those MCUs via bidirectional DShot600.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases carry current: one phase is "high", one is "low", and the third floats. Each phase spends:

- 120° (1/3 of the electrical cycle) as the PWM'd high phase,
- 120° as the low phase (LS held fully on),
- 120° floating.

Complementary PWM is applied on the high phase:

- **High-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the cycle × D
- **Low-side FET of the same phase:** conducts for 1/3 × (1 − D) (synchronous freewheel), plus 1/3 × 1 when its phase is the low phase

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,(2-D)}{3}$$

Summing over 3 phases × 2 switches gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3\cdot\frac{I^2R(D + 2 - D)}{3} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is a sanity check: there are always exactly two R_DS in series in the current path.
