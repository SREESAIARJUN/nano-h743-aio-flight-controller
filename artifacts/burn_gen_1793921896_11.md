## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Check

Several constraints in this specification interact badly. They are stated up front so the numbers below can be traced and challenged.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Calculations use 25.2 V as the worst case for switching loss. |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor phase path, 1–5 s | **Not** continuous. |
| Form factor | "Nano" AIO, about 32×32 mm PCB, 20×20 or 25.5 mm mounting | Small thermal mass. |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 40 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC | Representative class, e.g. 40 V "1 mΩ-class" PDFN3333. Substitute real datasheet values. |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal | AM32-style ESC firmware. |
| Dead time | 200 ns per edge | |
| Ambient / start temperature | 40 °C | |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 at 400 MHz, timer kernel clock 200 MHz | Standard ArduPilot H743 configuration. |

> **Architectural note:** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs dedicated ESC MCUs, typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 is the flight controller and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The design below follows that split.

> **Reality check:** 80 A per motor on all four motors simultaneously means about 320 A from the battery and about 85–90 W dissipated in a board weighing about 6 g. That is a **burst rating of roughly 1–3 seconds**, not a sustainable operating point. The math below quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each commutation sector, two phases are active: one high-side PWM'd and one low-side static.

- **PWM ON (duty D):** Q_H(A) → motor → Q_L(B)
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** Q_L(A) (synchronous rectifier) → motor → Q_L(B)

At every instant, exactly **two MOSFETs are in series** with the phase current:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R ≈ 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Per-motor and per-FET values at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 19.2\ \mathrm{W}
$$

Per-FET distribution over one electrical cycle, where each phase is high for 120°, low for 120°, and floating for 120°:

| FET | Conduction fraction | Loss at D → 1 (full-throttle burst) |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | D/3 | 6400 × 1.5 mΩ / 3 = **3.2 W** |
| Low-side | 1/3 + (1−D)/3 | **3.2 W** at D = 1; up to 6.4 W at D → 0 |

Averaging is thermally valid because the electrical frequency (several hundred Hz to more than 2 kHz) is much faster than the FET thermal time constants.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

Assume the battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad path is about 0.3 mΩ per motor (2 oz inner layers):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 1.9\ \mathrm{W/motor}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

One FET hard-switches per PWM period. The complementary FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns (gate driver about 1 A source/sink, R_g about 2–5 Ω):

$$
P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.97\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.05\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}
$$

Note that Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so it may be 2–3× higher at 80 A.
