## 0. Scope, Architecture Clarification, and Stated Assumptions

### Architecture

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)** running ArduPilot. It does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. That job belongs to four independent ESC MCUs, one per motor, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The design therefore splits into three domains:

```
 ┌──────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ────────────────┐
 │ TIM3 CH1-4 ── BDShot600 ──┐                            │
 └───────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┘
          ┌──────────┬───────┴──┬──────────┐
       ESC MCU1   ESC MCU2   ESC MCU3   ESC MCU4   (AM32, 48 kHz PWM)
          │          │          │          │
       FD6288Q ×4 gate drivers → 6× N-MOSFET per motor (24 total)
```

> ⚠️ **Engineering reality check.** Four motors at 80 A on a 6S nano-class AIO is a marketing-style burst rating. Simultaneous 4 × 80 A at high duty means more than 300 A of battery current, which no nano board's power entry can sustain. The model below quantifies exactly how long a burst is survivable. Treat that time limit as the actual specification.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

AM32 uses **complementary (synchronous) PWM** with 6-step commutation. At any instant, current flows through exactly two FET channels in series:

- **Active PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D. The low-side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side conducts 100% of the commutation sector.

The total channel resistance in the current path is therefore always 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

During burst at full load, trapezoidal phase current is approximately flat-topped, so I_rms ≈ I_ph = 80 A.
