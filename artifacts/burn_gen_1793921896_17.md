## Architecture, Loss Model, Bidirectional DShot Allocation, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Before the math, three architectural constraints shape everything that follows.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1, TIM8). Four sensorless BLDC channels need 12 complementary pairs plus BEMF comparators. | The H743 is the flight controller only. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| F2 | 24 power FETs in 5×6 mm packages need about 720 mm² of placement area. A 20×20 mm "nano" board has 400 mm² per side. | A true 20×20 board forces 3.3×3.3 mm FETs, whose R_DS(on) is roughly 2× higher. The 80 A rating is then only credible for sub-second bursts. **Recommendation:** 30.5×30.5 mm stack (ESC board + FC board), or accept a 50–60 A burst on 20×20. |
| F3 | At 4 × 80 A, the board dissipates about 100 W (§3). With prop-wash cooling, it can sustain that for only about 4–5 s (§4). | "80 A burst" must be specified as a duration (≤ 3 s recommended), and firmware must enforce a thermal derating model. |
