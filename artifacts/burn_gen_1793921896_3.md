## 0. Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors by itself. Production AIO and stack designs use this split:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot, emits bi-directional DShot600 |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (QFN32), running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware | 6-step / FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive |
| Power stage (×4) | 6 × 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 for a nano) | 3-phase bridge |

This document models the H743 to ESC-MCU DShot link plus the four power stages.

**Form-factor reality check:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 nano board is a **burst rating limited to seconds**, not a continuous rating. Section 3 quantifies this. Most real "80 A" 4-in-1 boards are 30.5×30.5 with 5×6 FETs.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FET positions in series. These are one high-side and one low-side. During PWM-off time, the complementary FET of the chopped leg carries the current instead of its body diode. Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

Hot-junction resistance:

$$
R_{DS,hot} = 1.5 \times 0.80\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.20\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Result:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.36\ W}
$$

Add copper and trace losses. For example, phase plus return path at ≈0.3 mΩ on 2 oz inner layers:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.92\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched per commutation sector. Under the linear-transition approximation:

**(a) V-I overlap**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**(d) Output capacitance**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 actively switching FETs; the driver dissipates most of this)

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Channel and Board Totals

| Loss term | W / channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 15.36 |
| Copper | 1.92 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 |
| **Total** | **≈ 19.0 W** |
| **Four channels at simultaneous 80 A burst** | **≈ 76 W** |

**Key insight:** At 80 A, conduction dominates (≈ 81%). Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only ≈ 1.6 W per channel. Lowering R_DS(on) or paralleling FETs is the effective lever.

**Paralleling FETs:** Two FETs per position halves P_cond to 7.7 W but doubles Q_g and C_oss losses. The trade is strongly positive.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET Steady Average

The 6 FET positions share conduction over an electrical cycle. Each FET conducts approximately 1/3 of the time at full current.

$$
P_{FET} \approx \frac{15.36}{6} + \frac{1.6}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3} \approx 2.56 + 0.27 \approx \mathbf{2.83\ W}
$$

The electrical frequency at 2–3 kHz is far faster than the die thermal time constant (on the order of ms). Averaging is therefore valid. Low-RPM stall or desync is the exception: one FET pair then sees ≈ 7.7 W continuous, which is the worst case.

### 3.2 Junction-to-Board Thermal Network

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (PDFN5×6) | 1.5 K/W |
| Solder plus thermal-via array (≥ 9 × 0.3 mm vias under the pad) | 3.0 K/W |
| **R_θJ-PCB** | **4.5 K/W** |

$$
\Delta T_{J-PCB} = 2.83 \times 4.5 = \mathbf{12.7\ K}
$$

At stall: 7.7 × 4.5 = 35 K.
