# Job #62: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Architecture & Mathematical Model

**Revision A, engineering baseline.** Assumed component values are marked **[A]**. Replace them with datasheet values from your selected parts before release.

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These two findings change the design, so they come first.

### F1. The H743 cannot run the four ESCs itself

Driving 4 motors × 3 phases at 48 kHz needs 12 complementary PWM pairs. The H743 provides:

- TIM1 and TIM8: 3 complementary pairs each, so 6 total
- HRTIM: 10 outputs

That is not enough for 4 independent commutation/FOC loops, and it would also consume the DShot timers.

**Baseline architecture:** The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Each ESC MCU generates its own 48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM. All switching math below applies to those ESC MCUs.

### F2. "80A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is transient-only

- 4 × 80 A means **320 A at the battery pads**.
- Steady state is physically impossible (§3.4).
- Realistic burst duration is about **1 s** (§3.5).

**Recommendations:**
- Market as "80A, 1 s burst / 40A continuous", or
- Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm format with 5×6 mm FETs.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss with thermal feedback

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through **exactly two channels in series**:
- During the PWM on-time: high-side A and low-side B.
- During the off-time: low-side A (sync rectification) and low-side B.

So the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

P_cond = 2 · I² · R_DS(on)(T_j)

**At T_j = 125 °C:** R_DS = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = 2.25 mΩ

P_cond = 2 × 80² × 2.25 mΩ = **28.8 W per motor**

**Per-FET average:** each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, so ≈ 28.8 / 6 = **4.8 W**. Peak instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is 14.4 W.

**Self-consistent junction temperature:** solve iteratively

T_j = T_ref + P_FET(T_j) · Z_th

Because P grows with T_j, thermal runaway occurs if

∂P/∂T · Z_th ≥ 1, where ∂P/∂T = (I²R_DS,25·α)/3 ≈ 0.016 W/K per FET.

With Z_th ≈ 15 K/W (§3.3), the loop gain is ≈ 0.24. That is stable, with ~30 % amplification of the cold-calculated temperature rise.

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched commutating leg)

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard: one high-side/low-side pair per motor at any time.

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_G) | 6 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.086 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

**Sensitivities:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw and t_r + t_f. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.1 W total, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W.

### 2.3 Copper / interconnect

- R_path ≈ 0.6 mΩ total per motor **[A]** (8-layer, 2 oz, plus pads and solder)
- P_Cu,motor = I² · R_path = **3.8 W**
- Battery-pad/bus copper: (320 A)² × 0.15 mΩ = **15.4 W**

This is significant. The battery pads need maximum copper and should be soldered directly to the XT60 leads.
