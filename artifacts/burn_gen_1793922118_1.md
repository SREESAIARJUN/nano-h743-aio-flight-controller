## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the AM32 default. In each 60° sector:

- The high-side FET of phase A PWMs at duty D.
- The low-side FET of phase A conducts during (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- The low-side FET of phase B is held on for the full sector.

Conduction loss for one motor channel at phase current I is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous six-step.

### 1.2 Temperature coupling

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{case} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}
$$

Solve these two equations by fixed-point iteration (3–4 iterations converge).

**Thermal runaway condition (per FET):**

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta JA} = I^2 R_0\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA} < 1
$$

At 80 A: I²R₀α = 6400 × 1.5×10⁻³ × 0.005 = 0.048 W/K.

- Stable steady state requires R_θJA < 20.8 K/W.
- On a nano board without airflow (≈15–25 K/W) the margin is marginal. Prop wash is mandatory.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j ≈ 100 °C

- R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × [1 + 0.005 × 75] = **2.06 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 2.06×10⁻³ = **26.4 W per channel**
- PCB copper: P_pcb = 2 × I² × R_pcb = 2 × 6400 × 0.5×10⁻³ = **6.4 W per channel** (spread across the board, not in the junction)

Per-FET split within a sector:

| FET | Conduction loss |
|---|---|
| Static low-side FET | I²R ≈ **13.2 W** while active |
| Switching high-side FET | ≈ 13.2·D W |
| Switching low-side FET (sync) | ≈ 13.2·(1−D) W |

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The heat-spreading time constant of the copper (ms range) is much longer than the electrical period at typical eRPM, so averaged per-FET dissipation is about **8.8 W**. The **13.2 W instantaneous** figure governs the junction-to-case rise.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (switching FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20×10⁻⁹ × 48 000 = **0.968 W**

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

Applies when the low-side body diode conducts during dead time before the high side turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}
$$

= 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 000 = **0.036 W**

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}
$$

= 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 000 = **0.048 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}
$$

= 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 000 = **0.307 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss occurs in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs. There are 2 switching FETs per channel:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}
$$

= 2 × 30 nC × 10 V × 48 000 = **0.029 W**

### 2.6 Per-channel and total budget (80 A burst)

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 26.4 | 105.6 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 | 1.2 |
| Gate drive | 0.03 | 0.1 |
| PCB copper | 6.4 | 25.6 |
| **Total** | **≈ 34.2** | **≈ 136.7** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst: the power delivered is 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is 7.26 kW, so losses are about **1.9 %**. The conduction loss is thermally lethal not because of efficiency, but because of the tiny heat-spreading mass.
