# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #45): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Warnings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. In every production 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel is commutated by its own small MCU, not by the H743. Typical choices are an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. This document models both sides of that interface.

**Form factor.** An 80 A burst rating per channel is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" ESC. It is marginal on 30.5×30.5 mm without a heatsink. All results below are reported as functions of burst duration so the rating can be defended honestly.

**Baseline parameters.** These are not tied to a specific datasheet. Substitute real values for the selected FET.

| Symbol | Meaning | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| f_sw | Switching frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | FET on-resistance at 25 °C (40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm, V_gs = 10 V) | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_ds temperature coefficient | 0.006 /K (R_ds,125 ≈ 1.6× R_ds,25) |
| t_r, t_f | Rise and fall times (driver ≈ 1 A source/sink, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC) | 25 ns each |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse recovery charge | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | Output charge | 40 nC |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 50 nC |
| V_f | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | Dead time | 100 ns per edge |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |

**Voltage margin.** A 40 V FET on 6S leaves only about 15 V of margin for ringing. This requires tight loop layout and local MLCC decoupling at every half-bridge. A 60 V FET is the safer choice, at roughly 1.5× the R_ds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. In any 60° sector:

- The PWM'd phase's high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- The same phase's low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1 − D.
- The return phase's low-side FET conducts continuously.

The duty fractions cancel, so the instantaneous copper (FET) loss per channel is independent of throttle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase current carries triangular PWM ripple of peak-to-peak amplitude ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

**Worked ripple values** (assumed motor phase inductance L_ph = 15 µH line-to-line):

- At D = 0.5 and E_bemf ≈ 12 V: ΔI ≈ 4.6 A.
- The ripple term ΔI²/12 = 1.8 A², which is negligible against I² = 6400 A².
- Ripple only matters with very low-inductance motors, below about 5 µH.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent self-consistent solution

On-resistance rises linearly with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Junction temperature depends on total loss through the thermal resistance R_th,ja:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\,P_{tot}(T_j)$$

Because conduction loss is linear in T_j, the system has a closed-form solution:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\,(P_{sw} + 2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha))}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway criterion.** The denominator must stay positive:

$$2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 2·6400·0.0012·0.006·R_th < 1, so **R_th < 10.8 K/W per channel**. This is the hard ceiling on the channel-to-ambient thermal resistance. Any design above it has no steady-state solution and runs away thermally.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C (cold start) | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 61 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 89 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | 24.6 W | 98 W |

**Per-FET loss.** Each of the 6 FETs per channel conducts during 120° of the electrical cycle. Averaged over one electrical revolution, each FET dissipates about 2I²R/6 = I²R/3 ≈ 4.1 W at 100 °C. The PWM'd high-side FET bears the switching loss on top of this (Section 2).

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

| Element | Assumption | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad / trace | 2 oz copper, ~0.3 mΩ effective, 2 traces in path | 3.8 W per channel |
| Current-sense shunt | 0.25 mΩ, shared bus shunt | (80·k)²·R, where k is the 4-channel current-sharing factor |
| Bus capacitors | I_C,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)) = 40 A at D = 0.5 (per channel, worst case) | Phase-staggered PWM across channels reduces combined ripple to about 50–60 A rms total |

The bus capacitor ripple requirement drives the bulk-capacitor ESR choice and the local MLCC count.
