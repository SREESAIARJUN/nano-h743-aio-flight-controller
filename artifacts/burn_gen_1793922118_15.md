## 0. Scope, Architecture Clarification, and Baseline Assumptions

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document assumes an AT32F421K8U7, which is common and supports 48 kHz PWM and EDT. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs.

**Feasibility warning.** Sustained 80 A per channel on a nano-class board (20×20 to 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally viable. The model below shows 80 A is a **burst rating of about 1 to 3 s** at this size. Validate with thermocouple and IR testing before publishing any rating.

The MOSFET values below are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts. Substitute your datasheet numbers; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry
The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active phase pair.

- At any instant, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current I.
- Each FET conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical, so its conduction fraction is δ_FET = 1/3.
- With synchronous rectification, the PWM duty D only moves current between the high-side FET and its complementary low-side FET. Total conduction loss is therefore almost independent of D.

### 1.2 Equations
Rds(on) depends on junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds(25)}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx 0.0059\ /\mathrm{K}$$

Per-channel conduction loss counts two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Average loss per FET:

$$P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

Copper (busbar, phase, and pad) loss:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2 R_{Cu},\qquad R_{Cu}=\frac{\rho\,\ell}{w\,t}\left[1+0.00393(T-25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
- P_cond,ch = 6400 × 2 × 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W per channel**
- P_cond,FET = 6400 × 1.8 mΩ / 3 = **3.84 W average per FET**
- Copper: assume R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ effective per channel path (2 oz plus stitched inner planes). P_Cu = 6400 × 0.4 mΩ = **2.6 W per channel**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing phase leg switches. The high-side FET hard-switches, and the low-side FET is the synchronous rectifier.

| Term | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 20 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.024 W** |
| C_oss charge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.5·1 nF·635·48 k = **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·30 nC·12·48 k = **0.035 W** (dissipated mostly in driver/Rg) |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 2.62 W** |

**Edge time derivation (check against layout).** With R_g,total ≈ 5 Ω and V_plateau ≈ 3 V, the gate current is I_g ≈ (12 − 3)/5 = 1.8 A. Intrinsic Miller time is Q_gd/I_g ≈ 2.8 ns. The 20 ns used above reflects common-source and loop inductance of about 1 to 2 nH at 80 A di/dt. Shrinking the power loop is the biggest lever on P_sw.
