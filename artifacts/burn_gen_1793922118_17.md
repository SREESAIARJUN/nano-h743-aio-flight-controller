# STM32H743 AIO (Job #49): Power Stage Thermal Model, Switching Losses, BDShot600 DMA Plan and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 (AT32F421 class). The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This split is how shipping AIO boards work: running 4× six-step commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection on the FC MCU, alongside ArduPilot, is not viable.

**Assumed design parameters.** Replace these with real datasheet values before release. Every result below scales with them, and the formulas are given so you can re-run them.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value assumed |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_burst | Per-channel burst current | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | Per FET, at V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ (40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm class) |
| α | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | 0.0055 /K |
| t_r + t_f | Effective current/voltage overlap | 40 ns total |
| Q_rr | Reverse-recovery charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss,tr | Time-related output capacitance | 2 nF |

> **Engineering flag:** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20) footprint is only plausible as a **short burst of a few seconds**. Section 3 quantifies this. The datasheet/marketing rating should be "80 A burst (≤2 s)", alongside a continuous rating of roughly 25–30 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step commutation with complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases carry current (for example, A+ to B−).

- **PWM on-time (D):** High-side FET on phase A, low-side FET on phase B.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** Low-side FET on phase A (synchronous rectification) and low-side FET on phase B.

So **two FETs are always in the current path**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 100) = 1.55\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple. For a triangular ripple of peak-to-peak amplitude ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right),\qquad r = \frac{\Delta I}{I},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}D - E)\,(1-D)}{2L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

where E is the motor back-EMF and L_ph is the per-phase inductance.

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 10 µH) at D = 0.9 during a full-throttle burst:

- ΔI ≈ 4–6 A, so r ≈ 0.07.
- The correction factor is ≈ 1.0004, which is **negligible at burst current**.
- It matters only at low current and low duty.

### 1.4 Burst result

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.55\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.8\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Each FET in the path dissipates about 9.9 W.

**Copper path losses.** For a 2 oz trace, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. With roughly 2 squares of effective path per phase, multilayer-paralleled to about 0.3 mΩ total:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the hard-switched FET (high side) sees V–I overlap loss. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero V_DS.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss charge/discharge | ½ · C_oss,tr · V² · f_sw | ½ × 2 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.03 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2·t_dead · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.04 W** (dissipated in the driver/regulator) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.4 W per channel** |
