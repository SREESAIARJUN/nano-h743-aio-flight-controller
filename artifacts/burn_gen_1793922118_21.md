## 0. Architecture Findings

Three design decisions shape everything below.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** Running ArduPilot plus 4× sensorless 6-step commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection on a single H743 is not a supported ArduPilot configuration. The standard and correct architecture is:
   - **H743**: runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 targets): one per motor, each driving a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

2. **An "80 A burst" rating is only meaningful with a duration and an ambient temperature attached.** Section 3 shows that on a 30.5×30.5 mm board, 80 A on all four motors produces roughly 100 W of losses. The board heats adiabatically at about 20 K/s, so the burst limit is roughly 3 s. The sustainable continuous rating is about 25 A per motor.

3. **Battery input current at 4×80 A burst approaches 320 A.** This is physically unrealistic for nano-class battery pads, an XT60, or AWG12 leads. If "80 A" means *total* board current, all thermal numbers below drop by roughly 16× (the I² scaling of (20/80)² applied per motor). **Confirm which interpretation the spec intends.**

## 2. Loss Model per Motor (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **During t_on:** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **During t_off:** current freewheels through both low-side FETs, or through both high-side FETs in high-side-PWM schemes.

At every instant, therefore, **two channels carry the full phase current, independent of duty cycle D**:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \,(2R_{ds,hot} + R_{cu})
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.8 + 0.4)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.6\ W}
$$

Of this, 23.0 W is dissipated in the FETs and 2.6 W in the copper.

**Per-FET average:** each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

$$
\bar P_{FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega}{3} \approx 3.84\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak while conducting is 11.5 W.
