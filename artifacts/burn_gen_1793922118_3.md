## 0. Architectural Findings

Three issues affect everything below:

1. **80 A burst in a "nano" form factor is thermally marginal to infeasible.** Section 3 shows four motors at 80 A dissipate roughly 60–120 W. On a 20×20 mm board this drives an adiabatic temperature rise of tens of K per second. 80 A must be specified as a time-limited burst, for example ≤1–2 s, with a firmware-enforced I²t limit. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board.
2. **The H743 should not drive the FETs directly.** The standard and recommended split is:
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600.
   - Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 or similar and generate the 48 kHz PWM. AT32F421 and STM32G071 are common choices.

   This model assumes that split.
3. **Pin and DMA mappings must be verified against the RM0433 alternate-function table and ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py`.** The mappings in Section 4 are a valid, conventional proposal, not a substitute for that check.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Bridge, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In any 60° sector, two phases carry current. With synchronous rectification, exactly one FET per active leg conducts at any instant, so there are always two FETs in series in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Each FET occupies the path for 1/3 of the electrical period on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Within that window, current splits between the high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) FET by duty cycle: HS conducts for D and LS for (1−D). The HS/LS split therefore matters for individual hotspots:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,D$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,(1-D)$$

At full throttle (D ≈ 0.9) the high-side FET is the hotspot.

**Results at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

| FET | R_hot | P_cond, bridge | P_cond, worst FET (HS, D = 0.9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 mΩ (5×6) | 1.45 mΩ | 2·6400·1.45e-3 = **18.6 W** | 6400·1.45e-3·0.9/3 = **2.78 W** |
| 1.8 mΩ (3×3) | 2.61 mΩ | **33.4 W** | **5.0 W** |

Because R_DS rises with temperature, conduction loss has positive feedback. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,Z_{th}(t)$$

A steady-state solution exists only if:

$$\alpha\, I^2 R_{25}\, Z_{th}/3 < 1$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses (48 kHz)

Only one leg is PWM-switched in each sector, so two FETs see switching events per PWM period.

**Hard-switching V–I overlap (the hard-switched FET):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·20\text{e-}9·48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{e-}9·25.2·48\text{e}3 = 0.036\ W$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9·25.2·48\text{e}3 = 0.048\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (synchronous FET):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8·80·100\text{e-}9·48\text{e}3 = 0.31\ W$$

**Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):**

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.048\ W$$

**Total switching-related loss:**

$$P_{sw,bridge} \approx \mathbf{1.4\ W}$$

### 2.3 Other Losses

**Shunt** (if used; 0.5 mΩ assumed):

$$I^2 R = 6400·0.5\text{e-}3 = 3.2\ W$$

This is significant. Consider a Hall sensor or FET-R_DS sensing for the 80 A path.

**Copper:** a 2 oz, 10 mm wide, 15 mm long pour has R ≈ 0.37 mΩ per layer. With 4 layers in parallel, R ≈ 0.09 mΩ, giving about 0.6 W per path.

**Bulk capacitor ripple current:**

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80·0.5 = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}$$

This occurs per bridge, at D = 0.5. Most of it is unshared between motors because the PWM phases are uncorrelated.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer Approximation)

```
Tj ──R_jc──┬── Tcase ──R_cb──┬── T_board ──R_ba(airflow)── T_amb
           C_j               C_case           C_board
```

Assumed values:
- R_jc = 1.0 K/W (5×6) or 2.0 K/W (3×3)
- R_cb (solder plus thermal vias) ≈ 2 K/W per FET
- R_ba ≈ 4–8 K/W for the whole board in prop-wash at ~10 m/s. On a 20×20 mm board this is the dominant term.

### 3.2 Short-Burst (Adiabatic) Board Rise

For t ≪ τ_board, the board absorbs nearly all the heat:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{m c_p}$$

|---|---|---|---|
