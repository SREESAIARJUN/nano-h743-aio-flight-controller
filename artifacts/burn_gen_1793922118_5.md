## 0. Architecture Clarification

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production "H743 + 4-in-1 ESC" stack, the work is split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot. Generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 firmware. 6-step commutation at **48 kHz PWM**, BEMF zero-cross detection. |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High-side and low-side drive. |
| Power stage | 24 N-MOSFETs (6 per motor) | Inverter bridges. |

Therefore:
- **Sections 1–3** (losses and thermals) apply to the ESC power stage.
- **Section 4** (DShot/DMA) applies to the H743.

### Feasibility warning

An 80 A *per motor* burst rating on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 "nano" footprint is not physically sustainable beyond a few seconds. The model below quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a ≤2–5 s transient rating, not a design point.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses (synchronous-rectified 6-step)

In 6-step commutation, phase current always flows through exactly two FET positions:
- one high-side position, which is PWM'd and alternates with its complementary low-side FET during the off-time, and
- one low-side position, which is held on.

With complementary (synchronous) rectification, the current is carried by an R_DS(on) element in both positions for essentially the entire period, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}\,k_T + I^2\,R_{pcb}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.0\times10^{-3})(1.5) + (80)^2(0.3\times10^{-3}) = 19.2 + 1.92 = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

The total is shared among 6 FETs. Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about **3.2 W** (copper loss excluded).

### 2.2 Switching (V-I overlap) losses

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^{3}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and dead time

| Loss | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | $P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | $P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | $P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw}$ | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | $P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2$ | **0.05 W** |

### 2.4 Totals

| Loss | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu) | 21.1 W | 84.4 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈23.8 W** | **≈95 W** |

### 2.5 Key insight: the effect of switching frequency

Conduction loss accounts for roughly 89% of the total. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore adds only about 1.3 W per motor (switching and dead-time terms roughly double).

The benefit of 48 kHz is lower current ripple and quieter motors. The cost is small at 80 A but proportionally larger at partial throttle.

Since dominant loss scales with **I²**:

| Phase current | Total loss (4 motors) |
|---|---|
| 40 A | ~25 W |
| 80 A | ~95 W |

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady state: not achievable

Typical junction-to-ambient resistance (R_θJA) is about 8–10 K/W for a 30×30 board in still air, and about 2–3 K/W with strong prop-wash airflow.

At 2.5 K/W: 95 W gives ΔT ≈ 240 K. **Continuous 80 A is impossible.** The burst rating is governed entirely by thermal capacitance.

### 3.2 Transient model (lumped Cauer network, per FET)

```
P_FET ─► [Rθjc=1.0 K/W] ─► Case ─► [Rθ_spread≈4 K/W] ─► Board Cu (C_board) ─► [Rθ_conv] ─► T_amb
           C_j ≈ 0.02 J/K          C_case ≈ 0.08 J/K
```

**Board thermal mass** (30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz):
- Copper: about 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.7 J/K
- FR4: about 4 g × 0.6 J/g·K ≈ 2.4 J/K
- FETs and components: about 0.5 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 4.6 J/K**

**Adiabatic board heating rate:**

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{95}{4.6} \approx \mathbf{20.6\ K/s}$$

**Junction-to-board rise per FET** (about 4 W per FET including switching share):

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 4 \times (1.0 + 4) \approx 20\ \text{K} \quad (\text{reached in roughly 0.1–0.3 s})$$

### 3.3 Burst duration limit

Starting at T_board = 50 °C, with T_j,max at 150 °C derated to 125 °C:

$$t_{burst} = \frac{125 - 50 - 20}{20.6} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ s}$$

Recovery after a burst:
- τ_cool = R_θBA × C_th ≈ 2.5 × 4.6 ≈ 11.5 s with airflow.
- Allow roughly 3τ (about 35 s) at cruise current before repeating a burst.

**Specification statement:** 80 A per motor for ≤2 s, 10% duty cycle, with prop-wash airflow. Rated continuous current is about 35–40 A per motor.

### 3.4 Mitigations (ranked)

1. **Lower R_DS(on):** use parallel FETs or 0.6 mΩ parts. Halving R_DS(on) cuts about 42 W total.
2. **Copper:** 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, with via stitching under the FET drain tabs (0.3 mm vias, ≥20 per FET).
3. **Thermal pad** to the frame or a heatsink plate. This adds C_th and reduces R_θBA.
4. **Firmware protection:** enable AM32 current limiting and temperature fold-back (thermistor near the hottest FET). Set ArduPilot `BATT_*` current limits.

## 4. ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 on the STM32H743

### 4.1 Protocol timing

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit rate | 600 kbit/s → T_bit = 1.667 µs |
| Frame | 16 bits (11 throttle, 1 telemetry request, 4 CRC) → 26.7 µs |
| Bi-directional mode | Inverted signalling. CRC is inverted. Line idles low. |
| Bit encoding | "1" ≈ 75% high, "0" ≈ 37.5% high |
| ESC response | About 30 µs after the frame end |
| Response encoding | 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 = **750 kbit/s** (T = 1.333 µs) |
