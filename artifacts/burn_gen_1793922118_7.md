## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does **not** run the 48 kHz motor PWM. The 4-in-1 ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs that run the commutation firmware and the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model therefore splits into two domains:

| Domain | Silicon | Firmware | Responsibility |

| Flight control | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm; LQFP100 won't fit 20×20) | ArduPilot | Bidir DShot600 TX/RX, eRPM telemetry, current/voltage ADC |

| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | — | High-/low-side gate drive |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification (complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T. The low-side synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side is fully on.

The duty terms cancel, so conduction loss is duty-independent:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of R_DS(on) is:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00150 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | **25.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** |

### 1.3 Electrothermal coupling (fixed point)

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS, which raises loss again. The coupled system is:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\,P(T_j), \qquad P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + P_{sw}
$$

Solving gives a closed form:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\,[\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw}\,]}{1 - R_{\theta,eff}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}
$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$
R_{\theta,eff}\cdot 2I^2 R_{25}\alpha \ge 1
$$

At 80 A this gives R_θ,eff ≥ 1/(2 × 6400 × 0.0015 × 0.0045) ≈ **11.6 K/W per channel**. A nano AIO with no heatsink is in this range under steady state. **80 A is therefore only valid as a time-limited burst** (§4).

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over rotation, the loss is roughly P_ch/6 per FET. The instantaneous peak, however, lands on the fully-on low-side: I²R = **13.4 W at 100 °C** for that 60° sector. At high eRPM the die thermal time constant (~ms) averages this. At low-speed high-torque (punch-out from hover), it does not.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (high-side, inductive load)

The rise and fall times are set by the Miller charge and gate current:

$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{6\,\text{nC}}{0.3\,\text{A}} = 20\,\text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Recovery current also adds to the high-side turn-on loss. With I_rr·t_rr ≈ Q_rr, this is roughly +0.04 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 20\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.012\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

Only the two PWM-leg FETs switch at f_sw, but six gates are budgeted for margin:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 6 = 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 6 = 0.086\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total and the frequency trade-off

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 1.94 + 0.08 + 0.01 + 0.61 + 0.09 \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W} \quad (\propto f_{sw},\ \approx\propto I)
$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about +1.35 W per channel at 80 A. In exchange, current ripple halves, so RMS heating in the motor and capacitors drops. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates about 10:1, so 48 kHz is justified.
