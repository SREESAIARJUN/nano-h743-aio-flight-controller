## 0. Scope and Feasibility

These points shape the rest of the model.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends commands over bidirectional DShot600. The **48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter** (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`), not an ArduPilot setting.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a burst rating only, measured in seconds.** Section 3 shows that sustained 80 A × 4 is about 100 W of dissipation in roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass. A 2–3 s burst is defensible. Continuous operation is not.
3. **Component values are representative, not tied to one part number.** Substitute your chosen FET's datasheet values into the equations. Every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Behavior
In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, AM32 default), two phases conduct at any time:
- During t_on, the current path is HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During t_off, the current freewheels through LS(A) → motor → LS(B).

So **two MOSFET channels always carry I_ph**, independent of duty cycle D. The duty only decides which FET of the PWM'd leg is on. Per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \left[ 2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \right] + I_{rms}^2 (R_{sh} + R_{cu})$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.3 RMS Current Including Ripple
The phase inductance L_ph gives peak-to-peak ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

Example for a 2306-class motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.5, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 6 V): ΔI ≈ 4.2 A, so I_rms ≈ 80.01 A. Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz, so take I_rms ≈ I_ph.

### 1.4 Numbers at T_j = 125 °C
- R_DS(125) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**
- FET conduction: 80² × 2 × 1.6 m = 6400 × 3.2 m = **20.48 W**
- Shunt and copper: 6400 × 0.55 m = **3.52 W**
- **P_cond per channel ≈ 24.0 W**, of which 10.24 W is in each conducting FET.

### 1.5 Electrothermal Fixed Point and Runaway Criterion
For one FET with junction-to-ambient resistance R_θ:

$$T_j = T_a + R_\theta\, I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for the fixed point:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta I^2 R_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when R_θ I² R₂₅ α ≥ 1.

At 80 A: I²R₂₅α = 6400 × 0.001 × 0.006 = 0.0384, so **R_θ must be below 26 K/W** to have any stable point at all. A nano-board FET in free air is about 35–50 K/W, so a steady state does not exist at 80 A. This confirms mathematically that 80 A is transient-only.

At 35 A (a plausible continuous rating), I²R₂₅α = 0.00735. With R_θ = 40 K/W and T_a = 40 °C:

$$T_j = \frac{40 + 40 \times 1.225 \times 0.85}{1 - 0.294} = \frac{81.65}{0.706} \approx 116\ °\text{C}$$

This is acceptable, which makes 35 A continuous / 80 A burst a consistent pair of ratings.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Its active FET (HS in complementary mode) takes the V–I overlap loss, and its synchronous partner takes the dead-time and recovery losses.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard Switching)
Transition times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{12\text{ nC}}{0.8\text{ A}} = 15\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so treat this as a lower bound. Use 2× margin, about 0.1 W.

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
