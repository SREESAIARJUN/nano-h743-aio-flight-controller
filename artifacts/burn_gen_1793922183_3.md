# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Architecture, Loss Models, DShot Allocation, Netlist

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the brief need correcting before design work starts.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, here an **AT32F421K8U7** or **STM32G071**. The H743 has no time budget for 4× sensorless 6-step commutation alongside the EKF. The 48 kHz PWM therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs, and DShot600 is the command link.

2. **80 A on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a *seconds-scale* burst, not a rating.** Section 3 shows four channels at 80 A dissipate about 115 W into a board with roughly 5 J/K of thermal mass. The resulting temperature rise is about 20 K/s. The honest spec is **80 A single-channel burst ≤2 s, 4×45 A continuous with airflow**. Anything else is marketing.

3. **DShot600 is set by ArduPilot parameters, not by the hwdef.** Bidirectional capture is the part that constrains timer and DMA choices (Section 5).

**Assumptions** (all "≈" values are typical datasheet figures; substitute the selected part's datasheet before release):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max |
| MOSFETs | 40 V class, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, 6 per channel, 24 total |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM
During any commutation sector, phase A is the PWM leg and phase B is the return leg:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** current flows LS_A (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_B.
- LS_B is on for the **whole sector**.

The per-FET RMS currents with phase current I and ripple ΔI are:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = D\left(I^2+\tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right),\quad I_{LS_A,rms}^2=(1-D)\left(I^2+\tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right),\quad I_{LS_B,rms}^2 = I^2+\tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The total conduction loss per channel is independent of D:

$$P_{cond}= 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\left(I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Because R rises with temperature, the loss feeds back on itself. The loop is self-consistent with T_j = T_board + P·Z_th(t), so solve iteratively. The design point uses T_j = 125 °C, giving R = 1.8 mΩ.

### 1.3 Ripple current
For a 2-pole-pair-equivalent winding, with phase-phase inductance L ≈ 15 µH (typical 2207 1900 KV motor) at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{15\mu\cdot48k}=8.75\ \text{A}\ \Rightarrow \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}=6.4\ \text{A}^2\ (\text{negligible vs }6400)$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per channel (125 °C) | 2 × 1.8 mΩ × 6406 = **23.1 W** |
| Hottest single FET (LS_B, 100 % of sector) | 1.8 mΩ × 6406 = **11.5 W** (rotates through 3 low-sides, so averaged over an electrical cycle ≈ 3.8 W each + its share of PWM duty) |
| Copper/shunt path (bus → FETs → pad, ≈0.35 mΩ incl. 0.25 mΩ shunt) | 80² × 0.35 m = **2.2 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg is static within a sector.

$$P_{sw}=\underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V–I overlap}}+\underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}}+\underbrace{Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{Coss charge/discharge (approx.)}}+\underbrace{2V_F I\,t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}$$

The gate drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$P_{gate}=2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$$

| Term | Calculation | W |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 |
| Q_rr | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 |
| Q_oss | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 |
| Dead-time diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.61 |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈2.66** |
| Gate (in driver) | 2 × 50 nC × 10 V × 48 k | 0.05 |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. At 24 kHz P_sw ≈ 1.3 W, but ripple doubles and audible and torque ripple increase. At 96 kHz P_sw ≈ 5.3 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise; AM32 exposes it as `PWM frequency`.

### Total per channel at 80 A, 125 °C

$$P_{ch}=23.1+2.66+2.2\approx \mathbf{28\ W}\quad(\eta_{stage}\approx1-\tfrac{28}{25.2\cdot80}=98.6\%)$$

For all four channels at 80 A: **P_tot ≈ 112 W**. Add about 3 W for the FC, BEC and LDOs, giving about **115 W**.
