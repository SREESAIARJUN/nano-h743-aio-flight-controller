## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Several requirements conflict at nano scale, so I am stating the assumptions up front.

| Item | Assumption / Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | H743 runs ArduPilot (FC). The 4-in-1 ESC uses **4× AT32F421 (AM32 firmware)**, one per motor. Each drives an FD6288Q-class 3-phase bootstrap gate driver. | The H743 cannot run 48 kHz six-step commutation for 4 motors and also run the EKF3/scheduler deterministically. DShot exists to decouple these. |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm mounting pattern, ~26×26 mm board outline, 8-layer, 2 oz inner / 2 oz outer copper | "Nano" class |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, design point V_bus = 25.2 V (max) | 40 V FETs give 1.6× margin for regenerative and ringing overshoot. |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25 = 1.5 mΩ (V_GS = 10 V), Q_g ≈ 50 nC, Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 15 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC | 24× 5×6 mm DFNs (~720 mm²) physically exceed a 676 mm² board side. **Replace all parameters with your selected part's datasheet values. The equations below are parametric.** |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead time 100 ns | AM32 standard mode |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility verdict (derived in §3):** 80 A per motor is achievable only as a **short burst (≈1–2 s with all four motors loaded simultaneously)** on a nano board with no heatsink. Continuous capability is roughly 25–35 A per motor with prop-wash airflow. The marketing "80 A burst" rating must be defined with a duration and a duty cycle, or it is meaningless.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This linear fit approximates R_DS(150 °C)/R_DS(25 °C) ≈ 1.75, which is typical of 40 V trench FETs. Use the datasheet's normalized curve if available.
