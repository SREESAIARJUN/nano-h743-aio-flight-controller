# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Design Reference, Job #56

## 0. Architecture and Stated Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, and in this one, the work is split across two kinds of processor:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC ×4 (one per motor) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) | AM32 |
| Gate drive ×4 | 3-phase bootstrap half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class) | n/a |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-ch 40 V MOSFET, PQFN 3.3×3.3 | n/a |

The responsibilities divide as follows:
- **48 kHz PWM** is generated by the ESC MCU (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`).
- **Bi-directional DShot600** is the link between the H743 timers and the AT32 input-capture pins.

Form factor: nano AIO, 25.5×25.5 mm PCB, 20×20 mm M2 mounting, 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers. Supply: 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max and 22.2 V nominal. The analysis uses 25.2 V as the worst case for switching loss.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model, 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Topology current paths
AM32 runs 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:
- **PWM phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier, with dead time excluded.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.
- **Floating phase:** carries no current (BEMF sensing).

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D) - \frac{2t_{dt}}{T_{sw}}\right] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used. That makes it the right worst-case model for a burst at any throttle.

Plugging in the burst values:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET loss
Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, and the loss splits evenly by symmetry:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{20.5}{6} = 3.41\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is in its 100%-on sector is 80² × 1.6 mΩ = 10.2 W. At eRPM ≥ 30k, each sector lasts under 0.35 ms. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant of about 5 ms, so the average value governs.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | P @ 80 A (per motor) |
|---|---|---|
| Per-ESC low-side shunt (2512, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Phase pad + plane spreading (2 oz × 8 L, est.) | 0.30 mΩ × 2 paths | 3.84 W |
| Bus planes, shared, @ 320 A total | 0.08 mΩ | 8.2 W (whole board) |

**Copper plane resistance check.** For 2 oz copper, R_sq = 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer. A phase path of about 3 squares, paralleled over 4 layers, gives 0.19 mΩ; adding via inductance/resistance brings the estimate to ≈ 0.30 mΩ.

**Design note:** the shared bus copper costs more than the shunts do. Use **4 dedicated V_BAT and 4 GND layers** in the 8-layer stackup (layers 2/3/6/7 power, 1/8 component and signal, 4/5 signal) and pad stitching with ≥ 40 vias of 0.3 mm per FET drain pad.
