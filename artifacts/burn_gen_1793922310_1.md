## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \textbf{[DS]}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths per motor

In 6-step synchronous mode, current always flows through one high-side and one low-side device:

- During the PWM on-time (D): HS_a + LS_b conduct.
- During the PWM off-time (1−D): LS_a (synchronous rectifier) + LS_b conduct.

So two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active for one third of the electrical cycle. Averaged per device:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}\quad(\text{distribution between HS and LS shifts with } D)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 80² × 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |
| P_cond per FET (avg) | 18.6 / 6 | 3.1 W |
| Instantaneous P while conducting | 80² × 1.45 mΩ | 9.3 W |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

These are often larger than designers expect:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot \frac{\rho_{Cu}\,(1+0.0039\Delta T)\,L}{w\,t}$$

For a 2 oz (70 µm) pour, 6 mm wide, 15 mm effective path, two layers in parallel:
R ≈ 1.72e-8 × 0.015 / (0.006 × 140e-6) ≈ **0.31 mΩ**, which gives **2.0 W per motor at 80 A**.

A 0.5 mΩ shunt adds I²R × duty share, about 1–3 W. Many 4-in-1 boards drop per-phase shunts and use a single DC-bus shunt instead.

**P_cond,total (per motor) ≈ 18.6 + 2.0 + ~1 ≈ 21.6 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg hard-switches. The low-side return FET (LS_b) is static during the commutation sector.

### 2.1 Loss components (per motor, one switching half-bridge)

**V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the sync FET during dead-time):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss,eff}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw}\quad(\times 2\text{ devices in leg})$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):
$$P_{g} = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\quad(\times 2)$$

### 2.2 Edge times

With gate resistance R_G and driver current:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+ Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,on}},\quad I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{DRV} - V_{plateau}}{R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}}$$

Example: Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 12 nC **[DS]**, V_DRV = 12 V, V_pl = 4.5 V, R_total = 10 Ω.
I_G = 0.75 A, so t_r ≈ 16 ns. Turn-off is similar with a lower plateau differential, giving t_f ≈ 24 ns. **t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns.**

### 2.3 Numbers (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz)

| Term | Values | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48k | **1.94 W** |
| P_rr | 60 nC **[DS]** × 25.2 × 48k | 0.07 W |
