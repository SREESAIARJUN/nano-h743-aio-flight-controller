## 0. Architectural Clarification and Design Basis

An STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any production 4-in-1 design, the system splits into two domains:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100) @ 400 MHz | ArduPilot, sensor fusion, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 running AM32 (or 1 MCU per channel with BLHeli_32) | Six-step / sinusoidal commutation, **48 kHz PWM**, telemetry reply |

The 48 kHz switching losses belong to the ESC MCUs and power stage. The H743 owns only the DShot timers and DMA.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

During any commutation sector, one high-side (HS) FET of phase A and one low-side (LS) FET of phase B conduct. With complementary PWM at duty D on the switched leg:

- **On-interval (D):** current flows through HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **Off-interval (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B.

In both intervals, exactly **two channels of R_DS(on)** are in series with the phase current, ignoring dead time. Therefore, independent of D:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The distribution is uneven. LS_B carries the full sector current, while HS_A and LS_A split it by D and (1−D). Rotation across sectors averages this. Per FET over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time on average.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase current carries PWM ripple Δi:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta i \approx \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Typical values: L_ph ≈ 15 µH (phase-to-phase / 2 for a 2207-class motor), D = 0.5, V = 25.2 V.

$$\Delta i = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\times10^{-6} \cdot 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \text{A}_{pp} \Rightarrow \frac{\Delta i^2}{12} = 6.4\ \text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

| T_j (°C) | R_DS(on) (mΩ) | P_cond per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt

| Element | Value | Loss per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + pour (2 oz, ~8 mm path) | ≈0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Shunt (if per-channel, 0.5 mΩ) | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W (only during HS/LS paths through shunt; ~1/1 duty in LS-return topology) |

**Design decision:** use a **single 0.25 mΩ total-current shunt** in the common GND return. At 4×80 A = 320 A, that is 25.6 W, which is unacceptable. Instead, **use per-channel 0.25 mΩ low-side shunts (1.6 W each)** or omit shunts and rely on the battery-side sensor. This draft uses per-channel 0.25 mΩ, 2512, 3 W (see netlist).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard-switching occurs on the PWM'd leg only (one HS/LS pair per channel at any time).

### 2.1 V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\times10^{3} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

More rigorously, t_r and t_f are dominated by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{G,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}} = \frac{9\,\text{nC}\cdot(4.7+1.5+\sim 3)\,\Omega}{12 - 4.5} \approx 11\ \text{ns}$$

That gives 11 ns of Miller time plus Q_gs2 time. The resulting ≈20 ns per edge is consistent with the design basis.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (after dead-time diode conduction)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, derate ×2 to **0.10 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \ (\text{HS+LS pair, approx.}) = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

This term is significant and is the main argument for minimizing dead time (AM32 `dead_time` parameter). Dropping to 60 ns saves 0.25 W per channel.
