# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Scope and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the power stage. In every production AIO and stacked design, the topology splits as follows:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --BDShot600 x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                       |-> 3-phase gate driver -> 6 N-MOSFETs per motor
                                       |-> 48 kHz PWM generated HERE
```

The 48 kHz switching-loss model therefore applies to the ESC MCU/FET stage. The DShot DMA model applies to the H743.

| Parameter | Value used |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (worst-case full charge) |
| Burst current per motor | I = 80 A (DC-link equivalent phase current) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss,eff = 1.5 nF, t_r = t_f = 20 ns |
| Temperature coefficient | k(T) = R(T)/R(25 °C) ≈ 1.5 at T_j = 110 °C |
| Dead time | t_dead = 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive | V_gs = 10 V |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous rectification) PWM |

> **Engineering flag:** 4 × 80 A = 320 A from a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is far outside what such a footprint can sustain. The model below quantifies exactly how short that burst must be. Substitute datasheet values for your actual FET; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation, current flows through exactly two phase legs at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **Switching leg:** the HS FET conducts for D and the LS FET (sync-rect) conducts for (1−D). One FET always carries the current.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held on and carries the current.

So at every instant, **two FET channels are in series with the motor current**, independent of D (dead time is treated in §2.4).

### 1.2 Equations

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ k(110^\circ C)\approx 1.5$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With PWM ripple, use $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. At 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~20 µH, line-to-line), ΔI_pp ≈ (V_bus·D(1−D))/(L·f) ≈ 6.5 A at D = 0.5. This adds about 0.05 % and is negligible.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$R_{hot} = 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0018 = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Each FET carries about 11.5 W time-averaged across commutation sectors. Peak instantaneous dissipation in a single FET during its conduction sector is about 11.5 W per conducting device. Because each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, the per-device average is ≈ 7.7 W, with thermal ripple at the electrical frequency.

### 1.4 Additional series resistances (per motor)

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if per-motor, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper, phase pad to FET (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Shared battery bus (at 320 A total, ~0.15 mΩ) | 0.15 mΩ | 15.4 W total (≈3.8 W/motor) |

Board copper losses are **not negligible** at this current. They often rival the switching losses.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the switching leg's HS/LS pair commutates at f_sw.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Rise and fall times depend on gate-driver current: $t_{r,f} \approx Q_{gd}/I_{drive}$. With Q_gd ≈ 8 nC and I_drive ≈ 0.4 A (e.g., an FD6288-class driver), the result is ≈ 20 ns.

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance (two FETs in the leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF}\times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$
