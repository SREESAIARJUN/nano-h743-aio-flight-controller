## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two premises in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

**1. The H743 does not run the ESC.**
On every viable 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It emits DShot600 frames to four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM and do BEMF commutation. The H743 has nowhere near enough advanced-timer complementary channels (24 needed for 4×3 half-bridges), and running ESC commutation inside ArduPilot is not supported. The model below therefore splits into:
- the **FC domain** (H743 to DShot to ESC MCU), and
- the **power domain** (ESC MCU to gate driver to MOSFETs at 48 kHz).

**2. "80 A burst per channel" on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a burst-only rating.**
The thermal calculations in Section 3 show it can only be sustained for a short time. Total burst input would be 4 × 80 A = 320 A, which the battery pads, the XT30/XT60 connector, and the board copper cannot carry. I model 80 A per channel as a **transient** and derive the maximum permissible burst duration. I do not treat it as a continuous rating.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Temperature-corrected on-resistance**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 2.0 × 1.6 = **3.2 mΩ**.

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- During t_on: HS(phase A) and LS(phase B).
- During t_off: LS(phase A) (sync-rect) and LS(phase B).

Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

**Ripple.** With motor phase inductance L ≈ 15 µH (two phases in series gives 30 µH) and worst case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{tot} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 4.4\,A$$

The ripple term, 4.4²/12 = 1.6 A², is negligible against 6400 A².

**Result**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 3.2\,m\Omega = \mathbf{40.96\ W}$$

**Per-device split.** Each MOSFET is in the current path roughly 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{40.96}{6} \approx 6.8\ W, \qquad P_{FET,peak} = 80^2 \times 3.2\,m\Omega = 20.5\ W$$

The peak figure applies while that FET is conducting, inside one commutation interval.

**Copper loss**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,m\Omega = 3.2\ W$$
