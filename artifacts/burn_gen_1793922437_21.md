# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

Several points need to be settled before the derivation, because they change the numbers.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. Each ESC channel runs its own commutation MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 with AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors. Everything below assumes this split.
2. **"Nano" vs 80A.** Twenty-four 5×6 mm MOSFETs plus bulk capacitance will not fit on a 20×20 board. The model assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz ESC board** stacked under the H743 FC. **80A is a burst rating (≈1–3 s), not a continuous one.** The thermal section shows why.
3. **Device parameters are representative.** Swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values. Every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the HS FET (for duty D) and the LS FET (for 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held on for the full 60° sector.

So at every instant the current flows through **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction
For the phase-current ripple, assume a line-to-line inductance L_LL = 15 µH:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\mu \cdot 48k} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple adds 0.1%, so it can be ignored at 48 kHz.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)
Treat R_DS(on) as a function of junction temperature:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}, \quad \alpha \approx 2.3$$

The worst case is T_j = 125 °C, where R = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6406 \times 2 \times 1.92\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W/channel}$$

Two smaller loss terms also apply:

- **Copper and connection losses.** Phase pads and the bus plane add about 0.3 mΩ per path. At 80 A this is ≈ 1.9 W per channel, spread across the board.
- **Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is in the kHz range, far faster than the thermal time constants, so per-FET heating averages to roughly 4.1 W.
