## 0. Architecture Clarification

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors itself. The correct partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100, preferred for nano) | ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 (QFN28/32) | AM32 (supports bidirectional DShot / EDT, 48 kHz PWM) |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 6-input, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN5×6 (one FET per switch position) | n/a |

The H743 talks to the four ESC MCUs over four bidirectional DShot600 lines. The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the AM32 configuration, not in ArduPilot.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step trapezoidal commutation

During each 60° commutation interval:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for fraction D. The low side conducts for (1 − D), which is synchronous rectification.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts for 100% of the interval.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The conduction loss per motor is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, because synchronous rectification removes the diode-conduction term from this calculation.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.36\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 2.2 Copper loss

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET averaging

Each switch position is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. At burst eRPM, the electrical frequency is around 1–3 kHz, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (around 10 ms). Each FET therefore sees a time-averaged dissipation:

$$
\bar{P}_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{motor,FET}}{6}
$$

### 2.4 Electro-thermal coupling

R_DS(on) depends on temperature:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Iterate this together with the thermal model in §4. The chosen k_T = 1.5 corresponds to T_j ≈ 125 °C, which is self-consistent with the burst limit derived in §4.4.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 k | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k | **1.23 W** |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.06 W** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistor) |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.8 W per motor** |

**Design sensitivities:**

- **Dead time.** Dead-time loss is nearly as large as overlap loss. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Verify that the shoot-through margin remains positive after the change.
- **Switching frequency.** Raising f_sw from 48 kHz to 96 kHz roughly doubles P_sw to about 5.6 W. The trade-off is smoother current and quieter motors.
