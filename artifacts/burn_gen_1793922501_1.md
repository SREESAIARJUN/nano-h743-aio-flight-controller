## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With trapezoidal (6-step) commutation and complementary PWM, two half-bridges conduct at every instant:

- **Driven leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is fully ON.

At any instant, the phase current therefore flows through exactly **two** channel resistances, independent of D (dead time is handled in §1.3):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence is modeled as:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, this gives R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.2 Burst conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

At 25 °C (cold start), the same calculation gives 12.8 W per motor.

**Per-FET distribution.** In 6-step operation, each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over a cycle, the 19.2 W is spread across 6 FETs, giving about **3.2 W average per FET**. The LS FETs carry a larger share at low throttle (D < 0.5), and the HS FETs carry more at high throttle.

### 1.3 Dead-time (body-diode) loss

During each dead-time interval, the current freewheels through a body diode. There are two transitions per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper loss

Assume the following path resistances:

- Bus and phase copper on 2 oz (70 µm) inner layers.
- A phase-pad-to-motor-wire path of about 0.25 mΩ.
- A shared DC-link path of about 0.3 mΩ.

Losses are then:

$$
P_{Cu,phase} = I^2 R_{phase} \times 2 = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega \times 2 = 3.2\text{ W per motor}
$$

$$
P_{Cu,bus} = I_{bus}^2 R_{bus}
$$

The bus current is approximately I·D per motor. With 4 × 80 A × D(≈0.85) ≈ 272 A total:

$$
P_{Cu,bus} = 272^2 \times 0.3\text{ m}\Omega \approx 22\text{ W}
$$

The second figure is a design-critical result. **At full 4-motor burst, bus copper dissipates as much as one motor's FETs.** The DC link must therefore use:

- 4 oz outer copper layers,
- solder-filled or bus-bar reinforcement on the battery pads, and
- short, symmetric paths from the XT60 to each half-bridge.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition loss

Per PWM period, one leg hard-switches (the HS FET commutates against the LS body diode or channel):

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} \, I \, (t_r + t_f) \, f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt. Budget 2× margin, giving 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

Using C_oss(er) ≈ 1.2 nF:

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.2\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.037\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Gate drive

Two FETs per leg are actively switched at f_sw (HS and complementary LS). The driver supply is V_drv = 10–12 V:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\text{ nC} \times 12 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.063\ W}
$$

This power dissipates in the driver IC and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.5 Total loss budget at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET channel) | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Hard switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Reverse recovery (2× margin) | 0.12 | 0.5 |
| C_oss | 0.04 | 0.15 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.25 |
| Phase copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| **Subtotal: ESC power stages** | **25.2** | **100.7** |
| DC-link copper (shared) | — | ≈22 |
| Input capacitor ESR (§2.7) | — | ≈1–3 |
| **Board total, 4×80 A burst** | — | **≈125 W** |

Mechanical output at 4 × 80 A × 22.2 V × D ≈ 6 kW gives an electrical efficiency of about 98%. The problem is not efficiency; it is **heat flux density** on a 9.3 cm² board.

**f_sw sensitivity.** Switching-related terms scale linearly with f_sw. Doubling to 96 kHz adds about 2.6 W per motor. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor but increases current ripple and acoustic noise. Conduction dominates (≈76%), so **R_DS(on) is the primary lever**, not f_sw.

### 2.6 Thermal model

#### Steady state (proves "burst" is mandatory)

A 30×30 mm board in prop wash has an effective board-to-ambient resistance R_θBA of about 1.5–2.5 K/W. At 125 W:

$$
\Delta T_{BA} = 125 \times 2 = 250\text{ K}
$$

This is not survivable, so **80 A must be time-limited**.

The sustainable continuous power for ΔT_BA = 70 K (40 °C ambient to a 110 °C board) is about 35 W total. That corresponds to approximately:

$$
I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{\frac{35}{125}} \approx \mathbf{42\ A\ per\ motor\ (all\ four)}
$$

This is consistent with the 45 A continuous target.

#### Transient (lumped capacitance + junction Foster network)

Board thermal capacitance:

- FR4: 30×30×1.6 mm ≈ 2.6 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 2.9 J/K
- Copper (8 layers, mixed 2 oz / 4 oz
