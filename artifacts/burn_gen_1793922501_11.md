# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #81)

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The numbers below depend on parameters not given in the brief, so each assumption is tagged **[A#]**. Replace them with datasheet values before release.

| ID | Assumption |
|---|---|
| A1 | Architecture: the STM32H743 is the **flight controller** only. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 does not run commutation. |
| A2 | Bus voltage 6S: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. |
| A3 | "80 A burst" means **per motor**, all 4 simultaneously (worst case). |
| A4 | MOSFET: 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on),max = 1.6 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V. One FET per switch position (6 per motor, 24 total). |
| A5 | Board 30.5×30.5 mm (a "nano" 20×20 board is addressed in §2.5), 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers. |
| A6 | Complementary (synchronous) trapezoidal PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time 100 ns. |
| A7 | Ambient 40 °C, T_j,max = 150 °C, design limit 125 °C. |

**Up-front verdict:** 80 A per motor continuous is not thermally achievable on a nano-class board. 80 A is a **~1–1.5 s burst** rating. The model in §2 derives that limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.6 × 1.6 = **2.56 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path

In 120° trapezoidal commutation, two phases are active at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current path always runs through exactly one switch in each active leg:

- During the on-time: HS(A) → motor → LS(B)
- During the off-time: LS(A) freewheel → motor → LS(B)

So the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle, to first order.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With n FETs paralleled per position, R → R/n.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.56\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{32.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period on average:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 32.8/6 = 5.5\ \text{W}$$

The worst FET is the always-on low side of the "return" leg at low duty. Its loss is bounded by:

$$P_{FET,peak-share} = I^2R \times \tfrac{1}{3}\times 2 \approx 10.9\ \text{W}$$

over its 120° window, averaged across the 60° sectors. Design to **≈5.5 W average and ≈11 W intra-cycle**. The thermal mass smooths the difference at eRPM > 1 kHz.

### 1.4 Copper conduction

For a 2 oz layer, R_□ = 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, rising ~1.4× at 100 °C. Assume a phase/bus path of about 4 squares on 4 paralleled layers:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{4 \times 0.25 \times 1.4}{4} = 0.35\ \text{mΩ}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.35\text{ mΩ} \times 2 \approx 4.5\ \text{W per motor}$$

This copper loss is non-trivial and often ignored. Use heavy copper pours and stitch them with via arrays.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches; the freewheel transition is soft (ZVS via the body diode).

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

The rise/fall times come from the gate charge and driver current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv}} \cdot \text{(plateau)}$$

Assume Q_gd = 8 nC and driver current ≈ 0.4 A, giving t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode is recovering at HS turn-on. Assume Q_rr = 40 nC:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

Assume Q_oss = 30 nC:

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V f \times 2 = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver IC and gate resistors, not the FET. Assume Q_g = 50 nC and V_drv = 10 V:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
