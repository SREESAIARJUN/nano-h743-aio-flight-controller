# STM32H743 FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #69): System Design Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Corrections

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware for H7. The correct architecture uses four ESC MCUs:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32, or EFM8/BB21 / Bluejay)
                                               └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

**Reality check on "80 A on a nano board".** 80 A per motor (up to 320 A·D bus current) on a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board is a **few-second burst rating**. It is not a continuous rating. The math in §3 shows this explicitly. Market it as "80 A burst (≤5 s), ~35–45 A continuous with airflow."

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM). At every instant, the motor current flows through exactly **two** FET channels:

- **ON interval (duty D):** high-side of phase A, then the motor, then low-side of phase B.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then low-side of phase B.

Therefore, independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

For phase inductance L_ph, the peak-to-peak ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

The RMS current then becomes:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}}$$

With L_ph ≈ 10 µH and 48 kHz, ΔI is about 4–6 A. The correction is under 0.3% at 80 A, so it is neglected.

### 1.3 Temperature-Coupled R_DS(on)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Junction temperature in turn depends on dissipated power:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\, Z_{th}$$

Solve this pair by fixed-point iteration (it converges in 3–4 steps). Here I take T_j ≈ 110 °C, which gives R ≈ 1.05 mΩ.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The loss therefore averages to roughly 13.4 / 6 ≈ **2.24 W per FET**. The electrical period at cruise is under 1 ms, which is much shorter than the die thermal time constant.

**Caveat.** Below about 1–2k eRPM (stall or startup), the instantaneous loss of 6.7 W sits in one FET pair long enough to heat the die. AM32's stall protection must cover this case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The complementary low-side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·600 ns·48 k | 1.84 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (×2 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw ×2 | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 3.0 W per motor** |

**Design levers.**
- Dead time is the largest switching term. Reducing it from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per motor. Validate that change against shoot-through using the driver's propagation-delay spread.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz doubles the switching loss (to about 6 W) and buys only marginal smoothness.

### 2.1 Total Power Budget (80 A, all four motors)

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.4 W | 53.6 W |
| FET switching | 3.0 W | 12.0 W |
| Phase and bus copper (see below) | ~1.0 W | ~4 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used) | ~1.6 W·D_eff | ~3 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 70 W** |

The copper estimate assumes 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers with a parallel pour, about 0.15 mΩ effective per phase path. It applies the same I²R model as the FETs.
