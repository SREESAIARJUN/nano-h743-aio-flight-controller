## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_hot = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**. Use ≈1.8 mΩ to include the package and solder joint.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd and one low-side stays fully on:

- **High-side (active leg):** conducts for duty D.
- **Low-side (same leg, sync rectifier):** conducts for (1−D).
- **Low-side (return leg):** conducts 100%.

Phase current always flows through exactly two FETs, so the instantaneous conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

Per-FET loss, averaged over the electrical cycle (each leg active 2/3 of the time):

$$P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot} D, \qquad P_{LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}(2 - D)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}$$

The worst-case single FET is the low-side at low D (instantaneous, while it is the return path):

$$P_{FET,inst} = 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 11.5\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Shunt and copper

- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ, per channel or shared): P = I²R = 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. Use 0.2 mΩ or a 2512/4-terminal part.
- **Copper:** for 2 oz copper (≈0.25 mΩ/sq), a 3-square path gives ≈0.75 mΩ, so **≈4.8 W per channel at 80 A**. This is non-trivial; stack the internal planes (see §5).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. These losses are per channel.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is as large as the overlap loss. Trimming dead time to about 100 ns, if the driver and FET allow it, saves about 0.6 W per channel.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W/FET}$$

Two FETs switch per channel, giving ≈0.06 W. The FD6288Q bootstrap and LDO add about 0.1 W.
