## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Warning

### 0.1 Partitioning

The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate motors directly, because ArduPilot contains no ESC firmware. The ESC side uses four dedicated motor MCUs running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware), connected to the H743 over bidirectional DShot600.

```
 6S LiPo ──┬── Shunt ── VBUS rail ──┬── ESC1: AT32F421 + FD6288Q + 6×MOSFET ── M1
           │                        ├── ESC2 ...                              ── M2
           │                        ├── ESC3 ...                              ── M3
           │                        └── ESC4 ...                              ── M4
           └── BEC 10V (VTX) / 5V / 3V3 ── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot)
                                            └── DShot600 BIDIR ×4 ──► ESC MCUs
```

### 0.2 Feasibility warning on "80A burst" for a nano form factor

On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm AIO board, 80A per motor is **not a continuous rating**. Section 1.6 shows that four motors at 80A put about 80W into a board with roughly 5 J/K of thermal mass, which gives about 3 s before the copper reaches its limit. The defensible marketing and spec language is:

> **80A burst (≤2 s, 25°C ambient, airflow), 45A continuous.**

All calculations below make this explicit.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110°C:

$$
R_{DS}(110) = 1.0 \times [1 + 0.0045 \times 85] = 1.38\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 1.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, phase current flows through exactly **two** FETs at every instant. During the PWM on-time this is the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B. During the off-time it is the low side of phase A (synchronous) and the low side of phase B. The energy is therefore duty-independent per motor:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.4\times10^{-3} = 17.9\ \text{W}
$$
