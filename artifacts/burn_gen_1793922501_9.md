# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC (Job #53): Power-Stage and Motor-Timing Design Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Findings

Three problems with the spec need resolving before the design can proceed. The math below supports each one.

| # | Issue | Finding | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **H743 as ESC controller** | The H743 is the flight controller. 4-in-1 ESCs run 4 independent motor MCUs (AM32/BLHeli_32 class: STM32G071, AT32F421, GD32E230). Bidirectional DShot is the protocol *between* the H743 and those MCUs. | Partition into an H743 FC section and 4× ESC MCU + gate driver + 6 FETs. This document models both. |
| F2 | **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board** | Total burst current is 4 × 80 = 320 A. Dissipation is about 125 W on roughly 4 cm² of PCB. The adiabatic limit is under 1 s (§3.4). Battery pads, bus copper and caps cannot carry 320 A at 20×20. | Choose one: (a) 30.5×30.5 or larger at 80 A burst, or (b) nano at 35–40 A continuous / 45 A burst. The model is parameterized so you can re-run either case. |
| F3 | **48 kHz PWM** | This is set in the ESC MCU firmware (AM32 `PWM_FREQ`), not by the H743. Switching loss scales linearly with it (§2). | Keep it, but budget about 2.4 W/channel at 80 A. Consider 24 kHz for heavy-lift motors. |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Modulation assumption

AM32-style trapezoidal drive is assumed. In each 60° commutation sector:

- The PWM phase high-side (HS) switches at duty *D*, and its low-side (LS) runs complementary at 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- The sink phase LS is held fully ON.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

Each phase therefore spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the sink leg, and 1/3 floating.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Use the datasheet normalized curve where available. A typical 40 V trench FET reaches ≈1.5× at 125 °C.

### 1.3 Per-device average conduction loss (phase current *I*)

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac13\,D\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$
$$
P_{LS} = \tfrac13(1-D)\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j) + \tfrac13\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The per-channel total is independent of *D*, because two devices are always in the current path:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The worst device is the LS FET at low duty: up to ⅔·I²R.

### 1.4 Worked numbers (6S, 80 A burst)

Assumed FET: 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS,25 = 1.5 mΩ (V_GS = 10 V), R_DS at T_j = 125 °C ≈ 2.3 mΩ.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I²·R (hot) | 6400 × 0.0023 = **14.7 W** |
| P_cond per channel | **29.4 W** |
| Worst FET (LS, D→0) | 9.8 W |
| Typical FET (D≈1, full throttle) | 4.9 W |
| P_cond, 4 channels | **≈118 W** |

R_DS is temperature-dependent, so this must be solved self-consistently. Iterate T_j ← T_a + P(T_j)·Z_th until it converges; §3.3 covers this. With 5×6 mm FETs at 0.7 mΩ (which only fit on 30×30 and up), the 4-channel figure drops to about 55 W.

### 1.5 Copper/shunt adders (often overlooked)

- **Bus/trace:** R_trace = ρ·L/(w·t). Example: 2 oz (70 µm) internal layer, 6 mm wide, 15 mm long gives ≈0.6 mΩ per layer. Paralleling 4 layers gives ≈0.15 mΩ, so 80 A × 2 paths adds about 1.9 W/channel.
- **Current shunt:** a 0.25 mΩ shunt at 80 A adds 1.6 W of system current-sense loss (shared shunt).
