# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the Job #47 spec sheet, so this design states its assumptions up front. Every constant is in one config block, so you can swap in your real motor and frame parameters.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, single-precision FPU, CORDIC optional |
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder per axis (AS5048A / MT6701) |
| Current sense | 2-shunt low-side per axis (a voltage-mode fallback is given in §1.5) |
| IMU A | ICM-42688-P on the **camera payload**: attitude and rate feedback |
| IMU B | ICM-42688-P on the **handle/base**: disturbance feedforward and vibration-frequency estimation |
| Gimbal order | Base → Yaw (z) → Roll (x) → Pitch (y) → Camera |

**Loop rates (bandwidth separation of at least 5× per stage):**

| Loop | Sample rate | Closed-loop bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| PWM | 40 kHz center-aligned | — |
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz |
| Rate/velocity | 4 kHz (IMU ODR 8 kHz, decimated ×2) | ω_v ≈ 2π·150 Hz |
| Attitude/angle | 1 kHz | ω_θ ≈ 2π·25 Hz |
| Cogging LMS adaptation | 1 kHz (background) | — |

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Electrical angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{0}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

Here θ₀ is the encoder offset, found at commissioning by applying v_d > 0 at θ_e = 0 and reading θ_m.

### 1.2 Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, i_a + i_b + i_c = 0)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

### 1.3 Park transform and its inverse

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha\\ v_\beta\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & -\sin\theta_e\\ \sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v_d\\ v_q\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Delay compensation.** Use θ_e + 1.5·ω_e·T_s for the inverse Park. This accounts for one sample of computation delay plus half a sample of PWM zero-order hold.
