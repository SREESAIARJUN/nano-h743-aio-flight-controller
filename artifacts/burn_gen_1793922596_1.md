## 1. System Architecture

```
          ┌──────────────────────── FC domain (3V3) ───────────────────────┐
 VBAT ──► │ STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz   IMU (ICM-42688-P, SPI1)              │
 (6S)     │  ├─ TIM3_CH3/CH4, TIM5_CH1/CH2 ─► Bidir DShot600 (4 lines)     │
          │  ├─ ADC: VBAT divider, ESC current sense (summed shunt amp)    │
          │  └─ UARTs, SDMMC/flash, baro (I2C2), OSD (SPI2)                │
          └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     │ DShot (single-wire, half-duplex, 3V3, 100 Ω series)
          ┌──────────▼──────────────── ESC domain (×4) ─────────────────────┐
          │ ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32-class FW)  │
          │  └─ 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q-class, bootstrap, 5–12 V)      │
          │      └─ 6 × N-MOSFET half-bridges ─► Motor phases A/B/C         │
          └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Important architectural point.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU that runs the commutation firmware. The H743 sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry over the bidirectional DShot line. Driving 12 half-bridges with 48 kHz synchronous PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection, and ArduPilot's scheduler all on one H743 is not a supported configuration.

## 3. Mathematical Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 3.1 Conduction Topology

Use six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification:

- At any instant, current flows through one high-side path and one low-side path.
- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS(x) and LS(y).
- During the off-time, the complementary sync FET conducts in place of the body diode.

Therefore the current traverses **two R_ds(on) in series 100 % of the time**, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

With α ≈ 0.005 /K, the multiplier at 125 °C is ≈ 1.5:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.4\text{ m}\Omega \times 1.5) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.1\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{26.9\ W}
$$

This splits as about 13.4 W in each of the two conducting FET positions. That is averaged across the three legs over an electrical revolution, but the peak per-device load is what matters for transient thermal behaviour.

**Self-consistent R_ds(on) vs. T_j iteration.** Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds(on), which raises loss:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\cdot 2I^2 R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j^{(n)}-25)\right]/2
$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the loop gain reaches 1:

$$
\alpha\, Z_{th}\, I^2 R_{25} \ge 1
$$

At 80 A, R_25 = 1.4 mΩ: α·I²·R_25 = 0.005 × 8.96 = 0.0448 /K. Runaway therefore occurs for an effective Z_th ≥ **22 K/W**. Any steady-state path to ambient worse than about 22 K/W per FET cannot sustain 80 A at all, which is the case for nano boards without forced cooling.

### 3.2 Switching (V-I Overlap) Loss

Hard switching occurs only on the PWM-switched FET of each commutation step. With sync rectification, the complementary FET switches at near-zero voltage:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Capacitive, Recovery, Dead-Time, and Gate Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| C_oss | ½ C_oss V² f_sw = 0.5 × 1 nF × 635 × 48 k | 0.015 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | 6 Q_g V_drv f_sw = 6 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.086 W (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |

### 3.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 26.9 W | 107.5 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time + Q_rr + C_oss | 0.69 W | 2.8 W |
| Gate drive | 0.09 W | 0.3 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ path) | ≈1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈30.5 W** | **≈122 W** |

Conduction loss dominates at about 88 %. The switching frequency matters very little at this current. **The design lever is R_ds(on) and copper, not f_sw.** The 48 kHz choice is justified by current ripple and acoustics. Its approximately 1 W switching penalty per channel is acceptable.

**Efficiency check.** Assume roughly 25 V × 80 A × modulation ≈ 1.6–2 kW electrical per motor at full throttle. 30 W of loss is about 98 % efficiency. That figure looks good, but it is concentrated in about 6 mm² of silicon per FET.

### 3.5 Frequency Sensitivity

$$
\frac{\partial P_{total}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac{1}{2}V I (t_r+t_f) + \tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 + Q_{rr}V + 2V_{SD}I t_{dt} + 6 Q_g V_{drv}
\approx 33\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

| f_sw | Δ loss vs. 48 kHz |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | −0.8 W per motor |
| 96 kHz | +1.6 W per motor |

### 3.6 Transient Thermal Model (Burst Capability)

Use a two-node Foster network:

**Node 1: Junction to local copper.** Use Z_th,J-local ≈ R_θJC + R_spread. For 2 oz copper with via arrays under the pad, R_spread ≈ 3 K/W. This node has a time constant τ₁ of tens of ms, so it reaches its final value within the burst:

$$
\Delta T_1 = P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{spread}) = 14.3\text{ W} \times 4.5 = 64\text{ K}
$$

Here P_FET is about 13.4 W conduction plus about 1 W switching/dead-time on the switched device.

**Node 2: Whole-board bulk heating.** This node is near-adiabatic for bursts under about 5 s, because R_board→air is roughly 8–15 K/W with prop wash.
