# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Architectural Correction

I don't have a requirements document for "Job #25," so every number below rests on the stated assumptions. Each one is a parameter: replace it with your datasheet value and the equations still hold.

**Architectural correction.** Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between the flight controller and a separate ESC MCU. If the H743 drove the gate drivers directly, DShot would be unnecessary, and you would also need 12 complementary PWM outputs plus BEMF/FOC ADC timing on the same chip that runs EKF3. Doing that conflicts with ArduPilot's architecture. The model therefore assumes:

- **H743** runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to:
- **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071)** running AM32, one per motor, each driving a 3-phase bridge, all on the same PCB stack.

| MOSFET | 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ Vgs = 10 V, T_j = 25 °C |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

Trapezoidal or FOC operation with complementary PWM means current always flows through exactly one FET per active leg, either the high side (HS) during D or the low side (LS) during 1−D. Two legs carry the phase current at any instant. The duty cycle therefore cancels out of the per-channel total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{HS}\,D + I^2 R_{LS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS/HS,\,return} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

(assuming R_HS ≈ R_LS).

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This is self-consistent with T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right) \cdot R_{th,j\text{-}board}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per channel = 2 · 6400 · R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.7 mΩ | 21.8 W |

Add copper trace and solder resistance. A realistic estimate is 0.3 mΩ per current path (battery pad → bridge → phase pad), which contributes:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\text{ m}\Omega \approx 1.9\text{ W per channel}$$

This loss lands in the board, not in the junctions.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, one leg is hard-switched at any instant.

**(a) V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\text{ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance (both FETs of the switching leg):**
$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\text{ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.307\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (2 FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

$$\boxed{P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.42\text{ W}}$$

The dominant terms are V–I overlap and dead time. Cutting dead time from 50 ns to 30 ns saves about 0.12 W per channel. Dropping to 24 kHz halves P_sw but raises current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.1 Total at 80 A burst (T_j = 100 °C)

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.4 W | 5.7 W |
| Copper / solder | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| **Total** | **22.5 W** | **≈ 90 W** |

Efficiency at the 80 A point: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A ≈ 1776 W per channel, so η ≈ 98.7%. Efficiency is not the problem; power density is.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

```
T_j ──R_jc(0.5 K/W)── T_case ──R_solder/vias(1.5 K/W)── T_board ──R_conv── T_amb
                                                           │
                                                      C_board (J/K)
```

### 3.2 Board heat capacity

| Material | Calculation | Mass | Specific heat | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | 9.3 cm² × 8 × 70 µm | 4.67 g | 0.385 J/gK | **1.80 J/K** |
| FR4 | 9.3 cm² × 1.6 mm | 2.75 g | 1.1 J/gK | **3.0 J/K** |
| FETs (24×) and passives | — | — | — | **~0.3 J/K** |
| **Total C_board** | | | | **≈ 5.1 J/K** |

### 3.3 Convection with prop wash

Using h ≈ 50 W/m²K over both faces (A = 1.86×10⁻³ m²):

$$R_{conv} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 10.7\text{ K/W}$$

**Continuous capability:** with T_amb = 40 °C and T_board,max = 105 °C:

$$P_{cont} = \frac{65}{10.7} \approx 6\text{ W total}$$

This corresponds to roughly 22–25 A continuous per channel with all four motors loaded, and about 35 A if only one is loaded. An "80 A" rating on a nano board is strictly a burst rating.

### 3.4 Burst duration

Because τ = R·C ≈ 55 s is much longer than the burst, the burst is nearly adiabatic:

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{conv}\left(1 - e^{-t/RC}\right) \approx T_0 + \frac{P}{C} t$$

The junction sits above the board by the per-FET loss times the junction-to-board resistance. Per-FET average loss is about (19.2 + 1.4)/6 ≈ 3.4 W, with peaks around 5 W on the switching leg. With R_j-board = 2 K/W, ΔT_j-b ≈ 7–10 K.

Limit: T_j ≤ 125 °C, so T_board ≤ ~115 °C. Star
