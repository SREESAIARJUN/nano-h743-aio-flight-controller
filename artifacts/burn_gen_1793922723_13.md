## 1. Loss Model (per ESC, one motor)

**Commutation model:** trapezoidal six-step with synchronous rectification. At any instant two legs conduct: one high-side FET in phase A and one low-side FET in phase B. Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The high-side FET of the active pair hard-switches at f_sw, and the low side free-wheels synchronously.

### 1.1 Conduction losses

The temperature-adjusted on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The current path always passes through two FET channels (series high side plus low side, counting the synchronous free-wheel FET):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts one third of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot I^2 R = \tfrac{1}{3}(9.6) = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Ripple correction:** this assumes DC-like phase current. With ripple ΔI the RMS current rises:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 20 µH, D = 0.9, V_bemf = 20 V gives ΔI = 4.9 A. The resulting increase is under 0.04 %, so it is negligible at 48 kHz.

### 1.2 Switching losses (hard-switched FET)

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Rise and fall times derived from the gate charge, where I_g is about 1 A for an FD6288 source with 2 Ω:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}} = \frac{12\text{n}\times 4.5}{10-3.5} \approx 8.3\ \text{ns (Miller only)}$$

The baseline of 40 ns total includes the Q_gs2 interval and layout inductance. Lower values are achievable, but they increase ringing and EMI.

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery (low-side body diode):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs per PWM cycle, worst case):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-ESC total at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 | 85 % |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.94 | 9 % |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 5 % |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 | <1 % |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side sensed) | 1.60 | (add if present) |
| **Total (without shunt)** | **≈22.5 W** | |

Efficiency at 25.2 V × 80 A × D ≈ 0.9 (1814 W) is about **98.8 %**.

**Design levers, in order of impact:**
1. **R_ds(on).** Paralleling 2 FETs per switch halves the dominant 19.2 W term to 9.6 W.
2. **Dead time.** Going from 200 to 100 ns saves 0.6 W; tune DEAD_TIME in AM32 against shoot-through margin.
3. **f_sw.** Switching losses scale linearly with frequency. At 24 kHz you save about 1.6 W, but motor ripple and acoustic noise rise.
