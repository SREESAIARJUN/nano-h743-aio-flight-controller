# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

You did not specify a MOSFET, a board size, or a cell count. All numbers below come from a **declared parameter set**. The equations stay valid if you substitute your datasheet values.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|

| I_pk | 80 A burst per motor | phase current, 6-step trapezoidal |

| R_ds,25 | 1.0 mΩ | 40 V MOSFET, PDFN 5×6 class |
| α_R | 0.0055 /K | R_ds temperature coefficient |
| Q_gs2 + Q_gd = Q_sw | 6 + 10 = 16 nC | switching charge |
| Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V | total gate charge |

| R_g,int | 1 Ω | internal gate resistance |

| ESC MCU | AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32 | the H743 commands the ESC; it does not commutate |

**Architectural note.** The H743 never sees 48 kHz. It only emits DShot frames. The 48 kHz switching lives in the four ESC MCUs. The two domains are coupled by thermal design and by ground/EMI, not by firmware.

**Physics caveat.** 80 A × 4 on a 20×20 or 25×25 board is a seconds-scale burst rating, not continuous. The model in Section 3 quantifies exactly how many seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current distribution, 6-step synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry I. Phase A's high side switches with duty D, its low side is the synchronous complement (1−D), and phase B's low side is held on.

Each FET conducts in a 120° window, i.e. 1/3 of the electrical period.

**Per-device RMS currents over one electrical cycle:**

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D\,I^2}{3},\qquad
I_{LS,sync,rms}^2 = \frac{(1-D)\,I^2}{3},\qquad
I_{LS,on,rms}^2 = \frac{I^2}{3}
$$

Because PWM rotates through the legs, each low-side FET is the "held-on" device for 1/3 of its window and the "sync" device elsewhere. Averaged over the cycle, the total per channel is simply:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Two devices are always in series, regardless of D.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds (coupled solution)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}
$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration. It converges in about 3 steps because $\partial P/\partial T \cdot R_\theta \ll 1$.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | Per FET (÷6, average) | Worst FET (D→1 HS / held LS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W | 3.01 W | 3.01 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W | 3.30 W | 3.30 W |

**Parasitics often ignored that matter at 80 A:**

- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ low-side, if present): $I^2 R_{sh}\cdot\frac{2}{3}$ gives about 1.07 W per channel.
- **Copper.** 2 oz (70 µm) copper is ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq. A 3 mm × 8 mm phase run is 2.7 sq, or 0.67 mΩ. At 80 A that is about 4.3 W per conducting trace pair. Use stacked inner planes plus via farms to get below 0.2 mΩ.
- **Solder and vias.** Budget 0.1 mΩ per device.

**Realistic total, conduction plus parasitics:** ≈ **22 W per channel at T_j ≈ 110 °C**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel hard-switches at a time (6-step).

### 2.1 Transition times (Miller-plateau model)

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{10-3.5}{4} = 1.63\ \text{A}
\;\Rightarrow\; t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 9.8\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{3.5}{3} = 1.17\ \text{A}
\;\Rightarrow\; t_{off} = 13.7\ \text{ns}
$$

(Add external R_g only if EMI or ringing demands it. Every 1 Ω roughly adds 15 % to t_sw.)

### 2.2 Loss components (per switching leg)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw}$ | 0.5·25.2·80·23.5n·48k = **1.14 W** |
| C_oss | $Q_{oss} V f_{sw}$ | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
