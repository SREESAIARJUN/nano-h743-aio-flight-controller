## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three issues need resolving before any numbers matter.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot to four (or one quad-core) ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The DShot/DMA plan (§4) applies to the H743; the loss model (§2) applies to the ESC power stage.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a thermal claim, not a silicon claim.** The FETs can conduct 80 A, but the board cannot dissipate the resulting heat for long. §3 derives the actual time limit. Expect about 1–3 s at 80 A per motor and about 20–28 A continuous with prop-wash cooling. Any spec sheet should state "80 A, ≤1 s burst" explicitly.

3. **48 kHz PWM** is valid (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz). It trades switching loss for lower motor current ripple and quieter operation. Its cost is quantified in §2.3.

**Commutation model:** 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification. The high side is PWM-modulated at duty D, and the complementary low side conducts during (1−D). At any instant, current flows through exactly two legs, meaning two FET channels in series.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ** (the design value used below).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

**Per motor.** Two conducting channels in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.00225 = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

This expression is independent of D because synchronous rectification keeps a channel in the path at all times.
