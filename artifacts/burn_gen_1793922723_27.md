## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC requires a commutation MCU per motor, or one MCU per two motors. This design uses:

| Block | Device (candidate) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, DShot600 bidirectional master |
| ESC MCU ×4 | STM32G071GBU6 or AT32F421K8U7 | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | High-side and low-side drive |
| Power FETs ×24 | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN (see 0.3) | 6 per motor |
| Current sense | 0.2 mΩ shunt + INA186A3 (or ESC-MCU op-amp) | Battery current |

### 0.2 Feasibility warning

A "nano" board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting, about 30×30 mm PCB) cannot dissipate 80 A continuous per motor. Section 2.5 shows that at full 4-motor load the board reaches its thermal limit in about **2 seconds** under adiabatic conditions. Treat "80 A" strictly as a **time-bounded burst rating**. The model below quantifies that bound and does not endorse 80 A as a continuous figure.
