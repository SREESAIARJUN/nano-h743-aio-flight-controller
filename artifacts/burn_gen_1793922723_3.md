# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Loss, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.**
- The H743 runs ArduPilot only. It does not commutate motors.
- Each motor has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32: STM32G071 (or AT32F421) driving an FD6288Q-class 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.
- Link: H743 → ESC MCU via bi-directional DShot600, one wire per motor.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification
At any instant, motor current flows through two legs. One leg PWMs: the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for 1−D. The other leg's LS FET is on continuously. Current therefore always passes through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution
Peak-to-peak ripple current:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{pp} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

RMS current including ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds less than 0.1 %, so it is negligible at 80 A. It becomes significant only below about 10 A or with low-L motors under 5 µH.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A
$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

This is about 11.5 W in each conducting FET at a 125 °C junction. At 25 °C it would be 15.4 W. This is a strong positive feedback loop, which Section 4 resolves iteratively.

### 1.5 Copper path losses
Phase/bus pour: 2 oz (70 µm), 4 mm wide, 5 mm long, 4 parallel layers.

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 5\times10^{-3}}{4\times10^{-3} \times 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.307\ \text{mΩ/layer} \rightarrow 0.077\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.077\ \text{mΩ} \approx 0.49\ \text{W per current path}$$

Budget about 1 W per motor for copper (phase out, GND return, and V_bus share).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only the PWMing leg switches. Per PWM period there is one turn-on and one turn-off.

### 2.1 Transition time from gate drive
Gate current at the Miller plateau (about 3.5 V):

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plat}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{2.2 + 1.0} \approx 2.0\ \text{A}$$

The ideal transition is t ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 4 ns. However, the FD6288 source/sink limit (about 1.5/1.8 A) plus common-source inductance stretch this. **Design value: t_r + t_f = 25 ns.**

### 2.2 Loss components (per motor, 80 A, 25.2 V)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·25 ns·48 k = **1.21 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f | 2·40 nC·10·48 k = 0.04 W |
| **Total in FETs** | | **≈ 1.9 W** |

**Notes:**
- Dead-time diode loss is the second-largest term. Reducing t_dt to 50 ns in AM32 saves about 0.3 W per motor. Validate that change against shoot-through on the bench.
- Under sinusoidal/FOC drive, all three legs switch, so switching loss is about 3× (≈5.7 W). This is a reason to stay with 6-step on this power density.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.95 W, at the cost of more ripple and audible noise.
