# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4‑in‑1 ESC: Design Model

Three points shape everything below:

1. **Architecture.** The STM32H743 should run ArduPilot only. ArduPilot does not run motor commutation for four motors on the flight-controller core. The 48 kHz bridge PWM therefore belongs to four dedicated ESC MCUs, assumed here to be AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600.
2. **Parameters are placeholders.** All MOSFET and driver values are typical figures for a modern 40 V, ~1 mΩ PDFN part. Replace them with your chosen datasheet values before sign-off. The equations remain valid when you do.
3. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally marginal.** The math below shows this is a 1–3 second rating at best on 20×20 mm, and only a few seconds on 30.5×30.5 mm. Treat it as a spec risk, not a given.

---

## 1. Operating Assumptions

| Symbol | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_burst | 80 A | Per-motor DC-link / phase current during burst |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | At V_gs = 10 V |
| k_T | 1.5 | R_ds multiplier at T_j ≈ 110 °C |
| t_r + t_f | 20 ns | Effective V–I overlap, gate driver ~1–2 A |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_oss | 30 nC | At V_bus |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body diode reverse recovery |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_drv | 10 V | Gate drive supply |
| R_θJC | 1.2 K/W | PDFN5×6. Use ~2.5 K/W for 3×3 |

The commutation mode is six-step with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two legs are active and current flows through exactly one FET per active leg. That means two FETs carry I² in series at all times.

---

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**General form, per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \cdot N_{series}$$

with

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**At 80 A burst** (N_series = 2, R_ds,hot = 1.5 mΩ):

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 1.5\text{m}\Omega \times 2 = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

- Per FET (time-averaged across the six-step sequence): ≈ 19.2 / 6 × 2 ≈ 6.4 W average.
- Per FET (instantaneous, while conducting): 9.6 W.
- Thermal design must use the instantaneous 9.6 W value for junction transients shorter than one electrical period. For bursts, use the averaged value.

**Electrothermal feedback.** Solve iteratively, because R_ds rises with T_j:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

With 1 mΩ parts, this converges in 2–3 iterations. If it diverges, you are in thermal runaway.

**PCB copper loss (often forgotten).** A 2 oz trace has R ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq. Phase and bus paths of about 3 squares add roughly 0.75 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.75\text{m}\Omega \approx 4.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switched at a time in six-step. Each term is per motor.

**a) V–I overlap (hard-switched FET):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Note that Q_rr also adds turn-on current in the opposing FET. Fold roughly 1.5× into P_VI if the datasheet curves show it.

**d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**e) Gate drive (high + low side of the switching leg):**

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This is mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors.

**Switching subtotal:** ≈ 1.7 W per motor.

**Design lever.** Dead time costs more than Q_oss and Q_rr combined. Tightening t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| PCB copper | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching (VI + oss + rr) | 1.05 W | 4.2 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

The resulting efficiency is about 98.7% of 80 A × 25.2 V × 4 = 8 kW. The efficiency is fine. The problem is thermal density.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network (Foster/Cauer hybrid per FET)

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.2]──┬──[R_spread ~8 K/W]──┬── Board node ──[R_θBA]── T_amb
                     C_j≈0.05 J/K          C_pad≈0.2 J/K    C_board
```

### 5.2 Board Heat Capacity (Adiabatic Burst Limit)

The volumetric heat capacities used are c_v ≈ 2.0 J/cm³·K for FR4 and 3.45 J/cm³·K for Cu.

| Board | FR4 vol | Cu vol (6 L × 2 oz, 80% fill) | C_board (incl. FETs/caps) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20×20×1.6 mm | 0.64 cm³ → 1.28 J/K | 0.134 cm³ → 0.46 J/K | ≈ 2.0 J/K |
| 30.5×30.5×1.6 mm | 1.49 cm³ → 2.98 J/K | 0.31 cm³ → 1.07 J/K | ≈ 4.5 J/K |

**Adiabatic rise rate:**

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{board}}$$

| Board | dT/dt at 103 W | Time from 50 °C to a 110 °C board limit (ΔT = 60 K) |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 | 51 K/s | **≈ 1.2 s** |
| 30.5×30.5 | 23 K/s | **≈ 2.6 s** |
