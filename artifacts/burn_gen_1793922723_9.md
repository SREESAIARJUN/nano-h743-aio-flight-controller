## 0. Architecture Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three points must be settled before the math, because they change the results.

**1. The STM32H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware, not ESC firmware. The board therefore needs a split architecture:

- **H743**: runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600.
- **4× ESC MCUs**: STM32G071 or AT32F421, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. They do 6-step commutation, gate drive and back-EMF sensing.

Everything below assumes this split.

**2. "80 A burst" on a "nano" form factor is a red flag.**

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery current at 4 × 80 A | 320 A |
| Total dissipation (derived in §3) | ~108 W |
| Heat source footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) | ~600–900 mm² |

The result in §4 is a burst capability measured in seconds, not a continuous rating. I have modeled a 30.5×30.5-mounting, 37×37 mm, 8-layer board. If the board really is a 20×20 nano, divide the burst time by about 2.5.

**3. Device parameters are assumptions.** Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S, fully charged | 25.2 V |
| R_ds(on),25 | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN, max | 1.3 mΩ |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step, synchronous rectification)

**Current path.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases carry the DC-link-referred phase current I at any instant.

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS(a) and LS(b).
- During the off-time with complementary PWM, current flows through LS(a) and LS(b). The body diode conducts only during dead time.

So exactly two channels conduct at all times, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

**At an 80 A burst:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times (1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5) = \mathbf{24.96\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Copper path.** Add trace and plane resistance from the bus to the phase pad. Assume about 0.2 mΩ effective per conducting path with 2 oz copper on 4 parallel layers:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. At a typical electrical frequency (7 pole pairs × 30 krpm gives 3.5 kHz) the commutation steps are much shorter than the die thermal time constant, so use the averaged value:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{24.96}{6} \approx 4.2\ \text{W}$$

**Positive feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The loop converges only when:

$$R_{\theta JA,eff} \cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = R_{\theta JA,eff} \cdot 2 I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha < 1$$

At 80 A this gives 2 × 6400 × 1.3 m × 0.005 ≈ 0.083 W/K, so instability requires R_θ > 12 K/W. Steady-state runaway is therefore not the limiting failure. The limit is adiabatic heating of the board (§4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive only **one leg switches hard** per PWM period; the other leg is held static.

| Term | Equation | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·27 ns·48 k | 1.306 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 n·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2·0.8·80·100 n·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipates, not the FETs) | 6·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw (upper bound) | 0.144 W |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 2.13 W** |

Design levers, in order of payoff:

1. **Dead time.** Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per ESC. Verify shoot-through margin against the gate driver's propagation-delay mismatch first.
2. **Switching frequency.** f_sw = 24 kHz halves the overlap loss, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise. At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 8 % of total, so frequency is **not** the main thermal problem. Conduction is.
