## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Who does what

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. On every production 4-in-1 design, the commutation and PWM run on dedicated ESC MCUs, and the H743 only sends DShot commands to them.

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 master, bi-directional telemetry decode | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot |
| 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF sensing | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), one per motor | AM32 |
| Gate drive | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN | n/a |

### 0.3 Feasibility flag

A 30×30 mm nano board cannot dissipate 80 A × 4 continuously. The analysis below shows that **80 A is a 1–3 s burst rating**. Spec sheets and ArduPilot current limits must reflect that.

Also, 4 × 80 A = 320 A at the battery. That exceeds XT60 (60 A continuous) and practical 30×30 bus copper. Assume **burst on one or two motors at a time** (yaw/punch transients), or adjust system-level expectations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd (high side at duty D, low side synchronous at 1−D), phase B is held low, and phase C floats. At every instant, exactly **two MOSFETs carry the full phase current**:

- During D: Q_AH and Q_BL conduct.
- During 1−D: Q_AL and Q_BL conduct.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected on-resistance

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Add PCB/via path resistance for each conduction pair, R_pcb ≈ 0.3 mΩ (2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, short loop).

### 1.3 Instantaneous conduction loss per motor

$$P_{cond,inst} = I^2 (2R_{ds,hot} + R_{pcb})$$

At I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times (4.5 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 30.7\,\text{W}$$

Breaking that down:

- MOSFETs: 6400 × 4.5 mΩ = **28.8 W**
- Copper: 1.9 W

### 1.4 Per-device average (thermal distribution)

Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the period (120°). That split is between PWM and synchronous rectification for the "PWM" phase, and full-on for the "low" phase. Averaged over the cycle:

$$\bar{P}_{FET} = \frac{28.8}{6} = 4.8\,\text{W per FET}$$

The worst device is the low-side FET of the clamped phase in a sector, which carries 14.4 W for 1/3 of the time. With a commutation period below 1 ms at burst RPM, it is much shorter than the FET thermal time constant (~100 ms die-to-case). So the **average** 4.8 W governs junction temperature rise.

### 1.5 Scaling to continuous rating (45 A)

$$P_{cond}(45\,\text{A}) = 2025 \times 4.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.7\,\text{W per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low side commutates near zero-voltage, so its losses are diode and reverse-recovery terms.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9}\times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\,W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, at high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\,\text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.018\,\text{W}$$
