## 1. Operating Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- The **high-side FET** of phase A is PWM-switched at duty *D*.
- The **low-side FET** of phase B is held on for the 60° sector.
- During the PWM off-time, the **low-side FET of phase A** synchronously rectifies the freewheel current.

The current path therefore always passes through exactly **two conducting FET channels** (Q_AH or Q_AL, plus Q_BL). Only Q_AH and Q_AL hard-switch.

Battery-side average current:

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph}$$

Input capacitor RMS ripple, per ESC channel:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; 40\ \text{A}$$

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha_R \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the T_j = 110 °C design point: R = 1.6 × 1.47 = **2.35 mΩ**.

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on P, the solution is a fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P_{tot}\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges when $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot Z_{th} < 1$. Otherwise you are in thermal runaway.

### 2.2 Per-FET conduction losses (one motor, one sector)

| Device | Conducts | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH (PWM high side) | fraction D | $P = D\, I^2 R$ |
| Q_AL (sync rectifier) | fraction (1−D − 2t_dt f_sw) | $P \approx (1-D) I^2 R$ |
| Q_BL (sector low side) | 100 % of sector | $P = I^2 R$ |

The sum per motor is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.35\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.1\ W}$$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries ⅓ of this, about 5 W.

Instantaneous peak on one die (Q_BL, or Q_AH at D→1):

$$P_{die,pk} = 80^2 \times 2.35\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.0\ W}$$

### 2.3 PCB copper conduction

Path per motor: battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pour → motor pad. Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ effective, with 2 oz layers paralleled.

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\text{ m} = 2.6\ \text{W/motor}$$

The battery entry copper carries $\sum I_{bat}$, up to 320 A × D. At D = 0.8 that is 256 A through roughly 0.15 mΩ, or **9.8 W**. This is frequently the forgotten dominant loss.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The parameters below are typical for this FET class at V_GS = 10 V and R_G,ext = 4.7 Ω with the FD6288 (~1.2 A source / 1.5 A sink):

| Param | Value |
|---|---|
| t_r + t_f (V–I overlap) | 10 + 10 ns |
| Q_rr (body diode, @80 A, di/dt ~1 A/ns) | 50 nC |
| Q_oss @ 25 V | 35 nC |
| Q_g total | 40 nC |
| Dead time t_dt | 100 ns (AM32 typical) |
| Body diode V_F @ 80 A | 0.85 V |

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (Q_AH)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times20\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

Q_AL's body diode conducts during dead time and is then recovered by Q_AH turning on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The recovery current also adds turn-on loss in Q_AH:

$$P_{rr,on} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.03\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.02\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.85\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in R_G and driver, not the FET)

$$P_{g} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.04\ \text{W}$$
