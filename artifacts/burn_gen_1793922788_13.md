# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture (Job #66)

## 0. Feasibility Flags

Three items should be settled at the design review before layout begins.

| # | Issue | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **80 A burst × 4 channels = 320 A from the battery bus** at full throttle. That is about 7 kW on 6S, which an XT60 or a nano-format bus cannot carry continuously. | The "80 A" rating must be defined as **per-channel, single/non-simultaneous, ≤5–10 s**. Otherwise specify an XT90 / AS150 lead and solder pads. |
| F2 | On a nano footprint (≤30.5 mm), the board's thermal resistance to air is about **5–10 K/W**. Steady-state dissipation above ~10 W is impossible. | Burst duration is **heat-capacity limited**, not Rth limited. See §3. |
| F3 | The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It does **not** commutate the motors. Real 4-in-1 designs use 4 dedicated ESC MCUs (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071) or one ESC MCU per 2 motors. | The netlist assumes **H743 FC + 4× AM32 ESC MCUs**, linked by bidirectional DShot. |

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two phases are active at any instant:
- **Low side of the return phase:** conducts continuously during the 60° sector.
- **PWM phase:** the high side conducts for D·T and the complementary low side for (1−D)·T.

The current is therefore always flowing through exactly **2 R_DS(on)** in series:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,\frac{I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds25}\,k_T}{n_p}, \qquad
I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)
$$

Here r = ΔI_pp / I_ph is the current ripple ratio set by the motor inductance:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

At burst, D → 1 and the back-EMF approaches the bus voltage, so r ≈ 0.1–0.2 and the ripple term adds less than 0.4 %. Ignore it.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$
