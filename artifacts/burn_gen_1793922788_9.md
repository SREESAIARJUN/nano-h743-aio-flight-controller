# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO "4-in-1" design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230.

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.
- The H743 generates DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional (GCR) eRPM telemetry.

The model below is partitioned accordingly.

**2. 80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The math below shows why: about 130 W of loss in a few grams of copper and FR4. Steady-state 80 A per channel is not physically achievable at this size.

**3. All component values are design assumptions, not vendor-verified numbers.** Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.006 /K, giving k_T ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

This creates a positive feedback loop: T_j ↑ → R ↑ → P ↑. Section 4.3 gives the stability criterion.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two conducting devices in series:

- the high-side FET (during D) or the low-side sync FET (during 1−D) of the PWM leg, and
- the low-side FET of the sinking leg.

So the instantaneous conducting resistance is 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle:

**P_cond,ch = I_ph² · (2·R_DS(on) + R_Cu)**

Excluding copper (R_Cu ≈ 0.3–0.5 mΩ on a 2 oz nano board, adding roughly 2–3 W more):

P_cond,ch = 80² × 2 × 2.4 mΩ = **30.7 W** (hot)
P_cond,ch = 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** (cold start)

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg is the PWM leg for 1/3 of the electrical period and the sink leg for 1/3. Averaged:

- **High-side FET:** P_HS = (I²R/3) · D
- **Low-side FET:** P_LS = (I²R/3) · (1 − D) + I²R/3

The low-side FETs carry more loss (worst case at low duty, high current). Combined with the 2·I²R path total, the loss is dominated by the low-side devices:

P_LS,max ≈ (2/3) · I² · R_hot = 0.667 × 6400 × 2.4 mΩ ≈ **10.2 W per low-side FET** (D → 0, e.g. stall or punch-out start)

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel switches at a time in 6-step operation.

**(a) Hard-switching overlap loss**

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 25 ns × 48 000 = **1.21 W**

**(b) Reverse recovery** (low-side body diode after dead time)

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 000 = **0.048 W**

**(c) Output capacitance** (two devices per switching event)

P_oss = 2 · ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 000 = **0.036 W**

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period)

P_dt = V_F · I_ph · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100 ns × 48 000 = **0.61 W**

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver or gate resistor, not the FET die)

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 000 = **0.048 W**

### 3.1 Switching total

P_sw,ch ≈ 1.21 + 0.05 + 0.04 + 0.61 ≈ **1.9 W per channel**

**Sensitivity:**
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.95 W; at 96 kHz about 3.8 W.
- At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 16:1. Raising f_sw for lower motor ripple or noise costs little thermally at burst. It matters more at mid-throttle cruise, where I² is small.
- Dead-time loss (d) is the largest switching term after overlap. Minimizing t_dt within shoot-through margin is the most effective switching-side optimization.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Total board dissipation at 80 A × 4 burst

| Source | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| Copper planes / traces | ~2.5 W | ~10 W |
| Switching | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| **Total** | **~35 W** | **~140 W** |

### 4.2 Board-level adiabatic burst model

For bursts under about 5 s, convection is negligible compared with stored heat, so the board heats adiabatically:

ΔT_board(t) ≈ P_total · t / C_th

Estimate C_th for a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with 24 FETs and capacitors:

- FR4: ≈ 3.5 g × 1.1 J/g·K = 3.9 J/K
- Copper: ≈ 2.0 g × 0.385 = 0.8 J/K
- FETs, capacitors, solder: ≈ 0.5 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 5.2 J/K**

dT/dt ≈ 140 / 5.2 ≈ **27 K/s**

| Burst duration | ΔT_board | T_board (from 40 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 27 K | 67 °C |
| 2 s | 54 K | 94 °C |
| 3 s | 81 K | 121 °C ⚠ |

**Burst rating: 80 A for ≤ 2 s, with a 40 °C board start.**
