**Architecture note.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) driving its own bridge. The H743 sends DShot600 to the ESCs and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry back. Everything in §3 concerns that link.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor on a true 20×20 or 25.5 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible beyond a few seconds. Even at 36×36 mm, §2 shows the rating is burst-limited (about 5–8 s), not continuous. Treat "80 A" as a time-bounded burst rating.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T<sub>j</sub> = 105 °C:

R<sub>DS</sub> = 0.70 × 1.44 ≈ **1.0 mΩ**

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- the active-leg high-side during D·T, or its complementary low-side during (1−D)·T;
- plus the static return-leg low-side.

This holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.0\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{12.8\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average is 12.8 / 6 ≈ **2.13 W per FET**.

Add the copper path (phase pours, vias, bus): estimate about 0.15 mΩ effective, giving 80² × 0.15 mΩ ≈ **0.96 W**.

### 1.3 Hard-switching loss (active-leg high-side only)

Transition times:

- t<sub>r</sub> ≈ Q<sub>gd</sub>/I<sub>g,src</sub> ≈ 12 nC / 0.6 A ≈ 20 ns
- t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 20 ns

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r+t_f) = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns}) = 40.3\ \mu J$$

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = 40.3\,\mu J \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The low-side turns on at near-zero V<sub>DS</sub> (soft switching), so it contributes only the secondary terms below.

### 1.4 Secondary losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | V<sub>SD</sub>·I·2t<sub>d</sub>·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q<sub>rr</sub>·V·f = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.05 W |
| C<sub>oss</sub> | 2·½·C<sub>oss</sub>·V²·f | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R<sub>g</sub>) | 2·Q<sub>g</sub>·V<sub>dr</sub>·f = 2·60 nC·10·48 k | 0.06 W |

### 1.5 Totals at 80 A burst

| | Per ESC channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 12.80 W | 51.2 W |
| Copper | 0.96 W | 3.8 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Secondary | 0.77 W | 3.1 W |
| **Total** | **16.5 W** | **≈ 66 W** |

Efficiency check: 66 W against 4 × 80 A × 22.2 V ≈ 7.1 kW electrical, so about 99.1% bridge efficiency. That is plausible.

**Conduction dominates (about 80%).** Lowering f<sub>sw</sub> to 24 kHz saves only about 4 W total. Lower R<sub>DS(on)</sub> or paralleled FETs are the effective lever.
