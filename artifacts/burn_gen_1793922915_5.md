## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Corrections

Several points in the request need correcting before any math, because they change the architecture:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design (and in ArduPilot's model), the H743 sends **bi-directional DShot600** to four ESC MCUs. Typical choices are AT32F421 running AM32, STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, or EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. Those MCUs generate the **48 kHz PWM**. The design is therefore split into two domains:
   - **FC domain:** H743, DMA, DShot, eRPM telemetry.
   - **ESC domain:** 4× (MCU + 3-phase gate driver + 6 MOSFETs).
2. **"Nano" and 80 A are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A, even as a burst, because of copper cross-section and thermal mass. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount pattern on a ~36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board**, with the ESC section split onto a stacked board if needed. Section 2.6 shows that 80 A is **burst-only (seconds)** and gives the true continuous rating.
3. **Component values are representative.** MOSFET parameters are for a generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN device. Before layout freeze, replace them with your chosen part's datasheet values; the equations are part-agnostic.
4. **Supply is 6S Li-ion/LiPo:** V_bus = 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal. 40 V MOSFETs give about 37% margin for regen and ringing spikes, which requires TVS protection and good bulk capacitance.
