| Parameter | Assumed value | Basis |

| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case) | Worst-case switching |

| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), six-step | AM32/BLHeli_32 style |
| MOSFET (each of 24) | 40 V, R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class | Representative |

| | R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W | Representative |
| Gate drive | ~1 A effective at the Miller plateau → t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 10 ns | Q_gd / I_g ≈ 6 ns plus margin |
| Dead time | 50 ns per edge | Typical |
| Board | 20×20 mm "nano", 8-layer, 2 oz copper | Nano form factor |

**Flag 1: architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU running ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC must have its **own** motor-control MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors and run ArduPilot. Bi-directional DShot is the link between the two.

**Flag 2: the 80 A spec.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is not thermally credible beyond about a 1–2 s burst. Section 1 proves this. If "80 A" means **total** (4 × 20 A), the design is comfortable. Both cases are computed.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, current flows through two phase legs in series:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D. The low-side FET (synchronous) conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the step.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle (dead time excluded):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot \big[\,D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS}\big] + I^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.5) = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| Shunt (per channel) | 0.5 mΩ |
| PCB copper, phase + bus, per path | ≈ 0.4 mΩ |

$$
P_{par} = I^2 (R_{shunt} + R_{Cu}) = 6400 \times 0.9\ \text{m}\Omega = 5.76\ \text{W}
$$

The shunt can be removed (phase-current sensing via R_ds) to save about 3.2 W.

### 1.4 Results (per motor, I = 80 A)

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond FETs = 2·I²·R | + parasitics | Total per motor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 5.76 W | 18.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 5.76 W | **25.0 W** |

**Per-FET peak (return-leg low side, 100% conduction at 125 °C):**

$$
P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{JC} = 9.6 \times 1.5 = 14.4\ \text{K above the case}
$$

Commutation rotates this duty across the 6 FETs. Average per FET is about 19.2 / 6 ≈ 3.2 W, but the instantaneous junction must be checked with Z_θJC at the electrical period. At 80 A it is usually short versus the die thermal time constant (~ms), so the averaged figure is reasonable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor:

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance (HS + LS C_oss charged/discharged)**

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1\,\text{nF} \times 25.2^2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per cycle)**

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}
$$
