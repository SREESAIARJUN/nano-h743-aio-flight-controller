# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation, and Reference Netlist

This document covers the architecture, power-stage loss model, transient thermal analysis, bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and a reference netlist. Assumptions are stated explicitly. Part numbers are representative and must be confirmed against current datasheets before layout freeze.

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Three corrections or flags determine whether this design succeeds.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On a 4-in-1 board, each motor (or the whole bridge) is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not the H743.

2. **An 80 A *per-motor* burst on a 20×20 mm nano footprint is thermally infeasible beyond about 1 s.** Section 3 shows that the 4-motor loss at 80 A is about 130 W, against a board heat capacity of only a few J/K. The numbers below quantify this. I recommend one of two paths:
   - Interpret the target as 80 A *total* burst (20 A/motor), which is a very comfortable nano design.
   - If you need 80 A *per motor*, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or 36×36 mm layout with 5×6 mm DFN MOSFETs.

3. **AIO noise coupling.** Co-locating a 130 W switching stage with an IMU on a 20×20 board requires the isolation measures in §6. Otherwise ArduPilot's EKF will see vibration and clipping from magnetic and ground-bounce artifacts, not mechanical vibration.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor bridge, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step complementary PWM, phase current flows through exactly **two FET channels in series at all times**:
- the PWM-ing high-side FET (duty D), or its complementary low-side FET (duty 1−D), and
- the statically-on low-side FET of the return phase.

Because synchronous rectification puts the freewheel current through a FET channel rather than a diode (dead time is handled separately), the conduction loss is nearly independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.0075 × 85) = **2.46 mΩ**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (2.46\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{31.5\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

**PCB copper loss.** Add the loss in the copper paths. On a nano board, the phase path, bus planes, and pads total roughly 0.6–1.0 mΩ per current path. Using 2 oz copper on 4 internal/external layers, the net is about 0.8 mΩ:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W}
$$

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS rises with temperature, so conduction loss is self-reinforcing. Solve the coupled equations:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j)
$$

This is stable only if the loop gain is below one:

$$
R_\theta \cdot 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha < 1
$$

At 80 A with R_θ,per-FET ≈ 40 K/W (nano PCB, 3.3 mm DFN), the gain is 40 × 2 × 6400 × 1.5e-3 × 0.0075 × (1/3 duty share) ≈ 1.0. **This is marginal and confirms that continuous 80 A on a nano board is a runaway condition.** The burst can only be time-limited (see §3).

### 2.2 Switching Losses (hard-switched PWM FET, one edge pair per period)

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance (Q_oss) loss.** Use the charge-based form, since C_oss is nonlinear:

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 (35\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.021\ W}
$$

**Body-diode reverse recovery.** This is the hard turn-on of the high-side FET into a conducting low-side diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (45\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.054\ W}
$$

The reverse-recovery current also adds turn-on overlap. Fold that in by derating t_r by about 30%, which adds **≈ 0.3 W** at 80 A.

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):

$$
P_{dt} = 2 V_F I\, t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 (0.8)(80)(50\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (two complementary PWM FETs per active phase pair). This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$
P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 (40\,\text{nC})(10)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-Motor Loss Budget at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction, 2·I²R_DS(T) | 31.5 |
| PCB copper | 5.1 |
| V–I switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Q_rr + recovery overlap | 0.35 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q_oss | 0.02 |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.04 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 39.3 W** |
| **Total 4 motors** | **≈ 157 W** |

**Key insight:** at 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 6% of the total. The design is **conduction-dominated**. Spending budget on lower R_DS(on), and accepting the higher Q_g that comes with it, is the correct trade.
