## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

Several points in the brief need clarifying before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071 or AT32F421. These MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to them. This document models that split.
2. **"Nano" form factor.** A 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm stack cannot sustain 80A per channel. Section 3 shows quantitatively that 80A is a time-limited burst rating, measured in seconds. The deliverable therefore includes a burst-duration limit, not a continuous rating.
3. **Representative component parameters.** I have no datasheet or BOM for Job #34. All MOSFET, driver, and shunt values below are representative and marked [A]. Substitute your selected parts' datasheet values into the equations. The model structure stays valid.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path topology

In 6-step operation with complementary PWM, the current path always contains exactly two MOSFETs at any instant, regardless of duty D:

- **PWM ON (fraction D):** HS_x + LS_y
- **PWM OFF (fraction 1−D):** LS_x (synchronous rectification) + LS_y

Ignoring dead time, the instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Per-device split.** Over one electrical cycle, each leg is active for 2/3 of the period:

- **High-side FET (the PWM'd leg):** conducts for fraction D of its active third.
- **Low-side FET:** carries current for the remainder of its active third plus its full static third.

The low-side FETs are therefore the hottest devices at low-to-mid throttle. The split equalizes only at D → 1.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(2-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

(Check: 3·(P_HS + P_LS) = 2I²R ✓.)

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and electrothermal stability

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j)$$

Solving the coupled equations in closed form (conduction-dominated):

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{\theta,crit} = \frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha} = \frac{1}{2(80)^2(0.001)(0.006)} = \mathbf{13.0\ K/W\ (per\ channel)}$$

A nano board's steady-state channel-to-air resistance is roughly 15–30 K/W. That exceeds this limit, so **steady-state 80A has no stable operating point.** 80A is strictly transient (see Section 3).

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80A

Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C, where R_DS = 1.6 mΩ:

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2·80²·1.6 mΩ | **20.48 W** |
| Shunt | 80²·0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Phase copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ path) | 80²·0.3 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| **Conduction subtotal / channel** | | **≈ 24.0 W** |

At T_j = 25 °C, FET conduction drops to 12.8 W.
