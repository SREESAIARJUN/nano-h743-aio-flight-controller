# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #35)

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

These constraints shape every calculation below.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay. Candidates are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51. That ESC MCU owns the 48 kHz PWM, dead time and commutation. Running 4× six-step commutation, BEMF sensing and ArduPilot on one H743 is not a supported configuration.
2. **"Nano" plus "80A" is a burst-only rating.** As derived in §3, a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board cannot dissipate 80A per motor continuously. The 80A rating is defined here as **≤2 s per motor, Tj ≤ 125 °C, starting from 40 °C ambient**. Continuous rating comes out to roughly 25–30A per motor with prop-wash cooling.
3. **All MOSFET parameters are design assumptions.** They are typical of 40V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; the equations are parameterized for that.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \;\Rightarrow\; R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.2 \times 1.55 \approx 1.86\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current paths in six-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, two phases conduct and the third floats:
- The **PWM leg** has its high side on for D·T, and its synchronous low side on for (1−D)·T.
- The **return leg** has its low side on for 100% of the sector.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two** channels in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase inductance L_ph adds ripple: Δi = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw).

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}$$

For L_ph = 15 µH at D = 0.5: Δi = 25.2×0.25 / (15e-6×48e3) ≈ 8.75 A. The correction is Δi²/12 = 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.4 Result at 80A burst

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.86\text{e-3} = \mathbf{23.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Six FETs share this load, each conducting about one-third of the electrical cycle, so the average is **≈3.97 W per FET**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Each PWM period has one turn-on and one turn-off.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·27e-9·48e3 | 1.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.036 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss(er)·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5e-9·635·48e3 | 0.023 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100e-9·48e3 | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·60e-9·10·48e3 | 0.058 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈1.73 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** ∂P_sw/∂f ≈ 36 µW/Hz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 0.86 W per motor but increases ripple current and acoustic noise. At 80A, conduction dominates by 14:1, so 48 kHz is justified. **R_DS(on) is the lever that matters; f_sw is not.**

### Total burst dissipation

$$P_{motor} = 23.8 + 1.73 = 25.5\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{4\times} = \mathbf{102\ W}$$

All four motors at 80A means a 320A battery draw. That is unrealistic for a nano airframe and is treated as the worst-case envelope.
