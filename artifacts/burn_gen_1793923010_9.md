# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #26): Electro-Thermal and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32) with three half-bridge drivers each. All "48 kHz" figures refer to ESC PWM frequency. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.3 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.08 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector:
- One phase is PWM'd. Its high-side conducts for duty D, and its low-side conducts (synchronously) for 1−D.
- One low-side FET is held fully on.

So at every instant, exactly **two FETs carry I_ph**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For an individual FET over one electrical cycle (conducts during 120° = 1/3 of the cycle):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\times
\begin{cases}D & \text{HS (PWM leg)}\\ (1-D) & \text{LS sync}\\ 1 & \text{LS static}\end{cases}$$

Averaged over the leg's PWM and static roles, this collapses to **(1/3)·I²R per FET** at full throttle.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 2.08 mΩ = **26.6 W** |
| P_cond per FET (average) | **4.4 W** |
| P_cond per FET (instantaneous while conducting) | 13.3 W |
| Phase-trace copper (≈0.25 mΩ per path, 2 paths) | +3.2 W per motor |
| Shared battery bus (0.15 mΩ at 4 × 80 × ~0.9 A_avg) | ≈ +12 W board-wide |

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, which depends on P_cond. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\,P_{FET}\!\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\right)$$

Thermal runaway is avoided if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_\theta < 1 \;\Leftrightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{3}I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1$$

At 80 A: (1/3)(6400)(1.3 m)(0.006)(5 K/W) = **0.083**. The loop is stable, with a large margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz (per motor, active PWM leg)

### 2.1 Gate transition times

Rise (turn-on):

$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/(R_{src}+R_g)}=\frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.18\,\text{A}}=7.6\text{ ns}$$

Fall (turn-off):

$$t_f=\frac{9\,\text{nC}}{V_{pl}/(R_{snk}+R_g)}=\frac{9\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}}=9.0\text{ ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·16.6 ns·48 k = **0.80 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | **0.07 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | **0.03 W** |
| Body-diode dead time | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f | **0.03 W** |
| **P_sw per motor** | | **≈1.54 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time loss is about 40 % of the switching total. Cutting t_dt to 50 ns (AM32 dead-time setting, if driver propagation matching allows) saves about 0.3 W per motor.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f. At 24 kHz, P_sw drops to about 0.77 W, at the cost of more current ripple.
