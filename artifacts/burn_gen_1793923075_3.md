# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #75): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These constraints are stated first because they shape every number below.

1. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The math below shows about 30 W dissipated per channel at 80 A, so about 120 W across four channels. A 20×20 board has a board-to-ambient thermal resistance of roughly 8–15 K/W even with prop wash. **The board can survive 80 A only for a few seconds, and only with a thermal-capacity budget.** This document treats "80 A burst" as **≤2 s at a ≤10 % duty cycle**. Marketing should not publish 80 A without that qualifier.
2. **Architecture.** The H743 is the flight controller. The 4-in-1 ESC section should have its own commutation MCU(s), for example AM32-class firmware on AT32F421/STM32G071 parts. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware, not in ArduPilot.
3. **Component values are assumptions, not datasheet quotes.** MOSFET parameters are representative of a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm class device. Every derived number is parameterized, so the final selected parts' datasheet values (at 125 °C) can be substituted in.

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.5 mΩ × 1.425 = **2.14 mΩ** (use about 2.2 mΩ with margin).

### 2.2 Current path per channel

In 6-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly one MOSFET in each active leg:

- **PWM leg:** HS for a fraction D of the period, LS for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** LS for 100 % of the period.

So at any instant two MOSFET channels carry I_ph, independent of duty cycle (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0022 = \mathbf{28.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Per-device distribution, averaged over the six commutation sectors: each of the six FETs carries the load about one third of the time, so the average is about **4.7 W per FET**. During a sector, the static-leg LS FET carries the full **14.1 W instantaneously**. Junction design must therefore use the **sector-instantaneous** value. The sector duration at high eRPM is about 100 µs to 1 ms, which is comparable to the die thermal time constant.

### 2.3 Copper and current-sense losses

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt})$$

Assume 2 oz copper with about 0.3 mΩ effective phase path, and a 0.25 mΩ shunt (or 0 Ω if the design uses R_DS sensing):

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.55\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.5\ W}$$

This loss is not negligible on a nano board. **Use 3 oz copper or add exposed copper with solder-buildable pads on the phase and bus paths.**

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. It has one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 800\text{ pF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.012\ W}$$

### 3.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The real value is often 2–3× higher at 80 A di/dt. Budget **0.15 W**.

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W. That is only safe if the driver propagation-delay mismatch is under about 15 ns.

### 3.5 Gate drive (all 6 FETs; only the PWM pair switches at f_sw)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

This power is dissipated mostly in the gate resistors and the driver, not in the FET.

### 3.6 Per-channel and total budget at 80 A burst

| Loss | W/channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 28.2 |
| Copper and shunt | 3.5 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (with margin) | 0.15 |
| C_oss and gate | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 34.5 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 138 W** |

At 80 A and 25 V, the output power is about 1.5–1.8 kW per channel at high duty cycle, so efficiency is about 98 %. The absolute heat, however, is about 138 W in about 4 cm². **The design is conduction-dominated.** Lowering R_DS(on) and adding copper matter about 10× more than f_sw. At 24 kHz, the switching-related losses drop from about 2.7 W to about 1.35 W per channel, which is only about 4 % of the total.

### 3.7 Bulk-capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; 40\text{ A}_{rms}\text{ at }D=0.5\text{ per channel}$$

For the four channels with unsynchronized PWM, the RSS is about 80 A_rms worst case. Mitigations:

- Fit a low-ESR bulk capacitor at the battery pads: 35 V, ≥1000 µF, external, and **mandatory**.
- Place **≥4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V MLCCs per channel** directly at the half-bridges.
- **Phase-stagger the PWM carriers** across the ESC MCUs if the firmware allows it.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network

```
Pj(FET) ─[RθJC 1.5 K/W]─ T_pad ─[Rθ,spread ≈ 2 K/W per FET site]─ T_board
T_board ─[Cth,board]─┬─[Rθ,BA]─ T_amb
                      │   Rθ,BA ≈ 12 K/W (still air) / ≈ 5 K/W (prop wash, ~8 m/s)
```

Board thermal capacitance, for a PCB plus FETs plus copper of about 9 g with an effective c_p of about 0.6 J/(g·K):

$$C_{th} \approx 5.4\ \text{J/K}$$
