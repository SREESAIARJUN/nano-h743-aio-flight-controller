## 0. Assumptions and System Architecture

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Motors | 3× gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (12 slots, 14 poles → p = 7 pole pairs), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Driver | 3-phase inverter, low-side or inline current sensing (2 or 3 shunts), V_bus = 12–16 V |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder per axis (AS5048A / MT6701 class) |
| IMU A | ICM-42688-P on the **camera payload**. It provides attitude and rate feedback. |
| IMU B | ICM-42688-P on the **base/frame** (vibration source side). It provides the base-motion feedforward and the adaptive-cancellation reference. |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7 class) with single-precision FPU |

### Loop rates and timing

```
 20 kHz  ─ Current (FOC) loop      : ADC-triggered ISR, Clarke/Park, PI, SVPWM
  8 kHz  ─ IMU sampling (both IMUs): ODR = 8 kHz, hardware AAF @ 536 Hz
  4 kHz  ─ Velocity loop           : PI + cogging FF + base-rate FF
  1 kHz  ─ Angle loop + attitude   : Mahony fusion, angle P/PI, frame transforms
```

The rule is that each inner loop needs a bandwidth of at least 5–10× the loop outside it, and each loop's sample rate must be at least 10× its own crossover frequency.

| Loop | Target crossover | Sample rate |
|---|---|---|
| Current | 1.5–2 kHz | 20 kHz |
| Velocity | 80–150 Hz | 4 kHz |
| Angle | 10–25 Hz | 1 kHz |

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop)

### 1.1 Electrical model (PMSM, dq frame)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$
$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$
$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2} p \left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \;\xrightarrow{\;L_d = L_q\;}\; \tau_e = K_t\, i_q,\quad K_t = \tfrac{3}{2} p \lambda_m
$$

Here $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}})$ and $\omega_e = p\,\omega_m$.

### 1.2 Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(i_a + 2 i_b)\quad(\text{with } i_a + i_b + i_c = 0)
$$

With three shunts, first remove the common-mode error:
$i_x \leftarrow i_x - \tfrac{1}{3}(i_a+i_b+i_c)$.

### 1.3 Park transform and its inverse

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha \\ v_\beta \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & -\sin\theta_e \\ \sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v_d \\ v_q \end{bmatrix}
$$

**Latency compensation.** The PWM update is applied one period later, so the voltage should be rotated using the angle predicted at the middle of the next period:

$$\theta_e^{\text{pwm}} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s$$

### 1.4 Current PI with decoupling (pole–zero cancellation tuning)

$$
v_d^* = K_p e_d + K_i \!\int\! e_d\,dt - \hat\omega_e \hat L_q i_q
$$
$$
v_q^* = K_p e_q + K_i \!\int\! e_q\,dt + \hat\omega_e(\hat L_d i_d + \hat\lambda_m)
$$

Choosing the gains as

$$
K_p = \omega_c \hat L,\qquad K_i = \omega_c \hat R
$$

places the PI zero at $R/L$, which cancels the electrical pole. The closed loop then becomes first-order:

$$
\frac{i_q}{i_q^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}
$$

Pick $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_s / 10$. For 20 kHz sampling, $\omega_c \approx 2\pi\cdot 1500$ rad/s.
