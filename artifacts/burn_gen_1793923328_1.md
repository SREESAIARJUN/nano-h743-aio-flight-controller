## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** On a production AIO board, the STM32H743 does **not** commutate the MOSFETs directly. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU drives three half-bridges through a gate driver such as the FD6288Q. The H743 cannot do 4× 3-phase sensorless FOC/6-step plus flight control with adequate timer resources and fault isolation. The model below follows this split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 100) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector, current flows through exactly two legs:

- **Non-PWM leg:** its low-side FET is held on, carrying I for 100% of the sector.
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the complementary low-side FET (fraction 1−D).

So the conducting path always contains **two** FET channels:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \quad (\text{equal FETs})$$

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and solder

Allow 0.3 mΩ per path for copper and solder. With 2 oz copper this is realistic; with 1 oz, double it.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.0003 = 1.92\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Numerical results per ESC channel

| Case | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A, T_j = 25 °C | 2 × 6400 × 0.00100 | 12.8 W |
| 80 A, T_j = 100 °C | 2 × 6400 × 0.00141 | 18.1 W |
| 80 A, T_j = 125 °C | 2 × 6400 × 0.00155 | **19.8 W** |
| 55 A, T_j = 100 °C | 2 × 3025 × 0.00141 | 8.5 W |

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts roughly ⅓ of the electrical period at near-full duty. The electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz at high RPM, far above the die thermal time constant, so the junction sees the average:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 0.00155}{3} \approx 3.3\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in any sector, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per ESC at any instant.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and is then hard-commutated.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle.

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

> This term is often overlooked. Halving t_dt to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, but only if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it. The FD6288 has roughly 20 ns mismatch, so verify on the bench.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

Switching-related loss scales linearly with frequency:

$$P_{sw,total}(f) = f_{sw}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}V I (t_r+t_f) + Q_{rr}V + \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V + 2V_f I t_{dt}\right] \approx 54\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$

| Frequency | Switching-related loss (80 A) |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.3 W |
| 48 kHz | **2.6 W** |
| 96 kHz | 5.2 W |
