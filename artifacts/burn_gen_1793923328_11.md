# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #39): Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlists, and Thermal Budget

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two premises in the brief need correcting before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented control (FOC) or six-step motor control for driving FETs directly. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or Bluejay. The H743 produces bidirectional DShot600 and reads eRPM telemetry. The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is a thermal-capacitance rating, not a power rating.** On a 20×20 mm board it is not physically credible at all. On a 30.5×30.5 mm board it is achievable for roughly 3–6 s, as derived in §3. I model the 30.5×30.5 case and flag where 20×20 fails.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

In six-step with synchronous rectification, the current path always contains exactly **two conducting devices**: the PWM-leg HS or LS (which alternate) plus the static-leg LS. This holds regardless of duty.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

With α ≈ 0.005 /K, k_T ≈ 1.5 at 125 °C.

| Case | R_ds hot | P_cond / channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5×6, 1.0 mΩ | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| 3.3×3.3, 2.5 mΩ (20×20 nano) | 3.75 mΩ | **48 W** | 192 W ✗ |

### Copper Path Loss

This term is not negligible. Phase and bus traces total ~0.3 mΩ per channel on 2 oz × 4 layers paralleled:

P_cu = I² · 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W / channel**

### Per-Device Distribution

- **At speed:** each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average is ≈ 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**.
- **At stall or low eRPM:** commutation is slow, so a single pair carries the full current: I²R = **9.6 W per FET**. This is the junction-temperature design case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS hard-switches and its LS soft-switches (ZVS after the dead time).

**Hard-switching overlap (HS):**

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·40\text{ns}·48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:** $P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}·25.2·48\text{k}=0.036$ W

**Reverse recovery (LS body diode):** $P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.048$ W

**Dead-time diode conduction:** $P_{dt}=V_f I\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw}=0.8·80·200\text{n}·48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$

**Gate drive (per switching leg, mostly dissipated in the driver):** $P_g=2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2·50\text{n}·10·48\text{k}=0.048$ W

**Total switching-related loss:** P_sw,ch ≈ **2.69 W / channel**

### Why 48 kHz Is Reasonable

Switching losses scale linearly with frequency. At 96 kHz they would be ~5.4 W per channel. At 24 kHz they drop to ~1.35 W but cause audible whine and higher current ripple. With motor L ≈ 15 µH (typical 5" class, line-to-line), the ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}\Big|_{D=0.5}=\frac{25.2·0.25}{15\mu·48\text{k}}\approx 8.75\ \text{A}$$

That is about 11% of 80 A, which is acceptable.

### Total Per Channel and Board

| Term | W / ch |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 2.69 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.8 W** |
| **Board (4 ch, worst case)** | **≈ 95 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 25.2·80·D. At D = 0.9 that is ~1.8 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98.7%. The percentage is high, but the absolute heat is the problem.
