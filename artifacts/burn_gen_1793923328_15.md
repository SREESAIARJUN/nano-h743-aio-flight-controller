# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Design Model and Allocation

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller only. It should not commutate the motors. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and the H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This is how every production AIO is built, and it is the model ArduPilot supports.

**Assumed component baseline.** No BOM was supplied. Replace these values with real datasheet values before release.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Phase current (burst, per ESC) | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, 5×6 mm PQFN, R_DS(on),25 = 0.70 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0055 /K |
| Rise / fall time (with FD6288Q driver) | t_r, t_f | 10 ns each |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time (per edge) | t_d | 50 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A × 4 on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint is not achievable with 3×3 mm FETs, and it is not thermally sustainable on any small board. The numbers below show that 80 A must be treated as a **sub-2-second burst rating**. A realistic continuous rating for this form factor is about 35–45 A per ESC. Section 2.5 quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.70\,(1+0.0055\cdot 85) = 1.03\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step, synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, one phase's high side PWMs with duty D. Its complementary low side conducts during (1−D). A second phase's low side is held on.

**Per-sector conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2R_{DS} = 2I^2R_{DS}$$

With synchronous rectification, the loss is **independent of duty**. Two R_DS are always in the current path.

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.03\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{13.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Per-FET average.** Electrical frequency is far above the junction thermal time constant (ms range), so the load averages across all 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 13.2/6 = 2.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt

| Path | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| 2 oz internal planes + vias (≈0.3 mΩ per path) | ≈0.3 mΩ ×2 | ≈3.8 W |

Copper loss is about 30% of FET loss. Use 3 oz outer copper and via farms of ≥20 × 0.3 mm vias per FET pad.

### 1.4 FOC alternative (for reference)

For sinusoidal drive at peak I_pk, conduction is I_rms² over three legs:

$$P = 3\left(\tfrac{I_{pk}}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 R_{DS}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

One high-side FET switches per PWM period, with one turn-on and one turn-off:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(100\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate drive

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | P_oss = Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/gate resistor, 2 FETs) | P_g = 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.048 W |

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A burst

| Term | Per ESC | ×4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 13.2 W | 52.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 5.4 W | 21.6 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W | 1.2 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈20 W** | **≈80 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full load is about 1 − 80/(4·25.2·80·D_eff). Even so, **80 W of heat in roughly 6 cm³ of board** is the binding constraint.

**Implication for f_sw.** Switching loss is less than 7% of the total at 80 A. At 48 kHz the design is conduction-dominated. Raising f_sw (e.g., to 96 kHz for quieter, smoother low-throttle response) costs only about 1.
