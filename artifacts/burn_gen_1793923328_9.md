# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Reference Engineering Package

## 0. Scope and Design Assumptions

Two architectural corrections come first, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** ArduPilot runs on the H743. Each ESC channel runs on its own small MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071, using AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 generates DShot600 frames and captures the bi-directional eRPM telemetry. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The two derivations are kept separate below.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80 A burst is a contradiction.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is not physically credible. Copper cross-section, FET footprint, and thermal mass are all insufficient; the model in §3 shows a 20×20 board would exceed T_j,max in roughly 2–3 s. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** paired with either a 20×20 or 30×30 H743 FC. If you truly need 20×20, de-rate to about 45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045·85) = **1.66 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path topology

**Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification.** At any instant the current flows through exactly two FET positions:

- The PWM'd phase, which alternates between the HS and LS FET with duty D.
- The static LS FET of the return phase.

Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[D R_{ds} + (1-D) R_{ds} + R_{ds}\right] = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D under synchronous rectification. Dead-time diode intervals are handled separately in §2.

**Sinusoidal/FOC alternative.** With peak current Î, the loss is $P = \tfrac{3}{2}\hat I^2 R_{ds}$ per channel. For equal RMS torque current this is about 25 % lower. Most AM32 builds, however, are 6-step.

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Add the copper resistance in series with the FETs. The approximate path from pad → FET → phase pad, on 2 oz with 4 paralleled layers, is R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ:

$$P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Numbers

| | Cold (25 °C) | Hot (110 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 2·6400·1.2 mΩ = **15.4 W** | 2·6400·1.66 mΩ = **21.2 W** |
| Per conducting FET (instantaneous) | 7.7 W | 10.6 W |

**Positive feedback.** Because P depends on T_j through R_ds, and T_j depends on P, iterate:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}$$

For thermal runaway to occur you would need $\alpha_R \cdot P \cdot R_\theta > 1$, i.e. 0.0045 × 10.6 × 1.5 = 0.07. The loop is therefore stable here. It becomes a concern only if R_θ to ambient grows large, such as with a potted or enclosed stack with no airflow.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. The return-phase LS FET is static.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt and I_F. Budget 2–3× the datasheet value, i.e. about 0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A, hot

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 21.2 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (de-rated) | 0.15 |
| C_oss + gate | 0.07 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.4 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 102 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Motor-side power at 80 A with D ≈ 0.85 is roughly 25.2 × 0.85 × 80 ≈ 1.7 kW per channel. The ESC loss is therefore about 1.5 %, which is plausible.
