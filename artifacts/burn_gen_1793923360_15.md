# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #43): System Architecture & Engineering Model

**Rev A, Preliminary Design Package**

## 0. Feasibility Findings to Resolve Before Layout

These findings change the scope of the job, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **ArduPilot does not commutate motors.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* bidirectional DShot600. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). | The architecture is H743 → 4× ESC MCU → 4× 3-phase gate driver → 24 MOSFETs (or 12 dual-packaged). |
| F2 | **4 × 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally sustainable.** The model below gives about 100 W of dissipation at 80 A on all four channels. The board's heat capacity limits this to about **1–2 s** of simultaneous burst. | Either define "80 A burst" as single-channel or ≤1 s, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor. Section 3.6 quantifies this. |
| F3 | **DC bus current at 4 × 80 A can reach about 250 A.** No nano-class battery pad, XT30, or XT60 tolerates this. | The 80 A figure must be a transient per-motor phase-current rating, not a system rating. |
| F4 | The STM32H743 in LQFP100 is 14×14 mm, which is too large for 20×20 alongside the ESC section. | Use **STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm)**, or split into a stacked FC + ESC pair. |

**Recommendation:** Build a stacked 20×20 FC (H743) plus a 20×20 4-in-1 ESC. Rate it at **45 A continuous / 60 A 10 s burst per channel**, and document 80 A as a ≤1 s single-channel peak. The full 80 A math is carried through below so the trade-off is explicit.
