# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Three Findings

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only. Motor commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs executing AM32 firmware. ArduPilot cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation on the flight MCU, so this split is standard and necessary.

The FC-to-ESC link is bi-directional DShot600, one wire per motor.

### 0.2 Baseline Parameters

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst current | I_burst = 80 A phase-equivalent DC per motor |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V class, 3.3 × 3.3 mm PQFN (the only package that fits 24 FETs on a 20 × 20 board; see 0.3) |
| Rds(on), 3.3 × 3.3 option | 1.8 mΩ max at 25 °C, Vgs = 10 V |
| Rds(on), 5 × 6 option | 1.0 mΩ max at 25 °C, Vgs = 10 V |
| Rds(on) temperature factor | k_T(T) ≈ 1 + 0.0045·(Tj − 25). Gives ×1.45 at 125 °C. |
| Switching | t_r = t_f = 15 ns with an FD6288-class driver; Qg = 40 nC, Qoss = 40 nC, Qrr = 30 nC |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge; body diode V_f = 0.8 V |

Every device parameter above must be replaced with the chosen part's datasheet maximum values before release.

### 0.3 Design Findings

1. **"Nano" form factor and 80 A are incompatible.**
   - 24 FETs at 5 × 6 mm need about 720 mm² of placement area plus drivers. A 20 × 20 board offers only 400 mm².
   - Even at 3.3 × 3.3 mm, the thermal mass cannot absorb a 4-motor 80 A burst for more than about one second (Section 3).
   - This document therefore models the board on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm (30 × 30 mount) footprint.
   - The nano case is reported alongside so the program can make an informed decision.
2. **80 A is a seconds-scale burst rating, not continuous.** Realistic continuous capability is about 25–30 A per motor (Section 3.4).
3. **An external low-ESR bulk capacitor (≥ 1000 µF) is mandatory.** On-board ceramics alone produce double-digit-volt ripple at 80 A (Section 2.6).
