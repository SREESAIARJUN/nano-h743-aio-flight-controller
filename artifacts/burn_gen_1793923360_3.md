# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): System Architecture Model

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Three facts constrain every number below.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware setting (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`), not an H743 timer.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a seconds-scale transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** The model below shows that four channels at 80A saturate the board's heat capacity in about 1–2 s. Treat 80A as a single-channel or short-transient figure.
3. **The MOSFET parameters are assumed representative values.** No part was specified, so I use a typical 40V PDFN3.3×3 device. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations. The equations themselves are the deliverable; the numbers are worked examples.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, as AM32 and BLHeli_32 do by default. At any instant:

- **Phase X (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Phase Y (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on for the full 60° sector.

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two channels in series**, independent of duty cycle (dead time is handled separately). In 6-step drive, motor current is approximately flat during a sector, so I_rms ≈ I_ph.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature Coupling (Fixed-Point Iteration)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\, Z_{th,jc}(t)
$$

Solve these together by iteration. If $P_{FET}\,R_{th}\,\alpha_R R_{ds,25} I^2 / P_{FET} \to 1$, the system goes into **thermal runaway**. Check this at your worst-case R_th.
