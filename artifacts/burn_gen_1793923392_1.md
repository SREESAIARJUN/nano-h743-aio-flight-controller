# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #60)

**Scope:** 80 A burst per channel, 6S (25.2 V max), 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600

---

## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Warning

Two findings shape every number that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs one dedicated MCU per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 runs ArduPilot and only *sends* DShot600 and *receives* eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching happens in the ESC MCUs. The H743's job is the timer and DMA allocation in Section 5.

2. **80 A burst on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally limited to seconds, not continuous operation.** The model below shows about 85–95 W of dissipation per board at full 4×80 A burst. That rating is only honest if it is time-bounded:
   - **80 A for ≤ 3–5 s**, with firmware current limiting.
   - **Continuous rating around 35–45 A** with propwash airflow.

   I recommend publishing the datasheet rating this way.

**Assumptions.** All values are representative. **Verify against the datasheets of the parts you actually select.**

## 1. Electrical Operating Model (6-step / trapezoidal)

### 1.1 Duty and current relations

With complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D:

- I_DC = D · I_m, where I_m is the motor phase current during conduction.
- Each phase conducts 120° per electrical half-cycle, in each direction.

**RMS current per MOSFET.** Each FET sits in the conduction path 1/3 of the electrical period, so:

$$I_{rms,HS}=I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS}=I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}\;\;(\text{LS on during its own 120° + freewheel of HS})$$

At burst the ESC is near full throttle, so D → 0.9–1.0. I evaluate two operating points:

| Case | D | I_m | I_DC |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (full stick) | 1.0 | 80 A | 80 A |
| B (worst switching) | 0.9 | 88.9 A | 80 A |

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx0.006\,/\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

This is a positive feedback loop: the hotter the FET, the higher its loss. It is solved iteratively in Section 4.3.

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 Per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Case A (D = 1, I_m = 80 A):** I_rms = 80/√3 = 46.2 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per FET |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 3.09 W |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 3.41 W |

**Case B (D = 0.9, I_m = 88.9 A), at 125 °C:**
- HS: I_rms = 88.9·√0.3 = 48.7 A → **3.79 W**
- LS: same 120° window plus 10% freewheel ≈ 51.3 A (approx.) → **4.2 W** (HS and LS combined)

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

At any instant, two FETs are in series (one HS, one LS of different phases).

$$P_{cond,ch}=I_m^2\cdot 2R_{DS}$$

Case A at 125 °C: 80² × 3.2 mΩ = **20.5 W per channel**

### 2.3 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase/bus path of 15 mm length and 4 mm width, on 2 oz outer copper plus one inner 1 oz plane in parallel.

- R_□(2 oz) ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□
- R_□(1 oz) ≈ 0.5 mΩ/□
- Parallel ≈ 0.167 mΩ/□

The path is 3.75 squares, so R ≈ 0.63 mΩ, rising to ≈ 0.8 mΩ hot. Including the shared + and – bus to the pads gives about 1.0 mΩ effective per channel.

$$P_{Cu,ch}\approx 80^2\cdot1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

> **Design implication:** copper loss is about 30% of FET conduction loss. Use 3 oz outer copper, stack all internal layers as parallel power planes under the bridge, and put heavy via arrays (≥ 0.3 mm drill, ~30 vias per FET drain pad).

## 3. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing FET switches hard. In Case A (D = 1) switching loss → 0, so **Case B is the worst case**.

### 3.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

The Miller plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 4.5 V. With a 10 Ω total gate resistance:

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g}=\frac{10-4.5}{10}=0.55\text{ A}\Rightarrow t_{r}\approx\frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}}\approx\frac{14\text{ nC}}{0.55}=25\text{ ns}$$

$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_g}=0.45\text{ A}\Rightarrow t_f\approx 31\text{ ns}$$

### 3.2 Loss components (per switching FET, V = 25.2 V, I = 88.9 A)

**V–I overlap**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=0.5\cdot25.2\cdot88.9\cdot56\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{3.01\ W}$$

**Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V^2f_{sw}=0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k}=0.023\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the complementary FET)

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}Vf_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.060\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead times per cycle, dissipated in the LS FET)

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8\cdot88.9\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.68\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g=Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.029\text{ W per FET} \;(\times 6 = 0.17\text{ W per channel})$$

### 3.3 Switching loss vs. frequency

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency:

| f_sw | P_VI | P_sw total per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.50 W | ~1.9 W |
| **48 kHz** | **3.01 W** | **~3.9 W** |
| 96 kHz | 6.02 W | ~7.8 W |

> **Recommendation:** 48 kHz is acceptable, but AM32 "variable PWM" (24 kHz at high throttle, 48 kHz at low throttle) recovers about 2 W per channel exactly when burst current flows.
