# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no access to Job #55's internal design files, MOSFET selection, layer stack or mechanical drawings. Everything below is a **parametric model built on stated assumptions**. Replace the values in §1 with your BOM and stack-up, then re-run the equations. Netlist part numbers are representative, not prescriptive.

**Architectural clarifications that affect the model:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a standard FC + 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or similar) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only produces bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The switching-loss model (§3) therefore describes the ESC side, and the DMA model (§5) describes the FC side.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** §4 shows that it is achievable only as a short transient (on the order of 1 to 3 s with all four motors loaded). A continuous 80 A rating is not achievable. Treat this as a design-review flag.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 2.1 Current Paths

In six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical period: 120° as the PWM phase and 120° as the "held" low phase.

Assume PWM is applied to the high side, with complementary low-side synchronous rectification:

- **High-side FET of the PWM phase** conducts for fraction D of its 120° window:
  P_HS = (1/3)·D·I²·R_ds(T_j)
- **Low-side FET** conducts:
  - 120° continuously as the held phase, plus
  - (1 − D) of its own PWM window (freewheel)

  P_LS = (1/3)·(2 − D)·I²·R_ds(T_j)
- **Per leg:** P_leg = P_HS + P_LS = (2/3)·I²·R_ds
- **Per channel (3 legs):** P_cond,ch = 2·I²·R_ds

Note: the per-leg and per-channel expressions are independent of D, but the HS/LS split is not. At low duty with high current (stall or punch-out from hover), the low-side FET is the hot device. Worst case is D → 0, giving P_LS → (2/3)·I²·R.

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

I²·R_ds = 6400 × 0.0018 = **11.52 W** (instantaneous, one FET on)

| Quantity | Formula | D = 0.95 | D = 0.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_HS | (1/3)·D·11.52 | 3.65 W | 1.92 W |
| P_LS | (1/3)·(2 − D)·11.52 | 4.03 W | 5.76 W |
| P_leg | (2/3)·11.52 | 7.68 W | 7.68 W |
| P_cond,ch (6 FETs) | 2·11.52 | 23.0 W | 23.0 W |

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Loss per Channel

P_Cu,ch = I²·(R_cu + R_sh) = 6400 × 0.65 mΩ = **4.16 W**

This term is often larger than designers expect on 1 oz nano boards. Use 2 oz or more for the power layers, with stitched via arrays (rule of thumb: ≥1 A per 0.3 mm via, so at least 40 vias per pad cluster at 80 A burst).

### 2.4 Self-Consistent Junction Temperature

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P. Iterate the following until it converges:

```
T_j(n+1) = T_ref + Z_th(t)·P(T_j(n))
P(T_j)   = k·I²·R_25·[1 + α(T_j − 25)]
```

Thermal runaway occurs if the loop gain reaches 1:

k·I²·R_25·α·Z_th ≥ 1

For a single LS FET at D = 0.5 (k = 0.5) with Z_th ≈ 20 K/W (steady-state, nano board):

0.5 × 6400 × 0.0012 × 0.005 × 20 = **0.38**

This is stable, but the loop multiplies the temperature rise by 1/(1 − 0.38) ≈ 1.6×. Include this factor in any steady-state estimate.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One leg per channel switches at any time, at 48 kHz.

### 3.1 V-I Overlap (HS Hard Switching)

P_VI = ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz
= **1.45 W**

Crossover time estimate: t_sw ≈ Q_gd/I_g + Q_gs2/I_g.

- With Q_gd ≈ 6 nC and an effective plateau gate current of 1 A (driver plus R_g), each edge is about 8 to 15 ns.
- Weaker drivers on nano ESCs (for example 3-phase pre-drivers with 0.5 A sink) roughly double this term.

### 3.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS)

P_dt = V_SD·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw
= 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz
= **0.31 W**

### 3.3 Reverse Recovery

P_rr ≈ Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.036 W**

The Q_rr-induced current spike also adds to HS turn-on loss. A conservative estimate adds about 0.05 W.

### 3.4 Output Capacitance

P_oss = C_oss,er·V²·f_sw = 1 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.03 W**

### 3.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in the Driver, Not the FET)
