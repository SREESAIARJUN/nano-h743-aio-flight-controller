## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)**. It does not commutate the motors. A production-grade 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz switching frequency is configured in the ESC firmware. ArduPilot only generates the DShot600 command stream and decodes the bi-directional telemetry.

```
            ┌─────────────── FC section ───────────────┐   ┌──────── ESC section (×4) ────────┐
 6S VBAT ──►│ BEC 5V/3A → LDO 3.3V → STM32H743VIT6     │   │ ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071)   │
            │  TIM3 CH1..4 ── DShot600 BIDIR ──────────┼──►│  → 3-ph gate driver (FD6288Q)    │
            │  ADC ← VBAT div, ESC current sense       │   │  → 6× N-MOSFET half bridges      │
            └──────────────────────────────────────────┘   │  ← BEMF dividers, shunt amp      │
                                                           └──────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each commutation sector, one leg is PWM-switched (HS/LS complementary) and one leg sinks continuously (LS on). Phase current *I* flows through:

- **Switching leg:** HS for fraction *D*, LS (synchronous) for fraction (1 − *D*).
- **Sink leg:** LS for 100 % of the sector.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The total is independent of *D*. The duty cycle only redistributes loss between HS and LS on the switching leg.

### 1.2 Current ripple correction

For a motor with phase inductance *L* (line-to-line *L*_LL), the ripple is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

Example: a 2207-class motor with *L*_LL ≈ 15 µH, *D* = 0.9, and *V*_bemf ≈ 21 V gives:

ΔI = (4.2 × 0.9) / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **5.3 A**, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 2.3 A².

This is negligible against 6400 A². At low throttle (*D* ≈ 0.5, low BEMF), ripple becomes significant. This is one argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, because ripple scales as 1/*f*_sw.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ |

Add PCB and package parasitics. For example, 2 oz copper with a 3 mm × 10 mm current path contributes about 0.8 mΩ per path, giving roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ effective per leg on an 8-layer stack-up. Use **R_eff = 1.5 mΩ hot** as the design value.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A burst

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Per-FET split at *D* = 0.9:

| FET | Loss |
|---|---|
| Sink LS | 9.6 W (worst device) |
| Switching HS | 8.64 W |
| Switching LS | 0.96 W |

**Conduction loss dominates. The sink-leg LS is the thermal hot spot.**

At 40 A continuous: P_cond = 2 × 1600 × 1.3 mΩ ≈ **4.2 W/ch**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz (per channel, switching leg)

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched HS:**

$$
P_{vi} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

(The induced extra turn-on current loss in the HS adds about 2× this. Budget **0.1 W**.)

**(c) C_oss charge loss:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1\,\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{n} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\,\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$

### 2.1 Switching subtotal

**P_sw,ch ≈ 2.2 W at 80 A.** This is linear in *I* and *f*_sw.

### 2.2 Total per-channel loss

| Condition | Conduction | Switching | Total per ch | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst, 48 kHz | 19.2 W | 2.2 W | **21.4 W** | **85.6 W** |
| 40 A cont., 48 kHz | 4.2 W | 1.1 W | 5.3 W | 21.2 W |
| 80 A burst, 24 kHz | 19.2 W | 1.1 W | 20.3 W | 81.2 W |

**Frequency trade-off.** Going from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.1 W per channel at burst. In exchange, it halves ripple, which reduces motor copper and iron loss and audible noise, and it improves low-throttle linearity. This trade is acceptable because switching loss is a small fraction of the total at high current.

**Efficiency check at burst:** P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × 0.9 ≈ 7.3 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 1 − 85.6/7300 ≈ **98.8 %**.
