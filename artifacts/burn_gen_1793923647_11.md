## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFETs:

- **PWM leg:** either the high-side (HS) or the low-side (LS) device carries current, as the leg alternates.
- **Return leg:** the low-side device carries current continuously.

This gives the per-ESC conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

### 2.2 RMS current including ripple

Phase-current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$I_{ph,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}\right)^2}$$

The peak-to-peak ripple is approximately:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical motor with L_ph ≈ 20 µH at high throttle, ΔI/I is about 10%. The RMS correction is therefore under 0.1%, so it can be neglected. **Use I_ph,rms ≈ 80 A.**

### 2.3 Temperature-dependent result

At T_j = 100 °C:

- R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.45 mΩ**
- FET conduction loss: P_cond,FET = 80² × 2 × 1.45 mΩ = **18.6 W per ESC**
- Copper loss (assuming a 0.5 mΩ phase and bus path): P_Cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per ESC**

### 2.4 Closed-form junction temperature and thermal runaway

Each FET dissipates on average P_ESC/6. Substituting the temperature-dependent R_DS gives a self-consistent junction temperature:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + \frac{R_\theta}{6}\left[P_{c25}(1 - 25\alpha) + P_{sw}\right]}{1 - \frac{R_\theta\,\alpha\,P_{c25}}{6}}, \qquad P_{c25} = 2I^2R_{25}$$
