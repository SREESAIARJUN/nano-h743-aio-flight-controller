# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 should not drive the power stage.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A production "H743 + 4-in-1" design is two subsystems:

| Subsystem | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) | Generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) per channel | AM32 (or BLHeli_32) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot responder |

The H743 needs only 4 timer channels for the motors. The 48 kHz switching happens in the ESC MCUs.

**Assumed baseline:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching loss) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor phase current, for ≤ 1 s |
| Board size | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mounting pattern, about 25×25 mm outline |
| Copper | 6-layer, 2 oz on all layers |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3 class |
| R_DS(on) | 1.2 mΩ typ, 1.6 mΩ max at 25 °C |
| Q_g | 30 nC |
| Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Q_oss | 40 nC |
| R_th(j-pcb) | ≈ 3 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, 2 A/1.5 A) |

Substitute your actual datasheet values. Every equation below is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant the motor current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path in series:

- **High side:** the PWM leg, shared by its high-side FET (duty D) and low-side FET (duty 1−D, synchronous freewheel).
- **Low side:** the commutated leg's low-side FET, on 100% of the time.

So the effective conduction resistance is always:

$$R_{path} = R_{DS(on),HS}\cdot D + R_{DS(on),LS}\cdot(1-D) + R_{DS(on),LS} = 2\,R_{DS(on)}$$

This holds when the high-side and low-side FETs are identical. It is independent of duty.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the multiplier is 1.4. A datasheet typically shows 1.4 to 1.6; use 1.5 for margin.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2\cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,(R_{shunt}+R_{trace})$$

Hot-junction case:

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times (1.6\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.5) = 6400\times 4.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Copper term, assuming 0.3 mΩ of phase/bus copper per channel:

$$P_{Cu}=6400\times0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

At 6-step, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period at full current, plus synchronous freewheel. Averaged over a revolution, the 30.7 W spreads across 6 FETs. However, the instantaneous per-FET peak is up to about 15.4 W, and the thermal time constant of a DFN die (~ms) is shorter than one electrical period at low eRPM. **Design to the 15.4 W instantaneous figure at low speed** and the ~5.1 W per-FET average at high eRPM.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Each cycle has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off of the active FET, plus the body-diode dead-time event of its complement.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

The rise/fall times come from gate charge and driver current:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{drive,src}},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{drive,snk}}$$

With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 12 nC through a 4.7 Ω gate resistor: t_r ≈ 12 ns, t_f ≈ 8 ns.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times20\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}=30\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.036\ \text{W}$$

Real Q_rr rises with di/dt. Budget ×3, giving **0.11 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.5\times40\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times(2\times200\,\text{ns})\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

At 48 kHz this term is larger than V–I overlap. Minimizing dead time (AM32 `dead_time` setting, driver-limited) is the highest-leverage switching knob.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_{gate}=Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times N_{switching}=30\,\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2=0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 30.7 |
| Copper / shunt | 1.9 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 |
| Gate drive | 0.03 |
| **Per channel** | **≈ 35.0** |
| **All 4 channels** | **≈ 140** |

Conduction dominates at 88%. Switching at 48 kHz is only about 7%.

Comparison with 24 kHz: switching-related losses drop to about 1.2 W per channel, saving about 4.7 W total. The cost is audible noise and higher current ripple. The board is conduction-limited, so frequency is not the lever that matters.

## 3. Thermal Calculation

### 3.1 Steady state is impossible at 80 A

A 25×25 mm board in prop-wash airflow (h ≈ 50–80 W/m²K, two sides) has:

$$R_{th,board-air}\approx\frac{1}{h\,A}=\frac{1}{60\times2\times(0.025)^2}\approx 13\ \text{K/W}$$

140 W × 13 K/W would give a 1,800 K rise. **80 A is strictly a transient rating.** The board's sustainable dissipation for ΔT = 60 K is about 4.6 W total. That corresponds to a continuous current of roughly 15–18 A per motor on this footprint.

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (lumped)

| Element | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (6 × 2 oz × 625 mm², ~60% fill) | ~1.4 g | 0.385 J/gK | 0.54 J/K |
| FR4 (625 mm² × 1.6 mm) | ~1.85 g | 1.1 J/gK | 2.0 J/K |
| 24 FETs + caps + connectors | ~1.5 g | ~0.7 | 1.0 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 3.5 J/K** |

### 3.3 Burst model

The model has two parts:

- **Board:** a first-order RC with τ_board = R·C ≈ 13 × 3.5 ≈ 46 s. Because the burst is much shorter than τ_board, the board heats essentially adiabatically:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t)\approx\frac{P_{tot}}{C}\,t=\frac{140}{3.5}\,t = 40\ \text{K/s}$$

- **Junction:** it sits above the board via the junction-to-PCB impedance. Its time constant is about 50 ms, so it reaches steady state within the burst:

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb}=P_{FET,peak}\cdot R_{th(j-pcb)}=15.4\times3=46\ \text{K}$$

### 3.4 Allowable burst duration

Starting from T_amb = 40 °C with a limit of T_j,max = 150 °C (derate to 135 °C):

$$40 + 40\,t + 46 \le 135 \;\Rightarrow\; t_{burst} \le \mathbf{1.2\ s}$$
