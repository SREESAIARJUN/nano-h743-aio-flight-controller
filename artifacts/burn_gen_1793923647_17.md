## 0. Scope, Architecture Reality Check, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the power stage directly. In every production ArduPilot 4-in-1 stack, the topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class)
                                            └─► 3-phase gate driver ──► 6 MOSFETs ──► motor
```

The power-loss model (§1–§3) applies to each **ESC channel**. The timer/DMA model (§4) applies to the **H743**. The netlist (§5) covers both domains and their interface.

### 0.2 Feasibility flag: "80A burst" on a nano board

A 20×20 mm nano ESC is realistically rated at **35–55A burst**. 80A per channel on that footprint is at or beyond physical limits. Section 3 quantifies this and shows that 80A is only survivable as a **sub-second burst** with very low R<sub>DS(on)</sub> devices. Treat 80A as the design stress case, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation structure

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, as in AM32/BLHeli_32).

- Within each 60° sector, exactly two phase legs carry current. One leg is PWMed; the other is held statically on.
- On the PWM leg, current alternates between the high-side (HS) FET for duty D and the low-side (LS) FET for 1–D. The current path therefore always contains **exactly two conducting FETs**, independent of D.
- Each leg carries current for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design-point T<sub>j</sub> of 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.2 \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 85] = 1.76\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Loss equations

Total conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Average conduction loss per leg (HS + LS combined):

$$P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{2}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I<sub>ph</sub>² | 6400 A² |
| P<sub>cond,ch</sub> | 2 × 6400 × 1.76 mΩ = **22.5 W** |
| P<sub>cond,leg</sub> | **7.5 W** |
| Instantaneous loss in a statically-on FET | I²R = **11.3 W** (this sets peak T<sub>j</sub>) |

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (often larger than designers expect)

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}\, \ell}{w\, t}\,[1 + 0.00393\,(T - 25)]$$

Example: 2 oz copper (70 µm), 3 parallel layers, ℓ = 15 mm, w = 4 mm, at 25 °C:

- R ≈ 1.72e-8 × 0.015 / (0.004 × 210e-6) ≈ **0.31 mΩ**
- Loss at 80A ≈ **2 W**, and this appears in each of the two current-carrying paths.

Including copper, budget **≈ 26 W conduction per channel**.

### 1.6 Stall caution

At low throttle, phase current exceeds battery current, because I<sub>bat</sub> ≈ D · I<sub>ph</sub>. Always model with **phase** current. The ESC current sensor typically reports battery-side current, which understates the FET stress.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The statically-on leg has no switching loss.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap loss (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$
