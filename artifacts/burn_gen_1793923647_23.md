# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Architecture (Job #35)

**Scope and caveats**

- **MCU roles.** The STM32H743 runs the flight controller (ArduPilot). It does not commutate the motors. Each motor is driven by its own small ESC MCU running AM32. That split is standard and assumed throughout.
- **80A is a burst rating only.** On a nano (20×20 mm) board it is board-heat-capacity-limited, as shown in §2.4.
- **Assumed components.** The MOSFET and gate-driver parameters are representative values. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values before you sign off.
- **Netlists.** These are reference schematics, not a DRC'd layout. Verify every pin and alternate function (AF) against the STM32H743 datasheet (DS12110) and the ArduPilot `hwdef` resolver output.

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model (per motor, then ×4)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) ≈ **1.41 mΩ**.

**Conducting path.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, two FETs conduct at every instant:
- the static leg's FET, and
- either the high-side or low-side FET of the PWM leg.

So for one motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

**Copper term.** Take R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for the pad + plane + solder path (2 oz, 6-layer).

**Numbers at 80 A:**

$$P_{cond} = 2(6400)(1.41\text{ m}\Omega) + 6400(0.3\text{ m}\Omega) = 18.05 + 1.92 = \mathbf{19.97\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle (1/3 duty). The average per FET is therefore about 18.05 / 6 ≈ **3.0 W**.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. Iterate until stable:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_c + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})\,R_{\theta JC}$$

This converges in about 3 iterations. In the regime of interest the instability margin is large, because ∂P/∂T × R_θ ≪ 1.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Two FETs per motor are commutating at any moment.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

Gate drive: the FD6288Q sources about 0.3 A and sinks about 0.6 A, with R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω.

Rise and fall times from the Miller plateau, using source current I_g,on = (V_dr − V_plateau)/R_g ≈ (12 − 3.5)/(4.7 + 1.5) ≈ 1.37 A, limited by the driver to about 0.3 A:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{8\text{ nC}}{0.3\text{ A}} \approx 27\text{ ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{8\text{ nC}}{0.6\text{ A}} \approx 13\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Output-capacitance and reverse-recovery loss**

$$P_{oss}+P_{rr} = (Q_{oss}+Q_{rr})\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 70\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.085\ W}$$

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

AM32 dead time is t_dt ≈ 100 ns at each of the 2 edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(d) Gate-drive loss** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} \times 2 = 45\text{n} \times 12 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.052\text{ W}$$
