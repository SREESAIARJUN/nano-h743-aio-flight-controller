## 0. Architecture Scope and Critical Review Findings

Four issues need to be settled before detailed design, because they change the numbers below.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---------|--------|
| F1 | The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU per channel (or one MCU for all four), such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. | The DMA section covers the FC side. The thermal and switching sections cover the ESC power stage. |
| F2 | **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80A per channel" are physically incompatible** for anything beyond a brief burst. Section 3.4 shows the board reaching its thermal limit in about 3 s with all four channels at 80A. Commercial 80A 4-in-1 ESCs are 30.5×30.5 mm or larger. | Either derate to about 45–55A continuous / 80A for under 2 s single-channel, or move to a 30.5 mm form factor. |
| F3 | The battery voltage was not specified. I assume **6S** (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal). I use 24 V for worst-case switching and 25.2 V for component stress. | Switching loss scales linearly with voltage. |
| F4 | 48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency. It is unrelated to DShot600's 600 kbit/s. | Both are treated separately below. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly one phase is sourcing current, one is sinking it, and one is floating.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw because of synchronous rectification.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the whole sector.

So at every instant, **two channels of R_DS(on)** sit in series with the motor current, independent of duty cycle. This is the key simplification.

### 2.2 RMS current including ripple

The phase inductance L_ph produces a triangular ripple of amplitude ΔI:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

**Worked example:** L_ph = 15 µH (typical 5" motor, line-to-line ÷ 2), D = 0.5, V_bemf ≈ 12 V.

ΔI = (12 × 0.5)/(15 µH × 48 kHz) = 8.3 A, so ΔI²/12 = 5.8 A².

Compared with 80² = 6400 A², the ripple contributes 0.09%. It is negligible at 80 A but matters at light load.
