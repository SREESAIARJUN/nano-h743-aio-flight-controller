| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 (one per motor) |
| Firmware | ArduPilot ≥ 4.3 (bidirectional DShot + ESC RPM telemetry) |

**Architecture clarification.** The H743 is the flight controller. It does not drive the MOSFET gates. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU that receives DShot600 from the H743 and drives the gate driver. A "4-in-1 nano with H743" is therefore either an AIO board or an FC + ESC stack. The math below applies to both.

**Design flags to resolve early:**
1. **80 A on a 30.5 mm board is a burst-only rating.** The math in §3 shows roughly 100 W of total ESC dissipation at full burst, which a nano board cannot sustain thermally.
2. **48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss** compared with 24 kHz for marginal acoustic and efficiency benefit on high-KV motors. It is justified mainly for low-inductance motors, where it reduces current ripple.
3. **A 40 V FET on 6S leaves about 15 V margin** for regen and ringing spikes. This is acceptable only with low-ESR bulk capacitance close to the bridge and a tight layout. Use 60 V parts if you plan for 8S.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × (1 + 0.5) = **1.8 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the HS conducts for fraction D, and the LS (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1–D).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the LS is held on for 100% of the sector.

The instantaneous conduction power per motor is therefore:

$$P_{cond,inst} = \underbrace{I^2R\,D + I^2R\,(1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{I^2R}_{\text{return leg}} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right), \qquad r = \frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_{avg}}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: a 2306-class motor with L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH, at D = 0.5 and 48 kHz:

ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20e-6 × 48e3) = **6.6 A**

This gives r = 0.082. The correction factor is 1.0006, which is negligible at 80 A. It matters only at light load or with very low-inductance motors, which is the actual justification for 48 kHz.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor | 2 × 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **23.0 W** |
| Hottest FET (return LS, during its 120° on-window) | 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W instantaneous** |
| Per-FET average over electrical cycle | 23.0 / 6 = **3.84 W** |
| **4-in-1 total conduction** | **92.2 W** |

The self-heating feedback loop (R_DS rises with T_j) converges, as long as:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th,ja} = 2I^2R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1$$

Check per FET at 80 A: 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 0.005 × R_th = 0.038·R_th. This is below 1 for R_th < 26 K/W, so the loop is stable, but there is no steady state under ~5 K/W on this board (see §3).
