# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Nano Stack): Electro-Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architecture correction

In a stack like this, the **STM32H743 does not commutate the motors**. It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The models below are split along that boundary.

### Feasibility flag

80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25×25) footprint is not physically sustainable. Even on 30.5/36 mm boards it is a **seconds-scale burst rating**. The thermal section quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### Commutation scheme

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. At any instant, three switches carry current:

- the PWM'd high-side (HS) FET, at duty D,
- its complementary low-side (LS) FET, at 1−D,
- the static LS FET of the return phase, at 1.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### Evaluation at T_j = 100 °C

- R_ds = 0.70 mΩ × 1.3375 = **0.936 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 0.936e-3 = **11.98 W per channel**

### Copper path loss

Assume a phase path (bus → FET → pad → motor wire) of 0.25 mΩ with 2 phases active:

- P_Cu = 2 × 6400 × 0.25e-3 = **3.2 W per channel**

### Per-device distribution

Each leg conducts 120° of 360°, so the per-FET average divides by 3. Within a 120° window:

- The **HS FET at high D** is the hottest. At D = 0.9 it sees 0.9·I²R plus all switching loss.
- The static LS FET sees full I²R, but only during its commutation sector.

Per-FET time-averaged conduction loss is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot k \cdot I^2 R \quad (k\le1)$$

This gives ≈ 2.0 W worst case per FET at 80 A.

### Electro-thermal coupling

R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve the fixed point:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j), \quad P(T_j)=P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_{th}P_0}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when α·R_th·P_0 → 1. With α = 0.0045, the runaway threshold is R_th·P_0 = 222 K. This is a hard design check.

## 2. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches, so the loss counts once per channel.

**(a) V-I overlap**

- t_r ≈ (Q_gs2 + Q_gd)/I_g,src = 12 nC / 1.0 A ≈ 12 ns, plus Miller/inductance margin → **15 ns**
- t_f ≈ 12 nC / 1.5 A ≈ 8 ns, plus margin → **12 ns**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(27\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

- P_rr = V·Q_rr·f = 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 k = **0.048 W**
- This also adds a turn-on current spike of roughly Q_rr/t_r ≈ 2.7 A.

**(c) Output capacitance**

- P_oss = ½·C_oss,er·V²·f = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.023 W**

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

- P_dt = V_f·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**

**(e) Gate drive**

- P_g = 2·Q_g·V_gs·f = 2 × 50 nC × 10 V × 48 k = **0.048 W**
- This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

**Total switching loss:** P_sw,ch ≈ **2.04 W per channel**

### Frequency sensitivity

$$\partial P_{sw}/\partial f \approx 42.5\ \mu W/Hz$$

- At 24 kHz, P_sw drops to about 1.0 W.
- At 96 kHz, P_sw rises to about 4.1 W.

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance racing motors, given current-ripple limits.

### Dead-time optimization

Dead-time loss is 30% of switching loss. Tuning dead time to the measured t_f plus a 30 ns margin (≈50 ns) roughly halves it.
