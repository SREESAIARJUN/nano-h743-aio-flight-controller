## 0. Feasibility Check

The numbers below show that **80A burst per motor on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 nano board is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond about 2–5 s** with one FET per switch position.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Phase current per motor (burst) | 80 A |
| Motors | 4 |
| Electrical power at 6S (25.2 V), full throttle | about 2 kW per motor, 8 kW total |
| Peak battery current (D≈1) | up to 320 A |

Most commercial "80A" nano stacks are rated for 10 s bursts at about 30×30 size, often with optimistic Rds(on) assumptions. Treat the analysis below as a tool for setting a realistic burst duration and derating curve.

**Architecture assumption:** The STM32H743 is the flight controller only. Each motor is commutated by its own small ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 does not drive the gates. DShot is the H743 → ESC-MCU link, which matches how every real 4-in-1 stack works.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right] \approx R_{25}\,k_T,\qquad k_T(125^\circ C)\approx 1.7$$

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active:
- **PWM-ed leg:** the high-side FET (Q_H) conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET of the same leg (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** its low-side FET is held fully on.

The PWM-ed leg therefore always carries I through *one* FET (HS or LS), and the return leg carries I through *one* LS FET. Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p}\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p} = \frac{2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

This is **independent of duty cycle**, which is why sync-rect ESCs are efficient at partial throttle. Phase current, not throttle, sets conduction loss.

### 2.3 Numbers (n_p = 1)

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times (1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.7) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.7\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.8\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,total} = 4 \times 21.8 = \mathbf{87.0\ W}$$

### 2.4 Per-FET average

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{21.8}{6} \approx 3.6\ \text{W (average)},\qquad P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{ds} = 10.9\ \text{W (while conducting)}$$

### 2.5 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

80A of phase current and up to 320A of bus current flow through copper. For a 2 oz (70 µm) layer:

$$R_{trace} = \rho_{Cu}\,\frac{L}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}(100^\circ C)\approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

Example: a 10 mm long, 5 mm wide phase pour on 2 inner+outer layers (2 × 70 µm):

$$R = 2.2\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.01}{0.005\cdot 140\times10^{-6}} = 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega \Rightarrow P = 80^2\times0.31\text{m} = 2.0\ \text{W per phase path}$$

Copper loss is comparable to a FET. **Use a 6-layer 2 oz stack minimum** and stitch every high-current pour with via arrays.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg hard-switches. Per motor, one HS FET switches hard, and its LS partner commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard-switching) loss

Transition time estimated from the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times20\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Q_rr rises with I and di/dt. At 80A, use the datasheet value at the actual di/dt, which can be 2–3× higher.

### 3.4 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} \approx 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated mostly in driver and gate resistors)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{kHz}\times2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$
