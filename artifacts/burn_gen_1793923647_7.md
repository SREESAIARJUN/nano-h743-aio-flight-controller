# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal and DShot Reference Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no access to a "Job #31" document, so every device parameter below is an **explicit assumption**. All equations are parameterized, so you can substitute datasheet values for your actual BOM.

**Architecture clarification.** In a "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. The ESC side has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, not the H743. The model treats them as separate domains joined by four DShot lines.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery (6S, worst-case full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, one per switch position) | — | — |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.006 /K |
| Gate charge: total / Q_gs2 / Q_gd | Q_g / Q_gs2 / Q_gd | 40 / 5 / 8 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss(er) | 1.0 nF |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V |
| Miller plateau | V_pl | 3.5 V |
| Gate resistance, on / off (total) | R_g,on / R_g,off | 5 Ω / 2 Ω |
| Dead time (AM32-typical) | t_dt | 300 ns |
| Switching frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| PCB phase-path copper resistance | R_cu | 0.5 mΩ |

> ⚠️ **Plausibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm board means 320 A aggregate if all four motors burst together. That is roughly 113 W of heat into about 4 J/K of thermal mass. Section 3 shows this only works for bursts of about 2 seconds or less. Treat "80A" as a burst rating that must be firmware-limited, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ** (round to 2.0 mΩ to include package resistance).

### 1.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At every instant, current flows through exactly two switches:
- one in the PWM leg (high-side for duty D, low-side for 1 − D), and
- the low-side of the return leg (held on).

Per channel:

P_cond,ch = I_ph² · (2·R_DS(T_j) + R_cu)
= 80² × (2 × 0.002 + 0.0005)
= 6400 × 0.0045
= **28.8 W**

This splits into **25.6 W in the FETs** and **3.2 W in the copper**.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET participates for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over rotation, each of the 6 FETs carries:

P_cond,FET ≈ 25.6 / 6 ≈ **4.3 W**

Peak instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R = **12.8 W**. This matters at low eRPM, where the commutation period becomes longer than the FET's thermal time constant.

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback

Because R_DS depends on T_j, the junction temperature must satisfy a fixed-point equation:

T_j = T_ref + P(T_j) · Z_th

Iterate until it converges. Thermal runaway occurs if:

dP/dT_j · R_th > 1, i.e. I²·R₂₅·α·R_th > 1

Here: 6400 × 0.0012 × 0.006 × 8 = 0.37. This is less than 1, so the design is stable but has significant positive feedback.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard-switched inductive load model:

### 2.1 V·I overlap

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:
- Turn-on: I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl) / R_g,on = 6.5 / 5 = 1.3 A
- Turn-off: I_g,off = V_pl / R_g,off = 3.5 / 2 = 1.75 A

Transition times:
- t_on = (Q_gs2 + Q_gd) / I_g,on = 13 nC / 1.3 A = 10.0 ns
- t_off = 13 nC / 1.75 A = 7.4 ns

Overlap loss:

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw
= ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 17.4 ns × 48 kHz
= **0.84 W**

### 2.2 Other switching terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · C_oss(er) · V_bus² · f_sw | 0.015 W |
| **Dead-time body-diode conduction** | V_F · I_ph · 2·t_dt · f_sw | **1.84 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver and R_g) | Q_g · V_drv · f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.038 W |

### 2.3 Switching totals

P_sw,ch ≈ 0.84 + 0.036 + 0.015 + 1.84 ≈ **2.73 W**

**Design insight.** Dead time dominates switching loss, at more than twice the V·I overlap term. Cutting dead time from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per channel. That gain is larger than the loss reduction from going down to 24 kHz. Verify shoot-through margin with the measured t_off of your gate driver before reducing it.

### 2.4 Total burst loss

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per channel | 28.8 + 2.73 ≈ **31.5 W** |
| 4 channels at 80 A simultaneously | **≈ 126 W** (113 W FET + 13 W copper) |
| Efficiency at 80 A, D ≈ 0.9 per channel | P_out ≈ 1.8 kW → **η ≈ 98.3 %** |
