# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #78): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** running ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC has its **own MCU(s)**, typically AT32F421, GD32E230 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware. The H743 never drives gates. It talks to the ESC over bi-directional DShot600 only. The math below is split accordingly.

**Design inputs.** No MOSFET part number or form factor was specified, so I used representative values. Replace them with your datasheet numbers. Every equation is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged |
| I_burst | 80 A per motor (phase current, trapezoidal) | Spec |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | Spec |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 class | Typical 80 A-class ESC FET |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V | Representative |
| k_T (125 °C factor) | 1.5 | Typical tempco curve |
| Q_g / Q_gd / Q_gs2 | 50 / 10 / 5 nC | Representative |
| Q_rr / Q_oss | 40 / 60 nC | Representative |
| V_f (body diode) | 0.8 V | Representative |
| Gate driver | FD6288-class, ~1.0 A source / ~1.5 A sink, V_drv = 10 V | Typical |
| Dead time t_dt | 100 ns (per edge) | Typical AM32 setting |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) | AM32/BLHeli_32 default |

> **⚠ Feasibility flag (stated up front):** "Nano" (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) and "80 A per motor" are physically in conflict. Section 3 shows that the board can hold 80 A for only **a few seconds**, even with prop-wash cooling. Treat 80 A as a short-burst rating with firmware current limiting, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 FETs and ≥ 3 oz copper.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, **exactly two FETs carry the full phase current at every instant**. Over one PWM period with duty D:

- In the PWM'd leg, the high side conducts for D and the low side for (1−D).
- In the return leg, the low side conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I^2\left[R_{HS}D + R_{LS}(1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{LS} \;\approx\; 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

The hot on-resistance is:

$$
R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\cdot k_T = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

Per motor channel:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Electrical frequency is in the kHz range, far faster than package thermal time constants, so averaging is valid:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 19.2/6 = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

**Copper and parasitic adders.** Phase copper plus solder pads plus via arrays typically add 0.3–0.6 mΩ per current path. With 0.4 mΩ in the 2-path loop:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} \approx 2.6\ \text{W per channel}
$$

**Bus capacitor RMS ripple.** For 6-step commutation the ripple current is approximately:

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}
$$

The worst case is at D = 0.5, giving 40 A_rms. With an ESR of 15 mΩ (combined bank), P_cap ≈ 24 W shared across the board. This is significant on a nano board. It is a reason for MLCC banks of ≥ 10 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel plus a low-ESR bulk electrolytic at the battery pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

### 2.1 Transition times

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}
\qquad
t_f \approx \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}
$$

### 2.2 V·I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times25\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Other switching-related terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 0.5 × 60 nC × 25.2 × 48k = 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k = 0.048 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.95 W** |

> **Design lever:** dead-time loss is about one-third of the switching loss. Dropping t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Confirm shoot-through margin on the bench first, using a 200 MHz probe on the switch node.

### 2.4 f_sw sensitivity

$$
P_{sw}(f) \approx 1.95\,\text{W}\times\frac{f}{48\,\text{kHz}}
$$

| f_sw | P_sw per channel | Approx. total loss per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.98 W | 22.8 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.95 W | 23.8 W |
| 96 kHz | 3.9 W | 25.7 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 90 %). Raising f_sw buys smoother low-throttle behavior at a small cost under burst load.
