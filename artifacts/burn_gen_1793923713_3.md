# Job #66: STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), 20×20 mm Nano Format
## System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot DMA Allocation, and Netlists

---

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth (Read First)

Three corrections determine everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an STM32G071, STM32F051, or AT32F421. The H743's job is to:
   - generate bidirectional DShot600 frames, and
   - decode the eRPM telemetry that comes back.

   The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math below shows roughly 80–90 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. A 20×20 board can absorb that for only about 1–3 seconds before the FETs approach their limits. The deliverable therefore specifies:
   - a time-limited burst envelope, and
   - a firmware current-limit and thermal-derating policy.

3. **The board is split across two PCBs.** At 20×20 the FC and ESC are almost always stacked: a top FC board plus a bottom 4-in-1 ESC board, joined by an 8-pin harness. I model them separately and treat the interconnect as a defined interface.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

Linear form:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Power-law form (more accurate for trench MOSFETs):

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{2.3}$$

| T_j | Multiplier | R_DS (max part, 1.3 mΩ at 25 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 °C | 1.65× | 2.15 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.86× | 2.42 mΩ |

### 2.2 6-step current paths

In each 60° commutation sector, current I flows through one high-side FET in phase A and one low-side FET in phase B.

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction D of each PWM cycle. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1 − D, minus dead time.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET in phase B conducts continuously for the whole sector.

So, ignoring dead time, two R_DS are always in the current path regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 2.3 Dead-time correction

During dead time, the body diode (forward voltage V_F ≈ 0.8 V) carries the current instead of the channel:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw}$$

With t_dt = 200 ns:

$$P_{dt} = 2 \cdot 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\times10^{3} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A burst (per channel)

| Case | R_DS | P_cond = 2·I²·R |
|---|---|---|
| Cold start, T_j = 25 °C | 1.3 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.0013 = **16.6 W** |
| Hot, T_j = 110 °C | 1.3 × 1.72 = 2.24 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.00224 = **28.6 W** |

### 2.5 Per-FET averaging

Each FET conducts for about one-third of the electrical period.

Example electrical frequency: a 14-pole motor (7 pole pairs) at 30,000 rpm gives f_e = 3.5 kHz, i.e. a 286 µs period.

Since 286 µs ≪ the die thermal time constant (~5–20 ms), the die sees average power:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot\frac{1}{2} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

That is about **2.8–4.8 W per FET** at 80 A, from cold to hot.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; one hard-switched transition pair per cycle.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

Transition times come from the gate drive. Use the Miller plateau V_pl ≈ 4.5 V, a 10 V drive, and R_G,total = R_drv + R_G,ext + R_G,int = 5 + 4.7 + 1 ≈ 10.7 Ω.

Gate current during the plateau:

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{10-4.5}{10.7} = 0.51\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{G,off} = \frac{4.5}{10.7} = 0.42\ \text{A}$$

Transition times (t_r = Q_gd / I_G,on, t_f = Q_gd / I_G,off, plus the Q_gs2 portion):

| Q_gd | t_r | t_f | t_r + t_f (with Q_gs2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 nC | 14 ns | 17 ns | ≈ 40 ns |

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\times10^{3} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

The body diode conducts during dead time, then the opposite FET turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

In practice, add the extra turn-on current spike to the VI term by multiplying P_VI by about 1.15.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}\cdot\tfrac12\cdot 2\ \text{(both FETs in leg)} = 25\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Gate drive

This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}\times 2\ \text{(switching pair)} = 30\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Switching total per channel at 80 A

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94\cdot1.15 + 0.036 + 0.030 \approx \mathbf{2.3\ W}$$

> **Design note:** at 48 kHz switching is only about 10% of total loss. Conduction dominates. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz (smoother audio/ripple) costs roughly 2.3 W per channel more, which is acceptable. Lowering R_DS(on) is the lever that matters.
