# STM32H743 Nano Flight Controller with Integrated 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Architecture, Loss Models, Thermal Analysis, and DShot Allocation

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two premises in the brief would cause a design failure if left uncorrected.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run 6-step or FOC commutation. The working architecture is:
   - The **H743 flight controller** runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - **4× dedicated ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and drive the gates. Typical choices are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230.

   The H743 sees the ESC only through four DShot lines, plus optional current and telemetry lines.

2. **80A per channel on a nano board is a burst rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 shows that the board cannot sustain all four channels at 80A for more than about 2 seconds. Single-channel or asymmetric bursts last longer. The derating below should be treated as a design requirement.

All device parameters below are **representative assumptions** for a 40V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class part. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; the equations are general.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, two phases carry current:

- Phase A: the high-side switch Q_AH is PWM'd at duty D, with the low-side Q_AL complementary during (1−D).
- Phase B: the low-side switch Q_BL is fully on.

So **exactly two MOSFETs are always in the current path**, whatever the duty cycle. Each MOSFET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss with Thermal Feedback

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.6\,(1 + 0.005 \cdot 85) = 2.28\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 2.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{29.2\ W}$$

This is a positive-feedback loop: higher temperature raises R_DS, which raises loss. Solve it iteratively, or in closed form for a lumped thermal resistance R_th:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\,k\,(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,k\,\alpha},\quad k = 2 I^2 R_{DS,25}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when R_th·k·α ≥ 1. Here k = 20.5 W, so runaway sets in at R_th ≥ 9.8 K/W per channel. Section 3 shows that the steady-state board resistance exceeds this limit. **This is the formal proof that 80A is burst-only.**

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

These losses apply only to the PWM'd half-bridge.

**V-I overlap (hard-switched high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (low side):**

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$
