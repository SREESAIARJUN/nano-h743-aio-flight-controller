## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Issues to Resolve First

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A production 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over **bidirectional DShot600**. Section 4 models the H743 side and Sections 1–3 model the ESC power stage. If you intended the H743 to drive the gate drivers directly, ArduPilot does not support that and it becomes a different project.

### 0.3 Issue 1: "Nano" and 80A ×4 are not physically compatible

At 80A burst, the four channels together dissipate **≈60–65 W** (Section 3). Peak battery current can reach **≈4 × 80 × D A**, which is up to 320 A at full duty.

- A 20×20 mm nano board cannot carry this. The FETs alone (6 per channel × 4 = 24 DFN5×6 parts) need more than 720 mm² of placement area.
- The realistic minimum is a **30.5×30.5 mm stack**: H743 FC on top, ESC below.
- The 80A figure must be specified as a **time-limited burst**. Section 3 derives the allowable duration (≈2–5 s).

### 0.4 Issue 2: Datasheet values must replace the placeholders

The FET parameters above are representative placeholders. Q_rr and t_r/t_f in particular depend on the gate driver, the gate resistor, and the layout. Validate them with a double-pulse test before you freeze the BOM.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 0.75 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.037 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss of the bridge is:

$$P_{cond,inst} = I_{rms}^2\,[D R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS} + R_{LS}]$$

With matched FETs (R_HS = R_LS = R), this simplifies to:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

The result is independent of D because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

RMS current including ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For a typical 2207 motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, so L_eff ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = **8.75 A**
- ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A²
- This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.04 A.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

This determines which FET is the hottest. Each phase spends 120° as high (PWM leg, if PWM is applied on the high side), 120° as low, and 120° floating.

Averaged over one electrical cycle:

| FET | Average loss | At D = 0.95, 80 A, 1.037 mΩ |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | (I²R/3)·D | **2.10 W** |
| Low-side | (I²R/3)·[1 + (1 − D)]·(½ weighting per sector arrangement) | ≈ 1.1–2.2 W, depending on the AM32 PWM-on-high vs. alternating mode |

Use the conservative value of **2.2 W per FET**.

### 1.5 Channel total

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.037\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{13.27\ W}$$

### 1.6 Board copper loss

The board copper is not negligible at this current.

- Sheet resistance of 2 oz Cu: R_□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, and about 0.33 mΩ/□ at 110 °C.
- Phase path from FET to motor pad, with 4 layers paralleled, is about 3 squares: R ≈ 3 × 0.33/4 ≈ 0.25 mΩ.
- Loss: P_Cu,ph = 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W per channel**.
- Battery bus: at D = 1 and 4 channels, the bus carries 320 A through roughly 0.08 mΩ of plane and pads, giving P ≈ **8.2 W for the whole board**.
