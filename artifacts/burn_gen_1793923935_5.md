# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Architecture, Loss Model, and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Two constraints shape the rest of this design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In ArduPilot, the H743 is the flight controller. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, performs commutation, and answers the bidirectional DShot600 frames. The H743 only sends DShot frames and captures the eRPM replies.

2. **80 A burst on all four channels is not thermally achievable on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5) board.** The model in §2–3 shows a board temperature rise of roughly 20 K/s at a 4×80 A load with no heatsink. "80 A burst" is only defensible under the following conditions:
   - per motor, not all four at once;
   - for 1–2 s or less;
   - on at least a 30.5×30.5 board with heavy copper.
   
   The numbers below quantify this so the requirement can be renegotiated with evidence.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Device and Temperature Assumptions

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| MOSFET | 40 V N-channel, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 package (e.g., 40 V / ~1 mΩ class) |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | R(T) = R₂₅·[1 + α(T_j − 25)], α ≈ 0.0045 K⁻¹ |
| R_DS(on) at T_j = 125 °C | R_hot ≈ 1.45 mΩ (use **1.5 mΩ** for design) |

### 2.2 Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

AM32 uses complementary PWM. In each of the six sectors:
- the **PWM phase** high-side conducts for D·T and its low-side conducts for (1−D)·T;
- the **return phase** low-side conducts for the full period.

Two FETs are therefore always in series with the motor current I. Per channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each phase acts as the PWM phase for 2 of 6 sectors and as the return phase for 2 of 6 sectors. Average conduction fractions are:

$$\bar{k}_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D,\qquad \bar{k}_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}$$

| Duty D | HS FET loss | LS FET loss |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (full burst) | I²R/3 = **3.2 W** | 3.2 W |
| 0.5 | 1.6 W | **4.8 W** (LS worst case) |

### 2.4 Copper Loss

Assume about 0.5 mΩ per phase path (pads, vias, inner layers) and two paths conducting:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6.4\ W/\text{ch}$$

With 2 oz copper on inner layers and stitched power planes this can drop to about 3.2 W. The 3.2 W figure is used below.

## 3. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM half-bridge switches.

| Term | Formula | Inputs | Loss / ch |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, 40 ns | **1.94 W** |
| Body diode, dead time | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 V, 80 A, 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 60 nC | 0.04 W |
| Gate drive | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 40 nC, 10 V | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Dead-time sensitivity.** The body-diode term is the second-largest switching loss. Each 50 ns of dead time costs about 0.3 W per channel at 80 A, so dead time should be tuned on the bench with a current probe.

### 3.1 Per-Channel and Board Totals at Burst

| Item | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per channel: 19.2 W conduction + 2.7 W switching + 3.2 W copper | **≈ 25 W** |
| Four channels | **≈ 100 W** |
| Plus BEC, gate drivers, and capacitor ESR | ≈ 3–5 W |

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped Board Heat Capacity (30.5×30.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 6 layers × 2 oz)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 1.49 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ = 2.75 g, × 1.1 J/g·K | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper | 9.3 cm² × 0.007 cm × 6 layers × 8.96 g/cm³ = 3.5 g, × 0.385 J/g·K | 1.35 J/K |
| 24 FETs and capacitors | | ~0.4 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 4.8 J/K** |

### 4.2 Adiabatic Burst Rise

For short bursts, convection is negligible.

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{100}{4.8} \approx \mathbf{21\ K/s}$$

| Burst duration | ΔT_board (4×80 A) | ΔT_board (1×80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 10 K | 2.6 K |
| 2 s | 42 K | 10 K |
| 5 s | 104 K ❌ | 26 K |

Starting from 45 °C ambient inside the frame, a 4-motor 80 A burst exceeds 100 °C on the board in about 2.6 s.
