# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and Timing Model (Job #4)

**Classification:** Engineering reference model. Parameters marked **[A]** are assumptions and must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values before layout freeze.

## 0. Feasibility Review

Before the derivations, the numbers below show that **80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm / 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO board is only possible for short bursts (seconds, not tens of seconds).** I recommend either redefining the spec as "80 A for ≤ 2 s, 25–30 A continuous" or moving to a 30.5×30.5 mm stacked ESC.

| Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A at full throttle | ~320 A battery current. This exceeds the XT60 and pigtail ratings by about 5×, and the battery-side current path becomes the dominant loss. |
| Board thermal mass ≈ 4–5 J/K | Total dissipation of ~64 W heats the board at ~15 K/s (Section 3). |
| Copper area for 24 FETs on a nano footprint | This forces 3×3 mm FET packages, which have higher R_DS(on) and higher R_θ than 5×6 mm parts. |

Architecture assumed:
- The **H743 is the flight controller only.** It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- The ESC MCUs are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware.
- One 3-phase gate driver per channel, six N-FETs per channel.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

Assume 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification). At any instant, two FET channels carry I_ph:
- **During t_on:** HS_x and LS_y conduct.
- **During t_off:** LS_x and LS_y conduct (synchronous freewheel).

The conduction loss is therefore approximately duty-independent:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 2·6400·0.70e-3 = **8.96 W** |
| 100 °C | 0.91 mΩ | **11.65 W** |
| 125 °C | 0.98 mΩ | **12.54 W** |

**PCB copper term.** A 2 oz (70 µm) trace at 0.25 mΩ/sq, with about 4 squares effective per path after paralleling across layers, gives R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ. The resulting loss is:

80² × 0.5 m = **3.2 W per channel**

This loss is distributed over the board, but it is not negligible.

### 2.2 Dead-time (body-diode) loss

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8·80·200\text{n}·48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Switching (V–I overlap) loss

The transition times follow from the gate-plateau charge and the driver current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = 15\text{ ns (use 20 ns with layout parasitics)}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·40\text{n}·48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.4 Capacitive, recovery and gate losses

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}·25.2·48\text{k}=0.060\text{ W}$$

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}=0.5·1.5\text{n}·635·48\text{k}=0.023\text{ W}$$

$$P_{gate}=2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2·80\text{n}·10·48\text{k}=0.077\text{ W}$$

The gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

### 2.5 Channel total at 80 A (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 12.54 |
| Copper | 3.20 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Switching | 1.94 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.16 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 18.5 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 74 W** |

### 2.6 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{1.94+0.61+0.16}{48\text{k}} = 56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per channel. Conduction losses still dominate by roughly 5:1, so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.**

### 2.7 Efficiency check at the burst point

At full duty:
- Input power ≈ 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W per channel.
- Losses ≈ 18.5 W, so η ≈ **99.1 %**.

The efficiency is fine; the problem is **heat density**, not efficiency.

### 3.2 Convective path (prop wash)

- h ≈ 100 W/m²K, which is generous for a buried AIO.
- Area = 8×10⁻⁴ m² (both faces), so G_conv ≈ 0.08 W/K.
- Steady-state ceiling for an 80 K rise: Q_max = 0.08 × 80 ≈ **6.4 W total**, or **1.6 W per channel**.

### 3.3 Transient response

The board follows a first-order response:

$$C_{th}\frac{dT}{dt}=P(T)-G(T-T_a)$$

The time constant is τ = C/G ≈ 54 s. That is much longer than the burst, so the board heats nearly adiabatically:

$$\frac{dT}{dt}\approx\frac{74}{4.3}\approx \mathbf{17\ K/s}$$
