# Job #50: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC)
## Electrothermal Model, DShot Timing/DMA Architecture, and Reference Netlist

**Document class:** Design intent, Rev A. All numbers come from the stated assumptions and must be validated on hardware (thermocouple and IR on the FETs, scope on gate and phase nodes).

---

## 0. Executive Summary

A nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board **cannot dissipate 80 A continuously.** The model below shows:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Loss per motor channel at 80 A, Tj ≈ 110 °C | **≈ 22–24 W** |
| Total loss, all four channels at 80 A | **≈ 90–95 W** |
| Steady-state board dissipation capability (in prop wash) | **≈ 6–9 W** |
| Adiabatic burst time from 40 °C to 105 °C board | **≈ 3–5 s** (all four channels at 80 A) |
| Defensible continuous rating | **≈ 25–30 A per channel** at full 4-motor load |

"80 A" must therefore be specified as a **≤ 3 s burst, single- or dual-channel** rating.

Two architectural clarifications drive the rest of the design:

1. **48 kHz switching runs on the ESC MCUs, not the H743.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM.
2. **Battery current is not phase current.** Battery current ≈ D × I_phase. Four channels at 80 A and full duty would draw ~320 A from the pack, which no nano power-entry path or connector can carry. Real bursts are limited by pack sag and by the copper.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path topology

In 6-step operation, phase X is PWM-driven high, phase Y is held low, and phase Z floats.

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS_X then LS_Y.
- **Off-time (1 − D):** current freewheels through LS_X (synchronously rectified) then LS_Y.

Two FETs are therefore always in the current path:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]
$$

At Tj = 110 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.425 = **1.43 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond,ch}(80\text{ A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.43\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.3\ W}
$$

Because Rds rises with temperature, this is a positive-feedback loop. Thermal runaway is avoided only while

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta,ja,eff} < 1
\;\Rightarrow\;
2 I^2 R_{25} k_T \, R_{\theta} < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.005 = 0.064 W/K. Stability therefore needs R_θ < 15.6 K/W from junction to effective ambient. Steady state violates this bound (see §5), so the burst limit is genuinely time-bound, not just a margin issue.

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution over one electrical cycle

Each phase spends 1/3 of the cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the low leg, and 1/3 floating.

| FET | Conducting fraction of cycle | I²R average share |
|---|---|---|
| HS (PWM leg) | D/3 | (D/3)·I²R |
| LS (PWM leg, sync rectification) | (1 − D)/3 | ((1−D)/3)·I²R |
| LS (low leg) | 1/3 | (1/3)·I²R |

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst):

- HS ≈ I²R/3 = **3.05 W**
- LS ≈ I²R/3 = **3.05 W**

The 18.3 W per channel is spread over 6 FETs, about 3.05 W each on average.
