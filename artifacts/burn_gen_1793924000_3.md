## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape the rest of the document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or GD32E230. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. Switching losses are therefore set by ESC firmware (PWM frequency, dead-time), not by H743 timer configuration.

2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm board is thermally marginal.** The model below shows the board can absorb an 80 A burst on one or two channels for about 1–3 s. All four channels at 80 A simultaneously would raise the board temperature at roughly **60 °C/s** with no airflow. Treat 80 A as a **per-channel, ≤1 s, non-simultaneous** rating unless thermal validation proves otherwise.

3. **All component parameters below are representative placeholders.** They reflect 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class devices. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected parts before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current Path

Assumptions:
- Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification, which is the AM32 default complementary PWM.
- Phase current I_ph = 80 A.

At any instant, current flows through exactly **two** FETs per motor channel:

- **PWM-ing leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D; the synchronous low-side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Sinking leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

The loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \quad (R_{HS}=R_{LS})$$

This is essentially **independent of duty cycle**. This matters: a stalled or heavily loaded motor at low throttle still produces full conduction loss.

### 2.2 Ripple Correction

Phase current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH (typical 2207 motor, phase-phase about 30 µH), V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12.6 V, D = 0.5, f = 48 kHz:

- ΔI = 12.6 × 0.5 / (30e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **4.4 A pk-pk**
- I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12 = 6400 + 1.6 → negligible (<0.03 %)

### 2.3 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This creates positive feedback, so the model must be solved iteratively (§4.3).

| T_j | R_ds | 2·I²·R_ds per channel | +PCB 2·I²·R_pcb | Total per channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 17.9 W | 3.8 W | 21.7 W |
| 75 °C | 1.72 mΩ | 22.0 W | 3.8 W* | 25.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.03 mΩ | 26.0 W | 3.8 W* | 29.8 W |

*Copper TCR (≈0.39 %/°C) adds roughly another 1.5 W at 125 °C.

**Four channels at 80 A, hot: ≈ 120 W conduction alone.**

### 2.4 Per-FET Loss at Steady 120° Commutation

Each FET conducts in a fraction of the electrical cycle:
- High-side: 1/3 × D
- Low-side: 1/3 × (1 − D) + 1/3

At D = 1 (full throttle), the worst-case FET average is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot6400\cdot2.03\,\text{m}\Omega = 4.3\ \text{W}$$

Within an electrical cycle (≈ 1–3 kHz eRPM frequency) the junction sees **13 W pulses**. The cycle is far shorter than the die thermal time constant, so averaging is valid for junction temperature.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only **one leg hard-switches** at a time:
- The high-side FET hard-switches.
- The low-side commutates through its body diode during dead-time, which causes reverse-recovery loss in the opposite device.
