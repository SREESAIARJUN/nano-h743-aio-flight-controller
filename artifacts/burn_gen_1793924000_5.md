## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Three issues need to be settled before the math, because they change the numbers.

**1. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.**
In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/STM32G0 running BLHeli. That MCU commutates the bridge and generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 flight controller only emits DShot600 frames and captures eRPM telemetry. Section 3 covers the H743 side and Sections 1–2 cover the ESC power stage.

**2. 80 A per channel at "nano" (20×20 mm) scale is not thermally sustainable.**
The model below shows why. I treat 80 A as a **burst** rating, bounded in time by board thermal mass. A credible 80 A burst design needs at least a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint, 8-layer construction with 2–3 oz copper, and 5×6 mm MOSFETs.

**3. Battery-side current.**
Four channels at 80 A means up to 320 A of phase current. DC bus current is roughly D·I_phase, about 290 A at D≈0.9. That exceeds XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and XT90 limits. 80 A × 4 simultaneous is not a real operating point. The design target should be 80 A on one or two motors transiently, with a pack-level limit of about 150–180 A peak.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Assumptions (reference MOSFET class: 40 V, 5×6 mm PQFN)

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS=10 V (max) | R_25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient (normalized @ T_j≈110 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Hot R_DS(on) | R_hot | 1.5 mΩ |
| Phase current (burst) | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Rise / fall time (driver-limited) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode V_f @ 80 A | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Q_rr | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Q_oss @ 25 V | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Q_g total @ 10 V | Q_g | 50 nC |

R_DS(on) temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\alpha},\quad \alpha \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3$$

### 1.2 Trapezoidal six-step with synchronous rectification

In every 60° sector, two phase legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D. The complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D) via synchronous rectification.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot}\cdot 1 = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

This result is independent of D, which is a useful property for burst modelling.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

The electrical period at burst speed is short compared with the die thermal time constant. For example, at 30 k RPM with 14 poles (7 pole pairs), f_e = 3.5 kHz and T_e = 286 µs, while τ_die is about 1–5 ms. Each FET can therefore be cycle-averaged.

Each leg is PWM-high for 1/3 of the cycle and static-low for 1/3:

$$\bar P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad \bar P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$$

At D = 0.9:

| FET | Fraction of I²R | Conduction power |
|---|---|---|
| HS | 0.300 | 2.88 W |
| LS | 0.367 | 3.52 W |

- Per leg: 6.40 W
- Per motor (3 legs): 19.2 W ✔

### 1.4 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

For 2 oz copper, the sheet resistance is R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C and about 0.30 mΩ/□ hot.

Assume 2 parallel internal layers plus 1 outer layer per phase path, with about 3 squares from FET to motor pad. Then:

$$R_{trace} \approx \frac{3 \times 0.30}{3} = 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu,phase} = 2 I^2 R_{trace} \approx 3.8\,\text{W per motor}$$

This term is often ignored and should not be. It is about 20% of FET conduction loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching occurs only on the PWM leg. That is 1 of 3 legs at any instant, so switching events per motor run at f_sw.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$
