# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #70): Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Timing Model

This document states its assumptions explicitly so that every number can be traced and re-derived. Two architectural points come first because they shape the rest of the model.

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Feasibility Flags

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 design the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Examples are the AT32F421 and the STM32G071. The model therefore splits into three independent domains:

- **FC domain:** H743 timers, DMA and DShot framing.
- **ESC domain:** ESC MCU, gate driver, MOSFETs, 48 kHz PWM.
- **Thermal domain:** shared PCB copper and airflow.

**2. 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is burst-only.** Section 3 shows the stage dissipates roughly 90–100 W at 80 A on all four channels. Steady-state operation would mean a temperature rise of several hundred kelvin. The design target must therefore be expressed as a **time-limited burst**, not a continuous rating. The model yields an envelope of about 2–3 s from a warm start.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary PWM)

**Commutation pattern.** In trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- The **PWM leg** conducts high-side for D·T_sw and low-side (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_sw. Its conduction is therefore continuous through one device or the other.
- The **return leg** low-side conducts 100 % of the time.

**Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,k_T R_{ds,25}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.6\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper and shunt losses:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\text{ W}$$

$$P_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\text{ W}$$

**Per-device average.** Conduction rotates through all six FETs over each electrical cycle. The average per FET is about 20.5 / 6 ≈ **3.4 W**, with a 10.2 W instantaneous peak during the device's 120° interval. At electrical frequencies above about 200 Hz the die thermal time constant (around 1 ms) smooths this ripple, so the average is the governing value.

**Mitigation by paralleling.** Using two FETs per switch halves P_cond to 10.3 W per channel. This is the strongest single lever, but it is hard to fit on a nano footprint.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in six-step operation. The terms are:

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched:**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = 1.45\text{ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\text{ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

**Total switching loss:** P_sw ≈ **2.2 W per channel**.

**Design note on dead time.** Dead-time loss (d) is about 30 % of the switching budget. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the bench before committing to the shorter value.

**Frequency scaling.** Switching loss is linear in f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel, but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.
