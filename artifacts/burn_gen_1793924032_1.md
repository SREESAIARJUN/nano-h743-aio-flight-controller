## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (e.g. GM4108), pole pairs p = 7, phase resistance R ≈ 10 Ω, inductance L ≈ 3 mH |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701) on each axis |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, single-precision FPU, center-aligned PWM |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI at 24 MHz, gyro ODR 8 kHz |
| Gimbal order | Base → Yaw (ψ) → Roll (φ) → Pitch (θ) → camera |

Rate hierarchy (each loop runs ≥ 4–10× slower than the loop inside it):

```
 20 kHz  : Current loop (FOC, PI on i_d / i_q, SVPWM)        ── PWM ISR
  8 kHz  : IMU read, dual-IMU fusion, notch/LPF chain       ── IMU DRDY ISR
  4 kHz  : Velocity loop (PI + accel FF + cogging FF)        ── decimated 1/5 of PWM ISR
  1 kHz  : Attitude estimator (Mahony), angle loop (P/PI)     ── decimated 1/20
```

## 1. FOC Current Loop (Innermost)

### 1.1 Electrical Angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

`θ_offset` is found at boot by applying a fixed voltage vector along the d-axis and reading the encoder.

### 1.2 Clarke Transform (Amplitude-Invariant)

Assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

### 1.3 Park Transform

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.4 Plant in the dq Frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

Torque equation for a surface-mount PMSM:

$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p \lambda_m i_q = K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.5 PI Controller with Decoupling and Back-EMF Feedforward

Pole-zero cancellation: choose K_i/K_p = R/L. The closed loop then becomes first order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c,\qquad \omega_c \le 2\pi f_{\text{PWM}}/10
$$

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Use i_d* = 0.

**Voltage limiting.** The output is clamped to the linear SVPWM hexagon's inscribed circle:

$$
|v_{dq}| \le V_{dc}/\sqrt3
$$

### 1.6 Discrete PI with Back-Calculation Anti-Windup

Sample period T_s, saturation function sat(·):

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_k &= K_p e_k + I_k\\
\bar u_k &= \mathrm{sat}(u_k)\\
I_{k+1} &= I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw}T_s(\bar u_k - u_k), \qquad K_{aw}\approx K_i/K_p
\end{aligned}
$$

### 1.7 Inverse Park and SVPWM (Min/Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{inverse Clarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta)
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big)
$$

$$
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$

This is mathematically identical to sector-based SVPWM and is branch-light.

## 2. Cascaded Angle → Velocity Control

### 2.1 Gimbal Kinematics (Why the Loops Use IMU Rates)

The goal is to hold the **camera** attitude, so the outer loops act on camera-frame quantities from the IMU. The encoders are used for commutation and for kinematic mapping.

For the Yaw → Roll → Pitch chain, the camera body angular velocity is:

$$
\boldsymbol\omega_{cam} = R_\theta^\top R_\phi^\top(\boldsymbol\omega_{base} + \dot\psi\,\hat z) + R_\theta^\top\dot\phi\,\hat x + \dot\theta\,\hat y
$$

The joint-rate demand from a camera-frame rate error therefore uses the inverse Jacobian J(φ, θ):

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\psi\\ \dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\end{bmatrix}_{cmd}
= J^{-1}(\phi,\theta)\;\boldsymbol\omega_{cam,cmd},
$$

$$
J^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}
-\dfrac{\sin\theta}{\cos\phi} & 0 & \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\cos\phi}\\[2mm]
\cos\theta & 0 & \sin\theta\\[1mm]
\sin\theta\tan\phi & 1 & -\cos\theta\tan\phi
\end{bmatrix}
\quad(\text{body axes } x=\text{roll},\ y=\text{pitch},\ z=\text{yaw})
$$
