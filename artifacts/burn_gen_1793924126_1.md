# STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power, Thermal & Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

**Job #4: Systems Architecture Package, Rev A**

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions & Design-Risk Flags

### 0.1 System partition

The STM32H743 runs **ArduPilot** as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware. This is the only viable topology, for three reasons:

* ArduPilot has no field-oriented or 6-step motor-control firmware for the H7.
* Bi-directional DShot assumes a separate ESC MCU that returns eRPM telemetry.
* A single H743 cannot generate 4 × 3-phase 48 kHz PWM while also running the EKF3 loop.

### 0.3 Design-risk flags

**⚠️ Flag 1: 80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows the board's thermal mass supports roughly 1–2 s at 80 A × 4. Sustained capability is about 20–25 A per channel. Marketing must quote it this way.

**⚠️ Flag 2: 4 × 80 A means 320 A of motor current.** Battery current is lower (I_batt ≈ D·I_motor), but at full throttle it approaches 300 A or more. XT60/XT90 connectors, battery pads and copper pours are the limiting elements, not the FETs. If "80 A" means total board current, divide every per-channel figure below by 4 in current, which is 16× in conduction loss.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, one phase is driven high and one phase is driven low:

* **PWM ON (duration D·T):** current flows through Q_H(phase A) → motor → Q_L(phase B).
* **PWM OFF (duration (1−D)·T):** the current freewheels through Q_L(phase A), which is synchronously rectified, → motor → Q_L(phase B).

So two MOSFET channels always carry the full phase current, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(per ESC channel, excluding dead-time interval)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 0.90 \times 1.383 = 1.24\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase inductance (L_ph ≈ 10–30 µH on typical 5" motors) gives a peak-to-peak ripple current:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-2V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

At L = 15 µH and D = 0.5, the worst case is ΔI ≈ 4.4 A. The correction term ΔI²/12 = 1.6 A² is negligible against 6400 A² (0.03 %). It is ignored below.
