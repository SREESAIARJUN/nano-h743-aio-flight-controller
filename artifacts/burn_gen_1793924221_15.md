# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #7)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET part number, board size, or cell count was specified, so this model uses a **reference parameter set**. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute the datasheet values of your selected parts.

### Architecture

This design follows the standard partition for AIO and stacked boards:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) --bidir DShot600 x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                               --> 3-phase gate driver --> 6 FETs per motor
```

The H743 does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot only sends DShot commands and receives eRPM telemetry. Commutation, PWM generation at 48 kHz, and dead-time are owned by the ESC MCU firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Conduction topology
The ESC firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32) drives the motor with 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary PWM, which gives synchronous rectification:

- The **PWM leg** conducts I through the high-side FET for a fraction D of each period, and through the low-side FET for the remaining (1−D).
- The **return leg** low-side FET conducts I continuously.

As a result, exactly **two FETs carry the phase current at every instant**. This holds regardless of D, apart from dead-time, which is handled in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

The loss itself raises T_j, so this must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta J\text{-}PCB}$$

### 1.3 Current ripple correction
The phase current carries ripple. Its RMS value is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

**Worst-case ripple example:** L_ph = 8 µH (a typical value for a low-kV 5–7" motor), V_bus − E = 12 V, D = 0.5, f = 48 kHz.
- ΔI_pp = 12 × 0.5 / (8 µH × 48 kHz) = 15.6 A
- Added RMS term = 15.6² / 12 ≈ 20 A², which is about 0.3% of I² at 80 A.

The ripple correction is negligible at burst current but not at light load.

### 1.4 Per-motor and per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2\,(R_{Cu,phase} + R_{shunt})$$

**At 80 A, assuming T_j converges near 105 °C:**
- R_ds(105 °C) = 0.9 × (1 + 0.005 × 80) = **1.26 mΩ**
- FET conduction: 2 × 6400 × 1.26 mΩ = **16.1 W per motor**
- Copper path loss (≈ 0.3 mΩ) and shunt (0.25 mΩ): 6400 × 0.55 mΩ = **3.5 W**

**Per-FET loss averaged over one electrical cycle:**
Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, but the PWM-leg conduction is split between the high-side and low-side FETs by D and (1−D).
- Average per FET ≈ 16.1 / 6 = 2.7 W.
- The return-leg low-side FET carries the full current for 120°. Its 120° window average is 8 W.

Use the **8 W, 120° window value** as the transient junction-stress case. At 48 kHz switching and an electrical frequency of 1–3 kHz, the thermal time constants are far longer than the commutation period. The junction therefore effectively sees the cycle-averaged value. Rotation between the FETs averages the per-FET load to about 2.7 W plus the switching share.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 Voltage-current overlap loss
The gate currents during the Miller plateau are:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 1.3\text{ A},\qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 0.7\text{ A}$$

The resulting transition times are:

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 11.5\text{ ns},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 21.4\text{ ns}$$

The overlap loss is:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_{on}+t_{off})\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 32.9\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.59\ W}$$

### 2.2 Other switching loss terms

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss,eff · V_bus² · f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.33 W per motor** |
