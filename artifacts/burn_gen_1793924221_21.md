## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Any Math

Several aspects of the request as worded need resolving first.

| Issue | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 role** | The H743 is the *flight controller* MCU. It does not run the ESCs. ArduPilot does not commutate motors. | Use 4× dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 (e.g., AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. |
| **"Nano" + 80A burst** | A nano/20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80A = **320A** at the input. At 6S that is about 8 kW. Connectors, the battery lead and the input bus copper are the binding constraint, not the FETs. | Treat 80A as a **≤2–4 s per-channel transient**, not simultaneous. Use the 30.5×30.5 mm minimum for the power stage. The thermal section shows why. |
| **48 kHz PWM** | AM32 defaults to 24 kHz. 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss. That is acceptable for smoothness, but it is a deliberate cost. | Model below includes it explicitly. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology
AM32 drives 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In every commutation interval, current flows through:
- the PWM leg: high-side for duty D, then low-side (synchronous rectification) for 1−D, and
- the return leg: low-side fully on.

The current therefore always sees **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of D. This is the key simplification.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 140 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 115) = **1.75 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Ripple correction
With phase-current ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:
$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

At r = 0.3 the correction is +0.75%. It is negligible at 80A with typical motor inductance, so it is ignored below.

### 2.4 Per-channel conduction loss
$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.75\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

### 2.5 Worst single FET (return-leg low-side, 100% on during interval)
$$P_{FET,pk} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 6400 \times 1.75\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{11.2\ W}$$

Over a full electrical cycle, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time. Thermal time constants of the FET body (≈ms) are longer than one commutation interval at high eRPM. At **low eRPM / stall-start bursts**, however, a single FET sees the full 11.2 W for tens of ms. **Design to the peak.**
