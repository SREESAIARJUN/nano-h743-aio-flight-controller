# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Finding

**An 80 A per-channel burst rating is possible on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board only as a short transient, roughly 2–4 s.** The board cannot sustain 80 A at any realistic airflow. The steady-state heat path is about 5–8 K/W board-to-air, while full-burst dissipation is about 80 W. Section 4 derives the safe burst-duration envelope. The architecture should treat 80 A as a firmware-enforced, time-limited current rather than a continuous rating.

**Architecture correction.** In 4-in-1 ESCs, the H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel, or one shared ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), handles commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC MCU. The model below covers the ESC power stage and the H743 DShot path separately.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.38 ≈ **1.24 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one phase is the PWM leg. Its high side conducts for duty D, and its low side conducts for (1−D) via synchronous rectification. A second phase holds its low side on. The current I therefore always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts on average one third of the electrical period. The per-FET RMS current is:

$$I_{rms,FET}=I\sqrt{1/3}\;\Rightarrow\;P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}\ \text{(averaged over a full electrical cycle)}$$

**At 80 A and 110 °C:**

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.24\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal coupling

Because R_ds depends on T_j, the loss and temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{jc}P_{FET}(T_j)\;\Rightarrow\;T_j=\frac{T_c+R_{jc}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{jc}P_0\alpha}$$

Here P₀ = I²R₂₅/3. The loop is thermally stable only while R_jc·P₀·α < 1. That condition holds easily here (≈ 0.01), so there is no local runaway. Runaway risk exists only at the board level, through T_c (Section 4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each switching event involves one hard-switched high side and one low side commutating through its body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2·50 nC·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | V·Q_oss·f_sw (Q-based; avoids C_oss nonlinearity) | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | 2·V_SD·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.06 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors) |
| **Total switching, per channel** | | **≈ 1.75 W** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time loss is about 35% of switching loss. Cutting t_dt to 50 ns, if the driver propagation-delay spread allows, saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- Faster edges (t_r+t_f → 10 ns) halve overlap loss, but raise dV/dt to about 2.5 V/ns. That worsens EMI coupling into the H743 IMU and baro, which share the same board.
- Rerunning the model at 24 kHz roughly halves switching loss. However, it increases current ripple and motor acoustic noise for low-inductance motors.

Conduction loss is about 75% of the total. **Paralleling MOSFETs or choosing lower-R_ds parts is the only meaningful lever. Switching-frequency tuning is secondary.**

## 4. Thermal Calculations

### 4.1 Steady-state check (why 80 A cannot be continuous)

Board convective area for a 30×30 mm board, both faces, is A ≈ 1.8×10⁻³ m². Under prop wash, h ≈ 75 W/m²K.

$$R_{\theta,ba}=\frac{1}{hA}\approx 7.4\ \text{K/W}\;\Rightarrow\;\Delta T_{ss}=86\times7.4\approx 640\ \text{K}$$

This is physically impossible, so the 80 A rating must be transient. Allowing ΔT = 70 K (25 → 95 °C board), the sustainable dissipation is about 9.5 W. Working backward through the loss model gives:

$$I_{cont}\approx\sqrt{\frac{9.5/4 - P_{sw}}{2R_{ds}+R_{sh}+R_{cu}}}\approx \mathbf{\sim 22\text{–}25\ A\ per\ channel\ continuous}$$

Switching loss is scaled to the lower current in that estimate. The result is consistent with real-world 25–35 A nano 4-in-1 boards.

### 4.2 Transient (lumped-capacitance) burst model

The board mass includes the PCB at about 4 g (FR4 + 2 oz Cu, c ≈ 1.0 J/gK) and 12 MOSFETs plus components at about 2 g (c ≈ 0.7 J/gK). This gives:

$$C_{th}\approx 4(1.0)+2(0.7)\approx 5.4\ \text{J/K}$$

The thermal time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 7.4 × 5.4 ≈ 40 s.

$$T_b(t)=T_0+P\,R_{\theta}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)\;\approx\;T_0+\frac{P}{C_{th}}\,t\quad(t\ll\tau)$$

At P = 86 W, the board heats at **≈ 16 K/s**.

### 4.3 Junction temperature during the burst

A conducting FET dissipates I²R_ds ≈ 7.9 W during its 120° conduction window, plus about 0.9 W switching on the high side. The transient impedance Z_θjc at a ~3 ms window (1 commutation period at ~20 k eRPM) is about 0.4 K/W, giving ΔT_jc ≈ 3.5 K. The case-to-board path through vias and pad is about 3 K/W at roughly 3 W average, giving about 9 K.

$$T_j \approx T_b + 13\ \text{K}$$

### 4.4 Burst-duration envelope (T_j ≤ 150 °C → T_b ≤ 137 °C)

| Start board temp T₀ | Max 80 A burst |
|---|---|
| 40 °C (cold start) | (137−40)/16 ≈ **6 s** |
| 70 °C (after hover) | ≈ **4 s** |
| 95 °C (after sustained hard flight) | ≈ **2.6 s** |
