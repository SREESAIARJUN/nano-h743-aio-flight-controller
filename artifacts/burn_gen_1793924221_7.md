## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

For phase current I with peak-to-peak PWM ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple term is ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw). For a typical quad motor (L_ph ≈ 10–20 µH) at 48 kHz, ripple adds <2 % to I²_rms. It is neglected below.

### 1.3 6-step complementary bridge, per motor

In any commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd and one leg's low-side FET is held on:

- The PWM leg's high side conducts for D·T.
- The PWM leg's synchronous low side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- The return leg's low side conducts continuously.

Summing these:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D. Each FET carries this stress for 1/3 of the electrical period, but bursts are long relative to the electrical period, so the motor-level average applies.

### 1.4 Copper and trace loss

Assume the effective current path from bus to bus is ~15 mm of 4-layer-paralleled 2 oz copper, 3 mm wide:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.003\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4}\approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega \;(\times1.4\ \text{hot}) \approx 0.43\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A, 125 °C

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Copper | 80² × 0.43 mΩ | **2.8 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2\,Q_g V_{GS} f}_{\text{gate (driver)}}$$

Assumed values: t_r + t_f = 20 ns, t_dt = 100 ns, V_F = 0.8 V, V_GS = 10 V **[A]**. Only the PWM leg switches.

| Term | Calculation | W |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20e-9 · 48e3 | 0.97 |
| Q_rr | 50e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 | 0.06 |
| Dead time | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100e-9 · 48e3 | 0.61 |
| C_oss | 0.5 · 1e-9 · 25.2² · 48e3 | 0.015 |
| Gate (2 FETs) | 2 · 40e-9 · 10 · 48e3 | 0.04 |
| **P_sw per motor** | | **≈ 1.7 W** |

**Design levers:**

- **Dead time:** at 48 kHz, dead-time loss is about 2/3 of overlap loss. Tune AM32 dead time to the gate driver's minimum safe value (typically 50–80 ns for a 3.3×3.3 FET).
- **Frequency:** switching loss scales linearly with f. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~0.85 W per motor at the cost of slightly worse low-throttle smoothness.

### Total per motor at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 |
| Copper | 2.8 |
| Switching | 1.7 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.7 W** |

**Board total with all 4 motors at 80 A: ≈ 95 W.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise above the board (fast, per FET)

The worst-case FET is the continuously-on low side:

- P_FET = I²R = 80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W
- With R_θJC + R_θ,solder/PCB-local ≈ 1.5 + 2.5 = 4 K/W, the rise is ΔT_J-B ≈ 38 K.
- This settles in about 50–200 ms (from the Z_th(t) curve), so it is effectively instantaneous relative to the burst.
