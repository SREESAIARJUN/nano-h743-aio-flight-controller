# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Flags

You didn't specify the MOSFET, board size, battery cell count, or cooling, so this model is **parametric**. Every result is computed for the baseline below. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations to get your own numbers.

| Parameter | Symbol | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case) |
| Burst motor current, per channel | I_m | 80 A |
| ESC PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature multiplier at 125 °C | k_T | 1.4 |
| Rise / fall time | t_r, t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V |
| Board | — | 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner (nominal; see flag 2) |

**Architectural flags (read before reviewing numbers):**

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.**
   - In an AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
   - Those are typically AT32F421 running AM32, or STM32G071/G031 running BLHeli_32/AM32.
   - The 48 kHz commutation PWM and switching losses live in the ESC MCU domain. They are two separate subsystems sharing a PCB.
2. **80A on a "nano" board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.**
   - Section 3 shows roughly 95 W total dissipation at 4×80 A.
   - No 20×20 or 30×30 board can reject that continuously.
   - The model below gives burst duration limits.
3. **The STM32H743 has no QFN package.**
   - Options are LQFP100/144/176 and (T/U)FBGA.
   - On a nano form factor, plan for LQFP100 (14×14 mm) or a BGA (which requires an HDI stackup).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, as used by AM32 and BLHeli_32.

At any instant, motor current I_m flows through exactly **two conducting switches**:
- one in the commutated leg, and
- one in the PWM leg (either the high side during ON, or the low side during OFF via synchronous rectification).

So the instantaneous conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow k_T \approx 1.4 \text{ at } 125\,^\circ\text{C})$$

### 1.2 Distribution per FET

Over one electrical period, each phase leg is active 2/3 of the time. Within that time, current splits between the high side and low side by duty D (for the PWM leg) or conducts fully (for the commutated leg).

Averaged over a period, the loss is spread across the 6 FETs approximately evenly:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2\,I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{6} = \frac{I_m^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

This average hides a peak. Instantaneous per-FET loss is I_m²·R_ds, which matters for transient Z_th (Section 3.3).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET (average) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 2.6 W |
| Hot (T_j = 125 °C) | 1.68 mΩ | **21.5 W** | 3.6 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper Losses (Often Larger Than Designers Expect)

Approximate copper resistance: R_cu = ρL/(wt), with ρ_Cu = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m (+0.393 %/K).

**Phase trace, FET to motor pad.** Assume 10 mm long, 4 mm wide, 2 oz (70 µm), on two parallel layers:

$$R = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.010}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}}/2 \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P = 6400 \cdot 0.31\text{m} \approx 2.0\ \text{W per phase path}$$

Two phase paths carry current at any time, so up to about **4 W per channel**, plus the bus (V+ and GND) planes.

**Battery pads and common bus.** These carry 4×80 = 320 A total. You need:
- solid V+ and GND pours on multiple layers, and
- a target bus resistance of ≤ 0.1 mΩ, which gives about 10 W at 320 A.

Plan pad-to-plane stitching with ≥ 30 vias (0.3 mm) per battery pad. At roughly 1 A sustained per via (thermal limit), that is adequate only for burst operation.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each switching event involves one high-side/low-side pair.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Transition (V–I Overlap)

$$P_{tr} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

A more rigorous version replaces t_r and t_f with the Miller plateau times:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery (Low-Side Body Diode During High-Side Turn-On)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_m.
