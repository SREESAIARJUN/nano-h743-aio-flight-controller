# STM32H743 Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Review Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC at 48 kHz needs four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 is the flight controller. It emits bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching model applies to the ESC MCUs.
2. **"80 A" must be defined.** This document uses **80 A RMS phase current per channel**, with a burst duration that falls out of the thermal model. It does not assume a fixed 10 s.
3. **The nano form factor is the binding constraint.** A 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate ~90 W steady-state. The thermal analysis shows the 80 A burst is **heat-capacity limited to roughly 3–8 s**, depending on mass and airflow. Marketing claims should be derived from Section 3, not chosen first.
4. **All component values are representative placeholders.** Substitute your selected MOSFET's datasheet values. The equations stay valid.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Operating assumptions

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_in | Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current, RMS (burst) | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | MOSFET R_ds(on) at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | 0.0045 /K |
| R_cu | PCB copper and solder path per channel | 0.30 mΩ |
| R_sh | DC-bus shunt per channel | 0.25 mΩ |

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.45) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM

During each 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** the high side conducts for D·T. The synchronous low side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Static leg:** the low side is on continuously.

Summing the conduction loss over the sector:

$$P_{cond,ch} = \underbrace{D I^2 R + (1-D) I^2 R}_{\text{switching leg}} + \underbrace{I^2 R}_{\text{static leg}} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, ignoring dead time.

**Per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.45\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

**Copper path:**

$$P_{cu} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(0.3\text{ m}\Omega) = 3.84\ W$$

The two-phase path is counted. Treat R_cu as the total loop resistance per phase leg.

**Shunt.** Bus current is I_bus,rms = I_ph√D. At D = 0.8:

$$P_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.8 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.28\ W$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (worst device)

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical (1/3 of the electrical period). A high-side FET conducts only for duty D within that window. A low-side FET conducts (1−D) as the sync rectifier plus 100% when it is the static leg.

At D ≈ 0.5, the worst-case low-side FET average is:

$$P_{FET,LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{6}I^2R + \tfrac{1}{6}\cdot 0.5\,I^2R \approx 0.25\,I^2R = 2.32\ W$$

The instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R = **9.3 W**. At electrical frequencies of 500–2000 Hz, the die Z_th smooths most of this ripple (Section 3.4).
