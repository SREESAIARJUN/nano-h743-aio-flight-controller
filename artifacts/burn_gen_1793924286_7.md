## 0. Architecture Decision (Read First)

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. Standard and correct partitioning:

| Function | Device | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, ArduPilot, DShot600 master | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | Sends bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| Per-motor commutation, 48 kHz PWM | 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 | Hard real-time FOC/6-step; isolates ESC faults from the FC |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, 6-ch) | Bootstrap high side, integrated dead time |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40V, PDFN3.3×3.3 | 6 per motor |

**Bus voltage assumption:** 6S, so V_bus,max = 25.2 V (design for 30 V transients; 40 V FETs give about 33% margin).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 MOSFET parameters (assumed)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | R(T) = R₂₅·[1 + α(T_j − 25)], α ≈ 0.005 /K |
| R_DS(on) at T_j = 125 °C | ≈ 1.8 mΩ (factor 1.5) |

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation

At any instant, two phases conduct. With synchronous rectification:

- During the PWM **on** interval (duty D), current flows through HS(x) → motor → LS(y).
- During the **off** interval (1 − D), current freewheels through LS(x) → motor → LS(y).

In both intervals exactly **two FETs** carry the phase current I. Total conduction loss per motor channel is therefore independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The per-FET split, over one electrical period, where each FET is active for 1/3 of the period:

$$
I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{D/3}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{(2-D)/3}
$$

The low-side FETs run hotter at low throttle. At burst (D → 1), the losses equalize.

### 1.3 Sinusoidal FOC alternative

For phase current amplitude Î:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 3\cdot\left(\frac{\hat I}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 1.5\,\hat I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

Add copper loss. For 2 oz copper with an effective path of about 0.25 mΩ per phase loop:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.6\ W}
$$

### 1.5 Electro-thermal feedback

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}
$$

With α = 0.005 and the parameters above, the loop converges within 3 iterations. It is stable as long as

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta < 1
$$

**Check:** 2·I²·R₂₅·α·R_θ = 2·6400·1.2e-3·0.005·(6.5/6 per FET share) ≈ 0.08 < 1. The loop is stable, with no thermal runaway at the device level.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per motor channel, one HS FET hard-switches per PWM cycle. The complementary LS FET switches near ZVS after dead time.

| Component | Formula | Assumed values | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, 20 + 20 ns | **1.94 W** |
| Body-diode dead time | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 V, 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss energy | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | Q_oss = 60 nC | **0.04 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 FETs | 60 nC, 10 V | **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Design levers:**

- **Dead time:** Each 50 ns reduction saves about 0.3 W per channel. AM32 dead time is configurable. Do not go below FD6288's ~100 ns internal minimum without scope verification of shoot-through.
- **Gate resistance:** The 20 ns edges assume R_g ≈ 2–4.7 Ω. Faster edges reduce loss but increase ringing and EMI on a nano layout.
- **f_sw tradeoff:** 24 kHz halves P_sw but raises current ripple and audible noise. 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for small, low-inductance motors.

## 3. Total Burst Loss Budget

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (125 °C) | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Copper | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| **Total** | **27.3 W** | **≈ 109 W** |

Board efficiency at full burst, with P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × ~0.95 ≈ 7.7 kW electrical: about 98.6%. The efficiency is good, but **109 W in a 30×30 mm board** is the real constraint.
