**Flag up front:** 80 A per motor (320 A aggregate) on a nano form factor is extreme. Most 20×20 4-in-1 ESCs are rated 25–45 A continuous. The thermal model in §3 shows this is a **time-limited rating of roughly 1–2 s adiabatic**. The spec must state the burst duration and duty cycle, or the rating is not meaningful.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
In six-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous/complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant. During the on-time, current flows through HS(phase a) → winding → LS(phase b). During the off-time, the PWM'd leg's complementary FET freewheels. In both states, current passes through **two R_ds(on) in series**:

$$P_{cond,M} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C with the max-spec part:

$$R_{ds} = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1+0.0055\times 85) = 1.91\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction
Phase current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

The RMS of a DC current with triangular ripple is:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I_M^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12}$$

For typical 5-inch motors (L ≈ 15–40 µH line-line), with D ≈ 0.9 at burst and E_bemf close to V_bus, the ripple term adds less than 1%. It is neglected below but kept in the model.

### 1.4 Electrical-cycle sharing
The two conducting FETs rotate among the six devices every 60° electrical. Per-device average:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,M}}{6}\cdot 2 \;\;(\text{each FET conducts }1/3\text{ of the cycle})$$

At low eRPM (sector dwell much longer than the FET thermal time constant τ ≈ 10–50 ms), use the **instantaneous** per-FET value P_cond,M / 2 for junction peak calculations.

### 1.5 Copper loss (do not ignore at 80 A)
Phase and bus copper per motor:

$$R_{cu} = \frac{\rho_{cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t\,n_{layers}}, \qquad \rho_{cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

Example: 8 mm path, 4 mm width, 4 paralleled layers (2×70 µm + 2×35 µm = 210 µm effective), at 100 °C:

$$R_{cu}= \frac{2.26\times10^{-8}\times 0.008}{0.004\times 210\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.215\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Positive bus plus GND return roughly doubles this to about 0.43 mΩ:

$$P_{cu}=80^2\times 0.43\text{ m}\Omega \approx 2.8\,\text{W per motor}$$

### 1.6 Worked result (per motor, T_j = 110 °C)

$$P_{cond,M} = 80^2\times 2\times 1.91\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.4\,W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches; the other conducting leg is static within the sector. Per motor:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (plus the Q_rr overlap term in hard-switched turn-on) | ½·40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.024 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs/leg; dissipated in driver + R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k = **1.23 W** |
| **P_sw,M** | | **≈ 2.31 W** |

Key observations:

- **Dead time dominates switching loss**, not the edges. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but you must verify shoot-through margin against driver propagation skew at temperature.
- Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz doubles all of these terms (+1.15 W per motor here). This is small relative to conduction loss, so 48 kHz is thermally justified by the reduction in acoustic and current ripple.
- **Edge-time sensitivity:** if a weak gate driver (e.g., FD6288-class, ~1 A) gives t_r + t_f ≈ 60 ns, overlap loss rises to 2.9 W per motor.
