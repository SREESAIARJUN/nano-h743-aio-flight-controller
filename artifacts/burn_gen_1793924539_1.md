## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every 4-in-1 architecture that works in practice, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32-class, or Bluejay firmware. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51. The H743 is the flight controller. It talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The design below follows that partitioning.

2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as per motor, for ≤2 s.** That means **320 A aggregate** at 6S, roughly 7 kW. On a true 20×20 mm nano footprint this is not physically achievable. The board cannot hold 24 power MOSFETs with adequate R_DS(on), and the copper cannot carry the current. The math in §3 shows this quantitatively. I specify a **30.5×30.5 mm power board** (stacked under a 20×20 FC) as the minimum credible form factor. Even then, 80 A is a burst-only rating.

3. **Drive scheme assumptions:**
   - 6-step trapezoidal commutation.
   - High-side PWM with synchronous (complementary) low-side rectification.
   - f_sw = 48 kHz.
   - V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full charge, worst case for switching loss).

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses at 80 A

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, two switches carry the phase current at every instant:

- **PWM on-state:** HS(A) + LS(B) conduct.
- **PWM off-state (after dead time):** LS(A) + LS(B) conduct.

So the instantaneous conduction loss per motor is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Averaged over a full electrical revolution, the 6 FETs share this loss approximately equally:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = 3.4\ \text{W}$$

During a FET's active 120° conduction window, its instantaneous loss peaks at about I²·R_ds = 10.2 W. This peak is what drives the junction transient in §3.3.

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches each cycle. The LS(B) switch is static within a commutation sector.

**Hard-switching (V-I overlap), HS FET:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction, LS FET (two edges per cycle):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx V_{bus} Q_{oss} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):**

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** ≈ **2.7 W per motor**.

> **Sensitivity:** P_sw is linear in (t_r + t_f) and f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor, but increases current ripple and audible noise. Faster edges (10 ns) halve P_sw but worsen ringing and EMI on a nano layout. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance 2–3" motors.
