# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture & Derivations

**Job #15: Engineering Design Package, Rev A (Preliminary)**

> **Scope and assumptions.** The STM32H743 does not commutate motors directly in any production ArduPilot design. This package assumes an **AIO stack**:
> - **H743 FC:** runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600.
> - **4× ESC MCUs:** AT32F421 running AM32. Each drives an FD6288Q-class 3-phase gate driver and 6× N-MOSFETs.
>
> All component values are representative. Every number marked **[V]** must be verified against the final datasheets and bench data.

---

## 1. Top-Level Architecture

```
 VBAT (6S, 25.2V max) ─┬─ Bulk caps ─┬─ ESC1..ESC4 power stages (6 FETs each, 24 total)
                       │             └─ Current shunt(s) → amp → ADC (FC + ESC)
                       ├─ Buck 5V/3A ── Buck 3.3V ── H743, IMU, baro, OSD
                       └─ Buck 10V ── gate drivers (if not drawn from VBAT LDO)

 H743 ──TIMx_CHy (bidir DShot600, 1-wire)──► ESCn MCU (AT32F421, AM32)
                                               └─► FD6288Q ─► 3 half-bridges ─► Motor n
```

**Form-factor warning.** A true "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot sustain 80 A per channel; Section 3.5 quantifies why. The numbers below assume a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner** board. 80 A is treated as a **per-channel, time-limited burst**.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly **two conducting devices**:
- the PWM leg (high-side during t_on, low-side during t_off), and
- the static low-side of the return leg.

At a flat-top current I:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Phase ripple at 48 kHz adds a correction:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With a typical 5-inch motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, ΔI ≈ 8 A), this adds about 0.1%, so it is neglected.

**Results by junction temperature:**

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W |
| 100 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 16.8 W |
| 125 °C | 1.44 mΩ | 18.4 W |

**Copper loss** must be added. Assume 25 mm of effective path at 4 oz-equivalent (stacked layers), 6 mm wide:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.025}{0.006\cdot 140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.51\ \text{m}\Omega \;\;(\times1.3\ \text{at}\ 100°C \approx 0.67\ \text{m}\Omega)$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.67\text{m} \approx 4.3\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

**Gate current during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{3} = 2.33\ \text{A}$$

The FD6288Q actually sources about 1.5 A, so **I_g ≈ 1.5 A** is used.

**Transition times:**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{14\text{n}}{1.5} \approx 9.3\ \text{ns}$$

Layout parasitics (common-source L ≈ 1 nH) are budgeted by setting **t_r = t_f = 15 ns**.

**Overlap (V-I crossing) loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs per PWM leg, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw} = 2\cdot70\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.067\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Per-Channel Total (T_j ≈ 100 °C operating point)

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 16.8 |
| PCB copper | 4.3 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| **Total / channel** | **≈ 23.3 W** |
| **All 4 channels at 80 A** | **≈ 93 W** |

**Interpretation:** at 48 kHz, switching is only about 9% of the total. **Conduction loss dominates**, so FET R_ds(on) and copper are the levers to pull; a lower PWM frequency buys little.

**Efficiency check:** at 80 A, motor power is roughly 25.2 × 80 × D. With 4 × 2 kW in, 93 W of loss gives about 98.8% stage efficiency. The problem is not efficiency; it is **heat density**.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State Network (per hot FET)

```
Tj ─[Rth_jc 1.0 K/W]─ Tc ─[Rth_c-pcb 0.5 K/W solder+pad]─ Tpcb_local
   ─[Rth_spread ≈ 8 K/W into 30.5² board, shared]─ Tboard ─[Rth_conv]─ Tamb
```

### 3.2 Per-FET Power Split

The static low-side carries I² · R_ds continuously during its 60° sector. The PWM leg splits conduction between its two FETs. Averaged over an electrical revolution, the 6 FETs share the load roughly equally:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{16.8 + 2.2}{6}\cdot 2 \approx 6.3\ \text{W peak-sector}$$

Sector-averaged, this is about **3.2 W per FET**.

**Junction-to-board rise:**

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}pcb} = 6.3 \cdot (1.0+0.5) \approx 9.5\ \text{K}$$
