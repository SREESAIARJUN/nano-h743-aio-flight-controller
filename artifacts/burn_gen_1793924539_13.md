# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #2): Thermal, Switching & BDShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

The H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The design is therefore partitioned into two boards:

| Subsystem | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 bidirectional output |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 running AM32 | 6-step sensorless commutation at 48 kHz |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3 class | Power stage |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M2), FC/ESC stacked | Nano class |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | |

**Important caveat:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 board is a burst figure only, valid for seconds or less. The model in §3 shows why. These parameter values are representative datasheet values and must be replaced with the actual part's figures before release.

**MOSFET model parameters** (representative 40 V / PDFN3×3 part):

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 2.0 mΩ max @ V_GS = 10 V | On-resistance at 25 °C |
| α | 0.0055 /K | Temperature coefficient: R(T) = R₂₅[1 + α(T − 25)] |
| Q_g | 40 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_oss | 40 nC | Output charge |
| Q_rr | 30 nC | Reverse recovery charge |
| t_r, t_f | 15 ns each | Rise/fall time with FD6288 + 4.7 Ω gate resistor |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Junction-to-case thermal resistance |
| t_dead | 100 ns | Dead time per transition |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with complementary PWM

At any instant, phase current I flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- **High-side PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts during D·T. Its synchronous low-side complement conducts during (1 − D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Low-side leg:** conducts continuously for the 60° sector.

So, independent of duty cycle, two R_DS(on) are always in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.83 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 3.10 mΩ |

### 1.3 Burst conduction loss at 80 A

Using T_j = 110 °C, so R = 2.94 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(2.94\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{37.6\ W\ per\ motor}$$

All four motors bursting simultaneously gives **150 W** of conduction loss alone.

### 1.4 Electrothermal feedback (iterative)

The junction temperature depends on the loss, which depends on the junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\,Z_{th}(t),\qquad P_{FET}=\tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R(T_j)\cdot k$$

Here k ≈ 2 accounts for each FET carrying current roughly one-third of the electrical cycle, either as the low side or as the high/sync pair.

Solving this as a fixed point gives a closed form:

$$T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+\beta R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\beta R_{25}\alpha},\qquad \beta = \tfrac{2}{3}I^2 Z_{th}$$

The design rule follows from the denominator: **thermal runaway occurs if βR₂₅α ≥ 1.** At 80 A with Z_th = 10 K/W (steady state):

$$\beta R_{25}\alpha = \tfrac{2}{3}(6400)(10)(0.002)(0.0055) = 0.47$$

This is stable, but it amplifies the temperature rise by 1/(1 − 0.47) ≈ 1.9×. That amplification is why the 80 A rating must be time-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Assumptions: V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case, full 6S) and I = 80 A. One leg per motor hard-switches at any time.

| Loss term | Formula | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(30 ns)(48 k) | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8(80)(200 ns)(48 k) | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC(25.2)(48 k) | 0.04 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2(40 nC)(10 V)(48 k) | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching loss** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Insight:** at 80 A, conduction loss dominates by about 17:1. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.1 W per motor but reduces acoustic noise and current ripple. Lowering R_DS(on), for example by paralleling FETs, is the lever that matters, not lowering f_sw.

**Ripple check.** Assumptions: L_phase = 15 µH (typical 2207 motor, line-to-line ≈ 30 µH) and D = 0.5.

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2(0.25)}{30\mu\cdot48k}=4.4\ \text{A}$$

The ripple is small relative to 80 A, so the ripple contribution to I²_rms (ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.6 A²) is negligible.

## 3. Total Loss & Thermal Budget

### 3.1 Loss summary (80 A burst)

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 37.6 W | 150.4 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, single shunt) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| PCB copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ path) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **43.3 W** | **≈ 173 W** |

Efficiency at burst is about 173 W lost against roughly 4 × 80 A × 22 V × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 6.3 kW in, so **η ≈ 97%**. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power *density* on 4 cm².
