## 0. Architecture Clarification and Stated Assumptions

**Where the 48 kHz switching happens.** The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. In a 4-in-1 ESC stack, the H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands. A separate ESC MCU per channel generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32E230 running AM32 or Bluejay. The model below is partitioned accordingly:

| Domain | Silicon | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 TX, eRPM RX via input capture DMA |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32 | 6-step or sinusoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead time |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver) | 12 V gate drive, bootstrap |
| Power stage (×4) | 6 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3 | Inverter |

**Assumed parameters.** No specific MOSFET was named, so these are representative 40 V PDFN3.3 values. Substitute your datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge (worst case for switching) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Burst phase current, per channel |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.8 mΩ | At V_GS = 10 V, T_j = 25 °C |
| α | 0.006 K⁻¹ | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient |

> **Feasibility flag:** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm 4-in-1 is only a short-duration burst rating. Section 3 quantifies how short. In summary, it is roughly 1 s from a warm start, not the 10 s often marketed.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path topology
With complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step commutation, exactly two MOSFETs carry the phase current at every instant:
- the active switch in the PWM'd leg, which is HS during D and LS during 1−D;
- the static switch in the other conducting leg.

Total conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)
$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds(on) (mΩ) | P_cond,FET per channel at 80 A (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.80 | 23.0 |
| 75 | 2.34 | 30.0 |
| 100 | 2.61 | 33.4 |
| 125 | 2.88 | 36.9 |

Copper loss: 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **1.92 W per channel**.

### 1.3 Phase current versus battery current
$$I_{ph} \approx \frac{I_{dc}}{D}$$

At high throttle (D ≈ 0.95), I_ph ≈ I_dc. At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current. ESC current limiting must therefore act on the phase or shunt measurement, not on the battery current reported to the FC.

### 1.4 Per-device distribution
Averaged over an electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries one sixth of the conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} \approx 5.6\ \text{W at } T_j = 100\,°C$$

Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is I² × R_ds = 16.7 W. At low eRPM, where commutation dwell is milliseconds, the device sees this peak quasi-statically. This drives the Z_th(t) analysis in Section 3.3.
