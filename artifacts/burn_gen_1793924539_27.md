## 0. Architectural Correction You Should Know About First

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation firmware. A buildable "H743 + 4-in-1 ESC" system has two parts:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 bidirectional master |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 6 outputs, bootstrap) | n/a | Drives the 6 FETs of each motor |

The "4-in-1" can be a stacked board or a single all-in-one PCB. The loss model is identical either way.

**Feasibility flag: "80 A burst" on a nano form factor.** At 80 A *phase* current on all four motors at high duty, battery current approaches about 4 × 80 × 0.9 ≈ 290 A. That is physically unrealistic for 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm pads, an XT60, or a nano battery. This document therefore defines:

- **80 A burst** = per-motor phase current, ≤ 2 s, one or more motors.
- Continuous rating is derived separately (§2.6).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step Synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases conduct and one floats. With complementary PWM on the commutating leg:

- **Active leg:** the high side (HS) conducts for fraction D, and the synchronous low side (LS) conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS conducts 100 % of the 60° sector.

So the motor current *always* flows through exactly **two** channels in series:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This holds independent of D, which is why burst current, not throttle, sets conduction loss.

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond,bridge @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.70 mΩ | 21.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | 23.8 W |

### 2.3 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current about ⅓ of the time, as HS-on, sync-LS or return-LS:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6} \approx 3.6\text{–}4.0\ \text{W @ 100–125 °C}$$

At low eRPM the electrical period approaches thermal time constants, and individual FETs see the full 2·I²R share for one sector. The worst-case instantaneous FET loss is I²R = 80² × 1.86 mΩ = **11.9 W**. This sets the stall/startup limit (§2.7).

### 2.4 Copper / Interconnect Losses (Non-Negligible)

Per motor, the 80 A path runs through: phase pad → phase pour → FET → bus pour → shunt.

| Element | Assumption | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase copper (2 legs) | 2 oz outer + 2× 2 oz inner, 8 mm long, 4 mm wide, paralleled | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Shunt (if per-phase or per-ESC low-side) | 0.25 mΩ | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W* |
| Solder/pad interfaces | 4 × 50 µΩ | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

\*If you use a single DC-bus shunt, it carries battery current I_bus = D·I_ph per ESC, not phase current.

**Copper total ≈ 3–4.5 W per motor at burst.**

### 2.5 Total Burst Conduction (All Four Motors)

$$P_{cond,total} = 4\,(23.8 + 3.5) \approx \mathbf{109\ W}\ \ (T_j = 125\ °C)$$

### 2.6 Continuous Current Rating

With an estimated board-to-air R_th(sa) ≈ 2.5 K/W in prop-wash for a 30×30 board, total, and a target ΔT of 70 K (40 → 110 °C board):

$$P_{allow} = 70/2.5 = 28\ \text{W total} \Rightarrow 7\ \text{W per motor}$$

$$I_{cont} = \sqrt{\frac{7 - P_{sw}}{2R_{hot} + R_{cu}}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{7-1.7}{3.7\text{e-3}+0.45\text{e-3}}} \approx \mathbf{36\ A\ continuous}$$

A realistic label would be **"35 A continuous / 80 A ≤ 2 s burst."**

### 2.7 Stall / Startup Limit

The worst single-FET loss is 11.9 W. With Z_th(j-c) ≈ 0.6 K/W at 10 ms, junction rise above case is about 7 K per 10 ms pulse. This is acceptable only because AM32 current limiting and stall protection cap it. Firmware **must** enable current limit at ≤ 80 A, using shunt feedback or the per-ESC current estimate.
