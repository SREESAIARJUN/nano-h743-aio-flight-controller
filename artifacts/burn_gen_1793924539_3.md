# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

An STM32H743 does not commutate motors directly in any practical 4-in-1 design. This document assumes a typical all-in-one (AIO) partition:

| Block | Device (reference choice) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz, VFBGA-100) | ArduPilot, DShot600 output, eRPM capture |
| ESC MCU ×4 | STM32G071GBU6 or AT32F421 (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class firmware) | 6-step or FOC commutation, **48 kHz PWM** |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 250 V) | High-side/low-side drive |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, R_DS(on) ≈ 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | Bridge |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | |

All device parameters below are representative values. Before release, replace them with the exact datasheet figures for the parts you select. The model is written parametrically so you can substitute values directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst per Channel)

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- one FET in the PWM leg (HS during on-time, LS during off-time), and
- one FET in the static low-side leg.

Total bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of an electrical cycle, so the per-FET average is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | 25 °C | 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond, bridge | 2·6400·1.0 m = **12.8 W** | 2·6400·1.5 m = **19.2 W** |
| P_cond, per FET | 2.13 W | 3.2 W |

### 1.4 Copper losses (often forgotten)

Phase and bus trace resistance follows:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\,\frac{L}{w\,t}, \quad \rho_{Cu,25} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}, \quad \alpha_{Cu} = 0.00393$$

Example: 15 mm path, 4 mm wide, 3 layers of 2 oz (210 µm total) gives R ≈ 0.31 mΩ, i.e. **+2.0 W** per channel at 80 A.

Budget **0.3–0.5 mΩ** for copper and solder per channel. On a nano board this is comparable to one FET.

### 1.5 Electrothermal convergence

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on P, solve the loop iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}(t)\cdot P\big(T_j^{(n)}\big)$$

Stability (no thermal runaway) requires:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot Z_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A with Z_th = 40 K/W (FET to ambient on a tiny board): 2.13 × 0.005 × 40 = 0.43 < 1. This is stable, but with little margin, and it confirms that steady-state 80 A is not viable (see Section 3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg switches hard per commutation interval. Losses are calculated per channel.

### 2.1 Gate-drive timing

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{drv,on} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,off} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}$$

Assumed values: V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3.5 V, R_drv ≈ 3 Ω, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, R_g,int = 1 Ω.

- I_g,on = 6.5/8.7 = 0.75 A
- I_g,off = 3.5/8.7 = 0.40 A

Switching charge Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 4 + 8 = 12 nC, giving:

$$t_r = Q_{sw}/I_{g,on} = 16\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f = Q_{sw}/I_{g,off} = 30\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss components

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·46 ns·48k = **2.23 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (Q_oss ≈ 40 nC) | **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw (Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, 80 A) | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw (V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns) | **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw (Q_g = 60 nC) | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching (per channel)** | | **≈ 2.95 W** |

Design levers:

- **Dead time** is the biggest secondary term. Cutting 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. AM32 exposes this setting; verify shoot-through margin on a scope first.
- **Switching frequency:** 48 kHz versus 24 kHz roughly halves 2.95 W to 1.5 W. 48 kHz is chosen for lower current ripple and audible noise on low-inductance motors.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals (T_j = 125 °C, burst)

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 2.0 W | 8.0 W |
| Switching | 2.95 W | 11.8 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR (I_rms ≈ 0.5·I_ph, ESR 5 mΩ effective) | 8 W at board level if all channels at 80 A (I_cap ≈ 40 A) | ≈ 8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Bus capacitor ripple current is a real failure mode. Use low-ESR polymer capacitors plus MLCCs placed directly at each bridge, and specify an external 35 V, ≥1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pigtail.
