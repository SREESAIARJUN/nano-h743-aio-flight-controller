## 0. Architecture Clarification

In a 4-in-1 design, **the H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** The partitioning is:

| Function | Silicon | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 master, eRPM telemetry decode | STM32H743 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot ChibiOS |
| 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF sensing | 4× ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32) | One per motor |
| Gate drive | 3-phase half-bridge driver ×4 (e.g., FD6288Q class) | Bootstrap high side |
| Power stage | 24 N-MOSFETs (6 per motor) | 2 conducting at any instant |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, exactly two FETs conduct at any time:
- **PWM ON:** HS(A) + LS(B)
- **PWM OFF:** LS(A) [sync rectify] + LS(B)

So the per-motor conduction loss is independent of duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

For low-ripple motor current, I_rms ≈ I_ph. The per-device split for the PWM leg is:

$$P_{HS}=D\,I^2R,\quad P_{LS,A}=(1-D)\,I^2R,\quad P_{LS,B}=I^2R$$

The commutated low side (LS_B) is the **hottest device**. It conducts 100% of its 120° interval.

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 111.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ | **30.7 W** | **122.9 W** |

### 2.3 Electrothermal Coupling (Runaway Criterion)
For a single device: P = P₂₅[1+α(T_j−25)] and T_j = T_ref + R_θ·P. Solving gives:

$$T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+R_\theta P_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_{25}}$$

**Thermal runaway occurs when αR_θP₂₅ ≥ 1.**

For LS_B at 80 A: P₂₅ = 80²×1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W.
- Runaway threshold: R_θ ≥ 1/(0.006×9.6) = **17.4 °C/W**.
- A DFN3.3 FET on a nano board with no airflow has a steady-state R_θ of roughly 30–50 °C/W.

**Conclusion: 80 A is not sustainable in steady state.** It is strictly a transient (burst) rating governed by thermal capacitance (Section 4).

### 2.4 Copper Losses (Often Larger Than Expected)
Use R_Cu = ρL/(wt), with ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m and 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm).
- A 10 mm × 3 mm phase trace gives ≈ 0.82 mΩ.
- At 80 A that is ≈ 5.2 W per trace.

Use stitched inner planes, multiple layers in parallel, and short phase pads.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per motor:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw (≈½C_oss V²f) | ≈ 0.030 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·30 nC·12·48 k = 0.035 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.35 W/motor** |

**Total per motor at 80 A, 125 °C: ≈ 30.7 + 2.35 ≈ 33 W. Four motors: ≈ 132 W.**

**Sensitivities:**
- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves ≈ 1.2 W per motor.
- It is negligible compared with conduction loss at 80 A.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance motors (lower current ripple and acoustic noise), not for efficiency.
