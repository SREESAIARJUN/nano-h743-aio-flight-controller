# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Design Reference

**Scope:** This covers loss modeling, transient thermal analysis, DShot DMA timer allocation, and a reference netlist. I don't have prior Job #6 documentation, so every assumption is stated and parameterized. Replace the placeholder device values with datasheet values for your chosen BOM before release.

---

## 0. Architecture Issues to Resolve First

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run BLDC commutation or FOC. A "4-in-1 ESC" on this board therefore needs **4 dedicated ESC MCUs**, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to them via bidirectional DShot600. The rest of this document assumes that split.
2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm nano footprint is not physically sustainable.**
   - 4 × 80 A = 320 A from 6S is about 8 kW.
   - No nano-class battery connector, trace geometry, or board thermal mass supports that.
   - The analysis below shows that a 20×20 board survives roughly 1 s of full 4-motor burst at 80 A.
   - **Recommendation:** either define 80 A as a *single-motor* or *total* burst rating, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with 8-layer 3 oz copper.
   - The math is parametric, so it applies to any of these choices.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

**Current path.** With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- During on-time D: high-side of phase A → low-side of phase B.
- During off-time (1−D): low-side A synchronous rectifier → low-side B.

Path resistance is therefore always 2·R_ds, independent of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current about 1/3 of the electrical period. That gives about 23.0/6 ≈ 3.8 W average per FET, with an instantaneous peak of I²R = 11.5 W.

The electrical frequency at high RPM (e.g. 30 k RPM, 14 poles → 3.5 kHz) is much faster than the junction thermal time constant (ms range). Averaging is therefore valid for junction rise:

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 3.8 \times 2.5 = 9.6\ \text{K}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET turns on near zero voltage after the dead time. The individual terms are:

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.307 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.060 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = 0.5·2 nF·635·48 k | 0.030 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 6·50 nC·10·48 k | 0.144 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.51 W** |

**Totals:**

$$P_{ch} = 23.0 + 1.5 \approx \mathbf{24.5\ W}$$

$$P_{4\times} \approx \mathbf{98\ W}$$

Conduction dominates at 94%. Lowering f_sw buys almost nothing; **R_ds(on) and copper are the levers.**
