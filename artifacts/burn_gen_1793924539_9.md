# Job #37: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each motor phase bridge is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware: either 4× small MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071) or one MCU per pair. This is how every production "AIO/stack" works, and the model below assumes it.

**Physical reality check.** 80 A burst per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is at the edge of physics. The math below shows it is a **~1–2 s** capability with all four motors at full load, not a sustained rating. A 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 form factor is strongly recommended.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path (6-step)

At any instant, one high-side and one low-side device carry the phase current. During PWM off-time the synchronous low-side of the PWM'd leg takes over the high-side's role. The series path is therefore always ≈ 2 × R_ds, independent of duty, ignoring dead time.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{Cu} \;+\; I_{bus}^2 R_{shunt}$$

Silicon only, at 80 A:

$$P_{cond,Si} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.003 = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the per-FET average is ≈ 19.2/6 ≈ **3.2 W**. The instantaneous dissipation in an active FET is 9.6 W.

Electrical frequency is around 1–3 kHz, i.e., conduction intervals of about 0.1–0.3 ms. At those durations the FET's transient thermal impedance Z_th(j-c) is far below its steady-state R_th(j-c), so using the cycle-averaged loss is valid.

### 1.3 PCB copper

For a 2 oz (70 µm) layer, sheet resistance ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.

Example: a phase pour that is 10 squares long, paralleled across 4 layers, gives about 0.6 mΩ. At 80 A that adds:

$$80^2 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{3.8\ W}$$

Copper loss is non-trivial. Use 2–3 oz on all power layers and heavy via stitching (≥ 20 vias of 0.3 mm per phase transition).

### 1.4 Shunt

Assume a 0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt. At full throttle (D → 1), I_bus ≈ 4 × 80 = 320 A:

$$320^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.6\ W}$$

This is a design problem. Options:
- Use per-motor shunts, or
- Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt (10.2 W at 320 A) with a high-gain current-sense amplifier (INA186-class, gain 200).

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg per motor is hard-switched at any time.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (body diode conducts during dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) C_oss charge/discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** (2 transitions per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
