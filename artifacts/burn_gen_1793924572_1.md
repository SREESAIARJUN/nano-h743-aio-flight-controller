# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Power, Thermal, and DShot Design Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz commutation PWM. In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, on a part such as the STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU drives the gate driver and MOSFETs.

The H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. The design therefore splits into two domains:

| Domain | Owner | Key concerns |
|---|---|---|
| FC (H743) | ArduPilot | DShot timing, DMA streams, cache coherency, telemetry capture |
| ESC (×4) | AM32 firmware + power stage | 48 kHz switching, conduction, dead-time, thermal |

### 0.2 Form-factor reality check

80 A per motor (320 A aggregate burst) is **not physically achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" board**. Copper cross-section, MOSFET footprint, and thermal mass all fall short. The calculations below assume:

- A 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC, **or**
- That "80 A" means the single-motor burst rating, validated with the transient model in §3.

If the board truly must be 20×20, the transient model in §3 shows burst capability falls to roughly 1–2 s. Realistic continuous current would be about 35–45 A.
