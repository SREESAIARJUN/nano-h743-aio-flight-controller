## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production-grade AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class running AM32 or BLHeli_32) that generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Folding commutation into the H743 would conflict with ArduPilot's scheduler and is not supported upstream.

```
LiPo 6S ──┬── TVS + Bulk ──► 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET] ──► Motors
          │                         ▲ DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor)
          └── 5V/3V3 BEC ──► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

**Baseline parameters** (all calculations below use these; substitute your datasheet values):

| Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge (worst case for switching loss) |
| I_burst | 80 A per channel | phase current, trapezoidal |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM |
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class |
| k_T (125 °C) | 1.6 | typical R_DS(on) temperature multiplier |
| t_r + t_f | 20 ns | at 80 A with FD6288-class driver |
| t_dt | 200 ns | dead time per edge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | body-diode forward voltage at 80 A |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | |
| Q_g | 60 nC @ 10 V | |
| Board | 20×20 mm "nano," 8-layer, 2 oz outer layers | |

> **Engineering flag:** "80 A" on a 20×20 board is achievable only as a **short burst**. Section 2.4 quantifies the limit, and the final numbers should be stated that way on the datasheet.

## 1. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly **one high-side path and one low-side path**. During the PWM off-time, current freewheels through the synchronous MOSFET instead of the diode. The instantaneous conducting resistance is therefore always ≈ 2 R_DS(on):

$$
R_{DS(on),hot} = R_{25}\,k_T = 1.5 \times 1.6 = 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot} = 2(80)^2(2.4\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

Copper path loss must be added. A typical 2 oz trace and plane path of ~0.3 mΩ per phase gives:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 I^2 R_{trace} = 2(6400)(0.3\times10^{-3}) = 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

**Per-MOSFET average:** each device conducts for roughly 1/3 of the electrical period, so the 30.7 W is shared across six devices, about **5.1 W average each**. The conducting device sees ~15.4 W instantaneous.

### 1.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg only)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

> Dead-time loss **exceeds** switching loss here. Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns (if your driver and shoot-through margin allow) saves ~0.6 W per channel.

### 1.4 Reverse Recovery and Gate Drive

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

The gate-drive term covers the two actively switching devices and is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

### 1.5 Channel and Board Totals at 80 A

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (MOSFET) | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Qrr + gate | 0.12 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 36.8 W** | **≈ 147 W** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 this is ≈ 1814 W per channel, giving η ≈ 98%. Efficiency is not the problem; **heat density is**. 147 W into 4 cm² equals 37 W/cm².

**Key insight:** conduction dominates at 96% of the loss. Switching frequency is nearly irrelevant at 80 A. R_DS(on) and copper are the only levers that matter.
