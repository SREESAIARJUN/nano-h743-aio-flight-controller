## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors itself, given 24 gate signals, 4× BEMF/ADC sensing, and fault latency. This design therefore uses the standard partitioning:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) @ 400 MHz, ArduPilot | Sends bi-dir DShot600, receives eRPM telemetry |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class, AM32 firmware | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, current limit |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q class, 3-phase half-bridge driver | 6 outputs, bootstrap high side |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3.3 | 6 per motor |

**Modeling parameters.** These are assumptions. Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values; every equation below is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged (worst-case switching) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Burst phase current per motor |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ | Typical at V_GS = 10 V |
| α | 0.006 /K | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (≈1.45× at 100 °C) |
| Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd | 10 nC | Switching charge |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF | Energy-related output capacitance |
| I_g | ≈1 A | Effective driver source/sink current during plateau |
| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward drop at 80 A |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| L_ph-ph | 20 µH | Motor phase-to-phase inductance (typical 2207-class) |

> **Engineering flag:** On a 20×20 mm nano board, 80 A per channel is a *transient* rating only. The model in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A sustained is thermally impossible. The deliverable is therefore a burst-duration envelope, not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current paths per 60° commutation sector

In each sector, phase X is PWM'd high, phase Y is held low, and phase Z floats:

- **High-side X:** conducts I for fraction D of each PWM period.
- **Low-side X (sync rect):** conducts I for fraction (1 − D).
- **Low-side Y:** conducts I continuously.

The instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle** in sync-rect mode. Each of the 6 FETs carries this duty for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is:

$$
\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}
$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A (worst case, } D=0.5)
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contribution is 0.06%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of the high PWM frequency.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence and the self-consistent solution

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.74\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

$$
\overline{P}_{cond,FET} = 3.71\ \text{W}
$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** Losses scale with T_j, so solve iteratively. Thermal runaway occurs when the loop gain reaches 1:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} = P_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th} \geq 1
$$

With P_25 ≈ 2.56 W/FET and R_th,j-a,local = 8 K/W, the loop gain is 0.12. The design is stable with margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. Its sync-rect complement turns on after the body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}
