# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Limits

Three issues change the shape of this design.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO with an H743, the flight controller runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, Bluejay or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, does commutation and returns bi-directional DShot eRPM telemetry. The H743 has no spare timers or DMA bandwidth to run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops while also running ArduPilot. The model below therefore splits into:
- the ESC power stage, driven by the ESC MCU at 48 kHz, and
- the FC DShot subsystem, run by the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" needs a definition.** Two readings are possible:

| Interpretation | Per-channel current | Verdict on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board |
|---|---|---|
| A: 80 A total (4 × 20 A) | 20 A | Feasible with 2 oz / 6-layer copper and airflow |
| B: 80 A per channel (320 A total) | 80 A | **Not feasible.** About 126 W dissipation, and connector and copper limits are exceeded (shown below) |

I compute both so the numbers make the decision for you.

**3. Device parameters are placeholders.** Values marked ⚙ are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs typical on nano ESCs. Replace them with your actual datasheet values before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology insight (6-step, synchronous rectification)

In 6-step commutation, at any instant exactly two phases carry the DC-side current I_ch.
- During PWM ON, current flows through HS(phase a) and LS(phase b).
- During PWM OFF with synchronous rectification, current freewheels through LS(phase a) and LS(phase b).

In both states the current path contains **exactly two MOSFETs**. This holds regardless of duty cycle D, apart from the dead-time intervals handled in §3.3. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ch}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\ \alpha \approx 0.006\,\text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 × 1.51 ≈ **2.26 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. The worst-case device is the low-side FET of the PWM'd leg at low duty, which carries current about (1/3 + (1−D)/3) of the time. Bounding it:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$$

### 2.3 Copper loss

$$R_{cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(80^\circ C) = 2.1\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

For one path, 15 mm long and 5 mm wide, in 2 oz (70 µm) copper:
- One layer: R = 2.1e-8 × 0.015 / (0.005 × 70e-6) = **0.90 mΩ**
- Four paralleled layers: **0.23 mΩ**

The loop runs from battery pad to phase and back, about 2 × path length, so R_cu,loop ≈ 0.45 mΩ per channel. The shared bus to the battery pad carries the total current, adding about 0.3 mΩ shared:

$$P_{cu} = I_{ch}^2 R_{cu,loop} + I_{tot}^2 R_{bus}$$

### 2.4 Results

| Case | P_cond per channel | P_cu per channel | P_bus | Total (4 ch) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 20 A/ch | 2 × 400 × 2.26 m = **1.81 W** | 0.18 W | 1.92 W | **≈ 9.9 W** |

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM cycle: the HS FET of the PWM'd phase. Its LS partner commutates softly through the body diode.

### 3.1 Hard-switching overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ch} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Where t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g,src and t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g,snk. The figure of 20 ns includes Q_gs2 and gate-loop inductance.

### 3.2 Capacitive and reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}, \qquad P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$

### 3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per cycle:

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I_{ch} t_{dt} f_{sw}$$

### 3.4 Gate drive

This is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = N_{sw} Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}, \quad N_{sw} = 2$$
