# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, BDShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Architectural Flags

Three issues affect the whole design and should be settled first.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter, not an H743 parameter. The rest of this document assumes this split.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A burst conflict thermally.** On a 20×20 mm board, 4×80 A produces about 110 W of loss (§3). Burst duration is then limited to roughly 1–3 s by the board's thermal capacitance (§4). I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm board, or rating 80 A as a ≤1 s burst with 40–45 A continuous.
3. **Component values are representative, not vendor-verified.** I parametrized a generic 40 V, approximately 1 mΩ PQFN 3.3×3.3 MOSFET. Every symbol below must be replaced with datasheet values from your chosen part before sign-off.

## 2. Per-ESC Loss Model at 80 A, 48 kHz

### 2.1 Conduction

In 6-step mode, two phases conduct at any instant. With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two channels: one high-side position and one low-side position. This is either the PWM-active FET or its synchronous-rectifier complement.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot (1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.6) = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The temperature dependence is approximately:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\,[1 + 0.006\,(T_j - 25)]$$

This creates positive feedback. Iterate the calculation with §4 until it converges. If the result diverges, you have thermal runaway.

### 2.2 Switching (hard-switched leg only)

Only one leg PWM-switches during each 60° sector. The first-order overlap loss is:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
