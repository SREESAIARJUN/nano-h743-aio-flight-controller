# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Design Basis, Loss Model, DShot Allocation and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues need to be settled before the math.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every viable FC/ESC stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is a setting on those ESC MCUs. The H743 does not generate it.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano/20×20 board is thermally marginal at best.** The numbers below show that four simultaneous 80 A channels produce about 90 W. Section 3 quantifies how many seconds that is survivable. Treat 80 A as a per-channel, short-burst (≤1–2 s), single-motor-dominant rating unless the board is 30.5×30.5 or larger with heavy copper.
3. **No component datasheet values were supplied.** Every parameter below is a stated assumption, typical of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm ESC MOSFETs. The model is fully parametric: replace the values with your BOM's datasheet numbers and re-evaluate.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

**Assumed topology:** 6-step trapezoidal commutation, as in AM32/BLHeli_32, with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM on the active phase.

During any commutation sector, two half-bridges are active:
- **The PWM phase.** The high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T.
- **The return phase.** The low-side FET is held on for 100 % of the sector.

The conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle because of synchronous rectification.

Adding the PCB copper path gives:

$$
P_{cond,tot} = 2I^2R_{hot} + I^2(R_{trace,+} + R_{trace,-} + R_{pad})
$$

**Numbers:**
- 2 · 80² · 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per channel**
- Copper (assumed 0.3 mΩ round-trip, 2 oz): 80² · 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**

Note that the copper term also self-heats: copper resistance rises about 0.39 %/K.

**Distribution across the six FETs.** Each FET sees I²R_hot = 9.6 W instantaneous while conducting, and averages roughly P_cond/6 ≈ 3.2 W.

**Electrical-thermal coupling must be iterated.** Use

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005/\mathrm{K}
$$

and iterate until T_j converges. Evaluating at a fixed 125 °C is conservative only if the converged T_j stays below 125 °C.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase switches. Per channel, per switching cycle, the loss terms are as follows.

**Hard-switching V-I overlap (high-side FET):**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (low-side FET, two edges per cycle):**

$$
P_{dt} = 2 V_{SD} I t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (this energy is mostly dissipated in the high-side FET):

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 35\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.021\ W}
$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET die):

$$
P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** P_sw, P_dt and P_rr scale linearly with f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.6 W per channel at 80 A. Moving to 96 kHz would add about 3.2 W more.

Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Trimming t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but you must verify shoot-through margin with the actual gate driver propagation skew.

## 3. Totals and Thermal Calculation

### 3.1 Power budget at 80 A

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Conduction (copper) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.4 W** | **≈ 97.7 W** |

At about 2 kW of electrical output per channel (25.2 V × 80 A), the stage efficiency is roughly 98.8 %. That sounds high, but 98 W is far beyond what a 20×20 mm or 30×30 mm board can dissipate continuously.

### 3.2 Steady-state thermal network (per FET)

The thermal path is a series chain:

T_j → R_θjc (about 1.2 K/W) → solder/via array → R_θcb (about 3–6 K/W for a 3×3 mm package on a 2 oz board with filled vias) → board → R_θba → ambient.

The board-to-ambient resistance can be estimated as:

$$
R_{\theta ba} \approx \frac{1}{h A_{eff}}
$$

**Assumptions for this estimate:**
- h ≈ 50–150 W/m²K under prop wash.
- A_eff ≈ 2 × 30×30 mm = 1.8×10⁻³ m².

That gives **R_θba ≈ 3.7–11 K/W for the whole board**.

**Steady-state conclusion.** At about 98 W, the board would rise 360–1080 K. Continuous 80 A on four channels is therefore physically impossible. The realistic continuous rating at a 60 K board rise is:

$$
P_{max} \approx 60/R_{\theta ba} \approx 5.5\text{–}16\ \text{W total}
$$

This corresponds to about **25–40 A per channel continuous**, depending on airflow. Derive the exact figure from your measured R_θba.

### 3.3 Burst (transient) capability

**Board heat capacity.** A 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with FETs and components has a mass of about 6 g. Its heat capacity is:

$$
C_{th} \approx m_{Cu} c_{Cu} + m_{FR4} c_{FR4} \approx 2(0.385) + 4(1.1) \approx 5.2\ \text{J/K}
$$

**Board rise during the burst.** The thermal time constant τ = R_θba · C_th ≈ 19–57 s, which is much longer than the burst. The board therefore behaves almost adiabatically:

$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{\theta ba}(1 - e^{-t/\tau}) \approx \frac{P t}{C_{th}} = 18.8\ \text{K/s}\ \ (\text{4 × 80 A})
$$
