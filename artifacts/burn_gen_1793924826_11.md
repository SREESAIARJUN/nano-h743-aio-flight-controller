## 0. Architecture Assumptions (read first)

Several things in the brief need clarifying, because they change the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/Bluejay-class firmware). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **bi-directional DShot600** to those MCUs. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency, set in ESC firmware, not by the H743.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per channel**, for ≤ a few seconds, at 6S (25.2 V max).
3. **"Nano" form factor is taken as 20×20 mm mounting** (~25×25 mm board). As shown in §3, **80 A per channel in this form factor is thermally marginal to infeasible beyond ~1–2 s bursts.** I would push back on that requirement or move to 30.5×30.5.
4. **Example MOSFET:** 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V, Q_g = 30 nC, C_oss = 1 nF, Q_rr = 50 nC, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W.
5. **Example gate driver:** FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver with bootstrap. Dead time is 100 ns.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This corresponds to roughly 1.5× at 125 °C, which is typical for trench FETs.

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × (1 + 0.375) = **2.06 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):**
  - The high-side conducts for D·T.
  - The low-side synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side conducts continuously.

Per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With complementary PWM, the result is **independent of duty cycle**, which makes it a useful design simplification.

### 1.3 Copper and trace loss

Assume a phase plus DC-bus path resistance of about 0.4 mΩ per channel (2 oz inner layers, short path):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0004 = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| Hottest FET (return-leg low-side, 100 % conduction) | 6400 × 2.06 mΩ = **13.2 W** |
| P_cond per channel | 2 × 13.2 = **26.4 W** |
| P_Cu per channel | 2.6 W |

### 1.5 Electrothermal self-consistency (runaway criterion)

With $P(T)=P_{25}[1+\alpha(T-25)]$ and $T = T_a + R_\theta P$:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_{25}\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway occurs if $R_\theta P_{25}\alpha \ge 1$.**

- Hottest FET: P₂₅ = 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W.
- Runaway threshold: R_θ ≥ 1/(9.6 × 0.005) = **20.8 K/W** (junction to ambient, steady state).
- An isolated 3.3×3.3 FET on a small, crowded board is often 25–40 K/W.

**Conclusion: 80 A continuous is fundamentally unstable in this package. It is valid only as a transient burst.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard-switching occurs at the high-side turn-on/off, with the synchronous FET body diode freewheeling during dead time.

| Loss | Equation | Parameters | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, 20 + 20 ns | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 V, 80 A, 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 50 nC | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charge | 2 × ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1 nF | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 30 nC, 10 V | 0.03 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

**Sensitivity:**
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel.
- Overlap loss dominates. Gate resistor choice (t_r/t_f) is the main lever, traded against EMI and ringing.
- Dead-time loss argues for the shortest safe dead time. AM32 lets you tune this.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Analysis

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst, all 4 channels

| Item | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 100 °C) | 26.4 W | 105.6 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 80 A) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **33.3 W** | **≈ 133 W** |

Board efficiency at full burst, with roughly 4 × 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW of electrical power, is about 98 %. However, 133 W into a ~25×25 mm board is the governing problem.

### 3.2 Board-level steady state (proves burst-only)

- Board area: 2 sides × 6.25×10⁻⁴ m² ≈ 1.25×10⁻³ m².
- Convection coefficient in prop wash: h ≈ 100 W/m²K.
- R_θ,board-amb = 1/(hA) ≈ **8 K/W**.
- Steady-state rise: ΔT = 133 × 8 ≈ 1060 K. **This is impossible, so the operating point can only be transient.**
- Sustainable continuous power for ΔT = 60 K is about 7.5 W total. That corresponds to roughly **20–25 A per channel continuous**, which is a realistic rating for this size.

### 3.3 Transient (adiabatic) board limit

Board thermal mass, including FR4, ~8 layers of copper, FETs, and caps (about 6 g):

$$C_{th} \approx m\,c_{eff} \approx 6\ \text{g} \times 0.7\ \text{J/gK} \approx 4.2\ \text{J/K}$
