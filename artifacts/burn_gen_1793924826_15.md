# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #40): Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Notes

Some aspects of the request need clarifying before the math, because they change the model.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In any practical 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. The switching-loss model below therefore applies to the ESC side, and the DMA/timer allocation applies to the H743 side.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A phase current per motor channel**, which is the standard ESC rating convention. Battery (bus) current is `I_bus = D·I_ph`, so four motors at 80 A and D≈0.9 implies roughly 290 A of bus current. That exceeds any nano-class pack and connector. Realistically, 80 A is a short transient (≲1 s), as the thermal section shows.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation and PWM scheme

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg, which is AM32 default behaviour. In each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction D. The low-side FET carries the freewheel current for (1−D), via synchronous rectification.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is held on for 100% of the sector.
- **Floating leg:** off (used for BEMF zero-cross sensing).

Over one electrical cycle, each leg spends 1/3 as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static low leg, and 1/3 floating.

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS-equivalent losses

With phase current I_ph (flat-top approximation) and R_ds(T_j) = R₂₅·[1 + α(T_j − 25)]:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{LS} = \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = \frac{2-D}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per motor (3 legs), the result is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The physical reason is that two FETs are always in the current path. The worst-case single FET is the low-side at low duty (D→0): `P_LS,max = (2/3)·I²·R_ds`.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

Assume T_j ≈ 110 °C, so R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **2.14 mΩ**.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I² | 6400 A² |
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 2.14 m = **27.4 W** |
| Worst low-side FET (D→0) | (2/3) × 6400 × 2.14 m = **9.1 W** |
| High-side FET at D=0.9 | 0.3 × 6400 × 2.14 m = 4.1 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper conduction

Phase and bus copper adds loss. A 2 oz layer has about 0.25 mΩ/□. Assuming roughly 0.3 mΩ of effective path per phase (multilayer, paralleled), the additional loss is about 2 × 6400 × 0.3 m ≈ **3.8 W per motor**. This is distributed across the board but adds to the bulk temperature rise. Increase copper weight or layer count if your layout gives a higher effective resistance.
