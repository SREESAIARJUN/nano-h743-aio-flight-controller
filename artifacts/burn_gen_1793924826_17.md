# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Electro-Thermal Model, Loss Analysis, DShot DMA Allocation and Netlist

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

No MOSFET, PCB stack-up or form factor was specified, so this model uses representative values. Swap in your datasheet numbers; every equation is parametric.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743's job is DShot600 output and bidirectional eRPM capture.

| Burst phase current, per channel | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

**Feasibility warning.** A "nano" board cannot sustain 4 × 80 A. The math below shows the burst is limited to about 2 seconds with all four motors loaded. Treat "80 A" as a transient rating and enforce it in firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In any commutation sector, current flows through two switches:
- the PWM-ing high-side (HS), complemented by its own low-side (LS) during the off-time, and
- the statically ON low-side of another phase.

**Instantaneous channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 (2R_{hot} + R_{Cu})$$

Here R_Cu is the PCB/pad path resistance, about 0.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400\,(3.84 + 0.5)\times10^{-3} = 24.6\ \text{W (FETs)} + 3.2\ \text{W (copper)} = 27.8\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET average over one electrical period** (each phase spends 1/3 of the time PWM-ing and 1/3 static-low):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{hot}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R_{hot} + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}$$

With I²R_hot = 12.29 W:

| Duty D | P_HS | P_LS |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 2.05 W | 6.14 W (worst LS) |
| 0.9 (typical burst) | 3.69 W | 4.51 W |

The low-side FET is the thermal hotspot at partial throttle. Place the most copper and vias under the LS FETs.

**R_DS(on) thermal feedback.** Conduction loss rises with junction temperature:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}[1+\alpha (T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Solve it iteratively with the thermal network in §3. It converges if $\partial P/\partial T \cdot R_{th} < 1$; for this design that product is about 0.07 per FET, so it is stable.
