# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

**Document:** Job #21, Architecture Baseline Rev A
**Scope:** Conduction and switching loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz), board-level transient thermal model, H743 timer/DMA allocation for ArduPilot BDShot600, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three findings shape everything below.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 section needs its own ESC MCU(s) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as STM32G071 or AT32F421 per channel. The H743 talks to them over BDShot600. The architecture below assumes 4× G071.

2. **80 A on a 20×20 mm nano board is a seconds-scale burst rating, never continuous.** The model in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A dissipates about 85–90 W. With realistic airflow, the board's steady-state thermal resistance is about 10–15 K/W. The burst window from 40 °C ambient is roughly 1.5–2.5 s. Comparable 20×20 AIO boards are honestly rated at about 12–25 A continuous. Marketing an "80 A" figure without a duration and ambient attached would be misleading.

3. **All device parameters below are representative placeholders** for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm (PDFN/DFN3333-class) MOSFET. Replace them with the datasheet values for the chosen part and re-run the model before freezing the BOM.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step commutation, exactly two phases carry current at any instant: phase X is PWM'd (HS/LS complementary) and phase Y has its LS held on. With synchronous rectification, the current path always runs through **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of duty D:

- During D: HS_X → motor → LS_Y
- During (1−D): LS_X → motor → LS_Y

So the conduction loss per channel is:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.2 Thermal feedback on R_DS(on)

Conduction loss depends on R_DS(on), which depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve self-consistently:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 75 °C | 1.25 mΩ | 16.0 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** |

Use the 125 °C figure for burst design: **P_cond = 19.2 W per channel**.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each of the six FETs conducts for about 1/3 of an electrical cycle. LS devices also absorb the (1−D) freewheel share. The average per FET is about 19.2 / 6 ≈ 3.2 W, with LS devices running about 10–20 % above HS at low D.

### 2.3 Switching (V·I overlap) loss

Only the PWM'd leg switches, one HS turn-on and one turn-off per period:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Secondary terms

**Coss loss:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

(Use E_oss from the datasheet if available. Q·V is an upper bound.)

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.05\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.04\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Copper and shunt losses (often the forgotten term)

**Current shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt per channel at 80 A gives I²R = 1.6 W. Consider 0.2 mΩ, or sense via R_DS(on) or the bus shunt.

**PCB copper.** Assume 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, and about 15 mm of current path at 5 mm width. Sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/sq for 2 oz, so 3 squares ≈ 0.75 mΩ, paralleled across 2 layers ≈ 0.37 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.37\text{ mΩ} \approx 2.4\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Totals

| Term | Per channel (W) | 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 | 76.8 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Coss + Qrr | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 | 0.2 |
| Shunt | 1.6 | 6.4 |
| Copper (phase + bus share) | about 2.4 | about 9.6 |
| **Total** | **about 24.9** | **about 99.7** |

**Interpretation.** Conduction dominates (about 77 %). At 48 kHz with fast-switching FETs, switching loss is minor. **Lower R_DS(on), and parallel FETs if area allows, buy far more than a lower f_sw.** For comparison, dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel.

### 2.7 Input capacitor ripple

The worst case is D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\text{ A per channel}$$

The four channels share the bus. Ripple partially cancels only if PWM phases are interleaved, and ESC firmware does not interleave across MCUs. Practical consequences:

- Low-ESR MLCC bank on the board: about 4–6 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 per channel.
- External 35 V low-ESR electrolytic of at least 470 µF, required at the battery lead.
- **The 80 A burst also sets the battery-lead and XT60 path rating.** At 320 A summed, the XT60 is outside its spec (about 60 A continuous). Flag this to system integration.
