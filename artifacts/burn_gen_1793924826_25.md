# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & Motor-Output Architecture

I don't have the Job #26 requirements on file, so this document is **parametric**. Every model takes the inputs in §0. Where I had to choose a value, I used a defensible typical figure and flagged it. Replace the flagged values with your actual MOSFET datasheet, PCB stackup and gate-driver numbers before sign-off.

---

## Executive Summary

The headline finding is that **80 A burst per motor on a 20×20 mm nano board is not thermally sustainable for more than about 1 second if all four motors burst together.**

| Scenario | Loss | Time to the thermal limit |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 motors at 80 A | ≈122 W | ≈1 s |
| 1 motor at 80 A | ≈30.5 W | ≈6–7 s |

The single-motor figure is dominated by conduction loss (I²R), not switching loss. Switching at 48 kHz contributes less than 10% of the total.

**Recommendations:**
- Specify the rating as **"80 A / 1 s burst, per motor, non-simultaneous"**.
- Enforce it in ESC firmware with current limiting.
- Alternatively, move to a 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 mm form factor with ≤1.0 mΩ FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases are active: phase A is PWM-switched and phase B is held low.

- **During D (on-time):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **During 1−D (synchronous freewheel):** LS_A → motor → LS_B

So the phase current **always** passes through exactly two conducting MOSFETs, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2\,\big(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\big)$$

The ripple-corrected RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D = 0.5):

- ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **8.75 A**
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 6.4 A²

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.04 A.
