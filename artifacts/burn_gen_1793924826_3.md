# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #11): Architecture, Loss Model and DShot Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Scope Corrections

These points change the design and should be agreed before layout.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented or six-step motor-drive code. In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BLHeli_S). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below follows that split.
2. **80 A burst per channel on a 20×20 "nano" board is not thermally credible** beyond about 1 s (see §3.4). Real 20×20 4-in-1s are rated at roughly 35–45 A burst. I model 80 A as specified and then show the limits. If "80 A" means the *total* across four motors (20 A each), the design closes easily. The 20 A case is included.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, the current path in every state is:

> V_bus → high-side FET (phase A) → motor (A–B) → low-side FET (phase B) → GND

During PWM-off, the phase-A low-side conducts in place of the high-side. So at every instant **exactly two FETs carry the phase current I**, regardless of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.4 Per-FET average loss

Each leg is active for 120° of every 360° electrical period. Within that window, the high-side conducts for D (duty) and the low-side for (1−D) of the PWM period.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}\,(1 + D),\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}\,(2 - D)$$

The worst FET at full throttle (D → 1) carries (2/3)·I²R_ds.

Each FET is the "upper" device in two of the six sectors. The (1+D) and (2−D) terms come from summing the commutating leg and the static leg of each phase.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The static leg's FET stays on for the whole sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle.

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j. Derate it ×2 when hot.

### 2.4 C_oss discharge

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET. Two FETs per leg switch.

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\,\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss term | Power |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.6 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr (hot, ×2) | 0.12 W |
| C_oss + gate | 0.07 W |
| **Channel total** | **≈19.4 W** |
| **4 channels, all at 80 A** | **≈77.5 W** |

Efficiency check: output power is 22.2 V × 80 A ≈ 1776 W per channel, so η_bridge ≈ 98.9%. The losses are small in relative terms. They are only a problem because of the board's tiny volume.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{P_{sw} + P_{dt} + P_{rr}}{f_{sw}} \approx 35\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.85 W per channel. Conduction loss dominates, so **R_ds and copper area are the levers, not f_sw**. Keep 48 kHz: it gives lower current ripple and quieter motors.
