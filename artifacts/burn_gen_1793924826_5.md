# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture assumption.** On a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller** running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel normally has its own small motor-control MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors directly. It emits bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below follows that split. If you intend the H743 to commutate all four motors itself, that is a different design: 24 PWM outputs plus back-EMF or FOC sensing. Tell me and I will redo the timer plan.

**Electrical assumptions.** Change these and the equations still hold.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, full charge |
| Burst current per motor | I | 80 A | Spec requirement |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | AM32/BLHeli_32 maximum setting |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 DFN class | Representative values, not a specific part |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 0.9 mΩ | |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ~0.6 %/K, about 1.5× at 110 °C | |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC | |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss | 1.5 nF | |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC | |
| Rise and fall times (driver-limited) | t_r + t_f | 40 ns | |
| Dead time | t_dt | 300 ns per edge | |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V | |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM | |

**Feasibility warning.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm or 25×25 mm "nano" board cannot sustain 80 A per motor for more than about 1–3 seconds without a heatsink and forced airflow. Treat "80 A" as a **short burst rating limited by transient thermal impedance**. It is not a continuous rating. If the requirement is 80 A total across four motors (20 A each), the design is comfortable.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α·(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ × [1 + 0.006 × 85] ≈ **1.36 mΩ**

This dependence creates **electro-thermal feedback**: higher temperature raises resistance, which raises loss, which raises temperature. The conduction loss must therefore be solved iteratively with the thermal model in Section 3.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In each 60° commutation sector, exactly one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS) carry the phase current. With synchronous PWM at duty D:

- **Active leg:** HS conducts for D, and the complementary LS conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the LS stays on continuously.

At any instant, two R_DS(on) are in series:

P_cond,motor = I² · 2 · R_DS(on)(T_j)

P_cond,motor = 80² × 2 × 1.36 mΩ = **17.4 W per motor**

### 1.3 Distribution across individual FETs

Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle, which is a duty of 1/3:

P_cond,FET(avg) = 17.4 W / 6 ≈ **2.9 W**

The **peak** dissipation while a FET is conducting is I²·R = 8.7 W. This peak sets the junction temperature ripple at low eRPM, where a commutation sector can last several milliseconds.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Battery pad to bus, 2 oz, 4 parallel layers, short run | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W, shared |
| Phase pour, per motor | ~0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |
| Current shunt, if fitted (0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | **1.6 W** (choose ≤ 0.2 mΩ or use a Hall sensor) |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only on the PWM leg, so one HS/LS pair per motor at any time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw

P_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40×10⁻⁹ × 48×10³ = **1.94 W**

The rise and fall times come from the Miller plateau:

t_r ≈ Q_gd · R_g,total / (V_drv − V_plateau)

With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_g = 4.7 Ω, V_drv = 10 V, and V_pl = 4 V, this gives about **9.4 ns** per edge. The 40 ns used above is a conservative allowance for layout inductance.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

P_dt = 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw

P_dt = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 300×10⁻⁹ × 48×10³ = **1.84 W**

This term is as large as the overlap loss. Cutting dead time from 300 ns to 100 ns saves about 1.2 W per motor. That is the cheapest efficiency gain available.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

P_rr = V_bus · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 kHz = **0.06 W**

### 2.4 Output capacitance

P_oss = ½ · C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.02 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET:

P_g = Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 60 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = **0.029 W per switching FET**

### 2.6 Per-motor and total budget at the 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 110 °C) | 17.4 W | 69.6 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 W | 7.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Copper + shunt | ~2.0 W | ~8 W |
| **Total** | **~23.3 W** | **~93 W** |

Output power is about 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is roughly 7.3 kW, so efficiency is about 98.7%. Even so, 93 W of heat on a nano PCB is the binding constraint.

### 2.7 Frequency trade

Only the switching terms scale with frequency:

P_total(f) = P_cond + k_f · f_sw, where k_f = (1.94 + 1.84 + 0.08) / 48 kHz ≈ 80 µW/Hz per motor

| f_sw | Switching loss per motor | Saving vs. 48 kHz | Effect on motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 kHz | 3.86 W | — | Lowest current ripple |
| 24 kHz | 1.93 W | ~1.9 W per motor, ~7.7 W total | Ripple rises |

The ripple at 24 kHz is acceptable for low-inductance (under 10 µH) racing motors only if that ripple-related motor heating is accepted.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (per FET, Foster/Cauer reduced)

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc ≈ 1.0 K/W, τ≈5 ms]──(case)──[R_cb ≈ 1.5 K/W via vias]──(PCB node)
                                                                          │
                                                C_pcb (lumped) ──[R_ba]── T_amb
```

### 3.2 PCB thermal mass (20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper)

| Component | Calculation | Mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper | 6 × 0.4 cm² × 70 µm ≈ 0.168 cm³ | 1.5 g | × 0.385 J/g·K = 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4 | 0.64 cm³ | 1.2 g | × 1.1 J/g·K = 1.3 J/K |
| 24 FETs + passives | ~2.5 g | 2.5 g | × ~0.7 J/g·K = 1.75 J/K |
| **Total C_pcb** | | | **≈ 3.6 J/K** |

### 3.3 Board-to-ambient resistance

In prop wash at about 10 m/s, with h ≈ 60 W/m²·K over 2 × 4 cm² of surface:

R_ba ≈ 1 / (60 × 8×10⁻⁴) ≈ **21 K/W**

A 25×25 mm board with a clip-on heatsink reaches about 6 K/W.

### 3.4 Transient response to a step burst

ΔT_pcb(t) = P·R_ba · (1 − e^(−t/τ)), with τ = R_ba·C_pcb ≈ 75 s

For t ≪ τ the response is effectively adiabatic:

ΔT_pcb ≈ P·t / C_pcb = 93 W / 3.6 J/
