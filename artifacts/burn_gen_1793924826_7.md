# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot BDShot600 DMA Model

**Job #25 · Reference design, revision A**

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

This document assumes the standard split architecture:

- **FC MCU:** an STM32H743 running ArduPilot generates bi-directional DShot600.
- **ESC MCUs:** four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) drive the bridges at 48 kHz.

An H743 directly commutating 12 FETs is possible, but neither ArduPilot nor AM32 supports that. If Job #25 actually intends a single-MCU AIO, Section 4 changes completely.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high side and one low side.

- At duty D, the high side carries I for a fraction D of each PWM period.
- With synchronous rectification, the complementary low side of the same leg carries I for (1 − D).
- The opposite leg's low side carries I continuously.

The instantaneous conduction loss in the current path is:

P_cond(t) = I² · R_DS(Tj) · [D + (1 − D) + 1] = **2 · I² · R_DS(Tj)**

This expression is independent of D, because synchronous rectification means two FETs are always in the path.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

R_DS(Tj) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(Tj − 25)], with α ≈ 0.006 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

### 1.3 Numbers per Channel

| Condition | R_DS | P_cond = 2·I²·R |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C (cold start) | 1.0 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C (hot) | 1.6 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.6e-3 = **20.5 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET Average

Each FET is in the path for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, at 2 of 6 positions. The average loss per FET is therefore:

P_FET,cond ≈ 20.5 W / 6 = **3.4 W** (hot).

### 1.5 PCB Copper and Shunt Losses

Assume about 0.3 mΩ of phase and bus copper plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt per channel (or a shared shunt):

P_Cu ≈ 6400 · 0.55e-3 ≈ **3.5 W per channel**

This heat goes directly into the board, so it counts in the transient model.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching loss. That is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM cycle.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap

P_sw = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20e-9 · 48e3 = **0.97 W**

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

P_rr = Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.06 W**

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

P_oss = ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw = 0.5 · 40e-9 · 25.2 · 48e3 = **0.024 W**

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

This is the dominant switching-related term at 48 kHz.

P_dt = V_SD · I · 2 · t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 2 · 200e-9 · 48e3 = **1.23 W**

Reducing dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify the shoot-through margin with the actual driver propagation-delay mismatch first.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · N_switching = 40e-9 · 10 · 48e3 · 2 = **0.04 W**

Most of this is dissipated in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

**≈ 2.3 W per channel.** Switching losses scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz this term drops to about 1.15 W, at the cost of motor current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst, Hot

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (125 °C) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **26.3 W** | **105 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full power is about 1 − 26.3/(25.2·80) ≈ 98.7%. The problem is not efficiency. It is heat flux density: about 105 W into roughly 13 cm² of board.

### 3.2 Board Heat Capacity (Lumped)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 1.296e-3 m² × 6 × 70 µm = 5.44e-7 m³ → 4.9 g × 0.385 J/gK | 1.9 J/K |
| FR4 | 1.296e-3 × 1.6e-3 = 2.07e-6 m³ → 3.8 g × 1.1 J/gK | 4.2 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | | ~0.6 J/K |
| **C_th** | | **≈ 6.7 J/K** |

### 3.3 Convective Path to Ambient

Assume prop-wash forced convection with h ≈ 100 W/m²K on both faces. Area = 2.6e-3 m², so:

G = 0.26 W/K → R_θBA ≈ 3.85 K/W

The time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 3.85 × 6.7 ≈ **26 s**.

### 3.4 Transient Board Temperature

T_board(t) = T_amb + P · R_θBA · (1 − e^(−t/τ))

During a burst, the steady-state asymptote (105 × 3.85 ≈ 400 K rise) is irrelevant. The rise is effectively adiabatic:

dT/dt ≈ P / C_th = 105 / 6.7 ≈ **15.7 K/s**
