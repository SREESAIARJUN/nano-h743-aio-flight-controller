## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two architectural points need to be fixed before any modeling, because they change the math.

**1. The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay firmware. Typical parts are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or EFM8BB51. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. Putting 4× FOC/6-step plus ArduPilot on one H743 is technically possible, but it is not supported by ArduPilot and would be a custom firmware project. The rest of this document assumes the standard split:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --BDShot600 x4--> 4x ESC MCU --> 4x 3-phase drivers --> 24 MOSFETs
```

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a transient rating, not a continuous one.** The analysis below shows that a 30×30 mm board can hold 4×80 A for only about 8 seconds before the board reaches 110 °C. That limit should appear explicitly on the spec sheet.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{T_j-25}=0.80\,\text{mΩ}\times1.0045^{85}\approx 1.17\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Conduction topology

The model uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° commutation sector:

- The **PWM leg** conducts I_ph through its high-side FET for duty fraction D.
- The same leg's **low-side FET** conducts for (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- The **return leg's low-side FET** conducts 100% of the time.

So two FET channels are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=I_{ph}^2R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D+(1-D)+1\right]=2\,I_{ph}^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The phase-current ripple is

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}}$$

For L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 8.75 A peak-to-peak. The RMS correction is:

$$I_{rms}^2=I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}=6400+6.4\ \Rightarrow\ +0.1\%\ \text{(negligible)}$$

### 1.4 Result

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2\times80^2\times1.17\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{14.98\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

**Board total: ≈ 59.9 W of conduction loss.**

Conduction dominates. The two levers are Rds(on) and parallel FETs:
- Halving Rds(on) halves this term.
- Paralleling two FETs per switch cuts it by roughly 2×, at the cost of area.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The model is per bridge.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(400\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k})=\mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is easy to overlook. Reducing dead-time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope before doing so.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k})=0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\cdot2=0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2(60\,\text{nC})(10)(48\,\text{k})=0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel and board totals

| Term | W/channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 14.98 |
| V-I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 17.8 W** |
| **Board (×4)** | **≈ 71 W** |
| Plus FC, regulators, copper I²R | +3–5 W |
| **Board total P_tot** | **≈ 75 W** |

Copper loss sanity check: a 2 oz pour of 0.25 mΩ effective path at 4×80 A on the shared battery feed adds about 1.6 W (I_bus is lower than I_ph by D).

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}}\approx\frac{1.45+1.23+0.17}{48\text{k}}\approx 59\ \mu\text{W/Hz per channel}$$

Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per channel. The cost is more current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET average loss

Each FET conducts roughly 1/3 of an electrical cycle. At burst eRPM, the commutation period (< 1 ms) is far shorter than the FET thermal time constants, so averaging is valid:

$$P_{FET}\approx\frac{17.8}{6}\approx 3.0\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Thermal network (Cauer simplification)

```
Tj --[Rth_jc 0.9 K/W]-- Tcase --[Rth_cs 1.5 K/W solder+vias]-- T_pcb
     (C ~0.02 J/K)                                              |
                              [C_board ≈ 8 J/K]   [R_conv ≈ 5.5 K/W]
                                                                |
                                                             T_amb
```

**Board heat capacity:**
- Copper: about 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K
- FR4: about 4 g × 1.1 J/g·K
- Components: about 1 J/K
- Total: ≈ 7.9 J/K

**Convection under prop wash:**
- Heat-transfer coefficient h ≈ 100 W/m²·K
- Area A = 2 × (0.03)² = 1.8×10⁻³ m²
- R_conv = 1/(hA) ≈ 5.5 K/W

**Board time constant:** τ = R·C ≈ 44 s

### 3.3 Junction over board

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb}=3.0\times(0.9+1.5)\approx 7.2\ \text{K}$$

This settles in under 100 ms.

### 3.4 Board burst transient

$$T_{pcb}(t)=T_0+P_{tot}R_{conv}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The steady-state asymptote is P·R = 75 × 5.5 ≈ 412 K rise. **
