## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The numbers below depend on these assumptions. Change any of them and the model must be recomputed.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Use 40 V MOSFETs. 8S would need 60 V parts and invalidates the switching numbers. |
| "80A burst" definition | 80 A **phase current per motor**, ≤ 5 s | Industry ESC ratings are per channel. |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM | AM32-style active freewheeling. |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, sensorless | |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, M3 mounting, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes | |
| MOSFET (representative 40 V, 5×6 DFN class) | R_ds(on),25 = 0.8 mΩ, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss,eff = 1.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 0.9 K/W | Replace with your actual BOM values. |

**Feasibility flag 1: "nano" vs. 80 A.** A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot sink the losses computed below, even for a burst. 80 A burst is only credible on 30.5×30.5 mm or larger, with airflow. On a 20×20 board, de-rate to roughly 35–45 A burst. The thermal section shows why.

**Feasibility flag 2: the H743 does not drive the gates.** In this architecture the H743 is the flight controller and runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (STM32G071 / AT32F421 running AM32) that runs commutation and 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. Driving 24 gates with closed-loop sensorless commutation from ArduPilot is not supported and would break the timing budget.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)}(110) = 0.8 \times (1 + 0.0065 \times 85) = 0.8 \times 1.55 = 1.24\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

Add the PCB/via/solder parasitic per conduction path, R_par ≈ 0.25 mΩ per FET (2 oz, stitched vias). This gives:

$$R_{eff} = 1.49\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

### 1.2 Six-step conduction topology

During any commutation sector, phase X (PWM side) and phase Y (low side, fully on) carry I_ph:

- **HS_X** conducts for a fraction D of each PWM period.
- **LS_X** conducts for 1 − D (synchronous rectification, ignoring dead-time).
- **LS_Y** conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant exactly **two channels' worth of R_eff** carry I_ph. The bridge loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{eff}$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.49\times10^{-3} = 19.1\ \mathrm{W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for one-third of the electrical cycle on average. The bridge loss is shared by 6 FETs over time:

$$\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{19.1}{6} \approx 3.2\ \mathrm{W}$$

The silicon-only share (excluding PCB copper) is about 2.65 W per FET.

**Worst-case FET:** at high D (≈ 0.95), the HS and fully-on LS devices carry nearly all of the loss:

$$P_{HS} \approx \frac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg (HS_X / LS_X) hard-switches. LS_Y switches once per commutation event, which is negligible.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (hard-switched FET)

Gate driver: about 1.5 A source / 2 A sink, with R_g,ext = 2.2 Ω. Plateau-limited transition time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g} \approx \frac{10\,\mathrm{nC} + 5\,\mathrm{nC}}{1\,\mathrm{A}} = 15\ \mathrm{ns}$$

This gives t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = 1.45\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

The I_rr overlap adds about 2× this in the HS FET. Budget **0.10 W**.

### 2.3 C_oss loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, C_{oss,eff}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.023\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns per edge and 2 edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = 0.61\ \mathrm{W}$$

This loss lands in **LS_X**. AM32 dead-time settings tighter than 100 ns reduce it, but they risk shoot-through with this Q_g.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

For two switching FETs per bridge:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET junction.
