## 1. Control Architecture

```
                 ┌────────── 1 kHz ──────────┐  ┌──── 4 kHz ────┐  ┌────────── 20 kHz ──────────┐
 q_ref ──►(Attitude P + rate FF)──► ω_ref ──►(Rate PI)──► i_q* ──►(+)──►(Current PI d/q)──► SVPWM ─► Motor
             ▲ quaternion error        ▲ inertial rate     ▲  i_q,ff (anti-cogging)   ▲ i_d,i_q     │
             │                         │ (gyro → joint)    │                          │ Clarke/Park │
         Mahony AHRS ◄── fused ◄── Dual ICM-42688-P ◄── vibration filters      Encoder θ_m ─────────┘
```

**Gimbal-specific design decision.** The velocity loop closes on the inertial angular rate of the camera, measured by the fused gyros and projected onto each joint axis. It does not close on encoder velocity. The encoder handles three jobs only:

1. Commutation.
2. Indexing the anti-cogging table.
3. Detecting joint limits and stalls.

Closing the rate loop on the encoder would stabilize the motor relative to the vehicle, which is the opposite of what the gimbal needs.

**Bandwidth separation.** Each loop is roughly 5–10× faster than the one outside it:

| Loop | Rate | Crossover |
|---|---|---|
| Current | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s |
| Rate | 4 kHz | ω_v ≈ 2π·60–120 Hz |
| Attitude | 1 kHz | ω_a ≈ 2π·10–20 Hz |
