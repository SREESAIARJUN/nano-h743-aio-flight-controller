# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Model (Job #25)

## 0. Architecture and Scope

**Who generates 48 kHz PWM.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.

- **Flight controller.** The H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESCs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- **ESCs.** Four dedicated ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and a 6-FET bridge.

The model below is split along that boundary.

**The 80 A rating is a short burst, not a continuous figure.** Section 3 shows that on a nano (20×20 mm) board:

- 80 A per channel is thermally sustainable only for roughly 1–2 s.
- At steady state, 80 A sits at the edge of thermal runaway.

Treat "80 A" as a short-burst marketing figure and design to the verified limits.

**All component values here are representative assumptions.** Replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_0\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 × (1 + 0.375) = **1.65 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Conduction loss for 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector:

- One leg is PWM'd: the high side conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low side conducts for (1 − D).
- One low-side FET is held fully on.

So at every instant, exactly two channel resistances carry the phase current:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.65\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is active for 120° (one third) of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac13 D\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac13\,I^2R+\tfrac13(1-D)\,I^2R$$

At burst throttle (D ≈ 1), each of the 6 FETs averages **≈ 3.5 W**, with a peak of **10.6 W** inside its 120° window. At typical electrical frequencies of 1–3 kHz, the die thermal time constant (~ms) mostly averages this. Even so, the 10.6 W figure sets the junction ripple.
