# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Loss, Thermal and DShot Timer Design

## 0. Architectural Correction

**The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack the partition is:

| Domain | Silicon | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 running ArduPilot | Attitude control, mixer, **bi-directional DShot600 master**, RPM telemetry consumer, harmonic notch |
| ESC (×4) | One small MCU per motor (STM32G071, AT32F421 or EFM8BB51) running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay | 6-step or FOC commutation, **48 kHz PWM**, dead-time, BEMF sensing, eRPM telemetry |
| Power stage | 3-phase driver (e.g. FD6288Q class) plus 24 N-channel MOSFETs | Conduction and switching |

Switching losses therefore depend on ESC firmware settings: PWM frequency, dead-time and complementary PWM. The FC's job is timing integrity on the DShot lines.

A second reality check is that **"80 A burst" on a nano-class board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 quantifies the burst duration.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{(T_j-25)}$$

Using the datasheet curve, R_DS(100 °C) ≈ 1.45 mΩ.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

With duty D, the PWM'd high-side carries I for D·(1/3) of the cycle. The synchronous low-side of the same leg carries I during (1−D)·(1/3) of the cycle. The commutating-low-side FET carries I for 1/3 of the cycle.

$$P_{cond,motor}= I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,]\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 3 = 2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The model is duty-independent with synchronous rectification. That is the key property.

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.45\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

Copper loss:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R_{Cu}=6400\times0.3\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

Per FET, averaged:

$$P_{FET}=I^2R_{DS}/3 = 3.1\ W$$

### 2.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

The gate currents through the Miller plateau are:

$$I_{G,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{G}}=\frac{10-3.5}{10}=0.65\text{ A},\qquad I_{G,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_G}=0.35\text{ A}$$

The voltage and current transition times are:

$$t_{v}= \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_G},\qquad t_{i}=\frac{Q_{gs}-Q_{g(th)}}{I_G}$$

This gives t_on ≈ 15 ns + 8 ns ≈ 23 ns and t_off ≈ 29 ns + 14 ns ≈ 43 ns.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·66 ns·48k = **3.19 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS) | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (energy-equivalent approx.) | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_SD·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·200n·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·50n·10·48k = **0.048 W** (dissipated in driver/R_G) |
| **Total switching (D < 1)** | | **≈ 4.5 W** |

Two caveats apply:
- **At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), PWM edges vanish.** Only commutation edges remain, at a few kHz. Switching loss collapses to roughly 0.3 W.
- **Worst-case combined stress is around D ≈ 0.85–0.95 at high current.** This happens during a sustained punch-out below 100 % throttle.

Dead-time diode loss scales linearly with t_dt. Cutting dead-time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Only do this if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it.

### 2.4 Totals

| Condition | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction + Cu, T_j = 100 °C | 20.5 W | 82 W |
| Switching (D ≈ 0.9) | 4.5 W | 18 W |
| **Burst total** | **≈ 25 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Electrical efficiency at burst is 100 W lost out of about 8 kW (4 × 80 A × 25 V). That is roughly 98.8 %, so the losses are small electrically but very large thermally.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

Use a Cauer chain per FET. The FETs then share the board as a common thermal mass:

```
P_FET ─┬─[R_jc 1.2 K/W]─┬─[R_c-pcb 2.0 K/W]─┬─────────[R_pcb-amb 6 K/W total board]─── T_amb
       C_j (≈0.02 J/K)  C_case(≈0.05 J/K)   C_board (≈4–6 J/K, lumped: Cu+FR4+FETs)
```

### 3.2 Steady state (shows why 80 A continuous is impossible)

With prop-wash convection, the whole-board R_θ,board-amb ≈ 3–6 K/W. At P = 100 W the board would settle 300–600 K above ambient. **80 A is therefore not continuous.**

The thermally sustainable current with ΔT ≈ 70 K and R_θ ≈ 4 K/W gives a budget of about 17.5 W total, or 4.4 W per motor. Solving 2I²R_DS + I²R_Cu ≈ 4.4 W gives I ≈ 35–38 A continuous per motor. That figure is consistent with honest ratings for this board class.

### 3.3 Burst (adiabatic board heating)

For a 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with 24 FETs and capacitors (≈ 7 g total):

$$C_{th}\approx m_{Cu}c_{Cu}+m_{FR4}c_{FR4}\approx (3\text{ g})(0.385)+(4\text{ g})(1.1)\approx 5.6\ \text{J/K}$$

The adiabatic temperature rise and burst limit are:

$$\Delta T(t)\approx\frac{P\,t}{C_{th}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad t_{burst}=\frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T_{allow}}{P}=\frac{5.6\times 60}{100}\approx \mathbf{3.4\ s}$$

The FET junction rides above the board temperature:

$$\Delta T_{j-board}=P_{FET}(R_{jc}+R_{c\text{-}pcb})\approx 3.1 \times 3.2 \approx 10\ \text{K}$$

**Spec statement:** *80 A burst ≤ 3 s per motor, all four motors simultaneously, from a 40 °C board, T_j
