**Architecture.** The H743 is the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), which handle commutation and 48 kHz PWM. The H743 does **not** generate the motor PWM. This separation is mandatory: ArduPilot is not a motor commutation firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd high and phase B is held low:

- **On-state (duty D):** current flows V_bus → Q_AH → motor → Q_BL → GND.
- **Off-state (1−D):** current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous) → motor → Q_BL.

The instantaneous conduction loss of the channel is:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

With synchronous rectification, **channel conduction loss is independent of duty cycle.** This is a key design insight: low-throttle, high-current cases (for example punch-outs from hover) are just as hot as full throttle.

The temperature-corrected resistance is:

$$
R_{hot} = R_{ds(on),25}\cdot k_T + R_{trace/solder}
$$

Assuming 0.2 mΩ of copper and solder per conduction path segment:

$$
R_{hot} = 1.3 \times 1.5 + 0.2 = 2.15\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Numerical result at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.15\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.5\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries an equal share:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{27.5}{6} \approx 4.6\ \text{W}
$$

**Distribution caveat.** The share is equal on average, but split differently by duty. High-side FETs take ∝ D and low-side FETs take ∝ (2−D) within their sectors. At low D, low-side FETs run hotter. Size the low-side FETs or their copper accordingly.

### 1.3 Ancillary conduction losses (per channel)

| Element | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt (0.1 mΩ, DC side) | (D·I)²·R_sh, D → 1 | 0.64 W |
| Phase pad/wire junction (~0.3 mΩ ×2 active) | 2·I²·R | 3.8 W |
| Bus copper (pour, 2 oz, ~0.15 mΩ) | (D·I)²·R | 0.96 W |

**Pad and wire termination losses are frequently the hidden hot spot.** Specify solder-cup pads at least 2.5×4 mm with thermal vias.
