## 0. Architecture Clarification and Governing Assumptions

The H743 does not commutate the motors directly. A production-grade "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" is an AIO (all-in-one) board:

- The **STM32H743VIH6** runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (STM32G071GBU6, running AM32) each drive one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz.

Running FOC for 4 motors on the H743 alongside the EKF is possible in principle, but it is not supported by ArduPilot and is not recommended.

> **Red flag up front:** 4 × 64 A = **256 A battery current**. XT60 (≈60 A cont.) and typical 12 AWG leads cannot carry this. "80 A burst" must be treated as a **per-channel, non-simultaneous or sub-2-second** rating. The math below quantifies exactly how short.

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step commutation two phase legs carry current at any instant. With synchronous rectification, each active leg always has exactly one FET on (HS during D, LS during 1−D). The conducting path therefore always contains **two R_ds(on)** in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Ripple correction: I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI²/12.

Motor phase-to-phase inductance is L ≈ 10 µH for a typical 2207 at 1900 KV. Take ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw) = 25.2·0.16/(10e-6·48e3) ≈ **8.4 A p-p**. This gives ΔI²/12 = 5.9 A², which is negligible against 6400 A² (+0.09 %).
