## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

Three constraints shape the rest of this document.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation or FOC stack for the FC MCU. The H743 also has only two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8) with complementary outputs and dead-time insertion, which is enough for 2 motors, not 4. HRTIM could be pressed into service, but you would be writing a custom ESC firmware inside a flight stack.

**Adopted architecture:**

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 x4──► 4x ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class,
                                               e.g. AT32F421K8U7 / STM32G071KBU6)
                                               └─ 3-ph gate driver ─► 6x N-MOSFET per motor
```

The 48 kHz figure is therefore the **ESC PWM frequency** (set in AM32), not an H743 timer output.

### 0.2 "Nano" form factor vs. 80 A

80 A per motor in a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm footprint is thermally impossible for anything but a few seconds (§3 quantifies this). The analysis below assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount, 36×36 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz copper** stack-up. On a 20×20 mm board, divide the burst time by roughly 2.5.

### 0.3 Operating point

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching) |
| Burst current | I = 80 A per motor (phase DC-equivalent during conduction) |
| ESC PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Ambient / start temperature | 50 °C board temperature at burst start (post-cruise) |
| Max board temperature | 110 °C (FR-4 Tg 170 °C, MLCC and solder margin) |
| Max MOSFET junction temperature | 150 °C |

### 0.4 Assumed MOSFET [A]

40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class device:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| Q_g (total) | 35 nC |
| Q_gs2 + Q_gd (switching charge, Q_sw) | 10 nC |
| V_plateau | 3.5 V |
| C_oss(er) | 1000 pF |
| Q_rr | 30 nC |
| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient α | 0.0065 /K |

**Note on 6S:** a 40 V part on 6S leaves about 15 V of margin for ringing. That is acceptable only with tight commutation loops and ≥ 470 µF of low-ESR bulk capacitance plus MLCCs at every half-bridge. If you cannot guarantee that, use 60 V parts and accept roughly 1.8× the R_DS(on).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between its high-side switch (during D) and its low-side switch (during 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The **return leg** has its low-side switch on continuously.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two** channels in series.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.0065 \times 75) = 1.785\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple-corrected RMS current

Phase inductance L_ph-ph = 20 µH **[A]**. Ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.06%, so it can be neglected at 80 A. It does matter for low-inductance motors (under 5 µH).

### 1.4 Conduction loss per motor

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.785\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.86\ W}$$

Distribution across the six switches:

| Switch | Loss | At D = 1 (full throttle) |
|---|---|---|
| High-side, PWM leg | D·I²R | 11.4 W |
| Low-side, PWM leg | (1−D)·I²R | ≈ 0 W |
| Low-side, return leg | I²R | 11.4 W, steady during that 60° sector |

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each FET carries current for 1/3 of the time, so the average is about 3.8 W per FET. However, the **instantaneous peak is 11.4 W per FET**. Because a PDFN die's thermal time constant (about 1–10 ms) is comparable to the commutation period at low eRPM, use the peak value for T_j at low speed and the average at high speed.

### 1.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns **[A]** and two transitions per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
