# Job #31: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC)

## Engineering Model: 80 A Burst Losses, 48 kHz Switching, ArduPilot Bi-directional DShot600 DMA

---

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

These points change the analysis, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In any practical 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The model below follows that split.
2. **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible beyond about 1–3 s.** The math in §3 shows why. I give the numbers rather than endorse the rating.
3. **Component values are representative and parametric, not quoted from a specific datasheet.** Where I name parts (FD6288Q, AT32F421), verify every parameter and pin against the current datasheets before layout. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready file.

## 1. Conduction Losses (Trapezoidal 6-step, Complementary PWM)

In each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for time D, the synchronous low-side for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** the low-side is on for 100% of the sector.

Total conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.00225 = \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Temperature dependence, solved iteratively with T_j:

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ /\text{K}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts about 120° of the electrical cycle. Electrical frequency is >500 Hz at burst rpm, much faster than the junction-to-board thermal time constant. So use the average:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx P_{motor}/6$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one transistor per motor hard-switches at any instant (the PWM leg high-side).

**Transition time**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{drv}} = 15\ \text{ns}$$

**V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

**Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 0.05\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET; six FETs)

$$P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.115\ W$$

### Per-motor loss summary at 80 A burst

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.80 |
| V–I switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.11 |
| Gate drive | 0.12 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if used) | ~1.6 |
| **Total** | **≈ 32.7 W** |

All four motors at burst: **≈ 131 W**. ESC efficiency at that point is about 1 − 32.7/(25.2·80·D_eff), which is acceptable electrically. The problem is purely thermal density.

**Sensitivity:** P_cond ∝ I², and P_sw ∝ f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per motor. **Conduction dominates**, so R_DS(on) and copper are the levers that matter.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-board (per FET)

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = \bar P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta c-b})$$

With P̄_FET ≈ 32.7/6 ≈ 5.5 W, R_θjc ≈ 1.5 K/W (DFN exposed pad), and R_θc-b ≈ 3 K/W (2 oz copper with via array under the pad):

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 25\ \text{K}$$

### 3.2 Board-to-air (steady state)

- Board area, both sides: 2 × 20 × 20 mm = 8×10⁻⁴ m²
- Prop-wash convection: h ≈ 80 W/m²·K

$$R_{\theta b-a} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 15.6\ \text{K/W}$$

At 131 W, the steady-state rise would be about 2000 K. **There is no continuous 80 A operating point.** The steady-state budget for a 65 K rise is about 4.2 W total, roughly 10–12 A per motor continuous. That figure is in line with how 20×20 AIOs actually behave once you strip marketing ratings.

### 3.3 Burst (lumped capacitance)

The board, FETs, copper and any heat spreader give C_th ≈ 6 J/K.

$$\tau = R_{\theta b-a} C_{th} \approx 94\ \text{s}, \qquad T_b(t) = T_0 + P R_{\theta}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$$

For t ≪ τ the board heats roughly adiabatically:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T_{allow}}{P}$$
