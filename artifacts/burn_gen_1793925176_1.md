# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

**Job #10: Nano Form Factor, 80 A Burst, Bidirectional DShot600**

---

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three architectural facts shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and sends DShot to four (or two dual-channel) ESC MCUs. Those MCUs run AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay, and typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. Commutation at 48 kHz needs dedicated advanced-timer complementary PWM with dead-time and BEMF comparators per motor, and one H743 cannot do that for four motors while also running the flight stack. This document models that two-tier architecture.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano board is a burst-only rating, and a short one.** The thermal model in §3 quantifies this. You should not expect it to hold for more than about 1 to 2 seconds.
3. **The parameters below are representative values, not a specific part's datasheet.** Before releasing the design, re-run every equation with the datasheet values of the MOSFET you actually select.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology and current paths

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, so at any instant two phase legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. During (1−D), the low-side FET of the same leg conducts the freewheel current with synchronous rectification.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is fully on for the whole 60° sector.

Neglecting ripple, the motor current flows through exactly **two FET channels at all times**:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$
