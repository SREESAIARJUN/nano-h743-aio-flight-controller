# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Engineering Package

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

In production designs the STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)** running ArduPilot. It does **not** commutate the motors directly. Each motor bridge needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, because:

- There are 24 complementary gate signals with dead-time. The H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 as advanced timers, which gives 2 bridges, not 4.
- Each bridge needs a BEMF comparator and sensorless commutation loop at 48 kHz PWM.

**Adopted architecture:**

| Board | Function |
|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 + IMU + baro + OSD. Outputs 4× bidirectional DShot600. |
| ESC | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32, 4× 3-phase gate drivers, 24× N-MOSFET |

### 0.2 Form-factor reality check

"Nano" (20×20 mm mounting) and "80 A burst per motor" conflict physically. The thermal model in §3 quantifies this:

- A 20×20 board survives roughly **1–2 s** at 4×80 A.
- A 30.5×30.5 board survives roughly **3–5 s**.

I design to 30.5×30.5 and give the 20×20 numbers for comparison.

These are assumptions. Substitute values from your actual MOSFET datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.86 mΩ**.

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the system is coupled. The fixed-point solution is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j) \Rightarrow T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}P_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}P_{25}\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs if R_th · P_25 · α ≥ 1. This is a real failure mode on undersized boards.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° commutation sector:

- Leg X (PWM leg): the high-side conducts for D·T. The low-side conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier.
- Leg Y (return leg): the low-side is held on.

At any instant, exactly two MOSFETs carry the current I. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each FET is active over 120° of the electrical cycle. The loss distributes across all 6 FETs, giving an average per FET of P_cond,motor / 6.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | Per FET (avg) | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.56 W | 61.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.69 mΩ | 21.6 W | 3.60 W | 86.5 W |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | 23.8 W | 3.97 W | 95.2 W |

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect losses (often forgotten)

For each motor:

- Phase trace: 3 mm wide, 6 layers × 2 oz (70 µm) paralleled, 8 mm long.
- R = ρL / A = (1.72e-8 × 8e-3) / (3e-3 × 6 × 70e-6) ≈ **0.11 mΩ** per path segment.
- About 3 segments carry current (input, bridge, output): R_cu ≈ 0.33 mΩ at 25 °C, and about 0.43 mΩ hot.
- P_cu = 80² × 0.43 mΩ ≈ **2.8 W per motor**.

Battery pad and shared bus (4 × 80 = 320 A worst case, but realistically 4 × 80 A peaks are rarely simultaneous):

- For a 0.15 mΩ bus at 320 A: P = **15 W**.
- This sizing drives pad and lead selection: 10 AWG pads, minimum 0.15 mΩ.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. The return leg is static within a sector, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(45\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced high-side turn-on overshoot adds roughly +30% to the turn-on E_on. A conservative overlap figure is therefore about 2.5 W.

### 2.3 C_oss charge/discharge

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

This counts the ½CV² term for each of the 2 FETs per leg.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 200\text{ ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not in the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2\cdot 50\text{n}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

This is per motor (the switching pair). The 12 V gate rail must supply 4 × 4.8 mA ≈ 20 mA average, plus bootstrap refresh.

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor

| Mechanism | Loss |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap incl. Q_rr penalty | 2.50 W |
| Q_rr | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| **FET switching total** | **3.22 W** |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.05 W |

### 2.7 Sensitivity to switching frequency

P_sw scales linearly with frequency: 24 kHz → 1.6 W, 48 kHz → 3.2 W, 96 kHz → 6.4 W.

48 kHz is a good compromise. It reduces current ripple for low-inductance motors (L ≈ 10–20 µH) while keeping P_sw at about 12% of P_cond at 80 A.
