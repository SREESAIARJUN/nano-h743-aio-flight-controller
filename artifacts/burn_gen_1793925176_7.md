## 0. Feasibility Check: Read This First

Three constraints shape everything below.

1. **Total current.** 80 A burst per channel × 4 = **320 A pack current**.
   - XT60 connectors are rated about 60 A continuous. XT90 and AS150 are the realistic options at this level.
   - Battery pads and 2 oz outer copper on a 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry 320 A, even briefly, without major I²R heating and voltage sag.
   - **Interpretation used here:** 80 A is the *per-channel* burst rating (≤5–10 s). Simultaneous 4×80 A is treated as the thermal worst case, not a sustained operating point.
   - The board is assumed to be **30.5×30.5 mm**. The nano 20×20 form factor is not credible for 80 A FETs. See §3.4.

2. **Partitioning.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 (AT32F421 or STM32G071). That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, not the H743.

3. **Burst-limited design.** The adiabatic analysis in §3 shows the board needs a burst time limit and telemetry-based derating. It is not a continuous 80 A design.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

In 120° trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification the current path always passes through **two FETs**: either HS + LS during the on-time, or LS + LS freewheel through the complementary switch during the off-time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)}{6}$$

**Self-consistent junction temperature.** R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}board}$$

Using R_ds,25 max = 1.0 mΩ:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond,ch | P_cond,FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.85 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** | **3.09 W** |

The 125 °C row is the design point.
