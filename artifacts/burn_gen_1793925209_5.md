## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor, Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In six-step commutation, two legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. Each FET position carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. With complementary PWM at duty D, current alternates between the active high-side FET and its synchronous low-side partner. The total conduction resistance stays 2·R_ds regardless of D.

**Per FET:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2}{3}\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 100 °C, R_ds = 1.8 mΩ:

- P_cond,FET = (6400/3)·0.0018 = **3.84 W** (worst-case FET at D → 1)
- P_cond,motor = 2·6400·0.0018 = **23.0 W**

**Electrothermal coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, which depends on P. Solve by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\cdot \frac{I^2}{3} R_{25}\left[1+0.0067\,(T_j^{(k)}-25)\right]$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. Thermal runaway occurs if 0.0067·Z_th·(I²/3)·R₂₅ ≥ 1. That threshold is not reached here: 0.0067·4.2·2133·0.0012 ≈ 0.072.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched FET (one per commutation sector) hard-switches. Its synchronous partner switches at near-zero V_ds after dead time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k = **0.023 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (driver side, per FET) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 50 nC·10·48 k = **0.024 W** |

**Total switching loss per motor ≈ 1.67 W.**

Switching loss is linear in f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 0.83 W, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise. At 48 kHz the conduction terms dominate by about 14:1.

### 2.3 Ancillary Losses (Per Motor)

- Shunt: I²·R_sh = 6400·0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. This assumes the shunt carries full current continuously. That holds for a DC-link shunt; per-leg shunts dissipate less.
- Copper: I²·R_cu = 6400·0.6 mΩ = **3.84 W**. This term is often overlooked; on nano boards it rivals a single FET's loss.

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Scope | Conduction | Switching | Shunt + copper | **Total** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 23.0 W | 1.67 W | 5.44 W | **≈ 30 W** |
| Four motors (F3 case, unrealistic) | 92 W | 6.7 W | 21.8 W | **≈ 121 W** |
| Two motors at 80 A + two at 40 A | — | — | — | **≈ 75 W** |

Efficiency check for one motor: output power ≈ 25.2·80·D. At D ≈ 0.9, output ≈ 1.8 kW, so ESC efficiency ≈ 98.3%. The losses are small relative to throughput but large relative to the board's thermal mass.
