## 0. Architectural Decisions and Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller only. Each of the 4 ESC channels runs its own MCU (AT32F421 / AM32 firmware, or EFM8 / BLHeli_S) and drives 6 N-channel MOSFETs via a 3-phase gate driver.

- The 48 kHz switching frequency is generated by the **ESC MCUs**, not the H743.
- The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.

**Form factor.** "Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot thermally support 4 × 80 A. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers**. Even then, 80 A per channel is a **≤1–2 s transient** rating with prop-wash airflow (proven in §4).

**Battery reality check.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S is about 8 kW. Few packs or XT60/XT90 connectors survive this. Specify 80 A as **per-channel burst** and roughly 45–55 A continuous per channel.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

At any commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D; the low-side (LS) FET of the same leg conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on 100 % of the sector.

Summing the conduction contributions:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[\,D + (1-D)\,] + I^2 R_{ds}\cdot 1 = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the key property of synchronous rectification.

### 2.2 At 80 A burst

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

- **Per FET (average over an electrical revolution):** 24.6 / 6 ≈ **4.1 W**. Each FET carries current for 120° electrical.
- **Thermal averaging:** the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms) is longer than the electrical period above roughly 5 k eRPM, so averaging over the revolution is valid.

### 2.3 Copper and shunt losses (per channel)

| Path | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase copper, 2 oz, 4 parallel layers, ~8 mm path | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Bus copper share | ~0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |
| Current shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

These add **≈ 2.6–4.2 W per channel**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Use V = 25.2 V (worst case) and I = 80 A. Only the PWM leg switches.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$
P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 30\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{n}(10)(48\text{k})(2) = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$

**Switching total ≈ 2.6 W per channel.**

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A, in exchange for lower current ripple and quieter motors.

- **Dead time is the largest optimization lever after edge rate.** Trimming t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W.
- **Edge rate can only go so far.** Faster edges increase ringing. Verify V_DS overshoot stays below 32 V, i.e. 80 % of the 40 V rating.

## 4. Thermal Calculations

### 4.1 Loss budget per channel at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.6 |
| Switching | 2.6 |
| Copper and shunt | 3.0 (mid estimate) |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 30 W** |
| **All 4 channels** | **≈ 120 W** |

**Efficiency:** at 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 2 kW per channel input, the loss is about 1.5 %.

### 4.2 Junction rise above board (steady-state component)

Per FET (4.1 W conduction plus switching share ≈ 4.5 W worst-case HS FET):

$$
\Delta T_{JC} = 4.5 \times 1.5 = \mathbf{6.8\ K}
$$

Junction-to-board rise is small, so **the board thermal mass is the limiting factor**.

### 4.3 Board lumped thermal capacitance (30.5 mm class, 36×36 mm PCB)

| Material | Volume | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (8 layers × 70 µm, ~85 % fill) | 0.62 cm³ | 5.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 2.1 J/K |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm) | 1.9 cm³ | 3.6 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 4.0 J/K |
| FETs, caps, connectors | — | ~3 g | ~0.7 J/g·K | 2.1 J/K |
| **Total** | | | | **C_th ≈ 8.2 J/K** |

### 4.4 Board-to-air resistance

- Convection: h ≈ 100 W/m²K under prop wash. Over 2 × 13 cm² of area, R_θBA = 1/(h·A) ≈ **3.8 K/W**.
- Time constant: τ = R·C = 3.8 × 8.2 ≈ **31 s**.

### 4.5 Transient (first-order Foster model)

$$
T_{board}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad
T_j = T_{board} + \Delta T_{JC}
$$

**Limit:** T_j,max = 125 °C (design margin; parts are rated 150 °C), T_a = 35 °C, so the allowable board rise is 125 − 35 − 6.8 ≈ 83 K.
