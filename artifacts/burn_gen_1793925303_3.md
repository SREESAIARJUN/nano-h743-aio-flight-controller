# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #7)

**Document scope:** This package covers the 80 A burst loss model, the 48 kHz switching loss model, the thermal model, the ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and the reference netlists.

**Status:** Engineering baseline. Values marked **[A]** are assumptions. You must replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually select before layout release.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Several facts determine the structure of this model:

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor (or a shared dual-motor MCU) has its own ESC MCU. This is typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That ESC MCU runs the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only emits DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The switching-loss model therefore belongs to the ESC firmware domain, and the DMA model belongs to the FC domain.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per motor.** With 4 motors at 80 A, battery current reaches 320 A. Three consequences follow:
   - An XT60 (60 A continuous) and a standard nano pigtail cannot carry this. You need an XT90 or direct-soldered 10 AWG, or you must reinterpret 80 A as a **total** burst.
   - A 20×20 mm "nano" form factor cannot dissipate the resulting losses for more than a few seconds (see §3).
   - **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 8-layer 2 oz copper, and rate it at 80 A per motor for ≤ 5 s and roughly 40 A per motor continuous. If the 20×20 mm form factor is mandatory, derate to about 35 A burst per motor.
3. **Commutation scheme assumed:** six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. At any instant, two half-bridges carry phase current and one of them is PWM-switching.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{(T_j-25)}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.0045¹⁰⁰ ≈ **1.57 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

During each 60° sector, current I flows through:

- **Leg A** (PWM leg): the high-side FET for duty D, and the low-side FET (synchronous) for 1 − D. Leg loss = I²R[D + (1 − D)] = **I²R**.
- **Leg B** (static low-side): **I²R**.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.57\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.1\ W}$$

Note that the result is independent of duty cycle, which is a property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is P_cond/6 ≈ **3.35 W** (assuming one FET per switch).

If two FETs are paralleled per switch, R_eff halves, giving P_cond ≈ 10 W per motor and 0.84 W per FET.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, but significant at 80 A)

| Element | Resistance | Loss at burst |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace, per phase | 0.3 mΩ **[A]** | 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ = 3.84 W per motor |
| Bus plane, total current 320 A | 0.15 mΩ | 320² × 0.15 mΩ = 15.4 W |
| Current shunt in battery return | 0.2 mΩ | 320² × 0.2 mΩ = 20.5 W |

The shunt figure is **unacceptable**. Use a 0.1 mΩ, 4-terminal shunt (10.2 W), or switch to a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS772 or MCS1802-class) or a lower-value metal-strip shunt.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. Its high-side FET hard-switches, and its low-side FET sees dead-time diode conduction plus reverse recovery.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2\,t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$
