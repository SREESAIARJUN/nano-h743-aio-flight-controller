# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

A few assumptions shape the whole document:

- **The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC), not the ESC MCU.** It runs ArduPilot and drives an integrated or stacked 4-in-1 ESC. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU. At 48 kHz PWM this is typically an EFM8BB21/BB51 running Bluejay, or an AT32F421 running AM32. The FC sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. Nobody runs commutation for four motors on the H743.
- **"80A burst" means 80A per motor phase.** Bus voltage is 6S (25.2 V full charge).
- **MOSFET parameters are representative** for a 40 V device in a 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, the only package that fits a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values. The equations are general; the numbers are illustrative.
- **The board does not sustain 80A continuously.** Section 3 shows 80A is a roughly 1–1.5 s transient on a nano board. Any "80A/10s" rating on a board this size should be treated as marketing until proven on a thermocouple rig.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, current flows through one high-side (HS) FET of leg A and one low-side (LS) FET of leg B.
- During PWM off-time, current freewheels through the LS FET of leg A, which is enhanced, not via its body diode.
- Averaged over a PWM cycle, the motor current always sees **two R_DS(on) in series**.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\text{–}0.008\ \text{K}^{-1}$$
