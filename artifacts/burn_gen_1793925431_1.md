# Job #19: STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC

## Engineering Design Package, Rev A (Architectural Baseline)

> **Scope note:** On a 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 does not commutate the MOSFETs directly. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 at 48 kHz PWM and drive the gate drivers and bridges. All math below follows that partition.

### 2.2 Commutation Topology

AM32 uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant:

- one high-side FET is PWM-switching at duty D;
- its complementary low-side FET in the same leg conducts during (1 − D);
- one low-side FET in another leg is held fully ON.

So, at any instant, **two FET-equivalents carry I_ph continuously**. Battery current relates to phase current by I_bat ≈ D · I_ph. The 80 A burst figure is therefore defined as **phase current**, the worst case for silicon.

### 2.3 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Leg conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,leg} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Motor conduction loss (switching leg plus static low-side):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Self-consistent solution.** R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Iterating with the thermal model in §3 converges at T_j ≈ 115 °C:

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P per FET (W) | P_cond,motor (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 0.700 | 4.48 | 8.96 |
| 100 | 1.015 | 6.50 | 13.0 |
| **115** | **1.078** | **6.90** | **13.8** |
| 125 | 1.120 | 7.17 | 14.3 |

### 2.4 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side commutates near-ZVS, with its body diode conducting during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

(Recovery current also adds to turn-on overlap. Budget a ×1.3 margin on P_VI if Q_rr is above 60 nC.)

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive, dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g:**

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

| Loss component | Per motor (W) |

| Conduction (2 FET-eq.) | 13.80 |
| V–I switching | 1.69 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 |

| PCB copper (see 2.6) | 3.2 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 19.4** |
| **All 4 motors simultaneous burst** | **≈ 78 W** |

**Hottest single FET:** the PWM'd high side, at 6.9 + 1.69 + 0.05 ≈ **8.6 W**.

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching-related loss scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | Switching-related loss per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.2 W |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 2.4 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 4.8 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (about 85 %). Choosing 48 kHz is justified by the gain in current-ripple and acoustic smoothness relative to the switching penalty.

### 2.6 PCB Copper Loss

Assumptions:
- 2 oz (70 µm) copper, ρ_Cu = 1.72 µΩ·cm at 20 °C, rising to about 2.3 µΩ·cm at 105 °C.
- Sheet resistance R_□ = ρ/t ≈ 0.33 mΩ/□ at 105 °C.
- Phase and bus path ≈ 3 squares, paralleled on 4 power layers, so R ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path.
- Two current paths per motor (bus in and phase return).

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This is why **8-layer, 2 oz on all layers**, with at least 4 layers stitched in power regions, is mandatory.
