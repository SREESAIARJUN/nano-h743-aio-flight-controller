# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Design Package Rev A

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Before any numbers, three points shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production ESC, each power stage is run by its own ESC MCU executing the 48 kHz commutation loop. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU.
   - This design uses 4× AT32F421 running AM32. A single STM32G071 managing all four channels is also viable.
   - Running motor commutation on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot and is not recommended.
2. **"Nano" with 80 A burst is thermally marginal.** I assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board (8-layer, 2 oz). On a 20 × 20 mm board, the numbers below roughly halve the allowable burst duration.
3. **Supporting data is needed before layout freeze.**
   - All MOSFET parameters are representative 40 V PDFN3333-class values. They must be replaced with datasheet values for the selected part.
   - The netlist is a reference topology, not a fabrication-verified netlist. It needs ERC/DRC and bring-up validation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation, two FETs carry the phase current at any instant:
- the high-side FET of the sourcing phase, and
- the low-side FET of the sinking phase (or its synchronous complement during the PWM off-time).

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With synchronous rectification, current flows through either the switching FET or its complement for the whole PWM period, so the loss is independent of duty cycle. Each FET conducts for 120° electrical. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{P_{cond}}{3}$$

This is spread over 6 FETs, with two conducting at a time.

### Conduction Loss vs Junction Temperature (80 A)

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| 85 °C | 1.52 mΩ | 19.5 W | 78.0 W |
| 110 °C | 1.66 mΩ | 21.2 W | 84.9 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 89.1 W |

### Thermal Runaway Check

Runaway requires the loss slope to exceed the cooling slope:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\alpha$$

At 80 A this is 0.069 W/K per channel. Stability requires:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} \cdot R_{th,total} < 1$$

This holds (stable) as long as R_th < 14 K/W per channel. Section 3 shows the design is stable but heat-capacity-limited.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one FET hard-switches per PWM edge pair: the PWM'd high side. Its complement switches at near-zero voltage after dead time.

### (a) V–I Overlap Loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### (b) Reverse Recovery of the Complementary Body Diode

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 30\,\text{n} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

The diode-side loss adds roughly 0.01 W more.

### (c) Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 0.042\ \text{W}$$

### (d) Dead-Time Body Diode Conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### (e) Gate Drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 40\,\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### Per-Channel Loss Budget (80 A, T_j = 110 °C)

| Mechanism | Loss |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 21.2 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.13 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side sensed) | 1.6 W (avoid; see §5) |
| **Total (no shunt)** | **≈ 23.4 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 94 W** |

**Context:** the mechanical output is about 25.2 V × 80 A × D. Near full throttle this is roughly 1.9 kW per motor, so efficiency is about 98.8%. The total loss is small in percentage terms but enormous for a 9 cm² board.

### Trade-offs

**Effect of switching frequency.** Switching-related losses are about 2.1 W of the 23.4 W. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel but raises current ripple and acoustic noise.

**Dead-time sensitivity.** Dead-time loss is the most frequency-sensitive term. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns (AM32 setting, verified with a scope against shoot-through) saves about 0.3 W per channel.
