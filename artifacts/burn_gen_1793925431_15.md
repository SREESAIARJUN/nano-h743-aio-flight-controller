# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Constraints

Two conditions must be accepted before the numbers mean anything.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the ESC side runs its own MCU per channel or per pair: STM32G071, AT32F421, or EFM8BB51, running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. This document models both halves:
   - the FC timer/DMA plan, and
   - the ESC power stage switching at 48 kHz.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 quantifies this. At 80 A per channel the board absorbs roughly 120 W, which it can tolerate for about 1 s. If "80 A" means total across all four motors (20 A per motor), the design is comfortable. I model both cases.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, phase current flows through **exactly two FET positions at every instant**:

- one leg is continuously ON (high or low side);
- the PWM leg alternates between its high-side switch and its synchronous low-side switch.

Therefore conduction loss is independent of duty cycle (to first order):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.2 Conduction loss at 80 A (electro-thermal fixed point)

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, iterate:

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_DS(on) | P_cond (per motor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W |
| 1 | 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W |
| 2 | 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** |

Design value: **P_cond ≈ 28 W per motor at T_j = 125 °C.**

Copper and shunt losses add to this:
- **PCB phase copper:** about 0.3 mΩ (2 oz, short pours) per phase, two phases in path. That gives 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W**.
- **Current-sense shunt** (on DC bus, 0.5 mΩ): bus current is I_bus = D·I_ph. At D = 0.8, I_bus = 64 A, giving **2.0 W**.

These are often omitted, but on a 20×20 board they are around 20% of the budget.

### 2.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz (hard-switched edge)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches; one FET turns on and one turns off per edge.

**Transition times from gate drive:**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{src}} \cdot k_{plateau},\qquad t_r \approx \frac{9\,nC}{0.6\,A} \approx 15\,ns,\qquad t_f \approx \frac{9\,nC}{1.0\,A} \approx 9\,ns$$

The source current is derated to 0.6 A at the Miller plateau because of R_G and driver headroom. The sink current is derated to 1.0 A.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 24\,ns \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,ns \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the low-side diode is recovered by the high-side turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\,nC \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Reverse recovery also injects additional turn-on current, I_rr·V·t_rr. With t_rr ≈ 20 ns, this adds **+0.15 W**.

**C_oss charge/discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 40\,nC \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs; dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{DRV}\,f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 45\,nC \cdot 12 \cdot 48\,kHz = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$
