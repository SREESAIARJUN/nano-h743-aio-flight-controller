### Critical findings to communicate up front

1. **80A per channel is a burst rating only.** On a 30×30 board, steady-state convective cooling supports roughly 25–35A continuous per channel. With all four channels at 80A, the junction thermal limit is reached in about 2–8 seconds, depending on heatsinking (Section 3).
2. **A true 20×20 nano form factor cannot be rated 80A burst** with any honest thermal model. If the product requirement is truly 20×20, the realistic burst rating is about 35–45A.
3. **The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in ESC firmware, not by the H743.** The H743 timer/DMA work concerns only the DShot600 link.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active:
- One leg is PWM-switching (high side and low side alternate, complementary).
- One low side is held fully ON.

The current I therefore always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

Temperature dependence:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution, for hotspot analysis

For the switching leg at duty D:

$$
P_{HS} = D\, I^2 R_{DS},\qquad P_{LS,sync} = (1-D)\, I^2 R_{DS}
$$

The held-on low side carries I²R_DS for its full 120° electrical interval. Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the time. So the average per-FET loss is:

$$
\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is I²R_DS. This matters at low eRPM, where the 120° interval exceeds the FET's thermal time constant (about 1–10 ms for a DFN5×6 junction-to-case).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | Avg per FET | Peak per FET |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 4.27 W | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 6.4 W | 19.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.67 mΩ | 21.4 W | 7.1 W | 21.4 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction (often forgotten)

| Element | R (estimate) | Loss @ 80A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Phase trace, 2 oz × 6 layers, ~8 mm | ~0.15 mΩ | ~1.0 W |
| Battery pad → DC bus (shared, 320A total) | ~0.05 mΩ | 5.1 W total (at 320A) |
| Solder joints / pads | ~0.05 mΩ | 0.3 W |

The DC bus is the overlooked killer. With 4 × 80A = 320A, every 0.1 mΩ costs about 10 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel, at V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80A:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{DT} = V_F\, I\, (2\, t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The recovery charge loss lands mostly in the opposing FET:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I. Expect 2–3× this value at 80A with fast edges, about 0.1–0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}
$$
