## 0. Scope and Architecture Constraints

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any ArduPilot-compatible design, the system splits into two parts:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control. Sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| 4-in-1 ESC | 4× STM32G071GBU6 (or AT32F421) | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time control. |

The 48 kHz switching loss model therefore applies to the ESC MCUs and gate drivers. The DMA/timer model applies to the H743.

### 0.2 Form-Factor Reality Check

80 A burst per motor (320 A aggregate) is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" stack. The governing constraints are:

- **Package size.** 12 MOSFETs at roughly 1 mΩ need 5×6 mm PDFN packages. 3×3 mm parts have 2–3× the R_DS(on) and about 3× worse R_θJC.
- **Copper area.** Reaching about 0.5 mΩ per phase path needs 8 layers of 2 oz copper.
- **Thermal mass.** The burst duration is limited by board heat capacity. Section 2.4 quantifies this.

This document assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board with 5×6 mm FETs**. The "nano" label applies to the FC only. If 20×20 is mandatory, see Section 2.6 for the de-rated numbers.

- **1/3 in the "high" sector:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1 − D).
