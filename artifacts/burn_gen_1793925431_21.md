# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Engineering Model

**Design status: preliminary, not fabrication-ready.** Every number below follows from stated assumptions. Before release, re-run the model with the selected MOSFET datasheets, the real stackup, and thermal-camera validation. Where the requirement itself is physically marginal, I say so.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The H743 does not commutate the motors. Like every production AIO, this design splits the work:

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz, rev V) running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs.
- **ESC MCUs:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each one performs commutation and 48 kHz PWM.
- **Gate drivers:** 4× FD6288Q three-phase half-bridge drivers.
- **Power stage:** 12 half-bridges, 24 MOSFETs (one per position as baseline). A 48-FET parallel variant is evaluated in §2.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A per ESC channel |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), center-aligned |
| MOSFET (generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN) | R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, α = 0.005 K⁻¹, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| Gate drive | V_GS = 10 V (driver bootstrap from 12 V LDO), t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm (36×36 outline), 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers |

### ⚠ Feasibility flag (read first)

Four motors at 80 A each is **320 A**, about 7.7 kW from the pack. On a 36 mm board this is only survivable as a **short, non-simultaneous burst**:

- XT60 connectors are rated about 60 A continuous.
- 12 AWG battery leads are about 100 A for seconds.

The model below quantifies how short the burst must be.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

Six-step trapezoidal commutation keeps two phases active at all times. With complementary PWM, the current flows through exactly:

- one high-side or low-side switch on the PWM leg (alternating between the two), and
- one continuously-on low-side switch on the return leg.

So at any instant **two R_DS(on) are in series** with the phase current, regardless of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

Temperature dependence of R_DS(on):

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

Assume a copper path (pads, planes, vias, phase pad) of R_Cu ≈ 0.30 mΩ.

### 1.2 Results at 80 A (I² = 6400 A²)

| Topology | P_FET (T_j = 125 °C) | P_Cu | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 FET / position | 6400 × 2 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** | 1.92 W | **21.1 W** |
| 2 FET / position (parallel) | 6400 × 2 × 0.75 mΩ = **9.6 W** | 1.92 W | **11.5 W** |

Per-device worst case (single FET on the continuously-on return leg): 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W in one 3.3×3.3 package**. This is the hotspot that limits the design.

### 1.3 Electrothermal self-consistency

Conduction loss raises junction temperature, which raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. With local thermal resistance R_θ (junction to local ambient):

$$T_j = T_a + R_\theta I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{25}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$\alpha R_\theta I^2 R_{25} \geq 1$$

For a single FET at 80 A: α I² R₂₅ = 0.005 × 6400 × 0.001 = 0.032 K/W⁻¹. Runaway therefore occurs at **R_θ ≥ 31 K/W**.

A 3.3×3.3 FET on a dense 36 mm board in still air has steady-state R_θJA of roughly 25–40 K/W. **80 A continuous is therefore unstable without airflow.** 80 A works only as a burst, which §4 sizes.
