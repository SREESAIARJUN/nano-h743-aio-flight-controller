# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Reference Architecture, Loss Model, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

**Revision:** A (preliminary). **Status:** Engineering reference. All component values must be verified against current datasheets before layout freeze.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Red Flags

Three issues need to be settled before the math, because they change it.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts running AM32, or EFM8/AT32 parts running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection, and gate drive all live on the ESC side. This document models both sides.

2. **"80 A burst" means phase current per channel, not battery current.**
   - Battery current per channel is approximately I_phase × duty.
   - At 100 % duty on 6S, 80 A per channel is about 2 kW per motor and 8 kW total. That is an X-class or long-range build, not a "nano."

3. **Form-factor conflict.**
   - A 20×20 mm stack with 3.3×3.3 mm FETs cannot sustain 80 A beyond roughly 1 s. The thermal mass is not there.
   - This design therefore assumes the following:

| Parameter | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm mounting, about 45×45 mm board |
| Copper | 8-layer, 2 oz |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, 5×6 mm PQFN |
| Mode | Burst only (≤ 5 s), not continuous |

If you need a true 20×20 nano, derate to about 45 A burst. Section 3.6 shows why.

## 3. Mathematical Loss Model

### 3.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous complementary PWM (AM32 default).

In each 60° sector:
- One high-side FET (HS_a) is PWM-switched.
- Its complementary low-side FET (LS_a) synchronously rectifies during the off-time.
- One low-side FET (LS_b) is held fully on.

Current I flows continuously through the motor, assuming the inductor ripple is small.

### 3.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | Multiplier | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 °C | 1.49 | 2.08 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.65 | 2.31 mΩ |

### 3.3 Conduction Loss

For each 60° sector:

$$P_{HS_a} = D\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j),\quad P_{LS_a} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j),\quad P_{LS_b} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The duty cycle cancels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + 2\,I^2 R_{cu}$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:
- FET conduction: 2 × 6400 × 2.31 mΩ = **29.6 W**
- Copper: 2 × 6400 × 0.6 mΩ = **7.7 W** (dissipated in the board, not the FETs)

**Per-FET peak:**
- Each FET is fully-on (the LS_b role) for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.
- It is the PWM leg for another 1/3, at duty D or 1−D.

Time-averaged at D ≈ 1 (full-throttle burst):

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{2}{3}(6400)(2.31\text{ mΩ}) = 9.9\text{ W}$$

The instantaneous peak in a sector is I²R = 14.8 W. This matters only at very low eRPM, where sector dwell becomes comparable to the FET's thermal time constant.

### 3.4 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per sector.

**(a) Hard-switching V-I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)**

Plateau voltage V_pl ≈ 3.5 V, driver R_on ≈ 6 Ω (ESC MCU-side gate resistor R_g = 4.7 Ω included):

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{9\text{nC}\times 10.7}{6.5} \approx 15\text{ ns},\quad t_f \approx \frac{9\text{nC}\times 7.9}{3.5} \approx 20\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.69\text{ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** of the LS body diode, if dead time lets it conduct:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\text{ W}$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales up roughly √(di/dt). Use **0.15 W** for margin.
