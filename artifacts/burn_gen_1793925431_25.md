# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #45): Engineering Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | Design at 25.2 V for worst-case switching |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel, 10 s | Continuous rating derived below |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz (ESC MCU) | Synchronous rectification (complementary) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class) | FOC changes the conduction model (see §1.4) |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, ~36 × 36 mm PCB, 8-layer 2 oz | 80 A/ch on a 20 × 20 board is not thermally credible (§3.4) |
| MOSFET (reference) | 40 V N-ch, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V, PDFN 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 class | 2 in parallel per switch position |
| Ambient | 40 °C worst case | |

**Architecture correction:** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does **not** drive the bridges directly. Each motor channel (or the whole board) has its own ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC MCU, and the H743 timer/DMA allocation concerns only DShot. The model below keeps these domains separate.

Every MOSFET, driver and capacitor value is a **reference assumption**. Substitute datasheet values for your selected parts and re-run the equations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This α matches the typical normalized curve of roughly 1.6× at 125 °C.

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.36\ \text{m}\Omega$$

With 2 FETs in parallel per switch position:

$$R_{sw} = R_{DS}/2 = 0.68\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step current path

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly one high-side and one low-side switch position. With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg alternates between its HS FET and its complementary LS FET, so one switch-equivalent conducts per leg at all times:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{sw}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.68\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{8.70\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 positions conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. The average loss per position is P_cond/6 = 1.45 W, which gives **≈0.73 W per FET**.

Peak instantaneous loss per FET while conducting is (80²·1.36 mΩ)/4 = 2.18 W. At high eRPM this is thermally averaged, because the electrical period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant.

### 1.4 Additional series resistances (must be included)

| Element | R (mΩ) | Loss per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase-pad + trace (2 oz, inner+outer paralleled) | ~0.4 | 2.56 W |
| Battery input planes (shared, 320 A total) | ~0.15 | 15.4 W total (all 4 channels) |
| Current shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 | 1.6 W |

The power-input path is the **largest single loss** on the board at 4 × 80 A. Use the widest planes possible, multiple layers in parallel, and solder-on pads for the battery lead.

*FOC note:* With sinusoidal drive, I_rms per phase = I_pk/√2. Total conduction becomes 3·I_rms²·R_sw, which equals 1.5·I_pk²·R_sw. At equal torque, the resulting loss is comparable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its HS and LS FET pair commutates hard once per PWM edge pair.

### 2.1 Turn-on and turn-off overlap (V–I crossover)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

The assumed rise and fall times are t_r = 15 ns and t_f = 12 ns, giving 27 ns combined. These assume a driver with ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink, R_g = 2–3 Ω, and 2 paralleled FETs.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (often underestimated)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
