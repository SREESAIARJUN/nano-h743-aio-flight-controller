## 0. Architecture Decisions and Scope

These points affect everything downstream, so they come first.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the motor controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor is commutated by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Driving 12 half-bridges directly from the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot and would compromise the flight loop. The ESC MCU used here is the STM32G071 (an AT32F421 is a drop-in alternative under AM32).
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor channel for ≤ 2 s.** All four channels at 80 A simultaneously means 320 A from the battery, about 8 kW at 6S. No XT60 or XT90, and no typical nano-class pack, will deliver that. The thermal model below shows the PCB cannot absorb it for long either. The spec should be stated as **"80 A single-channel burst / 45–55 A per channel continuous with airflow."**
3. **Nano form factor assumed:** 25.5 × 25.5 mm (20 × 20 mm mount), 8-layer stack, 2 oz copper on inner power layers. An FC/ESC stack is assumed, so the H743 board is thermally decoupled from the ESC board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

AM32 and BLHeli_32 use six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector:

- The **PWM leg** switches: its high-side FET (HS) conducts for duty D, and its low-side FET (LS) conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- The **return leg** has its LS FET fully on.
