# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two issues affect everything that follows.

**1. Partitioning.** ArduPilot running on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated ESC MCU per channel running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs, and they generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model therefore has two separate domains:

- **H743 (ArduPilot):** DShot600 transmit, eRPM telemetry capture, DMA.
- **ESC MCU ×4:** 48 kHz PWM, gate drive, power stage.

**2. 80 A burst on a "nano" form factor.** The numbers below show roughly 85 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. A 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board cannot sustain that. It is viable only as a **time-limited burst of a few seconds**, with firmware current limiting and thermal derating. I quantify that limit in Section 3.

**Component parameters.** All MOSFET and driver values are representative placeholders for a 40 V, roughly 1 mΩ, 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 class device. Replace them with the datasheet values of your selected part before release.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Assumptions

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current (peak of the trapezoid, treated as DC over the conduction interval) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | Complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) |
| R_ds,25 | 1.0 mΩ | At V_gs = 10 V |
| α | 0.006 /K | Temperature coefficient of R_ds |
| t_r + t_f | 20 ns | Effective V-I overlap |
| Q_g | 60 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| C_oss,er | 1.5 nF | Energy-related output capacitance |
| t_dead | 100 ns | Per edge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |

### 1.2 Conduction loss

With six-step commutation and synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series: one high-side and one low-side, either the active pair or the high-side plus the freewheeling synchronous low-side.

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 110 °C, R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = 1.51 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.51\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about 19.3 / 6 = **3.2 W**.

**Thermal runaway check.** Feedback is stable if

$$\frac{dP}{dT} \cdot R_{th} < 1$$

Here dP/dT = 2 × I² × R_ds,25 × α = 0.077 W/K. With an effective R_th of about 5 K/W, the loop gain is 0.38. That is stable but significant: it adds about 60% on top of the first-order estimate.

### 1.3 Switching losses (hard-switched leg only)

**V-I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} (2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors; 2 FETs switching):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-channel and board totals

| Loss | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (110 °C) | 19.3 W | 77.2 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total semiconductor** | **≈21.0 W** | **≈84 W** |

**Board copper and shunt losses (often ignored, and critical here):**

- **Battery bus:** I_bat ≈ D × ΣI. At D ≈ 0.85, that is about 270 A.
  - 2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of about 0.25 mΩ/sq.
  - A 3-square power path dissipates I² × R ≈ **55 W**.
  - Mitigation: heavy copper on 4 internal planes in parallel (effective ~0.06 mΩ/sq), and battery pads placed close to the FETs.
- **Shunts:** do **not** use a single 0.25 mΩ total-current shunt. At 270 A it would dissipate 18 W.
  - Use per-channel low-side 0.5 mΩ shunts in the 2512 metal-strip class.
  - The average current per channel at 80 A burst is about 68 A, so each dissipates about 2.3 W.
  - Alternatively, use phase-current-free sensing such as a Hall sensor (ACS-class or TMCS1100-class).

### 1.5 Optimal switching frequency check

Conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 20:1. Reducing f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel. **The design lever is R_ds,on, not f_sw.**

48 kHz is justified for:
- current ripple on low-inductance motors, where ΔI = V × D(1−D) / (L × f);
- acoustics.

## 2. Input Capacitor Model

Input ripple current per channel:

$$I_{C,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at D = 0.5, giving **40 A_rms per channel**.

Required bulk capacitance for ΔV_bus ≤ 0.5 V:

$$C \ge \frac{I_{ph} D(1-D)}{f_{sw}\,\Delta V} = \frac{80\times0.25}{48\text{k}\times0.5} = 833\ \mu\text{F}$$

**Recommendations:**

- **Per channel:** 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 for high-frequency decoupling (≈4 µF effective after DC-bias derating).
- **Board level:** pads for an external low-ESR 1000 µF/35 V or larger capacitor, mandatory and wired with short leads.
- **Optional:** 2× 100 µF
