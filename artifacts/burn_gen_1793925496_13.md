## 0. Architecture Assumption

An H743 does not commutate the motors itself in a production 4-in-1 design. The assumed topology is:

```
[STM32H743 FC, ArduPilot] --BDShot600 x4--> [4x ESC MCU (AM32/BLHeli_32 class)]
                                              --> [3-phase gate driver] --> [6x N-FET] --> motor
```

- PWM frequency: f_sw = 48 kHz.
- Commutation: trapezoidal six-step with complementary (synchronous) PWM.
- Bus voltage: 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in Six-Step Synchronous PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, phase current I flows through exactly two FETs at every instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is on continuously.
- **Third leg:** floating.

The conduction loss per motor is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Over one electrical cycle, the loss is shared approximately evenly across the six FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ /\text{K}$$

This creates a positive feedback loop: higher temperature raises resistance, which raises loss. Solve it by iteration (see §3.4).

⚠ **Example values:** R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ, α = 0.005, T_j = 145 °C. This gives R = 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numerical Result at an 80 A Burst

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Shunt and Copper Losses

- **Phase current vs. battery current:** the battery current per motor is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph.
- **Shunt loss** (low-side or bus shunt R_sh):

$$P_{sh} = I_{ph}^2 R_{sh}\,D_{eff}$$

  With ⚠ R_sh = 0.25 mΩ at D = 1, this gives 1.6 W per motor.
- **Copper trace/plane loss:** P = I²·ρL/(wt). For 2 oz copper, ρ/t ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq.
  - A 10-square phase path carries 2.5 mΩ, which costs **16 W at 80 A**.
  - Phase routing must therefore use stacked multi-layer pours with via arrays, not traces.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg hard-switches per PWM period. Each loss component:

| Component | Equation | ⚠ Assumed parameters | Per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, t_r+t_f = 40 ns | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 200 ns | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | Q_rr = 50 nC | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | Q_oss = 30 nC | 0.04 W |
| Gate drive (lost in driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | Q_g = 50 nC, 10 V | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 3.3 W** |

The rise and fall times can be estimated from the gate-driver strength:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

**Design trade-off:**

- Dead-time loss is about 40% of the switching loss. Minimize t_dt, but only to the point where shoot-through margin is still proven on the bench.
- Doubling f_sw doubles every term in the table above. That is why 48 kHz, rather than 96 kHz, is the right choice at 80 A.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total Dissipation at an 80 A Burst (All Four Motors)

$$P_{tot} = 4 \times (24.6 + 3.3 + 1.6) \approx \mathbf{118\ W}$$

### 3.2 Thermal Network

```
Tj --Rθjc(per FET)-- Tcase --Rθ(solder+vias)-- Tboard [Cth_board] --Rθ(conv)-- Tamb
```

### 3.3 Junction-to-Board Rise

- Average loss per FET: 29.5 W / 6 ≈ 4.9 W.
- With ⚠ Rθjc ≈ 1.5 K/W and Rθ(vias) ≈ 2 K/W, the junction sits about **17 K above the board**.

The die thermal time constant (milliseconds) is much longer than the electrical period at flight RPM. Averaging the loss is therefore valid.
