# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 Nano FC/ESC (Job #55): Power-Stage, Thermal & BDShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility

An "80 A burst" rating on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is very aggressive. The math below shows where that rating breaks down, so every result carries its assumptions explicitly.

The STM32H743 does not drive MOSFETs directly. The industry-standard and recommended topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──BDShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q 3-ph gate driver ──► 24× N-MOSFET
```

**Baseline design parameters.** These are representative values. Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually select.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, exactly one high-side or low-side switch in the PWM'd leg plus one low-side switch in the return leg carry the full phase current at every instant. That means **two FETs are always in series with I_ph**.

During the high-side ON portion (duty D), the current flows through HS(A) and LS(B). During OFF (1−D), it flows through LS(A), via sync rectification, and LS(B).

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on): fixed-point solution
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$
$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JC}$$

Substituting and solving for the per-FET average power (each of the 6 FETs carries current 2/6 of the electrical period on average):

$$P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_b - 25)]/6}{1 - \tfrac{2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{\theta JC}}{6}}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I_ph = 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 17.9 W | 71.7 W |
| 100 °C | 1.93 mΩ | 24.6 W | 98.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.10 mΩ | **26.9 W** | **107.5 W** |

**Copper loss:** P_cu = I² · R_cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per motor**. Add **≈0.4 W** for the shunt/bus path when a 0.25 mΩ current shunt is carrying the share of battery current.
