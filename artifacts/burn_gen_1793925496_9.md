# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Loss, and DShot/DMA Design Model

This document makes stated, labeled assumptions wherever the brief leaves something open. Every part number is a representative candidate. Verify each one against the vendor datasheet before layout release.

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points need to be settled before the math, because they change the model.

1. **ArduPilot on the H743 does not commutate motors.**
   - The H743 runs ArduCopter and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This is the MCU that generates the 48 kHz PWM and drives the gate drivers.
   - The 48 kHz switching model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage, not to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" is read as 80 A per motor channel, transient.**
   - On a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), that current is thermally possible only for about 1–3 s.
   - The model below quantifies this. A realistic continuous rating is about 20–25 A per channel.
   - If "80 A" means total (4 × 20 A), every number below gets much easier.
3. **Bus voltage assumed: 6S.**
   - V_bus,max = 25.2 V, nominal 22.2 V.
   - The FETs are rated 40 V.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-Step with Synchronous (Complementary) PWM

- At any instant, two legs are active: one PWM leg and one return leg whose low side is held on.
- With complementary PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs, whatever the duty cycle. Dead time is neglected here.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond}(80\,\text{A}) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.18\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

- Each of the six FETs carries current for about 1/3 of an electrical cycle.
- Per-FET average: P ≈ 27.9 / 6 × 2 ≈ **4.65 W**.
- Instantaneous dissipation while conducting is 13.9 W.
- Commutation periods are well under 1 ms at flying eRPM, which is far below the die thermal time constant. The average is therefore the valid basis for junction rise.

### 1.4 Copper and Interconnect

- Assume 0.5 mΩ effective path resistance per motor: pads, vias, and battery-pad spreading in 2 oz copper.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.5 Optional FOC/Sinusoidal Comparison

$$P_{cond} = 3\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS} = 3\,(I_{pk}/\sqrt2)^2 R_{DS}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. It has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per cycle.

**Transition times:**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 12\,\text{ns}$$

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(25\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (two FETs per active leg pair):**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$
