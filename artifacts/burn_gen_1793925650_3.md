# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #24): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architectural Scope and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates. In every practical 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, commutation, and zero-cross detection. The H743 is the flight controller: it runs ArduPilot, sends bi-directional DShot600, and receives eRPM telemetry.

The analysis below covers:

1. Power-stage losses for one ESC channel, scaled to four.
2. Transient thermal behavior during an 80 A burst.
3. The H743 timer/DMA allocation for bi-directional DShot600.
4. A reference netlist.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design junction temperature of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.5 \times 1.383 = 2.07\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Conduction topology during trapezoidal commutation

With synchronous (complementary) PWM, two phase legs are active in each 60° commutation sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

Total conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence.

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.07\times10^{-3} = 26.5\ \text{W per channel}$$

The worst-case single FET is the static low-side device:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 2.07\ \text{m}\Omega = 13.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase path of about 15 mm, 2 oz copper, an effective width of about 6 mm, and two parallel layers. Sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□, giving roughly 0.3 mΩ per path. The current passes through a phase path and a return path:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.6\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W per channel}$$

This loss is frequently omitted from estimates, but it heats the same copper that cools the FETs.

### 1.5 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on dissipated power. Iterate until the two agree:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{case} + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)\cdot Z_{\theta jc}(t)$$

This typically converges within 3 to 4 iterations. If it does not converge, you are in thermal runaway. That can happen at sustained 80 A on a nano board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its high-side and low-side FETs alternate.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

The low-side body diode recovers when the high-side FET turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss summary

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 1.45 + 0.05 + 0.02 + 0.31 = \mathbf{1.83\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds only about 0.9 W per channel. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates by a factor of roughly 15.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 26.5 W | 106.0 W |
| PCB copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching | 1.8 W | 7.3 W |
| Shunt (0.2 mΩ, if used per channel) | 1.3 W | 5.1 W |
| **Total** | **≈33.4 W** | **≈134 W** |

Total power at the motors is about 4 × 80 A × 22 V ≈ 7 kW, so the ESC efficiency at burst is about 98%. The problem is not efficiency. The problem is concentrating roughly 134 W in a 40×40 mm board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state, which shows that 80 A must be a burst rating

Assume a whole-board R_θ(board→ambient) of about 2.5 K/W with propwash airflow:

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 134 \times 2.5 = 335\ \text{K}$$

This is physically impossible. **An 80 A rating on this form factor is valid only as a time-limited burst.** Realistic continuous capability is in the range of 35 to 45 A per channel. Verify that figure by test.
