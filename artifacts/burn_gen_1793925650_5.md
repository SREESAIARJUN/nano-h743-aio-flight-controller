# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #43): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections

These three points change the design, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **80 A per channel is not survivable steady-state on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor.** Section 3 shows that 80 A is a time-limited burst rating of roughly 1 s or less. The continuous rating lands near 30–35 A per channel. Section 6 gives the derating curve, and marketing numbers should follow it.
3. **The component values below are representative, not datasheet-verified.** They describe a class of 40 V, ~1 mΩ DFN5×6 or 3.3×3.3 FETs. Every parameter in Table 1.1 must be replaced with datasheet values for the selected part. The model is written so you can do this directly.

**Commutation model.** The model assumes six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. In any sector:

- One phase's high/low pair is PWM-switching.
- A second phase's low-side FET is held on.
- The current path therefore always contains **two conducting FETs**.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Duty D is split between the high-side and its synchronous low-side, so the switching leg always carries I. The total is therefore duty-independent to first order:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS}] + I^2 R_{LS,static} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Iterating on T_j (the electrothermal loop) is required. At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.0(1+0.007\cdot85) = 1.60\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(1.60\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Board copper conduction.** This term is often forgotten. A 6-layer, 2 oz stack with roughly 15 mm effective path per phase pair gives R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ, after accounting for the copper tempco of 0.0039/K:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching (V-I overlap)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> Reducing t_dt to 100 ns halves this term. Do it only after measuring the shoot-through margin on the real gate driver.

### 2.4 Reverse recovery, C_oss, and gate drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The gate-drive term is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.5 Totals

| Term | Per channel @ 80 A | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| PCB copper | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Qrr + Coss + gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **25.4 W** | **101.8 W** |

**Efficiency at burst.** Per-channel input power is about V_bus·I·D_eff. At D ≈ 0.95 that is roughly 1.9 kW, so efficiency ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency looks excellent, yet the board cannot reject 100 W. That gap is the core design problem.

**Hottest single FET.** The FET holding the static low-side carries I² continuously for its whole 120° sector. Worst-case per-FET dissipation is therefore:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 2 + \text{switching share} \approx 6.8 + 1.6 \approx 8.5\ \text{W (averaged over an electrical cycle)}$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Foster/Cauer reduced)

```
Tj ──R_θJC(0.8)── Tcase ──R_solder+via(1.5)── T_board(node) ──R_θBA── T_amb
                                         │                      │
                                       C_FET(0.1 J/K)        C_board
```

R_θBA is about 12 K/W in still air and 4–6 K/W under prop wash.

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 1.6 mm, 6L 2 oz)

| Material | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-4 | 1.2 g | 1.1 J/gK | 1.32 J/K |
| Copper | 6 × 4 cm² × 70 µm × 8.96 = 1.5 g | 0.385 | 0.58 J/K |
| 12–24 FETs + solder | ~1.5 g | ~0.6 | 0.9 J/K |
| **C_board** | | | **≈ 2.8 J/K** |

A 30.5×30.5 mm board gives roughly 2.3× this value.

### 3.3 Burst duration limit (near-adiabatic, τ = R_θBA·C ≈ 34 s ≫ burst)

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{board}} = \frac{101.8}{2.8} \approx \mathbf{36\ K/s}$$

Junction offset above the board during burst:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET,max}(R_{\theta JC}+R_{solder}) = 8.5(2.3) \approx 20\ \text{K}$$

The allowable burst time follows from the junction limit:

$$t_{burst} = \frac{T_{j,max} - \Delta T_{j-b} - T_{board,0}}{dT/dt}$$

With T_j,max = 150 °C and a 25 K safety margin, the usable limit is 125 °C.

| Start board temp | All 4 channels at 80 A | 1 channel at 80 A (others ~20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 40 °C (cool start) | (125−20−40)/36 = **1.8 s** | ~5 s |
| 70 °C (after hover) | **0.9 s** | ~2.5 s |

**Conclusion:** the honest label is **"80 A burst, ≤1 s, 4 channels simultaneous."** The FET's Z_θJC(t) curve also matters for sub-100 ms pulses, so use the datasheet's transient chart for desync/stall events.
