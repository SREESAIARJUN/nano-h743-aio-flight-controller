## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Up-Front Engineering Findings

The specification combines a full STM32H743 flight controller with a 4-in-1 ESC rated "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mounting) form factor. Every number below rests on the assumptions in this section. **Replace them with your actual BOM parameters before releasing anything.**

### 0.1 Architecture Assumption

- **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 commands.
- Each ESC channel has its own small ESC MCU, assumed to be an **AT32F421 running AM32**. That MCU generates the **48 kHz** six-step PWM.
- The H743 does **not** commutate motors. The 48 kHz switching losses belong to the ESC power stage.

### 0.3 Critical Findings

1. **"80A" on a 20×20 board is a short burst rating only.** Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four channels reaches a 110 °C board limit in about **2 seconds**. Continuous capability is roughly **15–30 A per channel**, depending on airflow.
2. **H743 package and thermal coupling are constraints.**
   - LQFP100 is 14×14 mm. It is very difficult to fit on a 20×20 board alongside a gyro, baro, and OSD. TFBGA100 (8×8 mm) is the realistic choice.
   - Stacking the H743 directly over the FET array adds heat to the MCU. Check the temperature grade: suffix 6 is rated to 85 °C ambient, suffix 3/7 to 125 °C/105 °C.
   - Use a **two-board stack** (FC above, ESC below), or a thermal moat if single-board.
3. **Bulk capacitor ripple dominates input filtering.** Input RMS ripple current is about 40 A per active channel at D = 0.5. The board cannot carry enough MLCC capacitance for this. A **mandatory external low-ESR electrolytic** (≥ 470 µF / 35 V) on short leads must be specified in the user documentation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

At any instant two phases conduct:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between high-side (HS) conduction for fraction D and synchronous low-side (LS) conduction for fraction 1 − D.
- The **return leg** has its LS on continuously during that 60° sector.

The PWM leg always has exactly one FET conducting, ignoring dead time. Instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of R_DS(on) is approximately:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_hot = 1.5 mΩ.

Copper loss through the PCB path and motor pads adds:

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu}
$$

### 1.2 Numerical Result at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction, hot | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| FET conduction, cold (25 °C) | 2 × 80² × 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Conduction subtotal (hot)** | | **22.4 W** |

**Per-FET average (hot).** Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of an electrical cycle. The 19.2 W per channel spreads across six FETs, giving about **3.2 W average per FET**. The instantaneous peak is 9.6 W while that FET is conducting.

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with T_j through R_DS(on). The closed-loop equilibrium is:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\, I^2 R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta I^2 R_{25}\alpha}
$$

- A stable solution requires **R_θ · I² · R₂₅ · α < 1**.
- At 80 A per FET with α = 0.005 and R₂₅ = 1 mΩ, this means R_θ < 31 K/W at the FET level.
- That is satisfied junction-to-board, but **not** board-to-ambient at full burst (Section 3). Firmware current limiting is therefore mandatory, not optional.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one turn-on and one turn-off of the hard-switched FET, plus body-diode intervals during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap.** The rise and fall times are set by gate current:

$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 8\ \text{ns}

$$
