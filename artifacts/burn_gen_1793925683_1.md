# STM32H743 Nano FC with 4‑in‑1 ESC (Job #44): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

You did not specify the MOSFET part, cell count or board stack, so every number below is **parametric**. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations and the results scale directly.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case) |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 DFN class) | R_ds(on),25 | 0.9 mΩ typ |
| R_ds(on) tempco | α | 0.0045 /K |
| Rise / fall time at driver strength | t_r / t_f | 20 ns / 15 ns |

**Architectural correction.** The H743 should be the flight controller only. It cannot run 4× sensorless commutation at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The correct "4‑in‑1 nano" split is:

- **H743:** flight controller, DShot master.
- **4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071):** ESC MCUs running AM32 (or BLHeli_32), each producing 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- **Link:** bidirectional DShot600.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}=0.9\times1.45=1.31\text{ mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, two legs conduct: one high-side FET (PWM) and one low-side FET. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel current flows through the complementary FET rather than the body diode. The current path therefore always sees about two R_ds(on) in series.

$$P_{cond,motor}=I_{ph}^2\,(R_{ds,HS}D+R_{ds,LS'}(1-D)+R_{ds,LS})\approx I_{ph}^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor}=80^2\times2\times1.31\text{ mΩ}=16.8\text{ W}$$

Copper loss on the same path:

$$P_{cu}=I_{ph}^2R_{cu}=6400\times0.5\text{ mΩ}=3.2\text{ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Instantaneous loss while conducting | 80² × 1.31 mΩ = **8.4 W** |
| Cycle-averaged loss | 16.8 / 6 ≈ **2.8 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero voltage, so its switching loss is negligible.

| Term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48 k | 1.69 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly dissipated in driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

Design levers:

- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.85 W, but you pay ripple current and acoustic noise.
- Dead time costs about 0.3 W per 50 ns. Tune it to the minimum safe value; AM32 lets you set it.

Efficiency check: 4 × 80 A × 22 V ≈ 7 kW of output against 90 W of loss gives about 98.7 % efficiency. That is fine electrically but brutal thermally for a 20×20 or 30×30 board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped board model (first-order RC)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Thermal mass, C_th (8 g PCB + FETs + Cu, c ≈ 0.75 J/g·K) | ≈ 6 J/K |
| Board-to-air resistance with prop wash, R_th,ba | ≈ 2.5 K/W (measure this) |
| Time constant τ = R·C | ≈ 15 s |

Temperature rise during a burst:

$$\Delta T(t)=P\,R_{th}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The steady-state asymptote would be 90 × 2.5 = 225 K, so **80 A continuous is physically impossible**. Inverting for the allowable burst time with a 60 K rise budget (35 °C ambient → 95 °C board):

$$t_{max}=-\tau\ln\left(1-\frac{\Delta T_{allow}}{P R_{th}}\right)=-15\ln(1-60/225)\approx \mathbf{4.7\ s}$$

The initial slope is P/C ≈ 15 K/s. The ESC firmware temperature limit must therefore sample at ≥10 Hz, or it will overshoot.

### 4.2 Sustainable continuous current

Set ΔT = 60 K, so P = 24 W total, or about 6 W per motor. Since loss scales roughly as I², I_cont ≈ 80·√(6/22) ≈ **42 A per motor**.

### 4.3 Junction-to-board (Foster network, from datasheet Z_th curve)

$$\Delta T_{j-b}(t)=\sum_i P\,R_i\,(1-e^{-t/\tau_i})$$

- Per-FET peak: 8.4 W × R_θjc (about 1.2–1.5 K/W) gives roughly 12 K above the pad.
- The FET junction is therefore not the limiter; board bulk temperature is.

Required layout features:

- 2 oz (or heavier) inner layers.
- ≥ 12 × 0.3 mm filled vias under each drain pad.
- An exposed top-side copper pour.

### 4.4 Bulk capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms}=I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}\;\le\;40\text{ A per motor at } D=0.5$$

- Phase-interleaving is not guaranteed across asynchronous ESC MCUs, so budget about 50–60 A RMS total.
- One 35 V low-ESR electrolytic cannot carry this. Use **≥ 8× 10 µF X7R 1210 per motor** plus an external low-ESR bulk capacitor at the battery pads.

### 4.5 Sizing the Nano board for real 80 A bursts

The model points to three levers, in order of impact:

1. Increase thermal mass: thicker copper, or an aluminium heat spreader.
2. Lower R_ds(on): use paralleled FETs. Conduction loss halves per doubling.
3. Use 2-phase sensing at the battery shunt for firmware current limiting.
