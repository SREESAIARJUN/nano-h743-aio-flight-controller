## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Finding

The calculations below show that **a nano-format (≈26×26 mm) 4-in-1 ESC cannot sustain 80 A per motor beyond a few seconds**. It also cannot carry 4×80 A = 320 A (≈8 kW at 6S) through its battery pads and copper. I treat "80 A" as a short burst rating and derive the burst duration it actually supports. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, two MOSFETs carry the phase current at every instant:
- The PWM leg contributes either its high-side FET (during D) or its complementary low-side FET (during 1−D).
- The return leg contributes its low-side FET, held on.

So for one motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2\,(2R_{ds(on),hot} + R_{pcb})$$

The per-FET RMS current, averaged over an electrical revolution, is:

$$I_{rms,HS} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left(1 + (1-D)\right)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}}\ \text{(approx., split between fixed-return and freewheel duty)}$$

The total is invariant: $\sum_{6\,FETs} I_{rms,k}^2 = 2I^2$.

### 1.2 Numbers at I = 80 A

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 19.2\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{pcb} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 22.4\ \text{W}} \qquad P_{cond,4\times} = 89.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Temperature feedback
R_ds(on) rises with temperature. A typical curve is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

This is a positive feedback loop: P depends on R_ds, which depends on T_j, which depends on P. It converges only when

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2 R_{25}\alpha\,R_{th,ja} < 1.$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·1 mΩ·0.006 = 0.077 W/K. Thermal runaway therefore occurs if the effective R_th,ja exceeds about 13 K/W. A nano board in still air is in that range (see §3). This is a second reason the 80 A figure can only be a burst rating.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switching at any time. Per motor:

| Loss term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 30 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.036 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 25 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.030 W** |
| Gate charge (driver dissipation, 2 FETs/leg) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | 2·30 nC·10·48 kHz = **0.029 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.65 W / motor** |

The overlap time can be refined with a Miller-plateau calculation:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}} = \frac{6\,\text{nC}\cdot(4.7+1.5)\,\Omega}{10 - 4.5} \approx 6.8\ \text{ns (Miller only)}$$

Adding the Q_gs2 interval, gate inductance and driver current limits (FD6288-class drivers source about 1.5 A and sink about 1.8 A) brings each edge to roughly 15–25 ns. That supports the 40 ns total assumption.

**Effect of switching frequency:** going from 24 to 48 kHz roughly doubles the switching term (+1.3 W/motor). In exchange it halves current ripple and reduces motor iron/copper ripple loss. At 80 A, switching is only about 11% of total loss, so 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance quad motors.
