# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and BDShot Architecture (Job #48)

## 0. Architectural Correction

The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot. ArduPilot has no 6-step or FOC motor-control path. The architecture modeled here is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot)  ──BDShot600 ×4──►  4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                              └─► 3× half-bridge gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in AM32's PWM frequency setting, not by the H743.

**Form-factor warning.** 80 A burst per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" stack is beyond what the copper can carry or the board can soak thermally. The math below shows the burst time is only a few seconds. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board with the FC on a 20×20 mm or 30×30 mm stack. All numbers below assume a 30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz ESC board unless stated.

All numbers are engineering estimates. Validate component values against actual datasheets and with thermocouple and IR tests before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:

- the PWM leg, where the high-side and low-side FETs alternate synchronously, and
- the static low-side FET of the return phase.

This gives the total conduction loss per motor channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \big[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS,static} \big] \approx 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The approximation holds when all FETs are identical. With synchronous rectification, the loss is independent of the duty cycle D.
