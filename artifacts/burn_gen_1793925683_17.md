# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC Stack (Job #46): Architecture, Loss Model, BDShot Timer/DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

No component selections, stack-up, or mechanical constraints were supplied. Every number below therefore comes from **stated, replaceable assumptions**. The equations are the deliverable. Rerun them with your real datasheet values before committing to layout.

### 0.1 Architecture assumption

An STM32H743 does not drive the gates of a 4-in-1 ESC directly in any practical design. It runs ArduPilot, sends DShot600, and receives eRPM telemetry. The ESC side uses one small MCU per motor (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. "48 kHz" therefore refers to the **ESC-MCU PWM frequency**, not to anything on the H743.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" interpretation

80 A is **per motor, short duration**. A simultaneous 4 × 80 A load means 320 A from the battery. A 20×20 or 30×30 nano stack cannot survive that for more than a fraction of a second. Section 2 quantifies exactly how long it can last.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R = 1.5 × 1.405 ≈ **2.1 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Conduction losses

In any 6-step sector, current flows through **two device positions in series**:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1 − D.
- **Static leg:** one FET conducts 100 % of the sector.

Within the sector, conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R(D) + I^2R(1-D) + I^2R = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

Over one electrical revolution each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the time. The average per-FET loss is ≈ 26.9/6 ≈ **4.5 W**. The peak occurs during the FET's active sectors, with roughly 13 W instantaneous on the static-leg device.

Add copper and shunt losses:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2(R_{trace}+R_{shunt}) \approx 80^2 \times (0.3 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{3.5\ W}$$
