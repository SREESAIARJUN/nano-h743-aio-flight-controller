### Architectural flags (read first)

1. **The H743 does not drive the FETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs, which run commutation and gate drive. Running 4× sensorless commutation and the flight stack on one H743 is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A is a physics conflict.**
   - Six 5×6 FETs per channel (24 total) plus 4 drivers and 4 MCUs do not fit on a 20×20 mm board. The thermal model below assumes 30.5×30.5 mm.
   - Even at 30.5 mm, 80 A on all four channels simultaneously is limited to a few seconds (§3.4).
   - If "nano" means a 20×20 mm board is a hard requirement, the realistic rating is roughly 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on),hot} = 0.70 \times 1.6 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation sector:

- The **PWM leg** has its high-side FET conducting for duty D and its low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducting for 1 − D.
- The **return leg** has its low-side FET conducting for 100 % of the sector.

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.34\ W}$$

### 1.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Each PWM period has two dead-time intervals in which the body diode carries the current:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2\,t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times (2 \times 300\,\text{ns}) \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

> **Design lever:** reducing dead time to 150 ns, which this driver/FET pair supports with R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω, halves this term to 0.92 W.

### 1.4 PCB copper and shunt losses

**Phase trace and pour resistance.** Assume 2 oz copper on 8 layers, effective R ≈ 0.15 mΩ per phase path:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.15\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.92\ \text{W}$$

**Per-channel low-side shunt (0.2 mΩ):**

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.28\ \text{W}$$

- A single 4-channel board shunt is not viable at 320 A. It would dissipate 10 W at 0.1 mΩ
