# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano": Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture (Job #34)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**System partitioning:**
- The **STM32H743** is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot.
- Each ESC channel has its **own** MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.
- The H743 only sources bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- The H743 does **not** commutate the motors.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility warning.** "80 A burst per channel" on a 20×20 nano 4-in-1 is not physically sustainable beyond roughly one second. The math in §1–§3 shows this. The marketing figure on many nano ESCs (often single-channel, unspecified duration, 25 °C start) does not survive a 4-channel simultaneous analysis. I recommend either:
> - derating to **35–45 A burst per channel**, or
> - moving to a **30.5×30.5 mm** form factor.
>
> The full model is provided anyway so the decision can be made quantitatively.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current paths

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct and one floats:
- **Phase A (PWM'd high):** the high-side FET conducts during D·T_sw. The low-side synchronous rectifier conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Phase B (held low):** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

At any instant, the motor current therefore flows through exactly **two FET channels** in series.

### 1.2 Hot Rds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.8\,\text{mΩ} \quad (T_j \approx 125\,°C)
$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$
