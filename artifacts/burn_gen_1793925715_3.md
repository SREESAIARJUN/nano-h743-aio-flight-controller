## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step commutation

In any 60° commutation sector:

- One phase leg is PWM-switched with complementary (synchronous) rectification:
  - its high-side FET conducts for fraction D;
  - its low-side FET conducts for fraction (1−D).
- A second phase leg holds its low-side FET continuously ON.
- The third phase floats (used for BEMF sensing).

The phase current therefore always flows through exactly **two** conducting channels:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Note that conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used. This is a useful property for the model.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_ds = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect losses

These are often larger than designers expect on a nano board.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(100^\circ C) \approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\,\text{m}
$$

Example: a phase pour 10 mm long, 4 mm wide, on 2 oz (70 µm) copper, with 4 paralleled layers:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.01}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} \approx 0.20\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Assume a total of ≈0.4 mΩ in the current path (input pour + phase pour). Solder-pad resistance to motor leads adds another ≈0.1–0.2 mΩ.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Per Channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 2 · 80² · 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| FET conduction (cold) | 2 · 80² · 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W |

| Shunt (see §1.5) | — | budget separately |

### 1.5 Current-sense shunt warning

A single total-current shunt sees 4 × 80 = **320 A** during burst.

$$
P_{shunt} = I_{tot}^2 R_{sh}
$$

- 0.2 mΩ shunt: 20.5 W. This is not viable.
- 0.1 mΩ shunt: 10.2 W, giving a 32 mV signal at 320 A.

**Recommendation:** use a 0.1 mΩ, 2512 or 3920 metal-strip shunt with Kelvin sensing into an INA181A3 (gain 100, giving 3.2 V full-scale at 320 A). Alternatively, use a Hall-effect sensor (ACS772-class) to eliminate shunt dissipation.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs switching losses (one leg per channel at any time).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

The Miller-plateau transition time is set by gate-drive current:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.0\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}, \qquad
t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,snk}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} \approx 6.7\ \text{ns}
$$

Add ≈5 ns each for layout and gate-resistor effects (R_g = 4.7 Ω assumed). This gives t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 12 ns.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 27\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. Derate pessimistically by 3×, giving 0.11 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

There are two FETs in the leg:

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET die.

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$
P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.31 + 0.31 + 0.11 + 0.05 = \mathbf{1.78\ W}
$$
