# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #85): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture Analysis

## 0. Feasibility Verdict

The math below shows that **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 stack is not physically achievable as a sustained rating, and only marginal as a ~1–2 s burst.** At 80 A on all four channels the stack dissipates about 95–100 W into roughly 3–5 g of board. That is about 20× what the form factor can shed steady-state. Section 3 quantifies this.

There is also an architectural point. **ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives the ESCs via bidirectional DShot. Each motor channel (or one shared ESC MCU per pair, depending on design) needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The models below reflect that split.

All device parameters are **representative assumptions**, flagged as [A]. Replace them with datasheet values for the actual BOM before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In six-step operation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series: one high-side and one low-side, on different legs. During the PWM-off interval it flows through the complementary low-side instead of the high-side. Either way, there are two channels' worth of R_ds(on) in the path.

$$P_{cond}=2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Copper adds a further term:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2 R_{trace}$$

With 2 oz copper, internal pours, and short paths, assume R_trace ≈ 0.3 mΩ on the phase and bus path [A].

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond | + P_Cu | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 1.9 W | 14.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W | 1.9 W | **21.8 W** |

**Per-FET average:** six FETs share 2·I²·R over a commutation cycle, so

$$P_{FET,avg}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}$$

This gives about 3.3 W per FET at 125 °C.

The **instantaneous** load is twice the average (6.6 W during the FET's 120° conduction window). That is the figure used for junction transients, because the electrical period (typically 1–5 ms) is comparable to the die thermal time constant.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Each of the following terms is evaluated at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(50\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{2.42\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction (two edges):**

$$P_{dt}=2V_f I\,t_{dt} f_{sw}=2(0.8)(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{ k})=\mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V f_{sw}=50\text{ nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{ k}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}\approx\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver, not the FET):

$$P_g=2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2(60\text{ nC})(10)(48\text{ k})=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 3.8 W per channel.**

Two terms dominate: the V·I overlap and the dead-time losses. Both scale linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.8 W per channel. Trimming dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W.

### 2.3 Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction + copper (125 °C) | 21.8 W | 87 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 3.8 W | 15 W |
| ESC MCU, regulators, shunts, misc. | ~0.5 W | 2 W |
| **Total at 80 A, all motors** | **~26 W** | **~104 W** |

**Efficiency check.** At full throttle each channel delivers about 25.2 × 80 ≈ 2 kW, so electrical efficiency stays around 98.7%. The problem is not efficiency. **The problem is power density:** about 100 W into a 20×20 mm stack.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer approximation)

```
P_FET ─► [Rth_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W, Cth_j ≈ 0.02 J/K] ─► FET pad
       ─► [Rth_spread ≈ 4 K/W per FET into copper] ─► Board node
       ─► [Cth_board] ─► [Rth_ba] ─► T_ambient
```

### 3.2 Board-to-ambient (steady state)

- Exposed area is two faces: A = 2 × (0.02 m)² = 8 × 10⁻⁴ m².
- Prop-wash forced convection: h ≈ 80–120 W/m²·K.
- That gives R_th,ba ≈ 1/(hA) ≈ **10–15 K/W**.

Steady-state temperature rise at 104 W:

$$\Delta T_{ss}\approx 104 \times 12 \approx 1250\ \text{K}$$

This is impossible, so **no continuous 80 A rating exists**. The sustainable stack dissipation is:

$$P_{max}=\frac{105-40}{12}\approx 5.4\ \text{W total}$$

This corresponds to roughly **I_cont ≈ 20–25 A per channel**, which is consistent with real nano 4-in-1 boards.
