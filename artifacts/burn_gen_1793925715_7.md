# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #57): Reference Design Package

This package covers 80 A burst thermal and conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 DMA and timer allocation, and the schematic netlist.

---

## 0. Feasibility Finding

"80 A burst" on a nano-class 4-in-1 board is only achievable as a **short, time-limited transient**. The model in §3 shows the following for a 30×30 mm board in prop wash with all four channels at 80 A:

- **Steady-state rise would be about 450 K.** The board is limited entirely by thermal capacitance.
- **The realistic burst window is about 2–4 s**, starting from a 40 °C board.
- **A true 20×20 mm "nano" format cannot meet 80 A per channel** for even about 1 s without exceeding FET junction limits.

My recommendations:

- Rate the board at **80 A for 2 s per channel** and **about 35–40 A continuous**.
- Enforce the rating in ESC firmware with a current or temperature limiter.

All component values below are representative and must be re-verified against the final datasheets.

## 1. System Architecture

```
 2–6S LiPo ──┬── TVS + 4×470µF/35V low-ESR + 8×10µF/50V X7R ──► VBAT bus
             │
             ├─► 4× ESC channel: AT32F421 (AM32) → FD6288Q 3-ph driver → 6× 40V N-FET
             │        ▲ DShot600 bidir (single wire, open-drain-safe, 3.3V)
             │        │
             └─► FC: STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz
                    TIM3_CH3/CH4, TIM5_CH1/CH2 → M1..M4
                    Current sense: 0.25 mΩ shunt + INA → ADC
```

Each ESC MCU runs **48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM** with synchronous rectification.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss: Six-Step with Synchronous Rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, the current path is one high-side FET (HS) to the motor to one low-side FET (LS). The PWM'd phase alternates as follows:

- The **HS conducts for fraction D** of the PWM period.
- Its **complementary LS conducts for (1 − D)**, which is the synchronous freewheel.
- The **LS of the return phase conducts 100%** of the sector.

Summing the conducting resistance over the PWM period gives:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**. Averaged over one electrical revolution, it spreads across all 6 FETs:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}
$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.305 mΩ**. That gives:

- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.305×10⁻³ = **16.7 W per channel**
- P_cond,FET = **2.78 W per FET**

PCB copper must be included. Assume a 0.25 mΩ shunt plus about 0.30 mΩ of traces and vias in the current path:

- P_Cu = 6400 × 0.55×10⁻³ = **3.5 W per channel**

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. The hard-switched device is the HS; the LS sees diode recovery.

**Gate current during the plateau:**

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_g} = \frac{5.5}{3.5} = 1.57\text{ A},\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_g} = \frac{4.5}{2.5}=1.80\text{ A}
$$

**Transition times:**

$$
t_{on}= \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 7.6\text{ ns},\qquad t_{off}= 6.7\text{ ns}
$$

Apply a ×1.5 layout and common-source-inductance derating, giving t_on = 11.4 ns and t_off = 10.0 ns.
