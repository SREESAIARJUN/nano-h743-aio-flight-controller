# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack (Job #64): Engineering Model and Design Package

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

**A1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- ArduPilot runs on the H743 flight controller and sends DShot commands.
- Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), the AM32 reference part.
- The 48 kHz PWM and the commutation loop live on those ESC MCUs, not the H743.
- The design is therefore an FC board stacked on a 4-in-1 ESC board, or a single AIO board with five MCUs.

**A2. 80 A per motor is a burst rating only. It cannot be a continuous rating on a nano form factor.** Section 2 shows:
- At 80 A, each motor channel dissipates about 27 W, or about 108 W total.
- A 30×30 mm board can shed roughly 10–20 W continuously in prop wash. A 20×20 mm board can shed less.
- The feasible 80 A burst duration is about 3 s from a 40 °C start on 30×30 mm.
- On 20×20 mm, I recommend derating to about 55–60 A burst.

**A3. System current.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S, which is about 8 kW.
- An XT60 (~60 A continuous) cannot supply this.
- The battery cannot supply it either, unless it is a very high-C pack.
- Use XT90 or AS150 connectors, or accept that 80 A/motor applies to transient single-motor yaw or punch events.

**Assumed parameters.** All component values below are assumptions. Replace them with your selected MOSFET and driver datasheet values before tape-out.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel = 3 half-bridges = 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

**Temperature dependence.**

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.65 ≈ **1.98 mΩ** (use 1.9 mΩ worst-case typical).

**Six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.** At any instant two phases carry I:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D and the low-side for (1 − D), so the leg conducts 100 % of the time: I²·R_ds.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully on: I²·R_ds.

P_cond = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j) = 2 × 6400 × 1.9 mΩ = **24.3 W**

**Field-oriented control (sinusoidal) variant.** For comparison, P_cond = 3 · I_rms² · R_ds.

**Copper losses (add separately).** Phase and bus copper on 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers is about 0.3–0.5 mΩ per path:

P_Cu ≈ 2 × 6400 × 0.4 mΩ ≈ **5 W**

This copper loss is distributed across the board rather than concentrated in the FETs.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched PWM leg only)

**Turn-on/turn-off V–I overlap.**

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

Cross-check on switching time: t ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 12 nC / 0.6 A (effective, after R_g and the Miller plateau) ≈ 20 ns.

**Output capacitance.**

P_oss = ½ · C_oss,er · V² · f_sw · 2 = 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.046 W**

**Reverse recovery.**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.060 W**

**Dead-time body-diode conduction.**

P_dt = V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.61 W**

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · 2 = 60 nC × 10 × 48 kHz × 2 = **0.058 W**

Total switching-related loss: P_sw ≈ **2.7 W**

### 2.3 Totals at 80 A

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 24.3 W | 97.2 W |
