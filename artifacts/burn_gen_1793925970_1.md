# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Allocation and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Corrections

Two points need to be settled before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs one dedicated commutation MCU per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. The architecture below reflects this split.
2. **"80A" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only.** It is not a continuous rating. The math in §3 shows the board survives roughly 1–2 s at full burst. Treat the number accordingly.

**Design basis**

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, exactly two phases carry current at any time:
- one phase sources current (high side active),
- one phase sinks current (low side active).

With complementary PWM, the PWM'd leg's current always flows through either its high-side (HS) or its low-side (LS) FET. Duty cycle therefore only redistributes the loss between HS and LS. It does not change the total.

**Per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence of R_DS(on):**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /K$$

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 19.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Per-FET split at duty D:**
- HS on the PWM leg: $D \cdot I^2 R$
- LS on the PWM leg: $(1-D) I^2 R$
- LS on the static leg: $I^2 R$ (when the static leg is the low side)

The worst single FET is the static-on device: **80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W** for its 1/3 electrical-cycle window. At high eRPM it averages toward about 3.2 W per FET over a full rotation. At low eRPM or stall, use the full 9.6 W for local junction heating, because the commutation period then exceeds the junction time constant.

**PCB copper loss.** Assume about 0.4 mΩ of copper per phase path (2 paths in series):

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

This term is commonly ignored and should not be. On a nano board it is about 25% of the FET conduction loss.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. That is one half-bridge per motor at any instant.

### 2.1 Overlap (V–I crossover)

**Gate-charge transition time:**

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} + \frac{Q_{gs2}}{I_g} \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}+5\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A (effective)}} \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

**Overlap loss:**

$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Note that Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. Under hard switching at 80 A, budget 2× this value: **0.12 W**.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

The body diode conducts twice per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per motor

**P_sw ≈ 1.45 + 0.05 + 0.12 + 0.61 + 0.06 ≈ 2.3 W**

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per motor. The cost is higher current ripple and more audible noise.
