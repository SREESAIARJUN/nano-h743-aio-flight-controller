# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #23): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Reality Checks

Three points need to be settled before the math, because they change the model.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU.** 48 kHz PWM and commutation run on per-channel ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to them. Driving 24 gates directly from the H743 is possible but unsupported by ArduPilot and AM32, so this design does not do it.

2. **80 A is a burst rating, not continuous.** On a nano board (25.5 × 25.5 mm, M2 or M3 20 × 20 mounting), 80 A per channel is physically limited by board heat capacity. §3 derives the allowable burst duration rather than pretending it is a steady-state rating.

3. **Parameters are assumptions.** The values below represent a typical 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 mm (PDFN) or 5 × 6 mm MOSFET class. **Re-run the model with your selected FET's datasheet values before layout freeze.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.2 \times 1.383 = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Commutation topology (trapezoidal, 120° conduction, complementary/synchronous PWM)

In any 60° sector, current flows from a chopped leg (phase A) through the motor to a return leg (phase B):

- **Chopped leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw, and the low-side (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1 − D)·T_sw. Ignoring dead time, exactly one FET carries I at all times.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully on for the whole sector.

So at every instant, two FETs are in series with the load current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}$$

> **Correction note:** an earlier draft of this calculation gave 10.6 W. That figure omitted the factor of 2 for the series return-leg FET. 21.2 W per channel is correct for 2 × R_DS in the current path.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts in 2 of the 6 sectors (a 1/3 duty window):

- **High-side FET:** conducts D × 1/3 of the cycle.
- **Low-side FET:** conducts 1/3 as the return leg, plus (1 − D) × 1/3 as the synchronous rectifier.

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1 + (1-D)}{3}$$

At burst, assume D ≈ 0.9 (80 A demands near full throttle). With I²R = 10.6 W:

- P_HS = 10.6 × 0.30 = **3.2 W**
- P_LS = 10.6 × 0.367 = **3.9 W** (the hottest device)
- Check: 3 × (3.2 + 3.9) = 21.2 W ✓

The electrical frequency is roughly 3.5 kHz (14-pole motor at 30k RPM), which is far faster than the die thermal time constant (on the order of ms). Average power per FET is therefore the correct thermal input.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction

Assume a phase path of 15 mm, 4 mm wide, with 4 paralleled layers of 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.015}{4\times10^{-3} \times 4 \times 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.23\ \text{m}\Omega$$

At 100 °C, multiply by (1 + 0.00393 × 75) = 1.29, giving 0.30 mΩ.

The current path includes the battery leg, both phase legs, and the shunt (≈ 0.6 mΩ total copper excluding the shunt):

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.8\ W}$$
