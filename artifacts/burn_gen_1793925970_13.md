# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #10)

**Document class:** Architecture and analysis baseline, Rev A
**Scope:** Thermal and loss modelling at 80 A burst, 48 kHz PWM switching losses, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and top-level netlists

> **Engineering note up front:** 80 A per motor on a nano-class (20×20 mm mounting) board is at or beyond the physical limit. The model below shows that 80 A is achievable only as a **short burst of about 1–2 s**, with strong airflow and current-limit firmware. Treat 80 A as a burst rating, not continuous. All component parameters are **design assumptions** and must be replaced with datasheet values of the selected parts. Validate the results on a thermal test bench (IR camera plus thermocouples) before release.

**Rationale:**
- The ESC uses one MCU per motor (AM32 on AT32F421). This keeps commutation loops local and leaves the H743 free for ArduPilot.
- The H743 only generates DShot frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry that comes back.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model

### 3.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.55 = **2.33 mΩ**

### 3.2 Six-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any commutation sector, two half-bridges carry current:

| Device | Conduction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| PWM leg, high-side | duty D | I²·R·D |
| PWM leg, low-side (sync rectifier) | 1 − D | I²·R·(1−D) |
| Sink leg, low-side | 100 % | I²·R |

Total per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R\,(D + (1-D) + 1) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D.

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.33\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{29.8\ W/channel}$$

**Worst single device:** the sink-leg low-side FET dissipates I²R = **14.9 W** during its 120° electrical interval. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each FET carries one third of this, about 5 W. However, at low eRPM, sector dwell times are longer than the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms). The worst-case low-RPM high-torque burst must therefore use the **peak** 14.9 W against Z_th.

### 3.3 Electro-thermal fixed point and runaway criterion

Two coupled equations describe the junction:

- Loss: P = P₀[1 + α(T−25)], where P₀ is the loss at 25 °C
- Temperature: T = T_a + R_θ·P

Solving them together gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when α·R_θ·P₀ ≥ 1.

Worked example for a single FET:
- P₀ = 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W
- R_θ = 15 K/W (effective junction to air through local copper)
- α·R_θ·P₀ = 0.0055 × 15 × 9.6 = **0.79**

This is stable but close to the limit. The fixed point is:

$$T_j = \frac{40 + 144\times0.8625}{0.21} \approx 782\ ^\circ C$$

This is impossible in steady state. **80 A cannot be continuous.** It is only feasible as a transient burst; see §5.
