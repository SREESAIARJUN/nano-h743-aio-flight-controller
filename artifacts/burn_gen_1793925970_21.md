# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #19): Electrothermal, Switching-Loss and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

This package covers both halves of the system, because the request spans them:

- **Flight controller (FC):** an STM32H743 running ArduPilot, which generates DShot600 and decodes the bi-directional eRPM replies.
- **4-in-1 ESC:** four motor-control MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, each driving a 3-phase MOSFET bridge at 48 kHz.

The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. Commercial firmware does not do this, and it is not recommended.

### Feasibility flags (read first)

1. **"Nano" vs. 80 A.** A true 20 × 20 mm 4-in-1 cannot physically sustain 4 × 80 A, because MOSFET footprint, copper cross-section and connectors all fall short. The math below assumes a 30.5 mm board. Even there, 80 A is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not continuous (proven in §3).
2. **Aggregate bus current.** 4 × 80 A = **320 A** at the battery input. An XT60 is rated around 60 A continuous. A plausible full-throttle burst is closer to 4 × 40–50 A, but the input path must survive the stated case or be firmware-limited.
