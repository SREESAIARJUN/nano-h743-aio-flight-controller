# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Model (Job #26)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architectural split.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates directly.

- **Flight controller:** the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 commands.
- **ESC:** the 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

The loss model (§1–2) applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA/timer model (§4) applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### Commutation scheme
The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

- At any instant, motor current flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:
  - HS(x) + LS(y) during the PWM on-time, or
  - LS(x) + LS(y) during active freewheel.
- Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° per electrical cycle, so its conduction duty is 1/3.

### Temperature-dependent resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 90) = **1.59 mΩ**.

### RMS current including PWM ripple
For a triangular ripple of peak-to-peak ΔI:
$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple is set by the motor phase inductance:
$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Taking L_ph = 15 µH, D = 0.9, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 4 V:
- ΔI = 4 × 0.9 / (15e-6 × 48e3) = **5.0 A**
- Ripple contribution = 5² / 12 ≈ 2.1 A² (negligible against 6400 A²)

### Results per ESC channel
$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6402 \times 1.59\text{e-3} = \mathbf{20.4\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot I_{rms}^2 R_{ds} = \mathbf{3.4\ W}\ \text{average per FET}$$

### Electro-thermal fixed point
Because R_ds depends on T_j, the loss and temperature must be solved together. Iterate:
1. T_j(n+1) = T_pcb + P_FET(T_j(n)) × (R_θJC + R_θCS)
2. P_FET = (I² R_ds,25 / 3) × [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

This converges only if (I² R_ds,25 / 3) × α × R_θ < 1. Here: 2133 × 1e-3 × 0.0065 × 3.5 = **0.049**, so it is stable (no thermal runaway at the FET level).

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per commutation sector, and it uses an HS/LS pair. The table shows the components per ESC channel.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48e3 | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr × V × f_sw = 50e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss × V × f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f × I × 2 t_dead × f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 100e-9 × 48e3 | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2 × Q_g × V_gs × f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.37 W** |

### Per-channel and board totals

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per channel (conduction + switching) | 20.4 + 2.4 = **22.8 W** |
| All 4 channels at simultaneous burst | **≈ 91 W** |
| DC-link capacitor ESR (see below) | ≈ 1.9 W |

**DC-link capacitor loss.** The input ripple current is
$$I_{cap,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.3 = 24\ \text{A per channel}$$
With interleaving across channels, assume ~35 A total. At 1.5 mΩ ESR, the loss is about **1.9 W**. This is why low-ESR polymer capacitors plus a bulk low-ESR electrolytic on the pigtail are mandatory.

### Frequency trade-off
Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel, but increases ripple current (ΔI doubles, ~10 A). At 80 A, conduction loss dominates (~89%), so 48 kHz costs only about 5% extra. Keep 48 kHz for smoothness and lower audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3a. Steady state: not achievable at 80 A
Representative board-to-ambient resistance for a 20×20 mm 8-layer 2 oz board is R_θBA ≈ 12 K/W in still air, or about 4 K/W with prop wash.

$$\Delta T_{BA} = 91 \times 4 = 364\ \text{K}$$

This is not sustainable, which is why 80 A must be treated as a burst rating.

**Maximum continuous current.** Solve for a board rise of 60 K (40 °C ambient → 100 °C board) with prop wash:
- Allowable total power = 60 / 4 = 15 W, i.e., 3.75 W per channel.
- Solve 2 I² (1.4e-3) + 0.0296 I = 3.75 → **I_cont ≈ 30 A per channel, all four motors loaded.**

### 3b. Transient burst (lumped Cauer model)

**Node definitions:**
- Junction → case: τ ≈ 5 ms. This is fast and treated as quasi-static.
- Board (heat sink node): C_b = Σ m·c_p
  - FR4 ~2.0 g × 1.1 J/(g·K)
  - Copper ~2.5 g × 0.385 J/(g·K)
  - FETs plus pad ~1.0 g × 0.7 J/(g·K)
  - **Total C_b ≈ 3.8 J/K**
- Optional aluminium plate (4 g): add 3.6 J/K → **7.4 J/K**

**Board temperature during the burst:**
$$T_b(t) = T_a + P R_{BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/(R_{BA}C_b)}\right)$$

With τ = 4 × 3.8 = 15 s, the response is nearly a linear ramp, dT/dt ≈ P / C_b.
