## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.76\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, current flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg. In the PWM-chopped leg, the HS FET conducts for duty D and its complementary LS FET (sync rectifier) conducts for 1−D, ignoring dead time. The chopped-leg loss is therefore duty-independent:

$$P_{cond,chop} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}$$

The static leg conducts 100% of the sector:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.76\times10^{-3} = 22.5\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device and RMS view

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (two adjacent 60° sectors per leg position). Peak sustained power per device is therefore ~11.3 W, while the average per device is ~3.75 W. Thermal design must use **peak-sector** power for junctions and **average** power for the board.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | P @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper path (est.) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt (if per-motor) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W (×2/3 duty ≈ 0.85 W) |

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the chopped HS FET hard-switches. Its LS partner commutates through its body diode during dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched device:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.69\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction, two edges per period:**

$$P_{DT} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance, both FETs of the leg:**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive, two active gates.** This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{DRV} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 2.4\ \text{W}$$

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw and with V_bus. Moving to 96 kHz adds ~2.4 W per motor. At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by roughly 10:1, so **R_DS(on) is the primary design lever, not switching speed.**

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |

| Conduction (FETs) | 22.5 W | 90.0 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Copper + shunt | 2.8 W | 11.0 W |
| Gate driver quiescent/LDO | 0.1 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **27.8 W** | **~111 W** |

Efficiency at the 6S, 80 A, D≈1 operating point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 ≈ 8 kW, so η ≈ 98.6%. Efficiency is not the problem. **Heat density in a 26×26 mm footprint is.**
