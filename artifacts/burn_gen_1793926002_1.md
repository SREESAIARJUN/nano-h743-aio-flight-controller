# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #4): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Ground Rules

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller only. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own motor MCU running AM32 firmware, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over bi-directional DShot600. Running 4× six-step commutation on the H743 alongside ArduPilot's scheduler is not a supportable design.

**Parameter caveat.** All numbers below are derived from the stated parameter assumptions (Table 0.1). Replace them with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values and the equations still hold. I'm showing the derivations so the model can be re-run, not just the answers.

**Feasibility flag (read before committing to the spec).**

- "80 A burst" per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is achievable only as a *phase-current transient of a few seconds*.
- With all four channels at 80 A, the model below gives about 115 W of heat into roughly 3 J/K of board thermal mass. That is about 40 K/s of temperature rise.
- Battery-side current is D × I_phase per motor. A 4 × 80 A event at high duty is ~250–320 A through the board's input copper and connector. That is not survivable on a nano board.
- The spec must be stated as "80 A per-channel burst, ≤ 2 s, single-to-dual channel, with board-temperature-gated current limiting". Section 3.5 gives the firmware limits.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in six-step with synchronous rectification

AM32 runs trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM on the active leg. Per commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd (HS on during D, LS sync-rectifying during 1−D) and one leg's LS is held on.
