# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture & Derivations (Job #15)

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

Three constraints shape this design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is not an ESC firmware. The board therefore uses the standard split architecture:
   - **H743 (ArduPilot)** sends **bi-directional DShot600** to four **ESC MCUs**.
   - The ESC MCUs are AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running **AM32**.
   - Each ESC MCU drives one 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.
2. **"Nano" form factor and 80 A burst conflict.** The real limit is board thermal mass, not silicon. I size the board at **30.5 × 30.5 mm** (30.5 mm mounting pattern). A 20 × 20 board cannot place 24 power FETs plus the FC. Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is a **time-limited burst of about 5 s**, not a continuous rating.
3. **Total bus current is a system limit.** Four motors at 80 A each is **320 A** from the battery. No nano-class lead or XT60 survives that. The 80 A figure is a **per-motor transient** rating, for example asymmetric yaw/attitude recovery loads.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 75) = 1.34 \approx 1.4\ \text{m}\Omega$$

(Rounded up to 1.4 mΩ for margin; section 3.4 checks this assumption.)
