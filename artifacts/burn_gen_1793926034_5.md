# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power, Thermal and DShot Timing Model, Job #68

## 0. Architecture and Scope

Before the math, three constraints shape the design:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. The 48 kHz six-step PWM runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 timer budget therefore covers only DShot and capture, not the bridge PWM.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano-class board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) is a burst rating only.** The model below shows it is thermally capacity-limited to a few seconds.
3. **All component values are assumed, not verified.** The device parameters are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm-class MOSFETs. Substitute your actual BOM datasheet values before release. The netlist in §4 is a reference topology, not a verified production netlist.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Six-step operation.** In each 60° sector, one leg PWMs with complementary synchronous rectification, one leg's low-side is held on, and the third leg floats.

- **PWM leg.** The high-side conducts for D and the low-side for (1−D). Loss = I²·R(T_j)·[D + (1−D)] = I²·R(T_j).
- **Static leg.** Loss = I²·R(T_j).

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**At T_j = 125 °C:** R = 1.5 × 1.6 = 2.4 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}$$

This splits into about 15.4 W in the static low-side FET and 15.4 W shared across the PWM-leg pair. The static FET is the hotspot.

**Add copper and shunt losses.** Assume a 0.5 mΩ shunt plus 0.3 mΩ of trace per path: I²·0.8 mΩ ≈ 5.1 W per motor.

### Electrothermal Stability (Runaway Check)

The loop is stable while the self-heating gain stays below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot Z_{th} = 2I^2 R_{25}\alpha\, Z_{th} < 1$$

With Z_th(j-board) ≈ 2 K/W: 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 × 0.006 × 2 ≈ **0.23**. This is stable, but it amplifies the temperature rise by 1/(1−0.23) ≈ 1.3×. Include this factor in every steady-state estimate.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The hard-switched leg dominates. Per motor:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{V\text{-}I\ overlap} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V f}_{recovery} + \underbrace{2\cdot\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f}_{C_{oss}} + \underbrace{2\,V_f I\, t_d f}_{dead\ time} + \underbrace{2\,Q_g V_{gs} f}_{gate}$$

| Term | Calculation | Watts |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 kHz | 1.94 |
| Reverse recovery | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.05 |
| Dead-time diode | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 50 ns · 48 kHz | 0.31 |
| Gate (dissipated in driver) | 2 · 40 nC · 10 V · 48 kHz | 0.04 |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Interpretation.** At 48 kHz, switching is only about 7% of the loss. Conduction dominates, so pick the lowest-R_DS FET that fits rather than optimizing for Q_g.

**Gate-driver sizing.** The 20 ns edges require a peak gate current of about Q_gd/t_r. Assuming Q_gd ≈ 12 nC, that is roughly 0.6 A. Specify a driver of at least 1 A source and 1.5 A sink.

### 3.2 Two-Node Transient Model

The model has a junction node and a board node:

- **Junction:** C_j·dT_j/dt = P_FET − (T_j − T_b)/R_jb
- **Board:** C_th·dT_b/dt = ΣP − (T_b − T_a)/R_ba

Parameter values:

- R_jb ≈ 2 K/W per FET (R_th,jc plus solder and vias).
- R_ba = 1/(h·A), with A = 2 × 9×10⁻⁴ m².
  - At h = 80 W/m²K (propwash), R_ba ≈ 6.9 K/W.
  - At h = 20 W/m²K (hover in a duct), R_ba ≈ 28 K/W.

**Junction rise above board (static FET):** 15.4 W × 2 K/W × 1.3 runaway factor ≈ **40 K**.

To keep T_j ≤ 150 °C, the board must stay at or below 110 °C. Starting from a 40 °C board, the allowable rise is ΔT_b = 70 K.

**Burst duration (adiabatic, conservative):**

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T_b}{\Sigma P}$$

| Case | ΣP | Allowed burst |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 motors at 80 A | 153 W | 5.0 × 70 / 153 ≈ **2.3 s** |
| 1 motor at 80 A (assumes full board spreading) | 38 W | ≈ 9 s |

The single-motor figure is optimistic. Local spreading resistance limits it to roughly 5–6 s in practice.
