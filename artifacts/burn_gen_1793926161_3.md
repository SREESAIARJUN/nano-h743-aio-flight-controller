# Job #27: STM32H743 Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC

## System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Critical Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 must not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and has no FOC or six-step commutation engine. The architecture is therefore:
   - **H743 FC**, running ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600 commands.
   - **Four ESC MCUs** (Cortex-M0+/M4 class running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware), one per motor.
   - **Gate drivers** and **24 MOSFETs**.

   A single-MCU "4-in-1 on the H743" design is not supportable in ArduPilot.

2. **"80A" can only be a burst rating.** The thermal model in §3 shows:
   - Adiabatic headroom is about **4 s** at 4×80A.
   - Realistic continuous current is about **30–35A per motor** under prop-wash cooling.
   - Any marketing rating must specify duration.

3. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) is not physically viable at 80A.** Twenty-four PDFN5×6 MOSFETs, current shunts, and bulk capacitance cannot fit with adequate copper.
   - All calculations assume the **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** (36×36 mm outline) stacked with a **20×20 or 30.5×30.5 FC**.
   - A 20×20 ESC board would need 3.3×3.3 mm FETs at roughly 2× R_DS(on). This raises conduction loss by about 2× and cuts burst time to under 2 s.

4. **Component values are design requirements, not a specific vendor part.** Before release, verify against the actual datasheets selected by procurement:
   - R_DS(on)(T)
   - Q_gd
   - Q_rr
   - Z_th curves

## 2. Electrical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

**Commutation topology.** With six-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary synchronous PWM, exactly one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current at every instant:
- During the on-time D, the high-side of the PWM leg and the low-side of the return leg conduct.
- During the off-time (1−D), the low-side of the PWM leg (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side of the return leg conduct.

Total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Ripple contribution.** Ripple current on the phase inductance is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2 \quad (\text{ripple negligible: } <0.1\%)$$
