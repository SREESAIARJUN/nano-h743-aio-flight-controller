# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Design Package

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Design-Review Flags

**Corrected architecture.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM to a 3-phase gate driver. The model below follows that split.

**Feasibility warning.** "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 board is not thermally sustainable. The math in §2–§3 shows the board heats at roughly 40–60 K/s at 80 A. This should go to the product owner before layout freeze.

**Assumed parameters** (replace with your BOM datasheet values):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max |
| MOSFET | 30–40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3.3, R_ds(on),25 = 2.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| Temperature coefficient | α ≈ 0.0045 /K |
| Gate charge | Q_g = 30 nC |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr = 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss = 25 nC |
| Rise + fall time | t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns (hard-switched, ~2 Ω gate resistor) |
| Junction-to-case | R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| PWM frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode drop | V_f ≈ 0.8 V |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification (AM32 default) |

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 2.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.77 mΩ**. Use **2.8 mΩ** for the burst case.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation, two legs conduct at any instant:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D; the synchronous low side conducts for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side is fully on.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds} D + I^2 R_{ds}(1-D) + I^2 R_{ds} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of D because of synchronous rectification. Phase-current ripple adds a factor of (1 + r²/12), where r = ΔI/I. That is ≈1% at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance, so it is neglected here.

At I = 80 A:
- P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 0.0028 = **35.8 W per motor channel**
- The **hottest single FET** (return-leg low side, 100% on) carries 6400 × 0.0028 = **17.9 W**.

**PCB copper loss:** about 0.3 mΩ per phase path for 2 oz copper on a nano layout.
- P_Cu ≈ 2 × 6400 × 0.0003 = **3.8 W per channel**

### 1.3 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.015 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw = 2 × 30 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.03 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.68 W** |

### 1.4 Channel and board totals at 80 A

| Level | Calculation | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Per channel | 35.8 + 3.8 + 1.7 | ≈ **41 W** |
| Whole board, 4 motors | 4 × 41 | ≈ **165 W** |
| Electrical output | 4 × 80 A × ~22 V | ≈ 7 kW |

The board efficiency is about 97.7%. The absolute loss is still far beyond what a 20×20 board can reject.

**Frequency tradeoff.** Switching loss is only about 4% of total loss at 48 kHz.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 0.8 W per channel.
- Going to 96 kHz costs about 1.6 W per channel.

48 kHz is a reasonable choice for smoothness and noise. Conduction loss (I²R) dominates, so FET selection and copper weight matter far more than PWM frequency.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Thermal network

Lumped Foster/Cauer network:

```
P_FET ─[R_θJC 1.5]─ T_case ─[R_θCB 0.8]─ T_board(C_b) ─[R_θBA]─ T_ambient
```

**Board thermal capacitance** (20×20 FR4, 6-layer 2 oz, plus 24 FETs and caps, ≈ 4 g total):

$$C_b \approx \sum m_i c_{p,i} \approx 2.5\,\mathrm{g}\times0.9 + 1.2\,\mathrm{g}\times0.385 + 0.3\,\mathrm{g}\times0.7 \approx 2.9\ \mathrm{J/K}$$

**Board-to-air resistance:**
- About 12 K/W in still air.
- About 4 K/W with prop wash at roughly 10 m/s, using h ≈ 60 W/m²K × 2 sides plus fins from the stack.

### 2.2 Steady state (sanity check)

$$T_b = T_a + P\,R_{\theta BA} = 35 + 165 \times 4 = 695\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

This is **not achievable** as a continuous rating. Solving for the sustainable continuous current with T_b ≤ 90 °C:

$$P_{max} = (90 - 35)/4 = 13.75\ \mathrm{W}$$

$$\Rightarrow\ I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{13.75/4}{2\times(2.4+0.3)\times10^{-3}}} \approx 25\ \mathrm{A\ per\ motor}$$

(2.4 mΩ is used for R_ds at 90 °C.) This matches real-world nano-AIO ratings of 20–30 A continuous.

### 2.3 Burst (transient) duration

Treat the board as a single RC node:

$$T_b(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\qquad \tau = R_{\theta BA}C_b = 4\times2.9 = 11.6\ \mathrm{s}$$

For P ≫ the steady-state rating, the early heating is quasi-adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_b} = \frac{165}{2.9} \approx 57\ \mathrm{K/s}$$

**Junction temperature** of the hottest FET, using Z_θJC at about 1 s ≈ R_θJC:

$$T_j = T_b + P_{FET}(Z_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB}) = T_b + 17.9\times2.3 = T_b + 41\ \mathrm{K}$$

**Constraint:** T_j,max = 150 °C, derated to 135 °C, so T_b ≤ 94 °C.

$$t_{burst} = -\tau\ln\left(1 - \frac{94-35}{165\times4}\right) \approx 1.08\ \mathrm{s}$$

### 2.4 Recommended specification language

> **80 A burst ≤ 1 s, all four motors, from T_board ≤ 35 °C, with 5 s recovery at ≤ 25 A.**
>
> Or rate it as **"80 A peak, 1 motor."** That case is ¼ the board loss, ≈ 4.5 s burst.

**Mitigations:**
- Higher-performance FETs (≤ 1.2 mΩ PowerPAK/TOLL-class FETs do not fit on 20×20).
- 3 oz inner planes.
- A thermal pad to the frame.
- Moving to a 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 layout.

**Firmware protection:**
- AM32 temperature limit around 100 °C.
- ArduPilot `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`, plus ESC telemetry temperature logging via `SERVO_BLH_*` / EDT.
