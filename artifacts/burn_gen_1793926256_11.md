## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points change the model, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the FETs.** In a flight controller plus 4-in-1 ESC stack, or an all-in-one board:
   - **H743 (flight controller, ArduPilot):** generates DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.
   - **Four ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 targets):** generate the 48 kHz PWM and commutation.
   - The 48 kHz switching loss model belongs to the ESC side.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 or 25.5 mm) board is a burst rating, not a thermal rating.** Section 3 shows the board is adiabatically limited to seconds at 80 A. The continuous capability works out to about 30 A per channel. Market "80A" figures on nano boards are typically per-channel peaks lasting about 1 to 10 s.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

For 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, two FETs (one high-side, one low-side, on different phases) carry the phase current at every instant. Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period, either as the active switch or as the synchronous rectifier.

Per-FET RMS current at full duty:

$$I_{rms,FET} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}$$

Per-channel conduction loss across six FETs:

$$P_{cond} = 6\cdot\frac{I^2 R_{ds}k_T}{3} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}k_T$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(0.75\text{m}\Omega)(1.5) = \mathbf{14.4\ W}$$

The duty cycle does not reduce this first-order result under synchronous rectification. The current simply alternates between the high-side FET and the complementary low-side FET.

Shunt loss:

$$P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Copper trace and plane loss: assume about 0.3 mΩ effective, which adds **~1.9 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg commutates at the PWM rate at a time: the PWM'd high-side and its complementary low-side.

**V-I overlap (hard-switched high-side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.97\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ W$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V f_{sw} = 0.02\ W$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):**

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.06\ W$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.7\ W/channel}$$

The dead-time diode loss is about 40% of the switching loss. Tightening dead-time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.3 Bus Capacitor Ripple

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \max = 0.5\,I = \mathbf{40\ A_{rms}}\ \text{at } D=0.5$$

The four channels run asynchronously, so per-channel ripple adds roughly in RMS fashion, partly cancelling. The bulk capacitor bank still needs low-ESR polymer or MLCC.

With 4×10 µF X7R per channel (effective ~4 µF at 25 V DC bias) plus a shared 470 µF low-ESR electrolytic:
