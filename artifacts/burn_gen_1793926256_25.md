## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two scoping issues need to be settled before the model is meaningful.

**1. MCU partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. The ESC side needs a dedicated commutation MCU per motor (or one per pair), for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This document models both sides:

```
LiPo 6S ──┬── 4-in-1 power stage (24 FETs, 4× 3-ph gate drivers)
          │        ▲ 48 kHz complementary PWM
          │   4× ESC MCU (AM32)  ◄── bidir DShot600 ──►  STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
          └── BEC 5V/10V
```

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor.** The analysis below shows that four channels at 80 A dissipate roughly 90 W. A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board cannot sustain that. The numbers support 80 A only as a **per-motor burst of about 1–3 s**, and only if the following hold:
- MOSFETs are at least PDFN 5×6 class, which may force a 30.5×30.5 mm board.
- The board uses 2 oz or heavier copper.
- ESC firmware enforces thermal and current limits.

This is quantified in §3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- **On-time (D):** current flows through the high-side FET of the PWM leg, the motor, and the low-side FET of the sink leg.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current flows through the low-side FET of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification), the motor, and the low-side FET of the sink leg.

In both intervals, exactly **two FETs carry the full phase current**, apart from the dead time, which is handled in §2.3. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D, to first order)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature coupling

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a target T_j of 110 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.51\ \text{mΩ}$$

Because conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds(on), the model is a positive-feedback loop. The self-consistent junction temperature is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + 2I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}R_{th}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$2I^2R_{25}R_{th}\,\alpha \ge 1$$

With I = 80 A and R_th = 11 K/W (from §3), this product is 0.84. That is dangerously close to runaway in steady state, which is a second independent reason that 80 A must be burst-only.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 1.51 mΩ | **19.3 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | **1.9 W** |

**Per-FET average.** In 6-step each phase leg is active for 120° of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the cycle, the 2·I²·R is distributed across six FETs, giving about 3.2 W per FET. Two points qualify that average:
- The commutation period at typical eRPM is on the order of milliseconds, much shorter than the junction-to-case time constant of about 10–50 ms. The **average** therefore governs junction rise.
- Peak instantaneous dissipation in one FET is the full 9.7 W while it conducts.
