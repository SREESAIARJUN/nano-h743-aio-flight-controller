## 0. Architecture Assumptions

These assumptions set the scope. Challenge any that do not match your intent.

1. **Two-MCU-tier architecture.** ArduPilot on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. It acts as the flight controller and talks bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. These are AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, one per channel. "4-in-1 ESC nano flight controller" therefore means an FC/ESC stack or an AIO built on this split.
2. **Bus voltage.** 6S, with V_bus,max = 25.2 V. MOSFETs are rated 40 V.
3. **"80A burst" is per channel.** It means phase/DC current for at most 10 s, which is the industry convention.
4. **Form factor.** The board is 36×36 mm with 30.5 mm mounting holes, 8 layers, 2 oz copper. A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot thermally support 80A per channel. Section 3 quantifies why.
5. **Component values are representative.** The MOSFET parameters below describe a generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations. All results are parametric.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₀ | 1.8 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.45 %/K |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 30 nC |
| Gate-source charge after threshold + Miller charge | Q_gs2 + Q_gd | 8 nC |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(T_j) = R₀ · (1 + α)^(T_j − 25)

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.8 mΩ × 1.0045¹⁰⁰ = 1.8 × 1.566 = **2.82 mΩ**

### 1.2 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, phase current I flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg. The PWM'd leg alternates between high-side conduction (duty D) and low-side synchronous rectification (1 − D). The opposite leg's low side conducts 100% of the sector.

P_cond,ch = I²R_DS·D + I²R_DS·(1−D) + I²R_DS = **2·I²·R_DS(T_j)**

The result is independent of duty cycle. This is why synchronous rectification matters: without it, the (1−D) term would be V_f·I.

At I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 2.82 mΩ = **36.1 W per channel**

Each FET conducts on average 1/3 of the electrical cycle at full current-equivalent weight, so the per-FET share is:

**P_cond,FET ≈ 36.1 / 6 ≈ 6.0 W**

The loss is unevenly distributed. The PWM-side high/low FETs swap share with D.
