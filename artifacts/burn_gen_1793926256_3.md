# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #40): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In any 4-in-1 design:

- The **H743** runs ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- A **dedicated ESC MCU per motor** runs the 48 kHz complementary PWM. Typical choices are AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32.

The loss model in sections 1–3 applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model in section 4 applies to the H743.

### 0.3 Feasibility caveat

80 A per motor on a nano-format board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) is a **burst rating limited by thermal mass**, not a continuous rating. The math below quantifies this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, at any instant one phase is PWM'd (high side on for D, complementary low side on for 1−D) and one phase's low side is held fully on.

The current paths through the switches are:

- **PWM leg, high side:** carries I for duty D.
- **PWM leg, low side:** carries I for 1−D.
- **Static leg, low side:** carries I for 100% of the period.

### 1.2 Equations

R_hot = k_T · R_ds(on),25 = 1.5 · 1.0 mΩ = 1.5 mΩ

P_cond = I² · R_hot · [D + (1−D) + 1] = 2 · I² · R_hot

At 80 A:

P_cond = 2 · 80² · 1.5×10⁻³ = **19.2 W per motor**

This loss is **independent of duty cycle**, because synchronous rectification always keeps two FETs in the path.

For a design with two parallel FETs per switch position (R_eff = R/2), the conduction loss is P_cond/2 = 9.6 W.

### 1.3 Peak per-FET stress

The worst-stressed device is the static low-side FET:

P_FET,max = 80² · 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**

### 1.4 Ripple correction

The RMS current exceeds the DC current by the phase-current ripple:

I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12

Phase ripple is:

ΔI ≈ V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_phase · f_sw)

For a typical 5" motor with L ≈ 15 µH (line-to-line):

ΔI ≈ 25.2 · 0.09 / (15×10⁻⁶ · 48×10³) ≈ 3.2 A

This adds less than 0.02% to the loss and is negligible at this operating point. It does matter at low duty cycle with low-inductance motors.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, one leg switches per PWM period (hard turn-on and turn-off of the high side, soft switching of the low side).

| Loss term | Equation | Value at 80 A, 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f_sw | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs per leg) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48 k = **0.05 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |
| **Switching total** | | **≈ 3.3 W per motor** |

### 2.1 Frequency sensitivity

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency:

P_sw(f) ≈ 69 µJ · f

| Frequency | Switching loss per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.7 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.3 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.6 W |

48 kHz is a reasonable choice: it is above the audible range and keeps switching loss at about 15% of the total.

### 2.2 Dead-time tuning

Dead-time loss is about 37% of the switching loss. Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Validate the reduction with a shoot-through test at temperature.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Loss totals at the 80 A burst

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per motor (19.2 W conduction + 3.3 W switching) | ≈ 22.5 W |
| 4-in-1 power stage | ≈ 90 W |
| Shunts (0.5 mΩ, if per-motor sense is fitted) | 3.2 W per motor |
| Bus caps, connector, and copper I²R | ≈ 5–10 W |
| **Board total** | **≈ 100–110 W** |

### 3.2 Thermal network (Cauer approximation)

The network runs from junction to ambient:

```
P_FET ──[R_θJC 1.5 K/W]──┬──[R_spread ≈ 3–5 K/W per FET]──┬──[R_θBA]── T_amb
                         │                                │
                    C_FET ≈ 0.02 J/K                C_board ≈ 3–4 J/K
                                         (≈ 4 g FR4 + 2 oz Cu, 6–8 layers, plus heatsink pad)
```

The board-to-ambient resistance depends on airflow:

| Condition | R_θBA |
|---|---|
| Still air | 12–15 K/W |
| Prop wash, 5" quad at hover | 3–5 K/W |

### 3.3 Transient response

Junction rise over case (reaches steady state within milliseconds):

ΔT_jc = 9.6 W × 1.5 K/W = **14.4 K**

Local spreading rise:

ΔT_spread ≈ 9.6 × 4 = **~38 K**

Board bulk rise for a burst of duration t (adiabatic, valid when t ≪ τ_board = R_θBA · C_board ≈ 15–50 s):

ΔT_board(t) ≈ P_total · t / C_board = 100 · t / 3.5 ≈ **28.6 K/s**

Junction temperature:

T_j(t) = T_amb + ΔT_board(t) + ΔT_spread + ΔT_jc

### 3.4 Burst budget

With T_amb = 40 °C and T_j,max derated to 150 °C (from the 175 °C rating):

40 + 28.6·t + 38 + 14.4 ≤ 150 → **t ≤ ~2.0 s**

Accounting for the R_ds(on) temperature coefficient feedback (which is already in k_T), the realistic budget is about **1.5–2 s at 80 A** from a 40 °C start.

### 3.5 Continuous rating with prop wash

With R_θBA = 4 K/W and P_total scaling as I²:

40 + 4·P_tot + spread + jc ≤ 150 → I_cont ≈ **30–35 A per motor**

### 3.6 Design actions

1. **Use parallel FETs.** Two FETs per switch, or 0.6 mΩ parts, roughly halves conduction loss and is the single largest lever.
2. **Maximize thermal mass and spreading.** Use 2 oz copper on the power planes and a via array under the drain tabs (0.3 mm drill, 0.8 mm pitch, about 20 vias per FET). Add a 0.5 mm aluminum heat-spreader plate with a thermal pad.
3. **Enforce limits in firmware.** Use the ESC NTC to cut current at 110 °C board temperature. In ArduPilot, set `BATT_MAX_AMPS` or use ESC-telemetry-based thermal throttling.
