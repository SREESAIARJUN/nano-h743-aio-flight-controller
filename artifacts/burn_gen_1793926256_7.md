# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points need settling before the math, because they change the design:

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors directly.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. The 4-in-1 ESC needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them, and they return eRPM telemetry. Driving 12 half-bridges from the FC MCU at 48 kHz with sensorless BEMF would compete with the EKF and scheduler. ArduPilot does not support it anyway.
2. **"80 A burst per motor" on a 20×20 mm stack is physically marginal.** The math below shows the thermal envelope at that size is about 1 s at 4×80 A. I size the reference design for **30.5×30.5 mm** and give 20×20 numbers for comparison.

**Assumptions (change these and re-run the equations):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 5×6. R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ max at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V. Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, R_θJC = 0.8 K/W, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Hot-resistance factor | k_T = 1.6 at T_j = 125 °C |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, t_r = t_f = 10 ns at gate-drive current ≈ 1.5 A, dead time t_dt = 50 ns |
| Copper path | R_cu = 0.3 mΩ per phase path (pad, plane, solder pad, motor pad) |
| Ambient | T_a = 40 °C. Prop-wash convection h ≈ 60 W/m²K |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

In 6-step synchronous operation, phase current always flows through exactly **one high-side and one low-side switch position**. During the PWM-off interval, the current freewheels through the complementary FET, which is on with sync rectification. The conduction path resistance is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left( 2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on)} + R_{cu} + R_{shunt} \right)$$

With R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ (one shared shunt per ESC, in the DC return):

$$P_{cond} = 80^2\,(2\cdot1.6\cdot1.0 + 0.3 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.75\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.0\ W/motor}$$

Breaking this down:

| Element | Loss |
|---|---|
| Each active FET (hot) | 80² × 1.6 mΩ = **10.24 W** |
| Copper | 1.92 W |
| Shunt | 1.60 W |

**Thermal runaway check.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so P is itself a function of T_j:

$$k_T(T_j) \approx 1 + \alpha (T_j - 25),\quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

The loop gain is ∂P/∂T × R_θ = I²·R_25·α·R_θJA. A runaway condition exists when this reaches 1. Locally: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.006 × 3 K/W ≈ 0.115, so the system is stable. It is still a 13% amplification, which is already captured by using k_T = 1.6.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg is PWM-switching at any time (the other active leg is static during a 60° sector).

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched (high-side FET):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period, in the low-side FET):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (dissipated mostly in the high-side FET at turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (both FETs in the switching leg; dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Switching total ≈ 1.43 W per motor.** At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by 17:1. Raising PWM frequency to 96 kHz for quieter motors costs only about 1.4 W more per motor at 80 A. Raising R_ds(on) by 0.5 mΩ costs 6.4 W. **Spend board area on FETs and copper, not on driver speed.**

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.48 W | 81.9 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.52 W | 14.1 W |
| Switching + diode + Q_rr + Q_oss | 1.38 W | 5.5 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.4 W** | **≈ 101.7 W** |

Hottest single FET is the switching high side: 10.24 + 0.97 + 0.06 + 0.05 ≈ **11.3 W**.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 this is about 1.8 kW per motor, giving ≈ 98.6% efficiency. The losses are small relative to throughput but large relative to a 10 g board.

## 4. Thermal Calculations

### 4.1 Heat capacity (lumped)

**30.5×30.5 mm board** (≈ 930 mm² usable, 6-layer, 2 oz on all layers = 70 µm, 80% copper fill):

- Copper: 6 × 0.070 × 930 × 0.8 = 312 mm³ → 2.80 g × 0.385 J/g·K = **1.08 J/K**
- FR-4, 1.6 mm: 1.49 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/g·K = **3.03 J/K**
- 24 FETs (2 per position for 80 A; see §4.4), ≈ 0.12 g each, c ≈ 0.6 J/g·K: **1.73 J/K**
- **C_th ≈ 5.8 J/K.** An optional 3 g aluminium heat spreader adds +2.7 J/K, giving 8.5 J/K.

**20×20 mm board** (12 FETs): C_th ≈ **1.9 J/K**.

### 4.2 Steady-state resistance to ambient

$$R_{\theta,SA} = \frac{1}{h A_{both\ sides}} = \frac{1}{60 \cdot 2 \cdot 9.3\text{e-}4} \approx 9\ \text{K/W}\ (30\times30)$$

**Continuous capability:** with ΔT_avg ≤ 60 K, P_cont = 60/9 ≈ 6.7 W total. Solving per motor:

I_cont ≈ √(1.67 W / 3.75 mΩ, effective with parallel FETs) ≈ **20–25 A continuous per motor**.

This is why "80 A" must be a burst rating.
