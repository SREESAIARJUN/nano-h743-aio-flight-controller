# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot Timer Allocation

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Key Constraints

Three constraints shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller. Each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 with bidirectional DShot/EDT. Four AT32F421K8U7 parts are used here; STM32G071 is a drop-in alternative. Running four 48 kHz 6-step/FOC loops and ArduPilot on one core is not a sound design.
2. **"Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A. The math below assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm hole pattern on a ~41×41 mm, 8-layer 2 oz PCB**. Section 3.5 shows how the 20×20 case scales.
3. **"80 A burst" means per-channel motor (phase) current for a limited time.** It is not continuous, and it is not battery current. The thermal model computes the allowable burst duration.
