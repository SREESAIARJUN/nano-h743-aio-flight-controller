## 0. Scope Assumptions and an Early Design Warning

Every number below depends on a stated assumption. Change the assumption and recompute with the given formulas.

| Parameter | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal | Worst-case switching uses 25.2 V |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel (phase current, motor DC-equivalent) | Burst only, not continuous |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32/BLHeli_32 style |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal | FOC changes the conduction duty math; see §2.5 |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack, 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers | "Nano" interpreted as a 30×30 AIO |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25°C = 0.9 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V | Example class: Infineon OptiMOS 6 40 V, Toshiba U-MOS IX-H 40 V |
| Gate drive | 10 V, ~1 A effective source/sink | Assumes a bootstrapped 3-phase driver |

**Architecture interpretation.** An STM32H743 does not drive the bridges directly. The H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 (or BLHeli_32), for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071, with a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to these MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for bi-directional DShot telemetry.

**Early warning.** The thermal model in §3 shows that 4 × 80 A at once on a 30×30 board is a burst rating of a few seconds, not a sustained capability. The math is presented so you can defend (or derate) the marketing number.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.24 mΩ**

Use the datasheet's normalized R_DS(on)-vs-T_j curve in place of α for sign-off.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss: 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Static leg:** one low-side FET, on for 100% of the sector.
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET (duty D) alternates with the complementary low-side FET (1 − D).

Ignoring dead time, the channel current always flows through exactly two FET channels. Including current ripple (I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[\underbrace{1}_{\text{static LS}} + \underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}}\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**Ripple check.** Typical 5″–7″ motor phase-to-phase inductance is L ≈ 20–40 µH. Using L = 30 µH at D = 0.5 and V = 25.2 V:

ΔI = V·D(1−D) / (L·f) = 25.2 × 0.25 / (30 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A

ΔI²/12 = 1.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². So I_rms ≈ 80 A.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.24\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.9\ W}
$$

**Parasitic copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect):**

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Per-channel shunt (if fitted) | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| PCB plane, vias, solder pads (effective series) | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |

Recommendation: use a single shared shunt on the VBAT return with a ≤0.25 mΩ Kelvin-sensed element, or use R_DS(on) sensing. That recovers about 2–3 W per channel.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**(a) V·I overlap loss**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{DS}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw},\qquad t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}
$$

With Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 15 nC and I_g ≈ 1 A, each edge takes about 15 ns, so t_r + t_f = 30 ns:

P_VI = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}
