# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #57)

**Scope:** This covers (1) conduction and switching loss models at an 80 A burst, (2) transient thermal behavior, (3) the ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation on the H743, and (4) reference netlists.

**Assumptions:** I don't have your actual BOM, stack-up, or layout. Every parameter below is a stated assumption. Swap in your datasheet values, and the equations still hold.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no ESC firmware. A 4-in-1 AIO therefore has two domains:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz | ArduPilot | Sends DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 / BLHeli_32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Power stage ×4 | 6 N-MOSFETs + 3-phase gate driver | n/a | Half-bridges |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter. DShot600 is the FC-to-ESC link.

**Form-factor warning:** "Nano" usually means a 20×20 mm board. 24 MOSFETs in 5×6 mm packages do not fit at 20×20; that footprint forces 3×3 mm FETs at Rds(on) ≈ 2.5–4 mΩ. The model below uses 5×6 FETs on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. Section 3 then shows why even that board cannot sustain 80 A for more than seconds.
