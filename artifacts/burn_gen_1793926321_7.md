# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model (Job #59)

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every viable 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and drive the gate drivers. Section 3 covers the H743 side, and Sections 1–2 cover the ESC MCU and power stage. Merging the two would be a poor design: commutation ISR jitter would compete with the EKF and IMU loops.

**2. 80 A on a 20×20 "nano" board is a seconds-long transient rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model below shows this quantitatively. I give the maximum burst duration rather than repeat a marketing number.

**3. Device parameters are design assumptions, not a specific BOM.** I use a generic 40 V N-channel DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET. Substitute datasheet values for your chosen part, since every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Assumed parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| R_ds25 | R_DS(on) max at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.005 /°C (≈1.5× at 125 °C) |
| T_j | Assumed junction temperature | 100 °C |
| R_Cu | PCB copper path per phase (pad to pad) | 0.3 mΩ |

Temperature-corrected on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] = 1.0\,(1+0.005\cdot75) = 1.375\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (AM32 default)

At any instant two phases conduct:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for D·T. The complementary low side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side is on continuously.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous six-step.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.375\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Copper loss (two phase paths plus shared ground and bus return, approximated as 2·R_Cu):

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries current for 1/3 of the time, and the pattern rotates across six switches. The average loss per FET is:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{17.6}{6} = 2.9\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous loss is 8.8 W, in the return-leg low-side FET during its 120° window. At low eRPM, for example during a stall or a hard punch-out from hover, that 120° window lasts long enough to approach steady-state Z_th. **Low-RPM high current is therefore the worst case for an individual FET.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one hard turn-on and one turn-off of the high side.

### 2.1 Components

**(a) V–I overlap loss**, with t_r + t_f = 20 ns (FD6288-class driver, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, with t_dt = 100 ns per edge and V_f = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**, with Q_rr ≈ 50 nC at this di/dt:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**, with Q_oss ≈ 40 nC:

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate charge**, with Q_g = 60 nC, V_GS = 10 V, and two FETs per leg:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.2 Totals per channel at 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 17.60 |
| Copper | 3.84 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.2 W** |

**All 4 channels at 80 A: ≈ 93 W.**

Switching loss is under 8% of the total. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 0.8 W per channel. In return it reduces current ripple and motor iron/copper ripple loss
