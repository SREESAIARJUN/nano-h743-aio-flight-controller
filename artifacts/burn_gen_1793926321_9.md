## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Feasibility Flags

Three constraints shape the design before any math.

1. **The H743 cannot run motor commutation.** ArduPilot is a flight controller. It does not perform 6-step or FOC commutation. Each motor therefore needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This doc assumes **4× STM32G071GBU6 running AM32**.

2. **"Nano" and "80 A" are in tension.** On a 20×20 mm footprint, 80 A per channel cannot be made thermally or electrically credible. The 6 oz-equivalent copper needed for 320 A aggregate will not fit, and neither will 24 power FETs. The baseline is therefore a **30.5×30.5 mm two-board stack** (FC on top, ESC below). 80 A is treated strictly as a **burst rating, valid for seconds**. Section 3 derives the actual limit.

3. **Every number below rests on stated component assumptions.** Substitute your chosen FET datasheet values into the equations. The model structure stays the same.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous-rectified commutation

In any of the six commutation sectors, two phases carry current. One leg is PWM-modulated with complementary synchronous rectification. The other leg's low-side FET is held on.

- During D·T_sw, current flows through high-side (leg A) → motor → low-side (leg B).
- During (1−D)·T_sw, current flows through low-side (leg A, synchronous rectification) → motor → low-side (leg B).

At every instant, **exactly two FET channels plus the shunt and copper** are in series with the phase current, independent of D. Per channel:

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = I_{rms}^2\left[2\,R_{25}\,k(T_j) + R_{sh} + R_{cu}\right]
$$

The R_DS(on) temperature dependence can be linearized:

$$
k(T_j) \approx 1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25),\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{gives } 1.55 \text{ at } 110^\circ\text{C})
$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The phase current is DC plus a triangular ripple Δi, so

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta i = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" to 7" motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH (line-to-line ≈ 2L_ph = 30 µH effective), the worst case is D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta i = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\times10^{-6}\times48\,000} = 4.4\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta i^2}{12} = 1.6\ \text{A}^2
$$

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80 A. The 48 kHz frequency is doing its job here: ripple stays small, which keeps both rms loss and acoustic noise down.
