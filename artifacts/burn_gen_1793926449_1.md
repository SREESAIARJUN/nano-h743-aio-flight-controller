## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A DShot-based design is split across two kinds of processor:

| Block | Device (assumed) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100, 480 MHz) | ArduPilot. Sends DShot600 commands and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32 | 6-step commutation with 48 kHz complementary PWM. Each one replies to the FC with eRPM. |
| Gate drivers | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | Drive the high-side and low-side N-FETs. |
| Power FETs | 24 × 40 V N-FET, PDFN3.3×3.3 | Assumed: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C (V_GS = 10 V), Q_g = 45 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF, t_r = t_f ≈ 20 ns in this layout. |
| Supply | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum | |

All loss analysis below applies to the ESC power stage. The DShot analysis applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM
At any moment, two phases carry current:
- one leg is PWM-switched, with its high-side and low-side FETs alternating;
- the other leg has its low-side FET held on.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly **two FET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle. This ignores dead time, which is covered in §2.3.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.38 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** |

### 1.3 Distribution across the six FETs
Over one electrical cycle, each leg conducts for 2/3 of the time. The share between high-side and low-side depends on duty cycle D. As an average approximation:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W at } 125\,°\text{C}$$

The worst-case FET is the low-side FET at low duty cycle, where the low-side FETs conduct most of the time. Budget it at about 1.5 × the average, which is **≈ 4.8 W peak per FET**.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses
- **Shunt.** For a 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying total current I_tot = 4 × 80 = 320 A, I²R = **25.6 W**. A single shunt at this current is impractical. Either use 4 per-channel shunts (4 × 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 6.4 W total) or use battery-side sensing only.
- **Trace and pad resistance.** On a 6-layer stack with 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner copper, assume roughly 0.3 mΩ per channel. That gives 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W per channel**.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched leg per channel, with V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Capacitive (C_oss) loss, two FETs per leg

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction
Assume t_dt = 100 ns per edge, two edges per period, and V_f = 0.8 V.

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot45\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.15 |
| Copper | 1.90 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.8 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 95 W** |

Efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D_eff. At full throttle that is about 8 kW, so the electrical efficiency is fine (≈ 98.8 %). **The constraint is heat density, not efficiency.**

### 2.7 Sensitivity to f_sw
Switching-related loss scales linearly with frequency:

- 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel.
- 96 kHz costs about 2.6 W per channel.

Since conduction dominates at 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for acoustics and current ripple on low-inductance motors.
