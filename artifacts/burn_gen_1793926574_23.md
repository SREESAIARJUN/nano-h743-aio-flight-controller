# STM32H743 Nano Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17)
## Electrothermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, and in this one, the architecture is:

```
LiPo 6S ──► Power stage (24 FETs, 4×3 half-bridges)
               ▲ gate drive
          4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
               ▲ bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire per motor)
          STM32H743 FC (ArduPilot)
```

The H743 has no complementary-PWM/BEMF resources to spare for 4 motors alongside ArduPilot's scheduler. Its job is DShot generation and eRPM telemetry capture.

**Baseline assumptions.** All numbers below scale from these. Substitute your actual BOM values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching) |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class) | R_ds(on),25°C | 0.70 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | k_T | ×1.6 at T_j = 125 °C, so R_hot ≈ 1.12 mΩ |
| Gate–drain charge | Q_gd | 10 nC |
| Post-threshold gate–source charge | Q_gs2 | 5 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Body diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss(tr) | 1.5 nF |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Effective gate drive current | I_g | ≈1.5 A (FD6288-class driver) |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-motor instantaneous loss

In 6-step commutation, current flows through two legs at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts during the on-time D. The synchronous low-side (LS) FET conducts during the off-time (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held fully on.

Because the PWM leg is synchronous, exactly two channels are always in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left(2R_{ds,hot} + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right)
$$

FETs only:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.12\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.3\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Copper and shunt contributions:

- R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per conduction path (2 oz outer, 20×20 board, short pours) adds **1.9 W**.
- A 0.25 mΩ current shunt (if fitted) adds **1.6 W**.

$$
P_{cond,motor,total} \approx \mathbf{17.8\ W}
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET average

Each FET conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle (1/3 duty):

$$
\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 1.12\times10^{-3}}{3} = \mathbf{2.39\ W}
$$

At 80 A burst the electrical frequency is typically above 500 Hz. The per-FET thermal time constant (around 1 ms or more for the die plus clip) therefore averages the 120° pulses, and the average value is valid for T_j estimation. Add about 5 K of ripple margin.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_ds depends on T_j, so iterate:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/\text{K}
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + \bar{P}_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta jc}
$$

With R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W, the junction sits only about 3–4 K above the PCB. **The PCB, not the die, is the thermal bottleneck** (see §4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Per motor, per PWM period:

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Transition time is set by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}
$$

This is the ideal value. Doubling it to **20 ns** accounts for common-source inductance and V_gs plateau droop:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

Q_rr also adds about 10–20 % to turn-on current overshoot. That stress is absorbed in the derating of §2.1.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(tr)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\times 635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This is the largest secondary term. Trimming dead time to 50 ns (if gate-loop layout allows) halves it.

### 2.5 Gate drive

Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per motor

| Term | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 |
| **Total switching** | **2.71** |

**Effect of the 48 kHz choice.** Switching losses scale linearly with frequency. Compared with 24 kHz this costs about +1.35 W per motor. In return it halves current ripple, which matters on low-inductance motors (phase L < 20 µH) common on nano builds.
