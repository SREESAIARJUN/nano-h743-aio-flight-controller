**Architecture note.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each motor channel requires its own ESC MCU doing commutation and generating the bidirectional DShot (bdshot) eRPM reply. The H743's job is DShot generation and capture via timer DMA (Section 4).

**Key finding.** 4×80 A on a nano footprint can only be a **burst rating of roughly 1–3 seconds**. It is not a continuous rating. The math in Section 3 shows this. Please do not let marketing collapse "burst" into "continuous" on the datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)}(125) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Conduction topology (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

In any 60° sector, current flows from the high side of phase A to the low side of phase B. Three devices are involved:

- The high side HS_A conducts for duty D.
- Its synchronous partner LS_A conducts for 1−D, minus dead time.
- The low side LS_B conducts continuously.

The instantaneous conducting path is therefore always **two series devices**, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu} \;+\; I^2 R_{shunt}$$

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

**Copper.** Take 2 oz copper (70 µm, sheet resistance ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, ≈ 0.33 mΩ/□ hot). The effective phase path is ≈ 0.6 squares on a 2-layer parallel pour:

$$R_{Cu} \approx 0.33 \times 0.6 / 2 \approx 0.1\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Shunt.** A per-channel low-side or bus shunt of 0.25 mΩ gives 20 mV at 80 A.

### 1.4 Numerical result at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs | 80² × 2 × 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |
| Copper | 80² × 0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Total conduction** | | **≈ 31.0 W** |

Per-device average: each of the 6 FETs carries the "always-on" low-side role for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and shares PWM roles otherwise. Electrical frequency (≈ 1–3 kHz) is far above the thermal corner of the die, so averaging is valid:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 28.8/6 = 4.8\,\text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWMing high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side switches at near-zero voltage, because the body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

The transition times come from gate charge and driver current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{drive}} \approx \frac{(5 + 9)\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} \approx 14\,\text{ns} \;\rightarrow\; \text{use 15 ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the sync FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so recheck it at 80 A against the datasheet curve. It can be 2–3× the headline value.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per channel

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 1.45 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Coss | 0.02 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.1 W. Conduction dominates by roughly 14:1, so 48 kHz is affordable. The real lever is R_DS(on).
