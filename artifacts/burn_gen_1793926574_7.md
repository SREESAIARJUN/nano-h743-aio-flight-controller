# STM32H743 Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Engineering Model

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

I don't have access to a "Job #31" specification, so these are my working assumptions. Correct any that are wrong and the numbers below can be re-run.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| ESC rating | 80 A burst per channel (≤10 s), 60 A continuous |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (AM32/BLHeli_32-style) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, ~0.7 mΩ @25 °C, PQFN 5×6 (BSC007N04LS6-class), 6 per channel, 24 total |
| PCB | 8-layer, 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper |
| ArduPilot | 4.5+, ChibiOS, bidirectional DShot600 |

**Two items need design review before anything else:**

1. **Form factor.** A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot dissipate 4 × 80 A. Section 2 shows roughly 60 W total during a full burst. That needs a 30.5×30.5 mm board at minimum, realistically a split FC + ESC stack. Everything below assumes **30.5×30.5 mm with 24 FETs on the ESC board**.
2. **The H743 does not drive the gates.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) doing commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot to those MCUs. The switching model in §2 applies to the ESC MCU's output stage; the DMA model in §3 applies to the H743.

## 1. Electrical Model of One ESC Channel

### 1.1 Conduction paths (6-step, complementary PWM, duty D)

During any commutation step, two phases conduct:

- Phase A high-side (HS_A) switches with duty D.
- Phase A low-side (LS_A) conducts the freewheel current during (1−D), via synchronous rectification.
- Phase B low-side (LS_B) is fully on for the entire step.

Instantaneous conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\left[R_{HS}\,D + R_{LS}(1-D) + R_{LS}\right] \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

This approximation holds because R_HS ≈ R_LS for identical FETs, so the result is independent of D.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{2.3}$$

| Tj | Rds(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.18 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.37 mΩ |

Because loss rises with temperature, iterate to a self-consistent Tj (§4.4).

### 1.3 Copper path resistance (often neglected, frequently dominant)

Using sheet resistance ρ_□ = 0.17 mΩ/□ at 3 oz (ρ = 1.72e-8 Ω·m, t = 105 µm), with α_Cu = 0.00393/K:

| Path segment | Squares | R @25 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Battery pad → bus plane | 2 | 0.17 mΩ |
| Bus → HS drain (per FET) | 1.5 | 0.13 mΩ |
| Phase node → motor pad | 3 | 0.26 mΩ |
| LS source → GND plane + shunt | 1 | 0.09 mΩ + R_shunt (0.25 mΩ) |
| **Total R_Cu, loop of 2 phases** | | **≈ 0.9 mΩ** |

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}(T) \approx 6400 \times 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.3 \approx 7.5\ \text{W per channel at 80 A}$$

This is a real number. Copper loss is comparable to the FET loss, so treat the PCB as a heat source, not only a heatsink.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Constants for this section: V_in = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, HS FET only)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times are set by the gate driver (~2 A source / 3 A sink) charging Q_gd ≈ 12 nC:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{source}} = 6\ \text{ns},\quad t_f \approx 4\ \text{ns}$$

Add ~60% derating for common-source inductance in PQFN, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 16 ns:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(16\times10^{-9})(48\,000) = 0.77\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode conduction during dead time

With t_dt = 100 ns per edge, 2 edges per cycle, and V_f ≈ 0.8 V at 80 A:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\times10^{-9})(48\,000) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, induces HS turn-on loss)

Q_rr ≈ 60 nC at di/dt ≈ 1 A/ns:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 60\times10^{-9}(25.2)(48\,000) = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (Coss) loss

Q_oss ≈ 70 nC; for HS + LS on the switching leg, the energy is ≈ Q_oss·V:

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} = 70\times10^{-9}(25.2)(48\,000) = 0.085\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (lost in the driver, not the FET)

Q_g = 55 nC, V_gs = 10 V, 2 FETs switching:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(55\times10^{-9})(10)(48\,000) = 0.053\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel loss budget at 80 A, Tj = 110 °C

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| FET conduction, 2I²R, Rds = 1.25 mΩ | 16.0 W |
| Copper conduction | 7.5 W |
| V–I overlap | 0.77 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Coss | 0.09 W |
| Qrr | 0.07 W |
| Gate (driver) | 0.05 W |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.1 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneously** | **≈ 100 W** |

ESC efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 = 2016 W, so η ≈ 98.8% per channel.

### 2.7 Effect of switching frequency

The frequency-dependent terms total ~1.6 W per channel at 48 kHz versus ~0.8 W at 24 kHz.

At 80 A, conduction dominates (~94% of loss). Lowering f_sw buys almost nothing thermally. Keep 48 kHz for audible noise and current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{in} D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 2.2 A pp at 48 kHz, versus 4.4 A at 24 kHz.
