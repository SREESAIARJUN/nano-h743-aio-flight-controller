# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): System Architecture, Loss Model and Timer Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three findings shape everything below.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 should not commutate the motors. Production 4-in-1 ESCs use one small MCU per channel (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over DShot. | Architecture: H743 → 4× DShot600 (bidirectional) → 4× ESC MCU → gate driver → 6 FETs per channel. If the H743 drove the FETs directly, DShot would not exist in the design. |
| F2 | "80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board dissipates about **130 W across 24 FETs** (§2). On a board of about 4 g the heat-up rate is roughly **50 K/s**. | 80 A is survivable for **≈1 s** from a warm start, not as a sustained rating. A credible spec is **80 A / ≤1 s burst, ~25–30 A continuous with airflow**. For a longer burst, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs. |
| F3 | 48 kHz PWM is high for this current. Conduction losses dominate (~95 %), so frequency costs relatively little here. It does cost more at mid-throttle and at higher bus voltage. | Allow 24 / 48 kHz as a runtime setting (AM32 supports this). |

**Assumptions** (replace with datasheet values when the BOM is frozen):

- Bus voltage: 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V.
- FETs: 40 V N-channel, DFN 3.3×3.3, with R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ at 25 °C and 10 V V_GS.
- Switching: Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss = 1.5 nF, t_r = t_f = 15 ns.
- Ambient / start temperature: 40 °C.

## 1. Electrical Topology Per ESC Channel

Each channel is a 3-phase half-bridge (Q1–Q6) driven by complementary PWM with synchronous rectification. Six-step trapezoidal commutation is used.

In any commutation state:
- One phase is PWM'd (high-side / low-side complementary pair).
- One phase has its low-side FET held ON.
- The third phase floats, which is where back-EMF is sensed.

**Consequence:** the motor current always flows through exactly **two** FET channels:
- (HS for time D, or LS synchronous for time 1−D), plus
- the static LS FET.

Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 × 1.45 ≈ **2.2 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Phase current in a trapezoidal drive is nearly flat at I during conduction, so I_rms ≈ I per active path.

**PWM'd leg:**

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{DS}\,D, \qquad P_{LS,sync} = I^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

**Static low-side FET:**

$$P_{LS,static} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

**Total conduction per channel** (dead-time term neglected):

$$P_{cond} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

With I = 80 A, R_DS = 2.2 mΩ, and R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ (2 oz copper plus pads plus solder):

- FETs: 2 × 6400 × 0.0022 = **28.2 W**
- Copper: 6400 × 0.0005 = **3.2 W**

> Note: duty D drops out of the total. At 80 A the channel loses about 28 W whether the throttle is at 40 % or 100 %. That is why bursts are thermally hard.

### 2.3 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg, inductive load)

Overlap loss:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Reverse recovery of the complementary FET's body diode:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

Output capacitance:

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\times 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

Dead-time body-diode conduction (V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 50 ns, two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

Gate drive (two FETs switching):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.4 Per-channel and board totals at the 80 A burst

| Term | W per channel | W per board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 28.2 | 112.8 |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| Switching (overlap) | 1.45 | 5.8 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 | 0.4 |
| Dead time | 0.31 | 1.2 |
| **Total** | **≈33.3** | **≈133** |

**Efficiency check:**
- Input power ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 0.9 that is about 1.8 kW per channel.
- Loss is about 33 W, so efficiency is about 98 % electrically.
- The efficiency is fine. The *power density* is what kills a nano board.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx (1.45+0.41)/48\text{k} \approx 39\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.9 W per channel at 80 A.
- 48 kHz is justified for low-KV quietness and current-ripple reasons, not thermal ones.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, lumped)

```
Tj --[Rth_jc 1.2 K/W ; Cth_j ~0.02 J/K]-- Tcase --[Rth_cb 0.5 K/W]-- PCB node
PCB node: Cth_pcb ≈ m·c = 4 g × 0.6 J/(g·K) ≈ 2.4 J/K (whole stack, shared by 24 FETs + H743)
PCB → ambient: Rth_ba ≈ 8 K/W still air, ≈ 3 K/W prop-wash
```

### 3.2 Burst transient (board node dominates)

$$\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} = \frac{P_{tot} - (T_{pcb}-T_a)/R_{ba}}{C_{pcb}} \approx \frac{133}{2.4} \approx \mathbf{55\ K/s}$$

The loss to air is negligible in the first second.

Each FET dissipates about 14 W while it is in the active pair. Commutation rotates the duty so that each FET averages about 1/3 of the time, giving about **4.7 W average** per FET.

**Junction rise above PCB:**
- Instantaneous worst case: 14 W × Z_th,jc(10 ms) ≈ 14 × 0.6 ≈ **8 K**.
- Average-based: 4.7 W × 1.7 K/W ≈ **8 K**.

**Limit:** T_j,max design = 125 °C (rated 150–175 °C, derated).

$$T_{pcb,limit} \approx 125 - 8 - 2(\text{margin}) \approx 115\,°C$$

$$t_{burst} = \frac{115-40}{55} \approx \mathbf{1.4\ s}\ \text{(cold start)};\quad \approx 0.8\ \text{s from a 70 °C hover state}$$
