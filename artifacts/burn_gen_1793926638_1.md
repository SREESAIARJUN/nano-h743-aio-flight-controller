# Job #53: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC, Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Several requirements conflict with physics or with established practice. These findings constrain everything that follows.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. Four 3-phase bridges need 24 complementary gate signals, 4 BEMF comparator sets and per-channel current sensing. No production design does this, and ArduPilot has no firmware for it. | Architecture: H743 flight controller plus 4 dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600. 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. |
| F2 | 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 board is a **burst rating of a few seconds at most**. Steady-state dissipation is about 80–95 W. The board can reject about 6–10 W continuously. | 80 A is specified as a ≤2 s burst from ≤50 °C board temperature. Realistic continuous rating is 25–35 A per channel. |
| F3 | 4 × 80 A = 320 A from the pack. XT60 connectors (~60 A continuous) and 12 AWG leads cannot carry this. | Simultaneous 80 A on all four channels is not a valid operating point. The burst model assumes asymmetric load (yaw or punch transients). |
| F4 | A 20×20 board cannot hold an H743 (LQFP100/TFBGA) **and** 24 FETs plus drivers. | 2-board stack: FC 20×20 on top, 4-in-1 ESC 20×20 (or 25.5×25.5) below, with an 8-pin JST-SH interconnect. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

This expression makes conduction loss depend on T_j. Section 4 checks it for thermal runaway.

### 2.2 Per-channel conduction loss

The current path always passes through 2 switches, either as the active pair or as the PWM switch plus its synchronous partner:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET average

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0015}{3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

During its conducting third, one FET dissipates I²R = **9.6 W**. This instantaneous value matters for junction transients when the eRPM is low (stall or startup).

### 2.4 PCB copper loss (often omitted, significant at 80 A)

Assume a phase plus bus path of 0.4 mΩ with 2 oz copper, 4 layers paralleled.

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.56\ W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one FET per channel hard-switches per PWM cycle. Its synchronous partner commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) \, f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.326\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.015\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (6 FETs per channel; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \times 30\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.086\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching sensitivity to f_sw

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw,total}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 28\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- At 24 kHz, switching-related loss drops by about 0.67 W per channel.
- At 96 kHz, it rises by about 1.3 W per channel.
- At 80 A, conduction loss dominates by roughly 15:1, so 48 kHz is an acceptable choice.

## 4. Loss Totals and Thermal Model

### 4.1 Loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 2.56 W | 10.2 W |
| Overlap switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.33 W | 1.3 W |
| Qrr + Qoss + gate | 0.14 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.2 W** | **≈ 92.7 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Channel output power is about V_bus·I·D. At D = 1 that is 2016 W, giving η ≈ 98.9 %. The efficiency looks excellent, but the loss density is about 23 W/cm², which is the real limit.

### 4.2 Thermal network (Cauer approximation, per hottest FET)

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]──●──[Rθ_spread 6 K/W]──●──[Rθ_board-amb (shared)]── T_amb
                       C_j 0.05 J/K          C_local 0.4 J/K        C_board 4 J/K
```

- **R_θ,ba (whole board to ambient):**
  - 8 K/W in prop-wash airflow of about 5 m/s.
  - 20 K/W in still air.
- **C_board:** about 4.5 g of FR4, copper and components at about 0.9 J/(g·K), giving ≈ 4 J/K.

### 4.3 Steady state (proves 80 A cannot be continuous)

$$\Delta T_{board} = 92.7 \times 8 = 742\ \text{K} \quad\Rightarrow \text{impossible}$$

The maximum continuous board dissipation for T_board ≤ 100 °C at T_amb = 35 °C is:

$$P_{max} = 65 / 8 \approx 8.1\ \text{W total}$$

Back-solving for the current with all 4 channels equal:

$$I_{cont} = \sqrt{\frac{8.1/4}{2R + R_{Cu}}} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.03}{3.4\text{m}\Omega}} \approx \mathbf{24\ A}$$

This assumes all four channels run simultaneously. With a heatsink or a frame-coupled thermal pad, expect about 30–35 A.
