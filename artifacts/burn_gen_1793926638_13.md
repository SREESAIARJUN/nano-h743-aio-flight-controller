# STM32H743 Nano Stack: 80 A Burst Power Stage, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**The H743 cannot be the ESC MCU.** It should run ArduPilot as the flight controller. The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCUs, typically one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32. ArduPilot generates and receives bidirectional DShot on the FC side only. This document models:

- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot, generating bidirectional DShot600 via timer and DMA.
- **ESC:** 4 independent channels, each with an ESC MCU, a 3-phase gate driver, 6 MOSFETs, a 6S bus, and 48 kHz complementary PWM.

**Key findings, stated up front:**

1. At 80 A per channel, conduction loss dominates. It is about 23 W per channel at hot junction, versus about 1.7 W for switching.
2. On a 20×20 mm nano board, all four channels at 80 A (~100 W) heats the board at roughly 38 K/s. That is a **<2 s event**, not a rating. A single channel at 80 A gives about 7 s.
3. The shared current shunt becomes a dominant loss at 4×80 A if sized conventionally.
4. Bidirectional DShot600 on the H743 needs 4 DMA streams. With DMAMUX this is easy, but the BIDIR channel-pairing rule in hwdef must be followed.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology

With six-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification, two phases carry current at any instant:

- During t_on: the high-side (HS) FET of phase A and the low-side (LS) FET of phase B conduct.
- During t_off: the LS FET of phase A (synchronous) and the LS FET of phase B conduct.

So **exactly two channel resistances are in series with the motor current at all times**, independent of duty cycle. Dead-time intervals are the only exception (treated in §3).
