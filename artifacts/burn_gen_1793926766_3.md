## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

Three issues need resolving before the math can be trusted.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It is the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. | The H743 FC drives 4× DShot600 lines to the ESC board, which uses 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) MCUs running AM32. The **48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.** |
| 2 | **"Nano" at 80A burst.** A 20×20 mm ESC cannot dissipate the ~90 W calculated in §2 for more than about 1–2 s. | Use a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint with a 20×20 FC stacked or adapted. If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 45A burst. |
| 3 | **No MOSFET or battery was specified.** | Every equation below is parametric. The worked numbers assume the reference values listed next. Substitute your datasheet values. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

During each 60° commutation sector of trapezoidal drive:

- One high-side FET is PWM'd with duty D. Its complementary low-side FET conducts during (1−D) through synchronous rectification.
- One low-side FET in another leg is held fully on.

So **exactly two FET channels carry I at every instant**, independent of D (dead time excluded):
