# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and ArduPilot BDShot Timer Plan

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three assumptions in the brief would cause failures if left as written.

| Issue | Reality | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| H743 drives the ESC directly | ArduPilot has no FOC/6-step commutation for gate drivers. The H743 cannot also run four motor-control loops at 48 kHz. | Use the H743 as the flight controller. Add **4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071)** ESC MCUs running **AM32**, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot natively. |
| 80 A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board | The analysis below shows ~88 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. A 20×20 board has too little thermal mass for any useful burst. | The model assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** ESC stacked under a 20×20 FC. 80 A is a **≤2 s burst** rating, not continuous. |
| Bidirectional DShot on any timer pin | Input capture is **impossible on complementary (CHxN) outputs**. | All motor outputs go on TIMx_CH1–CH4 (non-N) pins. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In any commutation sector, current flows through one PWM leg and one static low-side FET.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Static low-side:** conducts for the full period T.

$$P_{cond} = D\,I^2R + (1-D)\,I^2R + I^2R = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is the main benefit of synchronous rectification.

With R_hot = k_T · R_DS(on),25 = 1.5 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

Per-FET peak (static low-side): 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**, during a 120° electrical window.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched leg)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.94\text{ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

**(d) C_oss (both FETs in the leg)**

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.05\text{ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 2.7 W per channel.**

### 3.1 Loss Budget

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.7 W | 10.9 W |
| Shunt (0.1 mΩ per channel) | 0.64 W | 2.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.5 W** | **≈ 90 W** |

Average per FET: losses rotate across the three legs, so each FET dissipates ≈ 22.5 / 6 ≈ **3.75 W**. The electrical frequency is above 1 kHz, so the junction effectively sees the average plus a small ripple.

**Design insight:** conduction dominates by about 7:1. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only ~1.3 W per channel. Halving R_DS(on), for example by paralleling or moving to 5×6 mm packages, saves 9.6 W per channel. Keep 48 kHz for acoustic and ripple benefits.
