## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**. Use this hot value for worst-case design.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous drive

With complementary PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two conducting switches:
- during the PWM-on interval: HS of the driven leg plus LS of the return leg;
- during the PWM-off interval: synchronous LS plus LS of the return leg.

The result is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The 48 kHz switching adds a triangular ripple on top of the phase current:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A (worst case, } D=0.5)$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (<0.1 %) at 80 A.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6404 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts about one third of an electrical cycle. Its average is therefore 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**.

Because the die thermal time constant (~ms) exceeds the commutation period at flight eRPM, the average value is valid for junction-rise calculations.
