# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #32)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Critical Finding

### 0.1 Architecture clarification

On a 4-in-1 design the **STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU**. The ESC half uses four dedicated small MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (for example STM32G071 or AT32F421 parts). The H743 does not commutate motors. It generates DShot600 and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. This package covers both halves:

- Power stage and thermal (ESC side)
- Timer and DMA allocation (H743 side)

### 0.3 Critical finding (stated up front)

**"80 A burst per channel" is not physically sustainable on a 20×20 mm board for more than about 1 second.** The derivation is in Section 3. Before layout, the spec owner must choose one of three options:

- **(a)** Redefine the rating as 80 A *total* (20 A per channel). This works on 20×20.
- **(b)** Keep 80 A per channel but limit the burst to ≤ 0.5–1 s and enforce it with firmware current limiting.
- **(c)** Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with a heat spreader.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which AM32 uses by default, two phases conduct in each 60° sector:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side switch (HS) conducts for duty D. The low-side switch (LS) synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Return phase:** the LS stays fully on.

Because of synchronous rectification, **exactly two MOSFET channels carry the phase current I at every instant**. So conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.34 ≈ **2.0 mΩ**. A conservative design value is **2.25 mΩ** (k = 1.5).

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, scaled by D or (1 − D). With load sharing over 6 FETs:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{3} \quad \text{(averaged over a revolution)}$$

The peak per-FET loss within a sector is the full I²R. Use that peak for low-eRPM and stall cases, where sectors last milliseconds and approach the thermal time constant of the die.

### 1.4 Copper conduction

Add copper loss on the phase and bus paths:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}$$

Assume 6 layers of 2 oz copper, about 4 mm effective width, and 10 mm path length. Then R_trace ≈ 0.25 mΩ at 20 °C and about 0.33 mΩ hot.

- At 80 A: **≈ 2.1 W per channel**, roughly 7 % of FET conduction loss. **This cannot be ignored on a nano board.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase switches. Per channel, one HS/LS pair commutates per PWM cycle.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (HS hard switching)

Transition times:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{14\text{ nC}}{0.7\text{ A}} = 20\text{ ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

At 80 A: ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw}$$

This is ½CV² per FET, for both FETs in the leg: 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.046 W**
