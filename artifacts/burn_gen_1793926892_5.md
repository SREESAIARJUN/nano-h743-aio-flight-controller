# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model (Job #20)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not drive the MOSFETs directly. The 4-in-1 ESC uses a dedicated ESC MCU per motor (or one multi-channel MCU), running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically on an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire.

```
6S LiPo ─┬─ 4× 3-phase bridge (24 FETs) ─ Motors 1–4
         │        ▲ gate drive (FD6288Q ×4)
         │   ESC MCU ×4 (AT32F421) ◄── bidir DShot600 ──► STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
         └─ 10V/5V/3V3 BECs
```

**Model parameters.** These are representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm MOSFET of the class that fits a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 nano layout. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |

| I_burst | 80 A per motor | burst phase current |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ (max) | at V_gs = 10 V |
| α | 0.005 /°C | R_ds(on) tempco |

> **Critical flag up front:** 80 A per motor (320 A total, about 8 kW) on a nano-format board cannot be sustained. Section 3 shows the result is a *time-limited* burst rating of roughly 1–2 s, governed by thermal capacitance rather than thermal resistance. If "80A burst" means the **total board** current (20 A per motor), the design is comfortable. Section 3.5 covers that case.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005·100) = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in trapezoidal + complementary PWM

At any instant, two phases are active:

- **Fixed leg.** One FET is on for the full 60° sector and carries I.
- **Switched leg.** The high-side FET carries I for duty D. The low-side FET (synchronous rectification) carries I for (1 − D).

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

The result is independent of duty, because synchronous rectification removes the diode path.

**Ripple correction.** Phase current ripple adds a term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With motor L_phase ≈ 10 µH, V = 25.2 V, D = 0.5, and f = 48 kHz:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V\,D(1-D)}{L f} \approx 13\text{ A}$$

The correction is ΔI²/12 ≈ 14 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

- **Average per FET:** P_cond,motor / 6 ≈ 3.2 W.
- **Instantaneous peak per FET:** 9.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.69\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Total switching loss per motor

$$P_{sw} \approx 1.69 + 0.05 + 0.05 + 0.61 + 0.04 = \mathbf{2.44\ W}$$

### 2.7 Sensitivity to switching frequency

| f_sw | P_sw at 80 A | P_sw/P_total |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.22 W | 6 % |
| 48 kHz | 2.44 W | 11 % |
| 96 kHz | 4.88 W | 20 % |

**Conclusion:** at 80 A, conduction loss dominates (~89 %). The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower acoustic noise and current ripple at small cost. Dead time is the largest secondary loss, so tighten it to 50–70 ns if the driver and FET allow.
