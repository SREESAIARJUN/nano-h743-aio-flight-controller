# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #43): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and One Up-Front Finding

**Architecture split.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) driving a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The models below follow that split.

**Baseline assumptions.** Change these and re-run the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET class | — | 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3, R_ds(on) 1.2 mΩ max @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 0.006 /K (gives ≈1.6× at 125 °C) |
| Gate charge Q_g / Q_gd / Q_gs2 | — | 60 nC / 12 nC / 8 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss,eq | 1.5 nF |
| Body-diode Q_rr / V_f | — | 40 nC / 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_d | 200 ns per edge |
| Gate-drive loop | — | 10 V drive, R_g,ext 2.2 Ω, R_g,int 1 Ω, driver ~0.8 Ω |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

> **Finding:** 4 × 80 A burst on a 20×20 "nano" board is not thermally credible beyond about 1 second. Section 3 quantifies this. The math supports one of three options:
> - (a) 80 A burst as a per-motor rating for ≤1 s,
> - (b) moving to 30.5×30.5 with paralleled FETs, or
> - (c) re-rating to roughly 35–45 A per channel.
>
> Treat everything below as an engineering model to validate with thermocouple and IR testing, not as a certified design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step commutation

In any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side leg (PWM'd) and one low-side leg (held on).

With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **Active high-side FET:** $P = D\,I^2 R_{ds}$
- **Synchronous low-side FET (same leg):** $P = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds}$
- **Static low-side FET (return leg):** $P = I^2 R_{ds}$

The sum is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(80^2)(1.92\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The average per-FET conduction loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{24.6}{6} \approx \mathbf{4.1\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak in a sector is ~12.3 W. That is relevant to Z_th at low eRPM, such as startup or stall.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

Assume R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ total path (2 oz outer layers plus internal planes, short loop) and a 0.1 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 (R_{cu}+R_{sh}) = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate current and transition times

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3.5}{4.0} \approx 1.6\ \text{A}$$

- Voltage transition: $t_v \approx Q_{gd}/I_g = 7.5$ ns
- Current transition: $t_i \approx Q_{gs2}/I_g = 5$ ns
- Total: $t_{sw} \approx 12.5$ ns per edge

Turn-off is usually slightly slower, so use 12.5 ns as a lower bound.

### 2.2 Loss components (per channel, one hard-switched leg active)

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $V I t_{sw} f_{sw}$ (on + off, ½ each) | 25.2·80·12.5n·48k = **1.21 W** |
| C_oss discharge | $\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw}$ | **0.023 W** |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_f I \,(2t_d) f_{sw}$ | 0.8·80·400n·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | $2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}$ | **0.058 W** |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 2.57 W** |

**Design levers.**
- At D → 100% (true full throttle) switching loss collapses toward zero. The worst case is about 80 A at 70–90% duty.
- Dead-time loss equals the overlap loss. Cutting t_d to 100 ns, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it, saves ~0.6 W per channel.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz doubles all of Section 2.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals (80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{ch} = 24.6 + 3.2 + 2.6 \approx \mathbf{30.4\ W}$$

$$P_{board} = 4 \times 30.4 + P_{logic}(\approx 1.5\,\text{W}) \approx \mathbf{123\ W}$$

Electrical efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × D. At D = 0.9 that is 1814 W per motor, so η ≈ 98.4%. Efficiency is fine; heat density is the problem.
