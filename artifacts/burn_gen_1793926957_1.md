# Job #51: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC, 80 A Burst Design Package

**Rev A: Engineering Analysis**

---

## 0. Executive Summary and Architecture Corrections

Two scope issues need correcting before the analysis, because they change the design.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no FOC or six-step motor-control firmware for the H743. The working architecture is:

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Each drives one 3-phase bridge (6 FETs).

All DMA and timer analysis below is for the H743-to-ESC DShot link.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, roughly 28×28 mm PCB) is thermally limited to about 1 s.** The model below shows roughly 125 W of total dissipation at 4×80 A, against a board heat capacity of about 3.5 J/K. That is roughly 35 K/s of temperature rise.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Recommended rating | 35 A continuous / 55 A for 10 s per channel |
| 80 A per channel | Limited to ≤1 s with active protection |
| Simultaneous battery draw at 4×80 A | 320 A, which a nano XT60/XT30 power path cannot carry |

I will derive the numbers so you can substitute your actual FET datasheet values.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha = 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step bridge current path

In any 60° sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM-ON:** current flows through HS(A) and LS(B).
- **PWM-OFF with sync rectification:** current flows through LS(A) and LS(B).

So **two FET channels always carry I_phase**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.25\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{28.8\ W}\ \text{(hot)}\quad /\quad 19.2\ \text{W (cold)}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 120° in its primary role. The PWM'd high-side carries D×I² and its complementary low-side carries (1−D)×I². The worst-case single FET is a low-side that is both a static return path and a sync rectifier:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds}\left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right]$$

At D = 0.5:

$$P_{FET,max} = 6400 \times 2.25\text{ mΩ} \times 0.5 = \mathbf{7.2\ W}$$

### 2.4 Copper conduction (frequently ignored, and significant here)

Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) outer layer plus 3× 1 oz inner layers paralleled, for an effective thickness t ≈ 175 µm. Assume a phase path 10 mm long and 4 mm wide.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.010}{0.004 \times 175\times10^{-6}} = 0.25\text{ mΩ}$$

The bus path in and out is about 0.4 mΩ per ESC share. Then:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times (0.25\times2 + 0.4)\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{5.8\ W/ESC}$$

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side hard-switches. The low-side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 900\text{ pF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.027\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ ns}\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

Two FETs are actively switched per sector:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 35\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{ k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.034\ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-ESC switching subtotal

**≈ 1.43 W**

The 48 kHz figure is defensible. Switching losses are about 4% of total at 80 A, so **conduction dominates**. Raising to 96 kHz would add only about 1.4 W per ESC, but it increases dead-time distortion.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss term | Per ESC | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| PCB copper | 5.8 W | 23.2 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 1.43 W | 5.7 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-ESC) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Gate driver and LDOs | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈37.8 W** | **≈151 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A×4 and 22.2 V (7.1 kW input) is about **97.9%**. That looks fine electrically, but it is not survivable thermally on a nano board.
