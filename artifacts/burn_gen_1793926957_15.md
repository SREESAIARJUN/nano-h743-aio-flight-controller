## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #68's BOM, stackup or layout files. Every numeric parameter below is a **declared assumption**. Each formula is written so you can substitute your datasheet values and re-run the numbers.

**Architectural note:** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. The 48 kHz PWM and the 80 A switching happen on the **ESC MCU** (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running Bluejay). The model therefore spans two processors.

**Feasibility flag:** 80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25×25 mm) footprint is aggressive. The model below shows it is survivable only as a **short burst** (seconds), with roughly 40 A continuous. Treat any vendor "80 A" rating on this form factor as a burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path Topology

In any 60° commutation sector (example: phase A PWM, phase B low):

- **PWM ON (duty D):** I flows through HS_A, the motor, then LS_B.
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** I freewheels through LS_A (sync rect), the motor, then LS_B.

There are always **two MOSFETs in series** with the current, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-Device Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each phase is PWM-high for 120° and held-low for 120°. The losses for each switch are:

| Device role | Conduction fraction of electrical cycle |
|---|---|
| HS of PWM phase | (1/3)·D |
| LS of PWM phase (sync rect) | (1/3)·(1−D) |
| LS of held-low phase | 1/3 |

The time-averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6}$$

The worst device instantaneously is the held-low LS, which takes the full I²R_DS for 120° windows:

$$P_{LS,peak} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

**Conservative case (T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.6 mΩ):**

- P_cond,FETs = 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W per channel**
- PCB copper (R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ: pads, vias, phase pour) = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W per channel**
- P_LS,peak = 10.2 W (during its 120° window)
- Average per FET = 3.4 W

**Self-consistent case:** iterate R_DS with the T_j result from §3. The iteration converges near T_j ≈ 75 °C, giving R_DS ≈ 1.3 mΩ and P_cond,FETs ≈ 16.6 W per channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the PWM leg only: one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\!-\!I\ \text{overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{E_{oss} f_{sw}}_{\approx \frac12 Q_{oss}V f_{sw}} + \underbrace{2\,V_F I\, t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}}_{\text{gate drive}}$$

| Term | Calculation | W per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.97** |
| Reverse recovery | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.5 × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.02 |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **1.23** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver) | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.06 |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

**Key insight:** At 48 kHz, dead time dominates over the overlap loss. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. In AM32, use the lowest dead-time setting that is validated against your driver's propagation skew on a scope.

**Frequency scaling:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds ≈1.15 W per channel at 80 A. In exchange you get lower current ripple and quieter motors.
