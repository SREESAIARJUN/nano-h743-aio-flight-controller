# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

**Scope note:** I don't have the Job #58 design files, BOM, or stackup. All component values below (MOSFET, gate driver, shunt, board mass) are representative parts I've chosen. They are labeled as assumptions. The equations are general, so you can substitute your actual datasheet values.

The model also shows that **80 A per motor on a nano-class 4-in-1 board is only survivable for a few seconds**. Flag this to the team before committing to a datasheet rating.

## 1. System Architecture and Assumptions

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► 4-in-1 ESC power stage (4 × 3-phase bridge, 24 FETs)
                          ├─ 4 × ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                          └─ Current sense (bus shunt) + VBAT divider
 STM32H743 FC ──DShot600 (bidir, 4 lines)──► ESC MCUs
              ◄─eRPM telemetry (GCR, same wire)──
```

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It generates DShot frames and decodes the eRPM replies. Commutation, the 48 kHz PWM, and dead-time are handled by the ESC MCUs.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At a target T_j = 115 °C:

R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.5 mΩ**

### 2.2 Per-ESC-channel conduction

In 6-step complementary PWM, the current path always crosses two devices:

- one high-side FET (or its synchronous low-side complement during the PWM off-time), and
- one low-side FET held fully on for 120° of the electrical cycle.

Neglecting ripple (justified because L_motor gives ΔI ≪ I_pk at 48 kHz):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Including ripple, replace I² with I_rms²:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

**At 80 A:**

P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W per motor channel**

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period. The average per FET is therefore 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**, with instantaneous peaks of 9.6 W during its conduction window.

### 2.3 Copper and interconnect losses (often larger than designers expect)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace} + R_{pad} + R_{wire,solder})$$

- Assume 0.3 mΩ per phase path on a 2 oz × multi-layer pour.
- Per motor: 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**.
- Bus input at 4 × 80 A = 320 A peak through ~0.15 mΩ ≈ **15 W**.

The 320 A battery current is also beyond what most XT60/XT90 + 12 AWG harnesses tolerate. Treat this as a system-level red flag.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. One active FET plus its complement per commutation sector.

**Hard-switching V-I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (complementary body diode):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not in the FET die):

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.6 W per motor channel.**

### 3.1 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per motor | × 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead-time | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Phase copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Bus copper/input | — | ~15 W |
| **Total** | **~25.6 W** | **~117 W** |

At 80 A the efficiency of the power stage itself is roughly 1 − 117 / (25.2 × 320 × ~0.8 avg duty) ≈ 98%. That sounds good, but 117 W into a ~30 × 30 mm board is the core problem.

**Design levers:**
- At 48 kHz, conduction dominates switching by about 7:1. Lowering f_sw buys little.
- Lower R_ds(on), or paralleling FETs, buys almost everything.
- Halving dead time (to 50 ns) saves about 0.3 W per motor.
