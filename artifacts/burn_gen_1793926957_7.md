| # | Issue | Resolution |

| 1 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation or FOC path. | The architecture uses five MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot. Four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class) run AM32 and generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Synchronous (Complementary) PWM

In every 60° sector, two phase legs carry current I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole sector.

At any instant, exactly **two FET channels are in series** with the motor current, regardless of D. That gives:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

with temperature dependence:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]
$$

Because R depends on T_j and T_j depends on P, this is a positive-feedback loop. It is solved iteratively in Section 3.4.

### 1.2 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the six FETs carries current for 120°. The duty-averaged loss per FET is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

With D ≈ 1 the PWM-leg high-side FET carries nearly all of its 120°. The thermal hot spot, however, is the **instantaneous** loss in a conducting FET, I²R. Its 120° on-period at, say, 1000 Hz electrical (about 0.33 ms) is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant (tens of ms). The averaged value is therefore valid for junction temperature, but each die experiences 3× ripple at the commutation frequency. That ripple is negligible at the junction because of thermal filtering.

### 1.3 Numerical Evaluation at T_j = 110 °C

$$
R_{DS}(110) = 1.2\,[1+0.006\cdot85] = 1.2 \times 1.51 = 1.81\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

| Loss term | Equation | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2·80²·1.81 mΩ | **23.2 W** |
| Per FET (averaged) | 80²·1.81 mΩ / 3 | 3.86 W |
| Shunt | 80²·0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| PCB copper | 80²·0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Conduction subtotal** | | **28.0 W** |

At T_j = 25 °C (start of burst), FET conduction is only 2·6400·1.2 mΩ = 15.4 W. The loss grows by about 50 % as the die heats.
