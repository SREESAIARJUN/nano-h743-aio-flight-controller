# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600

This package is a first-pass engineering model. Every MOSFET, driver, and thermal parameter below is a stated assumption. Replace each one with datasheet values for your chosen parts and validate on hardware before release.

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and the Headline Finding

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | "Nano" stack, 20×20 mm mounting (≈25×25 mm board), 6-layer, 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner copper |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 at 480 MHz, running ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4 × AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** |
| Gate driver | 4 × FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver with integrated bootstrap) |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, DFN3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25°C = 1.0 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V |
| Supply | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| Burst | 80 A phase current per motor |

**Headline finding:** 80 A burst on all four channels simultaneously is not thermally survivable on a nano-format board. The model below shows about 89 W of dissipation against roughly 1.5 J/K of thermal mass. That is a temperature ramp of about 60 K/s, so the board survives only about 1–1.5 s.

You can either keep the format and change the spec, or keep the spec and change the format:

- **Keep the nano format:** re-spec to 35 A continuous per motor, with 80 A as a ≤1 s single-motor transient.
- **Keep the 80 A rating:** move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger format with heatsinking.

The math follows.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation
In each 60° electrical sector, exactly one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current I_ph:

- During the PWM ON-time, the current flows through HS(x) and LS(y).
- During the OFF-time, it freewheels through LS(x) (synchronous rectifier) and LS(y).

So, ignoring ripple, two FETs carry I_ph at all times:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Each of the 6 FETs per channel carries an equal share over one electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on) (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.6) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction
The phase current carries ripple from the PWM switching:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH, V_bus = 25.2 V, D = 0.9, and V_bemf ≈ 20 V:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{5.2 \times 0.9}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.5\ \text{A}$$

The resulting RMS current is:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12} = 80.02\ \text{A}$$

Ripple is negligible at this operating point.

### 1.4 Results at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W** |
| P_cond per FET | **3.41 W** |
| P_cond, 4 channels | **81.9 W** |

The same burst at T_j = 25 °C gives 12.8 W per channel. This is why the thermal runaway loop (higher temperature → higher R_DS(on) → more loss) must be iterated (§3.3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The model is evaluated per channel. Only the PWM-switched leg (HS/LS pair of phase x) switches; phase y is static for the whole sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times scale with drive strength. With Q_gd = 8 nC, FD6288 source/sink ≈ 0.3/0.6 A, and R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω:

- t_r ≈ 12 ns
- t_f ≈ 8 ns

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss ≈ 1.5 nF effective)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC at 80 A)

$$P_{rr} = V\,Q_{rr}\,f = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (t_dt = 100 ns per edge, V_f = 0.8 V)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Tune AM32 dead time to the minimum safe value; 100 ns is conservative.

### 2.5 Gate drive (Q_g = 50 nC, V_drv = 10 V, 2 FETs switching)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Most of this is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.6 Switching-loss totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.97 W | 3.87 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.46 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 W | 0.48 W |
| **Total switching** | **1.70 W** | **6.81 W** |

### 2.7 Switching frequency trade-off
Switching losses scale linearly with frequency:

$$P_{sw}(f) = 1.70 \times \frac{f}{48\,\text{kHz}}$$
