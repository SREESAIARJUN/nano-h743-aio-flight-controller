# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

Two clarifications set the frame for everything below.

**1. Architecture partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller (FC) only. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU per channel, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot commutate four motors at 48 kHz while also running EKF3. The design is therefore:
- FC board (H743), and
- a stacked ESC board (4× ESC MCU + 4× 3-phase driver + 24 MOSFETs),
- linked by an 8-pin harness carrying DShot plus telemetry.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mounting) ESC is thermally marginal.** The model in §2 shows that if all four channels draw 80 A at once, the board survives only about 1.5–2 s before the junctions reach 150 °C. Treat 80 A as a *per-channel, short-burst, non-simultaneous* rating, and set firmware current limits to match (§2.7).

## 1. Commutation Topology and the Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal (AM32 default) or FOC-style sinusoidal drive, with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) on the active leg.

At any instant in 6-step drive, two of the three legs carry the full phase current:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T, and the low-side FET (synchronous) conducts for (1−D)·T, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held ON for the whole 60° sector.

As a result, the current always flows through exactly **two** channels in series, *independent of duty cycle*:

$$
R_{path} = 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}
$$

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, 80 A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big] \approx k_T R_{ds,25} = 1.4 \times 1.5 = 2.1\text{ mΩ}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{26.9\ W}
$$

The heat is split between the PWM leg (13.4 W, shared between its HS and LS FETs according to D) and the return-leg LS FET (13.4 W).

> **Hot-spot note:** the return-leg LS FET carries 13.4 W *alone* for a full sector. Size the copper for that worst case.

**Copper and shunt losses.** Assume:
- 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, with an effective phase and bus path of about 0.5 mΩ, and
- a 0.25 mΩ shunt (bus or low-side).

$$
P_{Cu+shunt} = 80^2 \times (0.0005 + 0.00025) = \mathbf{4.8\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching (V–I overlap) losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period.

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery and C_oss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 36\text{ mW}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 15\text{ mW}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 38\text{ mW}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 26.9 W | 107.5 W |
| Copper + shunt | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.09 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.4 W** | **≈ 133 W** |

**Interpretation:** at 80 A, conduction loss accounts for about 95% of the total. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per channel. The 48 kHz choice is therefore justified (quieter motors, lower current ripple, smoother low-throttle response). The thermal problem is caused by I²R, not by the switching frequency.

**Efficiency check, per channel at 80 A.** Assume a mean duty of 0.9, so electrical input is about 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 1.8 kW.

$$
\eta \approx 1 - \frac{33.4}{1800} \approx 98.1\%
$$

### 2.7 Thermal model

**Thermal network (Foster approximation), per hot FET:**

```
Tj ─[Zth_jc(t)]─ Tcase ─[Rth_cb ≈ 2 K/W, solder + vias]─ T_board ─[C_board]─┬─[Rth_ba]─ T_amb
                                                                           (prop-wash convection)
```

**Inputs**
- Hottest FET: P_FET ≈ 13.4 W (return-leg LS).
- Steady-state junction-to-case resistance R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W. At pulse lengths ≥ 0.5 s, Z_th,jc is already about 90% of this value.
- Local junction-to-board rise:

$$
\Delta T_{jb} \approx 13.4 \times (1.35 + 0.5) \approx 25\ \text{K}
