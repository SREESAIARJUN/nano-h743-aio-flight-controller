# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three issues change the design before any detailed math applies.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot commands. It has no FOC or 6-step commutation code. | Each motor needs its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only handles DShot600 and telemetry. |
| F2 | **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally transient only.** All four motors at 80 A produce about 100 W in roughly 3 J/K of thermal mass (§3). | 80 A is a **~2 s burst rating**. A realistic continuous rating is 25–35 A per motor with prop wash. Use a 30×30 mm format if you need more. |
| F3 | **A single shunt on the combined 320 A bus is not viable.** At 0.25 mΩ it dissipates about 25 W. | Use per-motor phase or low-side shunts at the ESC MCU, plus a Hall-effect sensor (or a 0.1 mΩ shunt) for battery current. |

The rest of this document models the system as specified and states every assumption so you can substitute your selected parts.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous/complementary PWM)

At any instant one high-side leg and one low-side leg carry the phase current *I*. With complementary PWM at duty *D*, the PWM-ing leg splits conduction between its high-side FET (fraction *D*) and its synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1−*D*). The total resistive path is therefore always two R_DS(on):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha_R
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.5 = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts during 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, so the loss is shared across 6 FETs:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \frac{11.52}{3} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}
$$

During a single 120° sector the instantaneous FET loss is up to 11.5 W. That matters at low eRPM, such as a stall or a hard punch from idle, where the sector lasts longer than the FET's thermal time constant (about 5–20 ms for PDFN).

**PCB copper conduction.** 2 oz copper has sheet resistance R_□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C (about 0.30 at 80 °C). Assume a phase path of about 3 squares, paralleled across 2 layers:

$$
P_{Cu,motor} \approx 80^2 \cdot \frac{3 \cdot 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega}{2} \approx \mathbf{2.9\ W}
$$

This is significant. Use all 4 layers for phase and power pours, and stitch with vias of 0.3 mm drill at 0.6 mm pitch or denser.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

The PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET switches near-ZVS but incurs body-diode conduction during dead time.
