## 0. Architecture Clarification

In this class of board, the **STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs**. The two halves are split:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743 (480 MHz, ArduPilot) | Attitude control; emits bidirectional DShot600 to the 4 ESC MCUs; consumes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC ×4 | Small Cortex-M0/M4 per channel [A: AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32] | Commutation, **48 kHz** complementary PWM, dead-time, back-EMF zero-crossing |
| Gate drive | 3-phase half-bridge driver [A: FD6288Q-class] | Bootstrap high-side drive, ~10–12 V gate rail |
| Power stage | 6 N-ch MOSFETs per channel (24 total) | 3 half-bridges per motor |

The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore belongs to the ESC MCU and power stage. The DShot/DMA analysis belongs to the H743.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path under 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched (say A) and one phase is held low (say B):

- **PWM on:** current flows Q_AH → motor → Q_BL
- **PWM off (sync-rect):** current flows Q_AL → motor → Q_BL

In both states exactly two channels conduct. Duty cycle cancels out of the sum:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Each FET conducts during 120° of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is P_cond,ch / 6. Peak instantaneous dissipation per conducting FET is P_cond,ch / 2.

### 2.2 Numerical values

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 80² × 2.40 m = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 6400 × 3.48 m = **22.3 W** | 89.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | 6400 × 3.84 m = **24.6 W** | 98.3 W |

### 2.3 Parasitic copper loss

Assume a phase path of PCB copper, vias, and pads totaling about 0.4 mΩ **[V: measure with 4-wire Kelvin]**. Add the battery pad to bus to return path, about 0.15 mΩ shared:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.4\text{ m}\Omega = 2.56\text{ W}, \qquad P_{bus} = (4\times80)^2 \times 0.15\text{ m}\Omega = 15.4\text{ W}
$$

The 320 A shared bus is a first-order thermal source. Use 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner planes, stitched bus-bar pads, and solder-loaded battery leads.

### 2.4 Electrothermal positive feedback

Because R_DS rises with T_j, conduction loss is self-reinforcing:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{\theta}\,I^2\,2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta k(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta k \alpha}, \quad k = 2I^2R_{25}
$$

For a stable solution you need R_θ·k·α < 1. With k = 15.36 W and α = 0.006, runaway occurs when **R_θ(ch→amb) > 10.8 K/W**. A nano board with no heatsink sits near this limit for continuous 80 A. This is why 80 A must be a time-limited burst rating.
