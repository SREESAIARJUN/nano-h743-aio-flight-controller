## 0. Architecture Decisions and Stated Assumptions

### 0.1 The H743 should not commutate the motors directly

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller only**. Each motor should be driven by its own dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) firmware, commanded over bidirectional DShot600.

Driving four motors from the H743 would require 24 dead-time-controlled gate outputs plus four synchronized BEMF/ADC sampling chains. The H743's TIM1/TIM8 advanced timers and HRTIM cannot cover that cleanly. It would also put commutation jitter on the same core as the EKF and the scheduler.

**Recommended topology:**

```
6S LiPo ─► 4-in-1 ESC (4× AT32F421 + 4× FD6288Q + 24× N-MOSFET) ─► Motors
                ▲ BDShot600 ×4 (single wire, bidirectional)
                │
          STM32H743 FC (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/Rx/GPS
```

### 0.2 Two form-factor flags to resolve before layout

| Issue | Finding |
|---|---|
| **"Nano" vs. 80 A** | A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 80 A/channel. Copper cross-section and thermal mass are insufficient (see §3). This analysis assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount / 36×36 mm board**. Even at this size, 80 A is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not a continuous one. |
| **4 × 80 A simultaneous** | At full throttle the battery current approaches **~320 A**. That exceeds XT60/XT90 ratings and most 6S packs. The "80 A burst" spec should be defined **per channel**, with a lower all-channel aggregate. |
