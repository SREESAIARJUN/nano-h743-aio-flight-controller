## 1. Architecture and Assumptions

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC each motor channel is commutated by its own ESC MCU, either a shared multi-motor MCU or, more typically, 4× STM32G071 / AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC-firmware parameter.

| Parameter | Symbol | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_pk | 80 A (≤5 s) |
| Continuous per motor | I_cont | 45–55 A (target; derived in §4) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (active freewheeling) |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, 2 FETs in current path at any instant |
| MOSFET (representative) | — | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3 for nano) |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_25 | 0.90 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temp coefficient | α | ≈ +0.4 %/K → ×1.5 at 125 °C |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge @ 25 V | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Effective switching transition (rise + fall) | t_r + t_f | 40 ns (driver-limited, R_G ≈ 4.7 Ω) |

**Nano form-factor flag:** 80 A/motor (320 A aggregate burst) on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm footprint is beyond what copper and thermal mass can support for more than a few seconds. The numbers below assume a 30.5×30.5 mm mount on a ~37×37 mm board. If "nano" means 20×20, derate the burst figure substantially (§4.5).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step with complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation sector:

- **PWM leg:** high-side (HS) conducts for duty D; low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** LS conducts continuously.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

So exactly **two MOSFET channels carry I_ph at every instant**, regardless of D (neglecting dead time, which §3 handles):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_J)$$

With trapezoidal current and low ripple, I_ph,rms ≈ I_DC,link/… only during conduction sectors. Over the full electrical cycle each phase conducts 2/3 of the time, but the instantaneous two-FET path is always loaded, so the formula above holds directly with I = phase current during conduction.

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)]$$

| T_J | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.17 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.26 mΩ (datasheet curves often show ×1.5, i.e. 1.35 mΩ; use 1.35 mΩ conservatively) |

### 2.3 Include PCB copper and shunt

$$R_{path} = 2R_{DS(on)} + R_{shunt} + R_{Cu,phase} + R_{Cu,bus}$$

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| 2 × R_DS(on) @ 125 °C | 2.70 mΩ |
| Shunt (bus-side, shared by 4 motors) or per-motor low-side | 0.25 mΩ (per-motor allocation) |
| Phase copper (pad → FET), 2 oz × multi-layer | ~0.20 mΩ |
| Bus copper + via fields | ~0.15 mΩ |
| **R_path** | **≈ 3.30 mΩ** |

### 2.4 Results at 80 A

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega = 17.3\ \text{W per motor}$$

$$P_{cond,path} = 80^2 \cdot 3.30\,\text{m}\Omega = 21.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

| Phase current | P_cond FETs (T_J = 125 °C) | P_cond total path |
|---|---|---|
| 30 A | 2.4 W | 3.0 W |
| 45 A | 5.5 W | 6.7 W |
| 55 A | 8.2 W | 10.0 W |
| 80 A | 17.3 W | 21.1 W |

**Per-FET distribution.** Over an electrical cycle each FET carries current about 1/3 of the time. The HS shares its interval with its LS sync-rectifier by D : (1 − D). At high throttle (D → 1) HS FETs dominate. Worst-case single FET at 80 A, D ≈ 1:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.0 \approx 2.9\ \text{W (cycle-averaged)}$$

The electrical frequency at hover/punch (eRPM 100k–200k → 1.7–3.3 kHz) is far faster than the die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms), so cycle-averaging is valid.
