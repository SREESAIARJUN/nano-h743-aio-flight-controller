## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** A 4-in-1 ESC uses four independent ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or EFM8 running AM32 or Bluejay. These MCUs commutate the bridges. The H743 sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry over the same wire (bidirectional DShot).
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor is a thermal-capacity problem, not a silicon problem.** The math below shows that the board, not the MOSFET rating, is the binding constraint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In any 6-step commutation sector, two phases conduct:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D, and its complementary low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully on.

The current path therefore always contains exactly two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{hot} = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{hot}$$

**Ripple correction.** Phase inductance creates triangular ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For a typical 5–15 µH motor at 48 kHz, ΔI is about 10 A. The correction is +8.3 A², or about 0.13%, so it is negligible.

**Result:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average:** each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, giving roughly 19.2/6 ≈ **3.2 W** per FET, averaged across the six positions.

**Temperature coupling.** R_DS(on) rises with temperature, so the loss and the temperature form a loop:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

This loop must be iterated with the thermal model in §3. At T_j = 110 °C, k_T = 1.51, which is consistent with the assumed 1.5.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per channel, per PWM period, there is one hard-switched high-side transition pair plus the dead-time and diode events of its synchronous partner.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap (hard switching) | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r+t_f) · f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz | **0.968 W** |
| Coss charge/discharge | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.048 W |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.048 W* |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 1.41 W** |

*Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

**Total per channel at the 80A burst:**

$$P_{ch} = 19.2 + 1.41 \approx \mathbf{20.6\ W}$$

**Board total, all four motors at 80A simultaneously:**

$$P_{board} \approx \mathbf{82\ W}$$

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** Switching losses scale linearly with f_sw. Going to 96 kHz adds about 1.4 W per channel. At 24 kHz you save about 0.7 W but gain audible noise and ripple. Conduction loss dominates at 93% of the total, so **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers that matter, not f_sw.**

**Additional resistive losses per channel at 80A (often forgotten):**
- **Shunt** (0.25 mΩ): 80²·0.25 m = 1.6 W, if in the per-channel return path.
- **Copper** (1 mΩ effective between pads, solder, and pours): 6.4 W.

Budget **≈ 26–28 W per channel** realistically, or about **105–110 W** for the board.
