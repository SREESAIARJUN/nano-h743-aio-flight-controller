## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues in the brief need to be resolved before the math is meaningful.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

In every production AIO board, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits **bidirectional DShot600** to dedicated ESC MCUs. Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM and handle BEMF zero-cross commutation. Typical ESC MCUs are 4× AT32F421, STM32G071 or STM32F051, running AM32 or Bluejay/BLHeli_32.

Driving 12 half-bridges with BEMF sensing directly from the H743 while also running EKF3 is not a viable design. This document models:

- **Domain A:** H743 FC running ArduPilot.
- **Domain B:** 4× ESC MCUs and power stages.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano form factor

The figure is interpreted as **80 A per channel, burst, 6S**, on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. A 20×20 board cannot carry it.

Section 3 shows the burst is **thermally time-limited to a few seconds** by board heat capacity, not by the MOSFET rating. This is the key design constraint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase (A) is PWM'd and one phase (B) is held low:

- **During the on-time (D):** current flows through Q_AH, then the motor, then Q_BL.
- **During the off-time (1−D):** current flows through Q_AL (synchronous rectification), then the motor, then Q_BL.

With synchronous rectification, **exactly two FETs are always in the current path**, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

With peak-to-peak ripple ratio r = ΔI/I:

```
I_rms² = I² · (1 + r²/12)
```

Motor inductance at 48 kHz gives r ≈ 0.2–0.4. The correction is ≤1.3%
