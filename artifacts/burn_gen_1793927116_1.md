# STM32H743 Nano AIO (Job #60): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Warning

These assumptions affect every number below, so they come first.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the STM32G071 or AT32F421. ArduPilot has no firmware path for the H743 to drive gates directly, so this split is required, not optional.

**Form factor.** "Nano" is taken to mean a 20×20 mm mounting pattern. The board outline is about 25×25 mm, with 8 layers of 2 oz copper.

**Battery.** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V at full charge.

**Representative MOSFET** (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3). Substitute values from your actual datasheet.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor at T_j ≈ 125 °C | ×1.5, giving 2.25 mΩ |
| t_r + t_f with the chosen gate resistor | 40 ns |
| Q_g | 50 nC |
| Q_rr | 50 nC |
| C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Body diode V_f | 0.8 V |
| R_θJC | 1.2 K/W |

**Warning: 80 A burst on a 20×20 board fails thermally.** Section 3 shows the math. Expect a defensible burst rating of about 40 A for ≤ 5 s and about 23 A continuous with good prop wash. The rest of this document gives the full derivation so you can re-run it with real parts or a larger board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in a trapezoidal BLDC drive

In six-step commutation with synchronous rectification:
- Exactly two phase legs carry the motor current I_m at any instant.
- One leg is held "low-side on" for the whole 60° sector.
- The other leg is PWM'd at duty D.
  - During the on-time, current flows through its high-side FET.
  - During the off-time, current freewheels through its low-side FET (synchronous rectification).

So at every instant, outside dead time, the current flows through exactly **two** FET channels in series. Per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{DS(on),hot}$$

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period. Per-FET RMS currents are therefore:

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I_m\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1-D}{3}}$$

The low-side FET carries more stress: it covers the full "sink" sector plus the freewheel time. **Size the thermal design around the low-side FET.**

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 28.8\ \text{W}$$

Worst-case low-side FET, as D → 0:

$$I_{LS,rms} = 80\sqrt{2/3} = 65.3\ \text{A}$$

$$P_{LS} = 65.3^2 \times 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

That 9.6 W lands in a single 3.3×3.3 mm package.

### 1.3 Parasitic resistances (same current)

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W (only if the shunt sits in the per-ESC return path) |
| Phase copper, 2 oz, ~8 mm × 3 mm, 2 layers | ~0.4 mΩ ×2 paths | 5.1 W |
| Solder pads and vias | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.6 W |

The copper term is easy to overlook and is not small. At 80 A, the PCB itself becomes a heater.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches, which is one high-side/low-side pair per ESC.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time events per cycle, t_dt = 100 ns)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_m (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs, V_GS = 10 V). This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.7 W per ESC at 80 A.**

**Frequency trade-off.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per ESC at 80 A, which is small next to 28.8 W of conduction loss. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower current ripple and quieter motors. It is not the thermal problem.

### 2.1 Per-ESC total at 80 A

$$P_{ESC} \approx 28.8 + 2.7 + 1.6 + 5.7 \approx 38.8\ \text{W}$$

**With all four motors at 80 A, the board dissipates about 155 W.**
