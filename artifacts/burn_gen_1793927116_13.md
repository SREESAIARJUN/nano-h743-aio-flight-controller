## 0. Scope, Assumptions and One Upfront Finding

No MOSFET, board stack-up or "80A" definition was specified, so this package uses representative component values and **labels every assumption**. Substitute datasheet values into the formulas; the equations themselves hold for any part.

| ID | Assumption | Value |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Supply | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (worst case), 22.2 V nominal |
| A2 | "80A burst" | **80 A per ESC channel, 10 s** (common marketing convention). The 80 A *total* case is also computed in §2.6 |
| A3 | MOSFET (representative 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3) | R_DS(on) = 1.5 mΩ max @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V; Q_g = 40 nC; Q_gs2+Q_gd = 12 nC; Q_oss = 40 nC; Q_rr = 50 nC; V_SD = 0.8 V; V_plateau = 3.5 V; R_g,int = 1.0 Ω |
| A4 | Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase, ~10 V V_drv, ~2 Ω effective source/sink |
| A5 | Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 48 kHz, dead time 50 ns per edge |
| A6 | Architecture | H743 is the **flight controller**. Each ESC channel is driven by its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32 or BLHeli_32-class). The H743 emits bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The H743 does not commutate motors. |
| A7 | Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm ESC PCB, 8 layers, 2 oz copper, ~70 % copper fill |

> **Key finding:** At 80 A *per channel*, a nano-format board **cannot sustain a 10 s burst** without forced airflow. The adiabatic limit is about 3 s before the board reaches +80 K (§3.3). At 80 A *total* (20 A per channel), the design is comfortable. The rating language for Job #59 must be clarified before the BOM is frozen.

## 1. Power Stage Topology and Current Paths

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, **exactly two MOSFET conduction paths carry phase current at all times**:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) switch conducts for fraction *D*. The same leg's low-side (LS) switch conducts as a synchronous rectifier for fraction (1−D). The body diode conducts during dead time.
- **Return leg:** the LS switch is on continuously for that 60° sector.

Each phase leg spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the return leg, and 1/3 floating (BEMF sensing).

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{hot} = 1.5 \times 1.55 \approx 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 2.2 Conduction losses (per channel)

Instantaneous loss with two paths conducting:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\text{m} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

Per-device average over an electrical cycle, with $P_0 = I^2 R_{hot} = 15.36$ W:

$$
P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}P_0,\qquad P_{LS} = \frac{1 + (1-D)}{3}P_0
$$

At burst, D → ~0.95:

- P_HS ≈ 4.9 W
- P_LS ≈ 5.4 W

The LS device is the hot spot. Place it with the best thermal via field.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS switch hard-switches. The LS switch turns on with zero voltage after body-diode conduction, but it injects Q_rr into the HS turn-on.

**Gate current at the Miller plateau:**

$$
I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{drv} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{2 + 2.2 + 1.0} = 1.25\ \text{A}
$$

**Transition time:**

$$
t_{sw} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.25\,\text{A}} = 9.6\ \text{ns}\quad(\text{rise} \approx \text{fall})
$$

| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V | 1.26 µJ |
