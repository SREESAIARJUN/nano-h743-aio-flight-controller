# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Power-Stage Model and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Scope, Architecture Assumptions, and Caveats

I don't have a specification for "Job #64," so this document states every assumption explicitly. Replace the bracketed parameters with your actual BOM values, and the equations will carry through.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC uses its own commutation MCU per channel, here assumed to be AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. They are linked by bidirectional DShot600 on 4 signal lines.

**Reality check, up front.** As derived below, four channels at 80 A dissipate about 85 W in a nano-format board. This is a burst rating limited by board thermal mass, measured in seconds. It is not a continuous rating. Any datasheet claim must state the burst duration and starting temperature.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

During each 60° electrical sector, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for fraction D. The low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for 1 − D.
- **Static leg:** the LS conducts for 100% of the sector.

So exactly two R_DS(on) are in series at every instant, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Adding PWM ripple, where ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\left(I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 120° of 360°.

| FET role (within its 120° window) | Loss when active |
|---|---|
| HS, PWM leg | D·I²·R |
| LS, PWM leg | (1 − D)·I²·R |
| LS, static leg | I²·R |

Averaged over an electrical cycle, the result is P_cond,ch / 6 per FET. This holds when the electrical period is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms). At 30,000 rpm with 14 poles, f_e = 3.5 kHz, so averaging is valid.

At low eRPM (startup or stall), the static LS sees I²R continuously for each sector. Use that value as the worst case.

### 1.4 Numbers (80 A, T_j = 125 °C, ripple neglected)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per channel | 2 × 6400 × 1.45 m = **18.6 W** |
| Instantaneous, static LS FET | 6400 × 1.45 m = **9.3 W** |
| Average per FET (high eRPM) | **3.1 W** |

### 1.5 Copper loss (frequently forgotten)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t},\qquad \rho_{Cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1+0.00393(T-20)]$$

Example: a 10 mm × 3 mm phase pour on 4 parallel 2 oz layers (70 µm each) gives about 0.20 mΩ, so **1.3 W per phase path at 80 A**. The battery input, shunt, and phase pads add a comparable amount. **Budget 2–3 W per channel for copper.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS hard-switches. The LS commutates softly through its body diode during dead time.
