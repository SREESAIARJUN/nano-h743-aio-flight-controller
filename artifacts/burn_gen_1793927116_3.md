# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Reference Design (Job #53)

## 0. Architecture and Scope

Most "H743 4-in-1" designs are not a single MCU running the motors. This design uses the usual split:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, bidirectional DShot600 master |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 / BLHeli_32 | 6-step commutation, **48 kHz PWM**, eRPM telemetry |

Every numeric result below depends on stated assumptions (MOSFET part, layer stack, airflow). Treat these figures as a sizing model. Before committing to an 80 A rating, validate it with a thermocouple or IR soak test and with the MOSFET datasheet SOA curves.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, three FETs carry current:
- the PWM'd high side, conducting a fraction D of the time;
- its complementary low side, conducting the remaining (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier;
- the return-phase low side, conducting continuously.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result does not depend on duty cycle. That is a useful property: burst loss is the same whether the motor is at partial or full throttle.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.51\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.3\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Averaged over the electrical cycle, each FET dissipates P_cond,FET = 19.3 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W**.

### 1.3 Copper interconnect loss (not negligible)

Assume the phase and bus path is 15 mm long, 3 mm wide, uses 2 oz copper, and is paralleled on 2 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{(3\times10^{-3})(70\times10^{-6})\times2} \approx 0.61\ m\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.61\times10^{-3}\times1.3_{(hot)} \approx \mathbf{10\ W}$$

This term often exceeds what designers expect. Mitigations:
- Use solder-mask openings with tinned copper on the phase and bus traces.
- Stitch the layers with via arrays of at least 12 vias per amp-path.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the high-side switch sees hard switching. Its synchronous partner commutates through the body diode during the dead time.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.04 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f (Q_oss ≈ 30 nC) | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f = 2·40 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.04 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 1.38 W** |

Effect of PWM frequency: P_sw scales linearly with f. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs only about 0.7 W per ESC. In return, 48 kHz cuts current ripple in low-inductance motors, which is the right trade on a nano frame.

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| Source | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.3 W | 77.2 W |
| Copper | 10.0 W | 40.0 W |
| Switching | 1.4 W | 5.5 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ per ESC) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **32.3 W** | **≈ 129 W** |

Efficiency at the full 4 × 80 A burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 × ~0.9 ≈ 7.3 kW input, so efficiency ≈ **98 %**.

The percentage looks excellent, but the problem is the absolute heat, not the efficiency.
