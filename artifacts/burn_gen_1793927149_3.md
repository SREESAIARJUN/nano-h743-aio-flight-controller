## 1. Field-Oriented Control (inner current loop, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

The Clarke transform is amplitude-invariant and assumes a balanced three-phase system (i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

The Park transform uses the electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 Plant model (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_d &= R\,i_d + L\,\dot i_d - \omega_e L\, i_q\\
v_q &= R\,i_q + L\,\dot i_q + \omega_e L\, i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m\\
\tau_e &= \tfrac32\, p\, \lambda_m\, i_q \equiv K_t\, i_q
\end{aligned}
$$

### 1.3 Current PI with decoupling (pole-zero cancellation)

Each axis is controlled by a PI regulator:

$$
C_i(s) = K_{p,i} + \frac{K_{i,i}}{s}
$$

Choose the gains so the controller zero cancels the electrical pole at R/L:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}
$$

The open loop then becomes ω_ci/s, and the closed loop is first order:

$$
\frac{i_q}{i_q^*} = \frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}
$$
