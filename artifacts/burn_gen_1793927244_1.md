## 0. Architecture Baseline and a Feasibility Flag

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | "Nano" = 20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm usable PCB, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal); FETs rated 40 V |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 @ 480 MHz, running ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32. The H743 does **not** commutate motors; it sends DShot. |
| Gate driver | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink) |
| MOSFETs | 24× 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class: R_DS(on) = 0.95 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V; Q_g = 55 nC; Q_rr = 45 nC; C_oss(er) ≈ 1.4 nF; R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time 100 ns |

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor × 4 = **320 A** from the battery. Section 2 computes ~95 W of dissipation in a part with a heat capacity of only a few J/K. This board can survive an 80 A *transient* lasting a few seconds. It cannot sustain 80 A. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 boards are realistically rated 40–55 A continuous on 6S. The 80 A figure should be specified as "≤ X s burst from T₀" (see §2.5).

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.95 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.38 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, phase current I_ph flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- **ON interval (D):** HS FET of the PWM leg, then the motor, then the held-ON LS FET of the return leg.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** LS FET of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification), then the motor, then the return LS FET.

So the instantaneous channel resistance is always 2·R_DS. The bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Averaged over the 6 FETs of one bridge:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

The hottest device is the return-leg LS FET. Its peak segment loss is I²R during its 120° window. Average loss per FET is still I²R/3, but the instantaneous loss pulses at the commutation frequency.

### 1.3 Relationship between DC current and phase current

$$I_{dc} \approx D \cdot I_{ph} \quad\Rightarrow\quad I_{ph} = I_{dc}/D$$

For an 80 A burst at full throttle, D ≈ 0.95 (AM32 caps duty to keep the bootstrap refreshed). This gives I_ph ≈ 84 A.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A burst (I_ph = 84 A)

| Term | T_j = 25 °C | T_j = 100 °C | T_j = 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_cond, bridge = 2·I²·R | 13.4 W | 17.9 W | 19.5 W |
| P_cond per FET (avg) | 2.2 W | 3.0 W | 3.2 W |

### 1.5 Copper and shunt conduction

- **Phase copper:** 2 oz ≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq. Assume ~3 squares per leg path on paralleled layers, giving ~0.3 mΩ effective round-trip. P_Cu ≈ 84² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **2.1 W per motor**.
- **Current shunt:** single 0.1 mΩ shunt on the battery bus. At 320 A, P = 320² × 0.1 mΩ = **10.2 W**.
  - This is not acceptable. Use per-ESC 0.25 mΩ shunts (80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W each), or a Hall sensor on the battery lead.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per bridge is hard-switching at any time (the PWM leg). The HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET soft-switches (ZVS) because of the dead-time body-diode transition.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$$

Driver current is ~1.5 A, and the Miller plateau charge Q_gd is ~12 nC. This gives t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 8–10 ns intrinsic. Allowing for gate resistance and loop inductance, assume (t_r + t_f) = 35 ns:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(84)(35\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.78\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, induced in the HS FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{ nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$
