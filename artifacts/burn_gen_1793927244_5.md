## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, exactly one leg sources current and one leg sinks it. Call them leg A (PWM-driven) and leg B (low side held on).

| Device | Conducts I during | Duty within sector |
|---|---|---|
| A-high | PWM on-time | D |
| A-low (synchronous rectifier) | PWM off-time minus dead time | 1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw |
| A-low body diode | dead time | 2t_dt·f_sw |
| B-low | entire sector | 1 |

Bridge conduction loss per channel, with phase current I and ripple ΔI:

$$
I_{rms,eff}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = I_{rms,eff}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1 - D - 2t_{dt}f_{sw}) + 1\right] \approx 2\, I_{rms,eff}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] + R_{Cu}
$$

The result is almost independent of D, because the synchronous rectifier takes over the off-time. This is why 80 A at partial throttle heats the FETs about as much as 80 A at full throttle.
