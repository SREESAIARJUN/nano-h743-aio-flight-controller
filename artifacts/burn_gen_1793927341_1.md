I don't have Job #27's requirements, BOM, or layout, so this package states every assumption and gives each model in parametric form. Substitute your datasheet values and re-run the numbers. Where the physics argues against the spec, I say so.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** On a 4-in-1 AIO board:

- **The H743** runs ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- **The ESC MCUs** (e.g., 4× AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) commutate the motors and set the 48 kHz PWM.

The power-loss model below therefore describes the ESC stage. The DMA allocation describes the H743.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R ≈ 0.9 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.35 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction

With synchronous rectification in 6-step commutation, the phase current always flows through exactly two channel-resistance paths:

- During PWM on-time: HS(A) and LS(B).
- During off-time: LS(A) and LS(B).

Either way it is two FETs in series. The duty cycle redistributes loss *between* devices but not the total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{trace}$$

Device losses for one channel:

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P per conducting FET | P_cond (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 5.76 W | 11.5 W |
| 125 °C | 1.35 mΩ | 8.64 W | **17.3 W** |

**Copper loss.** Assume 0.3 mΩ for the phase pad, battery plane share, and vias:

$$6400 \times 0.3\text{ mΩ} = 1.9\text{ W per channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution at duty D

| Device | Loss expression |
|---|---|
| HS (PWM'd) | I²·R·D |
| LS (complementary, same leg) | I²·R·(1−D) |
| LS (other leg, static on) | I²·R |

The statically-on low-side FET is the hottest device. Thermal design must target it, not the average.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side switch hard-switches. The complementary low-side switch turns on after dead time with its body diode already conducting, so it soft-switches (ZVS) and contributes reverse-recovery loss instead.

| Term | Equation | Value (25.2 V, 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 kHz = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS diode → HS) | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·60 nC·12 V·48 kHz = 0.07 W (mostly in driver/R_g) |

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.7\ \text{W}$$

Two notes on this result:

- **Dead time costs almost as much as the overlap loss.** Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, if the shoot-through margin allows it.
- **Frequency sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw: about 0.035 W/kHz per channel. Going from 48 to 24 kHz saves ~0.85 W per channel. Conduction loss (~90% of the total) is unaffected.

## 3. Total Loss and Thermal Model

### 3.1 Burst power budget

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 17.3 W | 69.2 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| Copper traces | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, H7 MCU | n/a | ~1.5 W |
| **Total** | **~20.9 W** | **~85 W** |

### 3.2 Steady state: 80 A is not continuous

Thermal network for the hottest FET:

$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta c\text{-}pcb}) + P_{board}\,R_{\theta pcb\text{-}a}$$

Assumed values:

- R_θjc ≈ 1.0 K/W
- R_θ(c-pcb) ≈ 2 K/W (solder plus thermal vias)
- R_θ(pcb-a) ≈ 3 K/W (30×30 board in propwash, h ≈ 100 W/m²K, both sides plus components)

Board-level steady state: ΔT = 85 W × 3 K/W = 255 K. **This is impossible.** The board would desolder.

### 3.3 Transient (burst) model

Use a two-node Cauer model:

$$C_{FET}\frac{dT_j}{dt} = P_{FET} - \frac{T_j - T_{pcb}}{R_{jp}}, \qquad C_{pcb}\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} = P_{tot} - \frac{T_{pcb}-T_a}{R_{pa}}$$

Board heat capacity:

- Copper: ~8 g × 0.385 J/g·K
- FR4: ~4 g × 1.1 J/g·K
- Components: ~1 J/K
- Total: C_pcb ≈ 8.5 J/K

The board time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 3 × 8.5 ≈ 25 s. Bursts shorter than about 5 s are therefore close to adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT_{pcb}}{dt} \approx \frac{85}{8.5} \approx 10\ \text{K/s}$$

Junction offset above the board: the hottest FET dissipates ≈ 8.6 W × 3 K/W ≈ **+26 K**. Its time constant is only milliseconds, so it reaches this quickly.
