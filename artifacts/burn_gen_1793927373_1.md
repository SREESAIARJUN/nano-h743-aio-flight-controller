# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #9)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points change the math, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not drive the FETs.** ArduPilot has no field-oriented or 6-step motor-drive firmware. The real topology is:
   - The **H743 runs ArduPilot** and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - **Four ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) each run 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM.
   - DShot is the only FC→ESC interface. Everything below assumes this split.
2. **"80 A burst" is per motor channel, for a bounded time.** A nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot dissipate 4×80 A continuously. The model below quantifies how long the burst can last.
3. **Component parameters are representative placeholders, not a specific part's datasheet.** Replace them with your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values. The equations stay valid. Assumed FET class: 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, logic-level.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.006 /K |
| Rise / fall time (in-circuit) | t_r, t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-FET RMS current in 6-step with synchronous rectification

Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg is:
- **"High" for 1/3 of the time.** The high-side FET conducts for a fraction D (duty cycle) and the low-side FET synchronously rectifies for the remaining (1 − D).
- **"Low" for 1/3 of the time.** The low-side FET is on 100%.
- **Floating for 1/3 of the time.** This is the BEMF sensing window.

The resulting per-FET squared RMS currents are:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{I^2 D}{3}, \qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{I^2 (1-D)}{3} + \frac{I^2}{3} = \frac{I^2(2-D)}{3}$$

Sum one high-side and one low-side FET, then multiply by 3 legs:

$$P_{cond} = 3 R_{DS}\left[\frac{I^2 D}{3} + \frac{I^2(2-D)}{3}\right] = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, which is physically correct: exactly two FETs always carry the current.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond per channel at 80 A (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 80 | 1.33 | 17.0 |
| 110 | 1.51 | 19.3 |
| 125 | 1.60 | 20.5 |

### 1.3 Copper parasitics (do not neglect)

Path per channel: bus → high-side FET → phase pad → motor → phase pad → low-side FET → ground, with roughly 0.3 mΩ of copper and vias per path.

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{Cu,eff} \approx 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W}$$

This heat lands in the PCB, not in the junction. It still counts against the board's thermal mass.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only one leg PWMs at a time, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel.

**(a) V·I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (body diode of the synchronous FET)

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I t_d f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs switched per period). Most of this loss lands in the gate resistors and driver, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.2 W per channel.**

Design levers:
- Cutting dead time to 50 ns recovers about 0.3 W.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz roughly doubles (a) through (e), adding about 2.2 W.
- At 80 A, conduction loss dominates about 9:1, so **R_DS(on) is the parameter to optimize, not switching speed.**

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (T_j = 110 °C)

| Term | Per channel (W) | 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.3 | 77.2 |
| Switching (a–e) | 2.2 | 8.8 |
| Copper / vias | 1.9 | 7.6 |
| **Total** | **23.4** | **93.6** |

Average loss per FET is about (19.3 + 2.2)/6 ≈ **3.6 W**. Instantaneous loss is higher during a FET's conduction interval, about 9.6 W while it carries 80 A. At the 100s-of-Hz electrical frequencies involved, this ripple is mostly filtered by the die's thermal capacitance.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (Cauer approximation)

```
P_FET ──[R_θjc 1.5 K/W]──┬──[R_θcb ≈ 3 K/W (pad+vias)]──┬──[R_θba ≈ 8–15 K/W total board→air, prop-wash dependent]── T_amb
                     C_j ≈ 0.02 J/K                   C_board ≈ 4–5 J/K (whole PCB + heatsink)
```

### 4.2 Junction rise over the board (quasi-steady within the burst)

Die and pad time constants are in the millisecond range, so the junction tracks the board almost immediately:

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET} (R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta cb}) = 3.6 \times 4.5 \approx 16\ \text{K}$$

At peak instantaneous loss (9.6 W), use the transient impedance Z_θ(t ≈ electrical half-period) of roughly 0.5 K/W. That adds about 5 K of ripple.

### 4.3 Board heating: the real limit

Assume an adiabatic case (convection is minor over a few seconds) with C_board ≈ 4.5 J/K for a 6-layer 2 oz board, about 4 g.

| Scenario | Loss (W) | dT_board/dt |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 channels at 80 A | 93.6 | ≈ 20.8 K/s |
| 1 channel at 80 A | 23.4 | ≈ 5.2 K/s |

### 4.4 Burst duration limit

Limits: T_j,max = 125 °C, junction-to-board offset ≈ 21 K, so T_board,max ≈ 104 °C. Starting board temperature is 40 °C.

$$t_{burst} = \frac{104 - 40}{20.8} \approx \mathbf{3.1\ s}\ \text{(all four motors)}, \qquad \approx 12\ \text{s (single motor)}$$

### 4.5 Electro-thermal stability check

Loss rises with temperature because R_DS rises:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2 I^2 R_{25} \alpha = 77\ \text{mW/K per channel}$$
