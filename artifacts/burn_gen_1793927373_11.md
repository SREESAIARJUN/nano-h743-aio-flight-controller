# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #2): Power-Stage Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

Before the math, several points need flagging. A review board would raise them anyway.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated motor MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only emits DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **80 A burst on a nano form factor is a thermal-time-limited rating, not a steady-state one.** The numbers in section 3 show 4×80 A simultaneous is survivable for roughly 2–3 s at most on a 30×30 mm board. Marketing "80 A burst" figures are typically per-channel, single-motor, ≤10 s, with airflow.
3. **No specific MOSFET part number was given.** I use a representative 40 V, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3) N-FET class device. Every result is given parametrically, so you can substitute datasheet values.
4. **The netlist is a reference topology, not a verified production schematic.** It must go through your ERC/DRC and the specific FET and driver datasheets before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology
This assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, which AM32 and BLHeli_32 both use by default. At every instant, phase current flows through exactly two FETs in series: one high-side and one low-side (or its synchronous counterpart). Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° conduction).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.55 ≈ **1.86 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

With a flat-topped (trapezoidal) current, I_rms ≈ I_ph inside the conduction window:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.86\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.8\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET loss

Per-FET instantaneous loss (during its conduction window):
$$P_{FET,inst} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 11.9\text{ W}$$

Per-FET average loss (1/3 duty):
$$P_{FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(11.9) = 3.97\text{ W}$$

### 1.5 Copper loss (do not omit on a nano board)

A phase trace of 2 oz copper, 4 parallel layers, 3 mm wide and 10 mm long has resistance:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.01}{0.003 \cdot 70\mu m \cdot 4} \approx 0.2\text{ m}\Omega$$

This adds **≈1.3 W per phase pair** (round it to 2.5 W per motor, including the battery-bus share). Note that the battery pads carry 4 × 80 = 320 A at full burst, giving P = I²R. Even 0.1 mΩ there costs 10 W.

### 1.6 Thermal electro-feedback (iterative solution)

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Solve the fixed point:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta,eff}\, I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\ T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{\theta}I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta}I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

**Stability criterion:** R_θ · I² · R_25 · α < 1.

At 80 A per FET (instantaneous): 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.0055 = 0.042 K⁻¹·W/… so runaway requires R_θ > 24 K/W. A well-soldered PDFN on a via farm (~8–12 K/W effective) is stable. A poorly-reflowed FET is not.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

During each PWM period, one FET hard-switches. Its synchronous partner switches near zero voltage, since the body diode conducts during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times60\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{2.90\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.5\times40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.024\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (body diode of the synchronous FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver/gate resistor, not in the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\text{ W/motor}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor at 80 A burst

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 23.8 |
| Copper (phase + bus share) | 2.5 |
| V-I switching | 2.90 |
| Dead time | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Total** | **≈ 30.6 W** |

**Board total, 4 motors at 80 A:** ≈ **122 W**. Efficiency at 25.2 V × 320 A (8.06 kW) is about 98.5%. The efficiency looks fine, but the heat *density* is the problem.

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + V_f I\,2t_{dt} + \ldots \approx 86\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- Going from 48 kHz down to 24 kHz saves ≈2.1 W per motor.
- Going from 48 kHz up to 96 kHz costs ≈4.1 W per motor.

Keep 48 kHz only if motor current ripple or acoustic requirements demand it.
