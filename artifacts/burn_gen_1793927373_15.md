# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture Model (Job #17)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Corrections

### Architecture correction

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It does **not** commutate the motors. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware, so a realistic design uses this split:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot)  ──4× bidir DShot600──►  ESC MCU(s) (AM32/BLHeli_32 class, e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071)
                                               └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

All "bi-directional DShot" analysis below refers to the H743 ↔ ESC-MCU link.

### Feasibility flags (read these first)

1. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is far outside steady-state capability.** The math below shows roughly 22 W per channel at 80 A. The board can sustain this only for **about 1–3 s** before the FETs reach their limit. Treat 80 A strictly as a burst rating, and enforce it with firmware current/temperature limiting.
2. **Board size.** The H743 in LQFP100 is 14×14 mm. Fitting it plus four 3-phase bridges on a 20×20 board is not realistic. **Recommendation:** use a stacked design, either a 20×20 FC stacked on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 ESC, or a 25.5×25.5 AIO with the H743 in TFBGA100.
3. **Placeholder component values.** All MOSFET parameters below are representative placeholders for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Re-run every equation with your actual datasheet values before committing.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

With 6-step trapezoidal drive and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), exactly two legs carry the phase current at any time:

- **PWM leg:** high-side conducts for time D, then its low-side conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** low-side conducts continuously during that 60° sector.

So the current always flows through **two FETs in series**, independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.3375 = **1.61 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)+I^2R_{Cu}$$

$$P_{cond,ch}=80^2\cdot 2\cdot 1.61\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and shunt loss

Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ for the bus-to-phase path on 2 oz copper.

$$P_{Cu}=6400\times0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET average

Each FET serves about one-third of the electrical cycle in a conducting role.

$$P_{cond,FET}\approx 20.6/6\approx \mathbf{3.4\ W}$$

The worst case is a low-side FET in a stalled or low-RPM sector, which can hold 6.4 W instantaneously.

### 1.6 Electro-thermal coupling

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so the loss must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,Z_{th},\quad P(T_j)=I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j gives:

$$T_j=\frac{T_a+I^2R_{ds,25}Z_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha I^2 R_{ds,25} Z_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator approaches zero:

$$\alpha I^2R_{ds,25}Z_{th}\to1$$

For one FET at 80 A: α·I²·R_ds = 0.0045 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = 0.0346 W/K.

- Runaway sets in at Z_th ≈ **29 K/W**.
- A 3×3 mm FET on a small, crowded board has a steady-state Z_th in the range of 25–40 K/W.

**This is the analytical proof that 80 A cannot be continuous.**
