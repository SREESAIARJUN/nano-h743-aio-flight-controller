# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): Reference Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

I have no project record for "Job #14," so this document states every assumption explicitly. Replace the values marked **[A]** with your actual BOM and layout numbers before signing off.

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot. In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel (or the whole board) runs its own ESC firmware on a separate small MCU. ArduPilot generates DShot600 on H743 timers and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry back. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, not by the H743.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 4 × 80 A (320 A, about 8 kW) on a "nano" form factor (20×20 mm) is not physically supportable. The bus copper alone cannot carry it, and there is no thermal mass to absorb the losses. The model below assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board. Even at that size, the result (Section 3) is that 80 A is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, exactly one leg sources current and one leg sinks it:

- **Sourcing leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET of the same leg (sync-rect) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Sinking leg:** the low-side FET is held fully on.

So at any instant the phase current flows through exactly two channels. The result is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts over 1/3 of the electrical period: 120° as an active switch, plus its sync-rect share. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Peak per-FET instantaneous loss** (the value that drives junction temperature within a sector):

$$I^2 R = 80^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Assume a phase path of ~0.30 mΩ (V+ pour, FET pads, motor pad, return) **[A]**:

$$P_{Cu,ch} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.30\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.92\ \text{W}$$

DC bus loss is computed separately because the bus carries the sum of all four channels' battery currents. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_phase, so at full throttle the bus carries ≈ 4 × 80 A = 320 A. With 0.10 mΩ of bus/pad resistance **[A]**:

$$P_{bus} = 320^2 \times 0.1\ \text{m}\Omega = 10.2\ \text{W}$$

**This is significant. Use thick bus pads and solder the battery leads directly at the board.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the hard-switched device (the active high-side FET) sees V·I overlap. The sync-rect FET switches at ~0 V, apart from the dead-time body-diode commutation.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Gate plateau ≈ 4.5 V. Driver current during the plateau:
- Turn-on: I_g ≈ (10 − 4.5)/R_g,total ≈ 1.1 A, with R_g ≈ 5 Ω.
- Turn-off: ≈ 0.9 A.

Transition times:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.1\,\text{A}} \approx 9\,\text{ns} \quad (\text{plus } Q_{gs2} \text{ share} \rightarrow \text{use } 15\,\text{ns})$$

$$t_f \approx 15\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{DS} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, dead time and gate drive

| Term | Formula | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.060 W |
| C_oss charge | ½ · Q_oss · V · f = 0.5 × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2t_d · f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6 · Q_g · V_g · f_eff | ≤ 0.17 W (only 2 FETs actively PWM: ~0.06 W) |

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.45 + 0.06 + 0.02 + 0.61 + 0.06 \approx \mathbf{2.2\ W}$$

**Design levers:**
- Reduce dead time to 50 ns if the driver and FET allow it. This saves ~0.3 W per channel.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves P_sw, but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.
