# Job #47: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package

**Scope:** 80 A burst loss model, 48 kHz switching analysis, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, reference netlist, and thermal budget.

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. It is the flight controller, and it runs ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and returns eRPM telemetry over bidirectional DShot.

The loss model below applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA plan applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

With complementary PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- During the on-time: the high-side FET of the PWM leg plus the low-side FET of the return leg.
- During the off-time: the low-side FET of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification) plus the same return low-side FET.

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple current:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{max} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ A_{pp}$$

RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{pk}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ A^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of the higher PWM frequency.

### 1.3 Conduction Loss per ESC Channel

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds,25}\,k_T = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.0\,m\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and Interconnect Loss

Assume an effective path resistance of R_cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel. This covers 2 oz outer copper, 1 oz inner copper, vias, and the solder pads to the motor and battery.

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,m\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, only one leg is hard-switched at any instant.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs per leg) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | **0.058 W** (dissipated in the driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching loss** | | **≈ 1.75 W per channel** |

### Design Levers

1. **Dead time.** This is the second-largest switching term. Dropping t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin on the bench first.
2. **PWM frequency.** At 24 kHz, the switching losses halve to about 0.9 W. However, ripple rises to 13 A_pp, and motor iron loss and audible noise increase. 48 kHz is a sound choice for low-inductance motors.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Component | Per Channel | ×4 Channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.75 W | 7.0 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| ESC MCU, gate driver, LDO | ~0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| H743 + 5 V/10 V BECs | — | ~2.5 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 99.7 W** |

**Electrical efficiency at full burst:** About 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8.06 kW is delivered. The losses therefore represent about 1.2%, so the ESC stage is roughly 98.8% efficient. Efficiency is not the problem. Dissipating 100 W on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board is.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-Board (Per FET)

Total channel loss is spread across six FETs over each electrical revolution. Commutation is fast relative to the die thermal time constant (milliseconds), so the averaged value applies:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{19.2 + 1.75}{6} \approx 3.5\ W$$

$$R_{\theta,jb} = R_{\theta,jc}\,(\approx 1.0) + R_{\theta,solder+via}\,(\approx 0.8) = 1.8\ K/W$$
