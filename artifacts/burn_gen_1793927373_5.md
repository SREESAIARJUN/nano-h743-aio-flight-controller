## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several points shape every calculation below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no motor-commutation layer. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four ESC MCUs (for example STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/BB51 running Bluejay). The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to them. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is set in ESC firmware (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz).
2. **"80 A burst" is ambiguous.** I model 80 A per motor (worst case) and also show the result for 80 A total.
3. **No specific MOSFET was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm DFN class device. All parameters are tagged **[A]** and must be replaced with your selected part's datasheet values.
4. **Bus voltage:** 6S, 25.2 V fully charged (worst case for switching loss).

| Symbol | Value [A] | Meaning |

| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs=10 V | On-resistance |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology
Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32 and Bluejay default, complementary PWM).

At any instant, current I_ph flows through two devices in series:
- the high-side (HS) or low-side (LS) FET of the PWM'd phase, depending on PWM state, and
- the LS FET of the return phase.

Because of synchronous rectification, the PWM'd leg always has exactly one FET conducting, apart from dead time.

### 1.2 Equations

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}$$

At 100 % throttle, phase current is approximately DC within a commutation step, so I_rms ≈ I_ph. At partial duty D, battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph, but phase current, and therefore conduction loss, stays high. This is why part-throttle punch-outs heat the ESC.

### 1.3 Numbers, 80 A per motor, hot (R_ds = 1.5 mΩ)

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\text{ mΩ} = 6400 \times 3.0\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period (LS FETs slightly more under sync-rect):
- **Instantaneous per-FET loss:** 80² × 1.5 mΩ = 9.6 W
- **Average per FET:** ≈ 19.2 / 6 ≈ 3.2 W

The electrical period at flight RPM is under 1 ms, far below the die thermal time constant (~10 ms or more), so the average value is what matters for temperature.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper, often larger than the FETs on nano boards

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}, \quad \rho_{Cu} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\text{m}\ (\times1.39\text{ at }125\,°C)$$

Example: a 10 mm phase path, 5 mm wide, 2 oz (70 µm), 2 layers in parallel:

R = 1.72e-8 × 0.01 / (0.005 × 140e-6) ≈ 0.25 mΩ, or about 0.34 mΩ hot.

Add VBAT/GND plane spreading of about 0.2 mΩ effective per path. That gives roughly 0.5 mΩ extra per ESC, or **+3.2 W at 80 A**. This must be in the budget.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, only one leg switches hard per commutation step.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V·I overlap)
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ W$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature, so derate ×2 for margin: **0.12 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (HS + LS of the switching leg)
$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\times 635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.6 Effect of frequency
Switching-dependent terms scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz they halve (≈1.4 W saved per ESC at 80 A). The cost is higher motor current ripple:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For example, L = 15 µH and D = 0.5 give ΔI = 8.75 A at 48 kHz and 17.5 A at 24 kHz. That ripple increases motor copper loss and ESC RMS current, which partly offsets the switching savings.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Term | Per ESC @ 80 A | ×4 (80 A/motor) | ×4 (80 A total, 20 A/motor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W | 4.8 W |
| Copper parasitics | 3.20 W | 12.8 W | 0.8 W |
| Hard switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W | 1.9 W |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.12 W | 0.5 W | 0.5 W |
| C_oss | 0.05 W | 0.2 W | 0.2 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W | 0.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W | 0.2 W |
| ESC MCU, LDO, shunt (est.) | 0.30 W | 1.2 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.5 W** | **≈102 W** | **≈10.2 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A/motor on 6S full throttle: input ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 ≈ 8.06 kW, so losses are about 1.3 %. The efficiency is fine. The problem is power density, not efficiency.
