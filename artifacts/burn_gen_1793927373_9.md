# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Constraints

All numbers below use **stated assumptions**, not a validated BOM. Before layout freeze, replace every parameter tagged `[A]` with datasheet values for your chosen parts.

**Feasibility constraints:**

1. **80 A per channel does not fit a 20×20 mm "nano" board.** Twenty-four power FETs plus drivers, shunts and bulk capacitance need at least a **30.5×30.5 mm** footprint with 8-layer, 2 oz power planes. Even then, 80 A is a **burst** rating of roughly 1–3 s, not continuous. Section 3 quantifies this.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This model uses an AT32F421 `[A]`. The H743 (ArduPilot) sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

**Operating point:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V, V_nom = 22.2 V |
| FET `[A]` | 40 V N-ch, R_DS(on),25°C,max = 1.2 mΩ, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss(er) = 1 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.0045 K⁻¹ `[A]`

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**. This is the design (worst-case) value.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

At every instant, two phases carry current I:
- **Switching leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T, and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) for (1−D)·T.
- **Non-switching leg:** the low-side conducts for the full period.

Exactly **two FETs carry I at all times**, independent of D. Ignoring commutation overlap and ripple:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I² · R_DS(on)(T_j)

Each FET conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle (worst case D → 1):

- I_rms,FET = I/√3 = 80/√3 = **46.2 A**
- P_cond,FET = I_rms² · R = 2133 × 1.74 mΩ = **3.71 W**
- P_cond,ch = 6 × 3.71 = **22.3 W** (check: 2 × 6400 × 1.74 mΩ = 22.3 W ✓)

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R (assumed) | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Low-side shunt (per channel, if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ `[A]` | 1.6 W |
| PCB power path (8L, 2 oz, 4 planes paralleled, ~2 squares) | ≈ 0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |

Many 4-in-1s use a single shared DC-link shunt instead. In that case, use **I_total = 4 × 80 = 320 A** for that shunt: P = 320² × 0.1 mΩ = 10 W, which is a real hotspot.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg hard-switches, with one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

P_VI = ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

The rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau: t ≈ Q_gd / I_g. With Q_gd = 8 nC and I_g ≈ 0.4 A (driver output through R_g), t ≈ 20 ns.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

P_rr = Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W**

The induced turn-on loss in the opposing FET is of the same order. Total is about 0.1 W, which is negligible.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

P_oss = 2 · ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw = 1 nF × 635 × 48k = **0.030 W**

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

P_dt = V_SD · I · (2·t_dt) · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48k = **1.23 W**

This term is significant. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel, but you must verify shoot-through margin against driver propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Gate drive

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.038 W**

This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Per-channel and board totals (worst case, T_j = 125 °C, 80 A)

| Loss term | W/channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (§1.2) | 22.30 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 |
| Shunt + copper | 2.60 |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.2 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 113 W** |
