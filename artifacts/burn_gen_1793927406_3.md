## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step synchronous PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:
- During the on-time: high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- During the off-time: low-side of phase A (sync rectifier), then low-side of phase B.

So, regardless of duty cycle D, two channel-resistances carry the full current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2\, R_{Cu}$$

Within a 60° commutation sector the current is approximately flat at the phase-current value, so I_rms ≈ I_pk for a burst-limited, current-controlled drive.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_ds(on) (α = 0.0045) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 80 °C | 1.25 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ |

Because R_ds rises with temperature, loss rises with temperature. This positive feedback is the thermal runaway mechanism in §3.4.

### 1.3 Copper path resistance

| Element | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Battery pad → bus plane (2 oz, ~15 mm, wide) | 0.10 mΩ |
| Via farm (40 × 0.3 mm vias, ~1.5 mΩ each, in parallel) | 0.04 mΩ |
| Phase plane → motor pad | 0.10 mΩ |
| Shunt (if low-side phase shunt) | 0.25 mΩ (only in path for part of the cycle) |
| **R_Cu effective** | **≈ 0.30 mΩ** |

### 1.4 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | 2·I²·R_ds | I²·R_Cu | **P_cond per channel** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 12.8 W | 1.9 W | 14.7 W |
| 100 °C | 17.2 W | 2.2 W* | **19.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 18.6 W | 2.3 W* | 20.9 W |

*Copper α ≈ 0.0039 K⁻¹ is applied to R_Cu as well.

Comparison at 55 A continuous, T_j = 100 °C: 2·3025·1.34 m + 3025·0.36 m ≈ **9.2 W per channel**.

**Per-FET split (worst case).** At low duty, the low-side FET of the PWM'd phase carries current for most of the period. Peak single-FET loss is therefore close to I²R_ds ≈ 80²·1.34 m ≈ **8.6 W** for that device during the sector. Averaged over the electrical cycle, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time per state, but at high eRPM the thermal mass does not filter this completely.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET turns on after the body diode has already clamped, so it switches at near zero voltage. Its losses are body-diode conduction and reverse recovery instead.

### 2.1 Loss components

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{E_{oss} f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2\,V_F\, I\, t_{dead}\, f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{n\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}}_{\text{gate drive}}$$

### 2.2 Transition time from the gate network

Transition time is set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,src}}$$

Example values: Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 12 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 4.0 V, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω, R_g,int = 1 Ω, R_src = 6 Ω.

- I_g,on = 6 V / 11.7 Ω = 0.51 A, giving t_r ≈ 23 ns.
- Turn-off uses an anti-parallel diode with 2.2 Ω and the driver sink of about 3 Ω: I_g,off ≈ 4 V / 6.2 Ω ≈ 0.65 A, giving t_f ≈ 18 ns.

### 2.3 Evaluation at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 41 ns · 48 kHz | **1.98 W** |
| Reverse recovery (Q_rr = 45 nC) | 45 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.054 W |
| C_oss (E_oss ≈ 0.5 µJ per FET, 2 FETs) | 1 µJ · 48 kHz | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode (V_F = 0.8 V, t_dead = 50 ns ×2) | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 50 ns · 48 kHz | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (Q_g = 45 nC, 2 FETs switching) | 2 · 45 nC · 10 V · 48 kHz | 0.043 W |
| **P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |
