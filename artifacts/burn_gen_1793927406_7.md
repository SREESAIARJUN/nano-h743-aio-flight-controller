## 1. Thermal and Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification, two FETs carry phase current at every instant:

- **During t_on:** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **During t_off:** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.

The conduction loss is therefore essentially independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}$$

Within that total, the split is P_HS = D·I²R and P_LS,A = (1−D)·I²R. The phase-B low-side FET dissipates I²R for the full commutation step. That makes the low-side FETs the thermal hotspot.

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

The worst single FET sees 9.6 W during its conducting step. Averaged over the 6-step cycle (each FET conducts 1/3 of the time), the per-FET average is about 3.2 W. The junction, however, follows the instantaneous value at the 120° step time scale.

Copper loss must be added. Assume 0.4 mΩ total for the shunt, phase trace, and pad path:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

### 1.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing FET hard-switches. Hard turn-on and turn-off:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 1.3 Secondary Losses at 48 kHz

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss · V · f | 0.03 W |
| Gate charge (Q_g = 60 nC, 10 V, 2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | 0.06 W (mostly in the driver) |

### 1.4 Total Loss per Channel and per Board

| Loss | W per channel @ 80 A |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 |
| Copper | 2.6 |
| Switching | 0.97 |
| Dead time, Q_rr, Q_oss, gate | 0.76 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.5 W** |
| **Four channels** | **≈ 94 W** |

Efficiency check: the electrical output is about 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is roughly 1.8 kW per channel, so the ESC is about 98.7 % efficient. The difficulty is heat removal, not efficiency.

Sensitivity of switching loss to frequency (all else equal):

| f_sw | P_sw + dead time per channel |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.79 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.58 W |
| 96 kHz | 3.17 W |

Conduction dominates by more than 10×. At this current, 48 kHz costs very little relative to 24 kHz.

### 1.5 Thermal Network

**Junction rise (per FET, quasi-steady over one commutation step):**

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC | ≈ 1.2 K/W |
| Solder and via array to inner planes | ≈ 1.0 K/W (≥ 12 × 0.3 mm filled vias under the drain pad) |
| ΔT_j-board | ≈ 9.6 W × 2.2 K/W ≈ **21 K** |

**Board heat capacity (lumped):**

| Element | Heat capacity |
|---|---|
| FR4, ~3 g × 1.1 J/g·K | 3.3 J/K |
| Copper, ~2.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K | 0.96 J/K |
| 24 FETs and capacitors | ≈ 0.4 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | **≈ 4.7 J/K** |

**Adiabatic burst heating:**

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} = \frac{94\ \text{W}}{4.7\ \text{J/K}} \approx 20\ \text{K/s}$$

Limit: T_j,max = 150 °C. Derate to 125 °C, which puts the board limit at 125 − 21 ≈ 104 °C. Starting from a 45 °C board:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{104 - 45}{20} \approx \mathbf{3\ s}\ \text{(no airflow)}$$

Prop-wash convection extends this slightly.

**Steady state (convection only):**

- h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K under prop wash
- Area: 2 × 30.5² mm² ≈ 1.86×10⁻³ m², so hA ≈ 0.11–0.19 W/K
- At ΔT = 60 K, the board can reject P_max ≈ 7–11 W total, or about 2–3 W per channel

**Allowable continuous current:**

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{P_{ch}}{2R_{hot} + R_{Cu}}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.5}{3.4\,\text{m}\Omega}} \approx \mathbf{27\text{–}35\ A}$$

**Conclusion:** label the board **35 A continuous / 80 A for ≤ 2 s** with a 10 s cooldown, or upgrade to a 30×30 board with a heatsink (R_θ,sink-air ≈ 8 K/W).
