**The analysis shows that 80 A per motor on a nano board can only be a 1–3 second burst rating.** Steady-state dissipation at 4×80 A exceeds 100 W, and a ~25 mm board cannot reject that. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat "80 A burst" as a time-limited rating with firmware enforcement, not a continuous capability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.4 × 1.45 = **2.03 mΩ**. Use the datasheet's normalized R_DS(on)-vs-T_j curve in place of α when available.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct and the third floats. One leg is PWM-modulated and one leg's low-side FET is held on. Because of synchronous rectification, the PWM leg's current always flows through either its HS FET (for time D) or its LS FET (for time 1−D). The path therefore always contains **exactly two FETs in series**, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

At I = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.03\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.0\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt contribution

Assume a 0.5 mΩ current-sense shunt (one per ESC, low-side return) plus ~0.3 mΩ of effective trace and via resistance:

$$P_{Cu+shunt} = 6400 \times 0.8\times10^{-3} = 5.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (needed for junction temperature)

Over one electrical revolution, each leg spends 120° as the PWM leg, 120° as the static low-side leg, and 120° floating. For the worst-case FET:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{DS},\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

The LS FET carries the static-leg interval plus freewheeling, so it is the hot device at low-to-mid duty. At D = 0.5 and 80 A:

- P_LS = (1/3)(0.5)(13.0) + (1/3)(13.0) = **6.5 W**
- P_HS = **2.2 W**

The low-side FETs are your thermal limiter. Give them the most via area.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The static leg does not, so these losses are counted once per motor.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f ≈ 35 ns (1 A driver, Q_gd ≈ 6 nC, R_g ≈ 2 Ω):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 50\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and output capacitance

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 25\,\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2\times30\,\text{nC}\times10\times48\,\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Frequency sensitivity

Every term in this section is linear in f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1 W per motor at 80 A. That is small next to the 26 W of conduction loss. At this current level, **48 kHz is not the problem; R_DS(on) is.**
