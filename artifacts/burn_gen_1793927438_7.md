## 0. Feasibility Finding

On a 20×20 mm "nano" board, **simultaneous 4×80A cannot be dissipated**, even as a burst. The model below shows that on a 30.5×30.5 mm board, a 4×80A burst lasts about 2.5 s before FETs reach their limit, and the board supports only about 20–25A per motor continuously. A 20×20 board has roughly 43% of that area and heat capacity, so the burst window shrinks to about 1 s.

**Recommendations:**
- Redefine the rating as **80A per channel, burst ≤ 2 s, non-simultaneous**, with a stated continuous rating.
- Or move to a 30.5 mm board with 2 FETs in parallel per switch.

The derivations are parametric, so you can substitute your actual FET datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.43 mΩ**. Use 1.5 mΩ as the hot design value.

### 1.2 Bridge-Level Conduction Loss
In each 60° commutation sector, two phase legs carry current I_ph.

- **Chopped leg:** The high side conducts for fraction D, and the synchronous low side conducts for fraction (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** One FET conducts continuously.

So at every instant exactly two FET channels carry I_ph, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(80^2)(1.5\times10^{-3}) + (80^2)(0.5\times10^{-3}) = 19.2 + 3.2 = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Worst Case
Each phase is active for 120° of every 360°, which is a fraction of 1/3. The worst FET is the low side at low duty (D → 0), or equivalently a static-leg FET:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.5\,\text{m}) = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The junction-to-case rise is therefore small: with R_θJC ≈ 1 K/W, ΔT_jc ≈ 3–4 K. **The bottleneck is board-level heat spreading, not the die.**

### 1.4 Electrothermal Fixed Point
Losses raise temperature, which raises R_ds, which raises losses. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\big(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\big)\cdot Z_{th}$$

This system diverges (thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta \ge 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_0 \alpha R_\theta \ge 1$$

At 80A with R_θ = 7.2 K/W per board quarter-share, the loop gain is **0.46×4 > 1 at the board level**. This means 80A is runaway-unstable in steady state. It is only valid as a time-limited burst, which is the strongest argument for current limiting in firmware.
