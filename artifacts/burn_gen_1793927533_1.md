## 1. Commutation Model

AM32 uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg. Within any 60° sector:

- **High-side (HS) FET** of leg A switches at duty D.
- **Low-side (LS) FET** of leg A is complementary, conducting for (1−D) minus dead time.
- **LS FET** of leg B is fully on.
- Leg C floats and is used for back-EMF zero-crossing detection.

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. **Two FETs are always in the current path**, ignoring dead time.

## 2. Conduction Losses

### 2.1 General form

Per sector, the losses in each conducting FET are:

$$P_{HS,A} = I^2 R_{ds} D,\qquad P_{LS,A} = I^2 R_{ds}(1-D),\qquad P_{LS,B} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

Summing over the three conducting FETs gives the total per-motor conduction loss, which is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

R_ds rises with junction temperature. This creates thermal feedback, solved iteratively:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx0.0058\ /K$$

Thermal runaway is avoided when the following condition holds:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta JA} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA,\text{eff}} < 1$$

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg (HS_A and LS_A) switches. The loss terms are below.

### 3.1 Voltage-current overlap (hard-switched HS)

The gate plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 4.5 V. Total gate resistance is R_g,tot = R_drv + R_ext + R_g,int ≈ 2 + 4.7 + 1 ≈ 7.7 Ω.

$$t_{r}\approx\frac{Q_{gd}R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{5\text{nC}\cdot7.7}{7.5}\approx5.1\text{ ns},\quad t_f\approx\frac{Q_{gd}R_{g,tot}}{V_{pl}}\approx8.6\text{ ns}$$

Adding the Q_gs-to-Miller transition, the effective total is about 10 ns per edge:

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, gate charge, and dead time

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

$$P_{oss}\approx Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

$$P_{gate}=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2\cdot30\text{n}\cdot12\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.035\ W}\ \text{(mostly in driver and R}_g)$$

$$P_{dead}=2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dead}\,f_{sw}=2(0.8)(80)(100\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Totals per motor at 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.80 |
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.10 |
| Shunt, 0.25 mΩ (§6.4) | 1.60 |
| **Per motor** | **≈32.1 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈128 W** |

**Design insight:** At 48 kHz, conduction loss is about 90% of the total. Lowering f_sw buys little. Lowering R_ds or paralleling FETs is the effective lever.

**Dead-time trade-off:** Reducing t_dead from 100 to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. The risk is shoot-through if driver propagation mismatch exceeds roughly 20 ns.
