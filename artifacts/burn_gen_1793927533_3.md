**Job #16, Revision A, Preliminary Architecture**

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Assumptions

### 0.1 What the H743 does and does not do

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate the motors.

- The H743 sends **bidirectional DShot600** to four independent ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU, an **AT32F421K8U7 running AM32** (BLHeli_32-class alternatives also work).
- The **48 kHz PWM** is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
- This split is how every production "AIO" board works. The H743 does not have enough advanced timers to drive 24 FETs and run ArduPilot at the same time.

### 0.2 Feasibility flag on the 80 A rating

80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) footprint is **only achievable as a short burst, measured in seconds**. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a transient rating. The sustained rating will land around 25–35 A per motor depending on airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM. In each 60° sector:

- **Phase A high-side (HS)** switches at duty D.
- **Phase A low-side (LS)** synchronously rectifies during (1−D).
- **Phase B low-side** is held fully ON.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, independent of D (dead time aside):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,max,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

This is solved iteratively against the thermal model in Section 3, because temperature raises resistance, which raises loss, which raises temperature. The thermal runaway condition is:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} \ge 1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th} \ge 1
$$

At 80 A with a junction-to-ambient-effective R_th of 5.4 K/W (from 3.3), the product is 2·6400·0.0015·0.006·5.4 = 0.62. That is below 1, so the system converges, but with a **1/(1−0.62) = 2.6× loss amplification** relative to the cold estimate. This is the key reason 80 A cannot be continuous.
