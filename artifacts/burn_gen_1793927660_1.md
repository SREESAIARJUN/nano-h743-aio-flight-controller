# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, and DShot DMA Plan

**Job #6, Revision A (engineering baseline)**

This document gives the full derivation and allocation tables. Every MOSFET parameter is a placeholder. Swap in the values from your chosen part's datasheet and the equations still hold.

## 0. Critical findings

The calculations below produce three conclusions. Read these before the rest.

1. **80 A per channel is not sustainable on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** At 80 A on all four channels the board dissipates about 88 W. A nano board's lumped heat capacity is about 2.5 J/K, so it heats at roughly 35 K/s. It reaches the derating limit in about 2 s (Section 3.4).
   - "80 A burst" is credible only for **≤1–2 s on a 30.5×30.5 mm board** with heavy copper.
   - A realistic nano rating is about **35–45 A burst and 25–30 A continuous**.
2. **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly.** The standard and recommended split is:
   - H743 as flight controller, generating bidirectional DShot600 and running ArduPilot.
   - 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) with 3-phase gate drivers.

   This keeps ArduPilot's real-time loop free of 48 kHz commutation interrupts.
3. **All four DShot outputs go on a single advanced timer (TIM1)** with UP-DMA plus input-capture DMA. On the H743, DMAMUX makes this conflict-free (Section 4).

## 2. Conduction loss model

### 2.1 Commutation topology

Under 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous rectification (AM32 default), exactly two legs are active at any instant:

- **PWM on-time (D):** high-side FET of phase A and low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** low-side FET of phase A (sync rectification) and low-side FET of phase B conduct.

The phase current I therefore always flows through **two MOSFET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle. This holds outside the dead time, which is handled separately in 2.3.

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Under 6-step operation, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period. Summed over all six FETs per channel, the result is the expression above.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Assumed reference FET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 mm, logic-level):

| Parameter | Value used |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | 0.005 K⁻¹ |
| Q_g (total) | 60 nC |
| Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Q_rr | 50 nC |
| t_r + t_f (with driver) | 40 ns |
| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V |
| R_thJC | 1.5 K/W |

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005·100) = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Parasitic copper and connections

Add the trace, via, and solder-pad resistance in the current path:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{path}, \qquad R_{path} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}$$

Example: a 2 oz (70 µm) pour, 4 mm wide, 15 mm effective length, ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m gives about 0.92 mΩ per layer. With 4 paralleled layers that is about 0.23 mΩ. Include phase pads, giving ≈ 0.4 mΩ effective.

### 2.4 Conduction results (per ESC channel)

| I (A) | P_FET @ T_j = 125 °C (2·I²·1.5 mΩ) | P_copper (I²·0.4 mΩ) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30 | 2.70 W | 0.36 W | 3.06 W |
| 45 | 6.08 W | 0.81 W | 6.89 W |
| 60 | 10.8 W | 1.44 W | 12.2 W |
| **80** | **19.2 W** | **2.56 W** | **21.8 W** |

## 3. Switching loss model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg incurs hard-switching loss. That is one half-bridge per channel at any time.

### 3.1 Loss components

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

**(b) Output capacitance discharge**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery** (the low-side diode recovers when the high side turns on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs of the switching leg; this power dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw}$$

### 3.2 Numerical evaluation (V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, t_dt = 100 ns)

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.935 W** |
| P_oss | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| P_rr | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.614 W** |
| P_g | 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k | 0.058 W (driver) |
| **P_sw total (FETs)** | | **≈ 2.66 W** |

**Design levers:**
- 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise.
- Going to 96 kHz doubles P_sw to about 5.3 W per channel, which is not justified at 80 A.
- Cut dead time to about 50 ns only if the driver and FET timing margins allow it. This saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 3.3 Total dissipation at 80 A

| Item | Per channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 2.56 W | 10.2 W |
| Switching | 2.66 W | 10.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| **Total** | **24.5 W** | **≈ 98 W** |

Efficiency check: 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × D (D ≈ 0.85 at full throttle) ≈ 6.85 kW delivered, so the 98 W loss is about **1.4 %**. Efficiency is fine. The problem is **heat density**, not efficiency.

### 3.4 Thermal model

**Junction temperature (steady-state, per FET)**

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{thJC} \quad (\text{with } R_{thJB} \approx R_{thJC} \text{ for bottom-cooled packages})$$

Each FET carries about one-third of its channel's conduction loss plus its share of switching loss. At 80 A, per-FET dissipation is about (19.2 + 2.66)/3 ≈ **7.3 W**, giving ΔT_jc ≈ 7.3 × 1.5 = **11 K**. The junction-to-board step is acceptable. **The board itself is the bottleneck.**

**Board lumped heat capacity**

| Format | Copper (8 L × 2 oz equiv.) | FR4 | Components | C_th total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nano 20×20 | 2.0 g × 0.385 = 0.77 J/K | 1.5 g × 1.1 = 1.65 J/K | ~0.3 J/K | **≈ 2.7 J/K** |
| 30.5×30.5 | 4.7 g → 1.8 J/K | 3.5 g → 3.9 J/K | ~0.6 J/K | **≈ 6.3 J/K** |
