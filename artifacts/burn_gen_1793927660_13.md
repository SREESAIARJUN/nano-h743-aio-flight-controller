# Job #36: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architecture correction
The STM32H743 cannot be the commutation controller for four motors. Twelve half-bridges plus BEMF comparators per motor exceed its practical resources, and no flight-stack firmware supports it. The architecture is therefore:

- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC:** 4 × dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). Each has its own 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs, for 24 FETs total.

### Feasibility warning
80 A per channel on a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 "nano" ESC can only be a **burst rating of a few seconds**, never continuous. The math in §3 shows why. Treat 80 A as the transient design point and about 40–50 A as the realistic continuous rating with prop-wash airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, exactly one high-side and one low-side FET carry I_ph at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the switching leg alternates HS and LS, but **the current always flows through 2 × R_DS(on)**.

**Hot resistance:**

R_hot = R₂₅ · [1 + α (T_j − 25)] = 1.0 mΩ · [1 + 0.005 · 90] ≈ **1.45 mΩ** (round up to 1.5 mΩ)

**Per channel:**

P_cond,ch = I_ph² · 2 · R_hot = 80² · 2 · 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**

**Per FET:** each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, so the duty is 1/3:

P_cond,FET = I_ph² · R_hot / 3 = 6400 · 1.5 mΩ / 3 = **3.2 W** (averaged over the electrical cycle)

**Copper and solder path:**

P_cu,ch = I² · R_cu = 6400 · 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**

**All 4 channels:**

P_cond,total = 4 · (19.2 + 3.2) = **89.6 W**

**Self-consistency check.** R_hot depends on T_j, so iterate:

T_j(n+1) = T_ref + P(T_j(n)) · Z_th

At 80 A this converges only because the event is a burst. In steady state it runs away (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The worst case is hard switching at full current (V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A).

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | **0.048 W** |
| Coss | Q_oss · V · f_sw (approx.) | **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.058 W** (mostly in driver/R_g) |
| **Per channel** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |
| **4 channels** | | **≈ 10.8 W** |

**Notes**

1. **At true 100% duty, switching loss approaches 0.** The worst-case combined loss occurs at roughly 85–95% duty with high current, which is what the table models.
2. **Overlap loss dominates.** Lowering R_g speeds the edges and cuts this loss, at the cost of more ringing and EMI. A good starting range is R_g ≈ 2.2–4.7 Ω, plus a phase-node RC snubber if V_ds overshoot exceeds 80% of BV_DSS.
3. **Effect of switching frequency.** Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz doubles every row except conduction. That is the trade for lower current ripple and quieter motors.

## 3. Total Thermal Model (80 A Burst)

**Total burst dissipation:**

P_total ≈ 89.6 (conduction + copper) + 10.8 (switching) ≈ **100 W**

### 3.1 Lumped board thermal capacitance (30.5×30.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 8-layer 2 oz)

| Mass | Calculation | Heat capacity C_th |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 1.44 cm³ · 1.85 g/cm³ = 2.7 g · 1.1 J/gK | 3.0 J/K |
| Copper (50% fill) | 0.25 cm³ · 8.96 = 2.3 g · 0.385 | 0.87 J/K |
| FETs, driver, caps, solder | estimate | ~0.6 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 4.5 J/K** |

**Board-level heating rate (adiabatic):**

dT/dt = P / C_th = 100 / 4.5 ≈ **22 K/s**

### 3.2 Junction rise above board

Per FET, the worst FET sees conduction plus its share of switching: ≈ 3.2 + 1.4 ≈ 4.6 W.

| Path | Z_th (burst > 1 s) | ΔT |
|---|---|---|
| Junction → case | ~1.2 K/W | 5.5 K |
| Case → PCB (pad + vias, ≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias) | ~6 K/W | 28 K |
| **Junction → board** | | **≈ 33 K** |

### 3.3 Burst time budget

Assume T_amb = 40 °C, a T_j limit of 150 °C, and a design margin to T_j = 125 °C.

Board temperature limit: T_board,max = 125 − 33 = 92 °C

So ΔT_board,allow = 92 − 40 = 52 K

Allowed burst duration:

t_burst ≈ 52 / 22 ≈ **2.4 s** (adiabatic; airflow adds roughly 20–40%)

Including convection (h ≈ 80 W/m²K in prop wash, A ≈ 18 cm² two-sided gives R_conv ≈ 7 K/W, τ = R·C ≈ 31 s):

ΔT_board(t) = P · R_conv · (1 − e^(−t/τ)) → at t = 2.5 s: 700 · 0.077 ≈ 54 K
