# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and BDShot Architecture Model

## 0. Architecture Review: Issues to Resolve Before Layout

Three points in the brief change the design, so I'm stating them up front.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Commutation, BEMF sensing and bidirectional telemetry need dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor. Typical parts are the AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) or an EFM8/STM32G071-class part. This document models a **two-tier system**: H743 FC → 4× DShot600 BDShot lines → 4× ESC MCUs → 4× three-phase bridges.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) with 80 A burst per channel is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows that all four channels at 80 A dissipate about 130 W. A 20×20 stack can absorb that for only about 1–2 s. Either define "burst" as ≤1 s single-axis transients, or move to 30.5×30.5 mm.
3. **All device parameters below are placeholders** (marked ⟨param⟩) taken from typical 40 V PQFN3.3 MOSFET datasheets. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values. Treat the netlist as a reference topology, not a fab-ready release.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step synchronous operation, exactly two FETs carry phase current at every instant: one high side and one low side. During PWM-off time, the PWM'd leg's complementary FET carries the current instead. So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS,hot} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution (worst device).** The non-PWM'd low side conducts the full current for 120° of each electrical period (1/3 duty):

$$P_{FET,max} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \cdot 0.0024}{3} = 5.1\ \text{W average}$$

The instantaneous value is 15.4 W. Electrical frequency is roughly 1–3 kHz, which is shorter than the die thermal time constant (ms). The ripple therefore adds only about 1–3 K. Use the average together with a small Z_th(t) correction.

**Temperature coupling.** R_DS depends on T_j, so solve iteratively:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot Z_{th,jc}(t)$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations. Thermal runaway occurs if $\partial P/\partial T \cdot Z_{th} \ge 1$, which is not a risk at these values.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (one PWM'd leg per channel at any time)

**Transition times**, where the effective gate current is set by the driver and gate resistor:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,off}}$$

With R_g = 10 Ω, assume t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30n·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Coss | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw = 2·30n·10·48k | 0.03 W (in driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 30.7 W | 122.8 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| PCB copper (est., 8L 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ path per channel) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **~34.8 W** | **~139 W** |

Conduction dominates by 14:1. To improve the design, lower R_DS(on) or parallel FETs. Lowering f_sw does very little. At 48 kHz switching is only about 6 % of losses, so you could raise f_sw for acoustic or ripple benefit at little cost.
