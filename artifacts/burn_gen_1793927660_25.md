| Form factor | "Nano" 20×20 mm (M3); 30.5×30.5 mm evaluated as alternative | |

**Architectural clarification:** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. It sends DShot600 frames to four dedicated ESC MCUs, and those MCUs run the 48 kHz commutation. The model is therefore split into two domains:

- **Power stage** (§1–3): ESC MCUs, gate drivers, and MOSFETs.
- **Control interface** (§4): H743 timers, DMA, and bidirectional DShot.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (details in §3):** 4 × 80 A for 10 s on a 20×20 mm board is not thermally survivable. The board's adiabatic heat capacity is about 3 J/K against roughly 900 J of burst dissipation. Realistic options are:
> - "80 A burst" defined as a ≤2 s single-channel transient, or
> - a 30.5×30.5 mm board with a heatsink, rated for about 4–5 s.
>
> Treat this as the governing constraint for the program.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification)

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM phase:** the high side (HS) conducts during D·T. The synchronous low side (LS) of the same leg conducts during (1−D)·T. One device in that leg always carries the current.
- **Return phase:** the LS is on for the full sector.

As a result, two MOSFETs are always in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

Peak-to-peak phase current ripple, with L_ph ≈ 10 µH phase-to-phase (typical 2306–2807 class motor):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bat} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{10\,\mu\text{H}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz}} = 13.1\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 14.3 = 6414\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds about 0.2%, which is negligible. The worst case is at D = 0.5.

### 1.4 Results at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per FET | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 6414 × 1.6 mΩ = **10.26 W** | **20.5 W** |
| PCB copper (est. 0.3 mΩ per phase path, 2 paths) | — | 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W** |
| **Conduction total** | | **≈ 24.3 W** |

The copper term is not negligible. On a nano board, phase pours are often 0.2–0.5 mΩ, which is comparable to the FET itself. Use 2 oz copper on at least the outer layers and duplicate the power pours on inner layers.
