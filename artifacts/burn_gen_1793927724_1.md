### Architecture

The H743 does not commutate motors directly. The standard architecture, assumed here, has three layers:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **ESC control:** four independent ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay) handle commutation.
- **Power stage:** 4 × (3-phase gate driver + 6 N-MOSFETs).

### Key findings (read these first)

1. **The 80 A rating is a burst, not continuous.**
   - At 80 A on all four motors the board dissipates about 140 W.
   - The thermally-limited burst is about **1 s** (§1.5).
   - The realistic continuous rating is **about 30–35 A per motor** with prop-wash cooling.
2. **Conduction dominates at burst.** It is about 33 W per motor, versus about 2 W for switching plus dead time.
3. **Bidir DShot600 on the H743 needs four DMA streams for motors** (2 × TIMx_UP plus 2 × capture). DMAMUX makes this easy, but you must group the four motors on two timers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 100 °C | 2.32 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.56 mΩ (design corner) |

### 1.2 Current-path topology

In 6-step operation exactly two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel current flows through the complementary FET's channel, not its body diode, except during dead time.

Per leg:

- The leg carries current for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.
- During its 120° "high" sector, the HS FET conducts for fraction D and the LS FET (synchronous) for 1 − D.
- During its 120° "low" sector, the LS FET is fully on.

Per-FET RMS-squared currents:

$$I_{HS}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I^2,\qquad I_{LS}^2 = \frac{1+(1-D)}{3}I^2$$

Per motor (6 FETs):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\cdot\frac{2}{3}I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.56\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{32.8\ W}$$
