## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings follow from the math in Sections 1–3.

1. **The ESC MCUs drive the FETs, not the H743.** In a 4-in-1 architecture, each channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32) and a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 only sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is the ESC firmware's switching frequency.
2. **"Nano" and "80A burst" conflict.** At 80A per channel, the power stage dissipates about **69 W** total (Section 2). A 20×20 mm board cannot absorb that for more than about 1–2 s. My recommendations:
   - Use a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC.
   - Rate 80A as a **≤5 s burst** only. The derived continuous rating is ≈ 45–50A per channel with prop-wash airflow.
3. **Keep the H743 off the ESC copper.** The ESC board reaches about 110°C at the end of a burst. The H743 LQFP100 is rated to Tj = 125°C (industrial grade 140°C on some variants). Its IMU drifts badly with thermal gradients. Use a separate stacked FC board.
4. **All-four-channels-at-80A is a 6.4 kW event** (≈290A from a 6S pack). A single battery-line shunt cannot handle it (Section 2.6). Size the battery connector, XT60/XT90, and pack C-rating accordingly, or treat 80A as a per-channel peak and not a simultaneous one.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, two phases carry I_ph at any moment:
- the **PWM leg**, whose current alternates between the high-side and low-side FET because of synchronous rectification (complementary PWM);
- the **static leg**, whose low-side FET is fully on.

With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg conducts through *a* FET for the whole period, D + (1−D) = 1, apart from dead time.
