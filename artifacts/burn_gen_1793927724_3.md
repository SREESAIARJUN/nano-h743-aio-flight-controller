# Job #55: STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC
## 80 A Burst Thermal/Loss Model, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Netlists

---

## 0. Executive Summary and Feasibility

The model's first result is that a nano form factor cannot sustain 4 × 80 A, even briefly. At 80 A on all four channels, the power stage dissipates about **100 W**. A 20 × 20 mm board has about **2.1 J/K** of heat capacity, so it heats at about **48 K/s**. It hits a 110 °C copper limit in about **1.5 s** from a 40 °C start.

To make "80 A burst" a defensible spec, I recommend one of these:

| Option | 80 A burst capability |
|---|---|
| **A** (recommended) | Rate 80 A as a **single-channel, ≤1 s** burst. Rate all four channels at 35–40 A continuous, 55 A for 5 s. |
| **B** | Move to 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers, 5 × 6 mm FETs. 4 × 80 A then lasts about 4–5 s. |
| **C** | Keep nano and add a bonded heat-spreader (Al, ≥3 g). This roughly triples the burst time. |

**Architecture note.** The H743 does not drive the gates directly. Each ESC channel uses its own low-cost MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, AM32 firmware) with a 3-phase gate driver. The H743, running ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600 to these four MCUs. All of the DMA work in §3 lives on the H743 side.

**All component parameters below are representative assumptions.** Substitute datasheet values for your chosen parts. Every equation is parameterized so you can do this directly.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, the current always flows through **two FETs in series**:
- either the PWM-leg high-side FET (for fraction D) or its low-side sync FET (for fraction 1−D),
- plus the static low-side FET of the return leg.

So the conduction loss is nearly independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds25}\, k_T = 2(80)^2(1.0\text{ m}\Omega)(1.5) = \mathbf{19.2\ W/channel}$$

Copper and via losses add to this:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The gate currents are:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}} = \frac{7}{5.7} = 1.23\text{ A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} = \frac{3}{3.2} = 0.94\text{ A}$$

The ideal transition times follow from the charge that must move during each phase:

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.1\text{ ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 10.7\text{ ns}$$

Common-source and loop inductance (about 1–2 nH in a nano layout) slow these edges. I use practical values of **t_on ≈ 20 ns** and **t_off ≈ 25 ns**.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(45 ns)(48k) | 2.18 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance (C_oss) | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.030 W |
| Gate drive (driver-side) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.029 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.9 W/channel** |

**Design levers:**
- Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would drop to about 1.1 W, but you would trade away low-throttle smoothness.
- Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Trimming t_dt to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel.
