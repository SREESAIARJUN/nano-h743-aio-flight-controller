# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the specification for Job #47, so this design uses common gimbal-hardware assumptions, listed in §0. Each assumption is a single constant in the code and can be changed without touching the derivations.

## 0. Assumptions

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G474 class: Cortex-M4F, 170 MHz, single-precision FPU |
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5 Ω, L ≈ 2 mH (L_d ≈ L_q), K_t ≈ 0.08 N·m/A |
| Bus | V_dc = 12 V |
| Current sensing | 2-shunt or 3-shunt, sampled at the PWM center |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (MT6835/AS5048A class), one per joint |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI at 24 MHz, ODR 8 kHz, mounted roughly symmetric about the plate center |
| Loop rates | Current loop 20 kHz; rate loop 4 kHz; attitude loop 1 kHz; IMU sampling 8 kHz with decimation |
| Gimbal order | Yaw (base) → Roll → Pitch (camera) |

## 1. Field-Oriented Control: Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)\quad(\text{assuming } i_a+i_b+i_c=0)
$$

Park transform, with electrical angle $\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}})$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 Plant Model and Decoupling

The surface-mounted PMSM in the rotor frame is:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The cross-coupling terms are cancelled by feedforward:

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \hat\omega_e \hat L\, i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \hat\omega_e(\hat L\, i_d + \hat\lambda_m)
$$

After decoupling, each axis reduces to $G(s) = \dfrac{1}{Ls+R}$.

### 1.3 Pole-Zero Cancellation Tuning

Choose $K_i/K_p = R/L$ so the PI zero cancels the plant pole. With a target closed-loop bandwidth $\omega_c$:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
T(s) = \frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

Keep $\omega_c \le 2\pi f_{\text{pwm}}/20$ to account for the one-sample computation delay plus the ZOH half-sample delay. At 20 kHz, use $\omega_c = 2\pi\cdot 1000$ rad/s.
