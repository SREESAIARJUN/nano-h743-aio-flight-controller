## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

### Partitioning

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own motor MCU, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. Example: AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32.

| Block | Device / Spec |
|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz SYSCLK, TIM1 kernel clock 200 MHz |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate driver (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver, about 1.5 A source / 2.5 A sink |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst rating | 80 A per motor channel, 10 s |

The 6S bus at 25.2 V leaves only about 37% derating on 40 V FETs. Inductive spikes must stay below 32 V. This requires a bulk low-ESR capacitor plus local MLCCs; see §5.

### Assumed MOSFET Parameters

These are representative of a 40 V, ~1 mΩ PQFN device. Substitute datasheet values for the final part.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| On-resistance @ 25 °C, Vgs = 10 V | R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| Temp. coefficient (normalized @ 125 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Gate-drain charge | Q_gd | 12 nC |
| Gate plateau voltage | V_pl | 3.0 V |
| Output capacitance @ 25 V | C_oss | 1.5 nF |
| Body diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θJC | 1.0 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D. The low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D) − 2·t_dt·f_sw.
- **Static leg:** the LS FET is on continuously for the 120° sector.

### 1.2 Temperature-Corrected Resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Instantaneous Conduction Loss per Motor Channel

At any instant, the current path contains one FET of the PWM leg (HS or sync-LS) and one static LS FET:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Parasitic Resistances in the Path

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Copper, 2 oz × 4 paralleled layers, ~15 mm path | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Current shunt (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Solder joints / motor pads | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |

Using the parasitic copper value as the realistic case:

$$
P_{cond,total} \approx 19.2 + 1.6 = \mathbf{20.8\ W/channel}
$$

### 1.5 Per-FET Average Loss

Each FET conducts during 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, so the loss is spread across the six FETs:

$$
\bar{P}_{FET,cond} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

### 2.1 Transition Times

Transition times come from gate charge and gate drive current. With R_g,total = 2.5 Ω and V_drv = 10 V:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{7}{2.5} = 2.8\ \text{A}\ (\text{driver-limited to }1.5\ \text{A})
$$

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} = 8\ \text{ns}\ (\text{use } 20\ \text{ns with } L_s \text{ and Miller effects})
$$

For a conservative estimate, take t_r + t_f = 40 ns.

### 2.2 Overlap (V–I) Loss

The PWM-leg HS FET is the hard-switched device:

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{sw,VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery Loss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance Loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Dead-Time Body Diode Conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns and two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
