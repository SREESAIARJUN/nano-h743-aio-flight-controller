# STM32H743 FC + 80 A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #31)

> **Read this first:** I don't have Job #31's BOM, stack-up or MOSFET selection. Every number below rests on the stated assumptions (Section 0). The equations are general. Substitute your datasheet values before sign-off. I also flag one finding that should go to a design review: **an "80 A burst" rating on a nano-class board is thermally capacitance-limited to a few seconds if all four channels burst together.** Section 3 shows the derivation.

## 0. Architecture & Assumptions

**Architecture assumed:** A single-board stack. The STM32H743 flight controller runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The H743 does **not** commutate motors. Motor control on the H7 is not supported by ArduPilot, and a 48 kHz six-step loop belongs on dedicated MCUs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step current distribution

In complementary six-step operation, each phase leg is active for 2 of 6 sectors (1/3 of the electrical period).

- When a leg is active, its high-side conducts for duty D.
- Its low-side conducts for 1 − D (synchronous rectification).
- The return leg's low-side conducts continuously.

RMS current per device:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I_{ph}^2 ,\qquad I_{LS,rms}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I_{ph}^2 \ \ \text{(averaged over role rotation: } \tfrac{1}{3}I_{ph}^2 \text{ at } D\to1)$$

At any instant, exactly **two** channel resistances carry I_ph. This gives the useful invariant:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D for synchronous PWM)}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}(80)^2(1.6\,\text{m}\Omega) = 3.41\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.6\,\text{m}\Omega) = 20.5\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction (frequently forgotten)

**Copper.** The path is roughly 15 mm of 4 × 2 oz planes, about 0.25 mΩ effective round-trip per channel at 100 °C:

$$6400 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.6\ \text{W/ch}$$

**Common shunt.** The worst case is all four channels at 80 A, so I_bat ≈ 4 × D × 80. At D ≈ 1 this is about 320 A:

$$P_{sh} = 320^2 \times 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W}$$

This is a design flaw at a 0.2 mΩ shunt. Use 0.1 mΩ, or Kelvin-sensed copper plus a per-channel low-side shunt.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches (one hard-switched pair per channel at any time).

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Transition times:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{sw} R_{g,on}}{V_{drv} - V_{pl}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}\cdot 5}{7} = 10.7\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_f = \frac{Q_{sw} R_{g,off}}{V_{pl}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}\cdot 3}{3} = 15.0\ \text{ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(25.7\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.24\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**This is as large as the overlap loss.** Trimming dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify against shoot-through margin on the scope at T_j,max.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, C_oss and gate charge

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

Gate charge loss is about 0.029 W per FET. Most of it dissipates in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

### 2.4 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.24 + 1.23 + 0.06 + 0.02 \approx \mathbf{2.55\ W/ch}$$

**Scaling law (use this for frequency trades):**

$$P_{sw} \propto f_{sw}$$

Dropping from 48 to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.
