# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bidirectional DShot Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Architecture Corrections

I don't have the Job #18 BOM, stack-up, or board outline. Every number below comes from the stated assumptions. Replace them with your datasheet values and the equations still hold.

**Correction 1: the H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or GD32 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU does commutation and the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. The loss model below therefore applies to the ESC MCU's switching, not to the H743.

**Correction 2: 80 A burst on a nano board is a thermal-time problem, not a FET problem.** "Nano" usually means a 20×20 mm mounting pattern. As Section 3 shows, 80 A per motor on that board area is survivable for roughly 1–2 s, not continuously. Commercial "80 A" ratings at this size are burst/marketing figures. Section 3.4 explains how to state the rating honestly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = **1.55 mΩ**. Iterate this together with the thermal model in Section 3, because the two are coupled.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation step, two legs carry the phase current I_ph:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the whole step.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}$$

Because of synchronous rectification, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. Battery current, by contrast, does depend on it: I_bat ≈ D·I_ph.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.55\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.8\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

Two non-FET conduction terms also matter at this current:

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase copper / pads / vias (2 paths, 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ each) | 0.6 mΩ | 3.8 W |
| Shunt resistor (if used, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

At 80 A, the copper losses are comparable to a whole FET. Shunt loss is often avoided entirely on nano ESCs by using virtual current sensing.

**Per-FET peak:** the conducting FET pair splits about 19.8 W, so the worst individual FET dissipates about **10 W** instantaneously. The time-averaged value is lower because conduction rotates through all six FETs over an electrical cycle (each FET is active for 120° of every 360°).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The static leg changes state only at commutation, which is negligible.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Body diode during dead time | V_f × I × 2 t_dt × f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr × V × f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.04 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss × V × f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g, 2 FETs) | 2 × Q_g × V_gs × f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching, per channel** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Design levers:**

- Dead-time diode loss is about 25% of switching loss. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns, if shoot-through margin allows, saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- At 24 kHz, switching loss halves (≈ 1.35 W), at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise. At 80 A burst the dominant term is conduction loss, so 48 kHz is affordable.

### 2.1 Gate drive sizing

For a 20 ns Miller transition:

$$I_g = \frac{Q_{gd}}{t} = \frac{10\,\mathrm{nC}}{20\,\mathrm{ns}} = 0.5\ \mathrm{A}$$
