# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package, Job #29

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

The design rests on a few assumptions that need to be stated first.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm (M3/M2 mounting) | A 20×20 board cannot dissipate 80 A-class losses |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | 4S scales linearly in the switching terms |
| Topology | 3-phase full bridge ×4, 24 MOSFETs | Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| ESC MCU | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 | **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. |
| ESC PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz | |
| "80 A burst" | Per motor, ≤ 2 s, then derating | See §2.5. 80 A continuous is not achievable here. |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C | Use your actual part's datasheet values |

This architecture is standard for AIO boards. The H743 never sees 48 kHz switching. It only generates DShot frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.49 ≈ **2.23 mΩ**. Use 2.25 mΩ. Since loss rises with temperature and temperature rises with loss, iterate to a fixed point (§2.4).

### 1.2 Current path in six-step synchronous PWM

In each 60° commutation step:
- One phase is "high." Its high-side (HS) FET conducts for D. Its low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- One phase is "low." Its LS FET conducts 100% of the step.
- The third phase floats.

Two FETs therefore carry the full current at every instant:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each phase spends 1/3 of the time "high" and 1/3 "low":

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{(1-D)+1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at the 80 A burst, D ≈ 1

- Per motor: P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 0.00225 = **28.8 W**
- Per FET (D = 1): HS = LS = 6400 × 0.00225 / 3 = **4.8 W**
- Low throttle (D → 0) concentrates loss in the LS FETs: up to 2/3·I²R. Size thermals for the LS FETs.

### 1.5 Ripple correction

Add the RMS of the inductor current ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH, worst case D = 0.5 gives ΔI ≈ 8.75 A. The ripple term is 6.4 A², under 0.1%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per step (the PWM'd high phase). That leg's HS FET is hard-switched; its LS FET switches with body-diode commutation.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The edge times come from gate drive. The driver sources and sinks about 1.5 A into a Miller plateau with Q_gd ≈ 6 nC, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 10 ns:

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(200\,\text{ns}) \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing dead time in AM32 (if the gate driver timing permits) gives the largest single gain here.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, C_oss, and gate charge

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Q_rr | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.04 W |
| Gate (6 FETs) | 6 · Q_g · V_gs · f_eff ≈ 6 × 35 nC × 10 V × 48k / 3 | 0.03 W (in the driver) |

### 2.4 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A

| Source | Loss (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs, 2.25 mΩ) | 28.8 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 |
| Current shunt, 0.5 mΩ (if per-motor sensing) | 3.2 |
| PCB copper, ~0.4 mΩ effective per path | 2.6 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 36.9 W** |
| **×4 motors** | **≈ 148 W** |

Bridge efficiency at 80 A is 1 − 36.9 / (25.2 × 80) ≈ **98.2%**. The efficiency figure looks good, but 148 W cannot be dissipated from a 30×30 mm board continuously.

### 2.5 Electro-thermal fixed point

Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{amb} + P(T_j^{(n)}) \cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

Convergence requires dP/dT · Z_th < 1. If it is not, the design runs away thermally and the firmware current limit must intervene.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network: junction → case → PCB → air

| Element | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| R_th(j-c), PDFN3.3 | 1.5 K/W | datasheet class |
| R_th(c-PCB) via solder + thermal vias | 3 K/W | ~12 × 0.3 mm vias, filled |
| Board spreading, per FET region | 8 K/W | 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner |
| C_th, board total | ≈ 5 J/K | ~3 g Cu (0.385 J/g·K) + ~3 g FR4 (1.1 J/g·K) + components |
| R_th, board→air, prop wash 8 m/s | ≈ 1.2 K/W total | h ≈ 60 W/m²K over ~2 × 9 cm² plus components |

### 3.2 Burst (adiabatic board), all four motors at 80 A

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{148}{5} \approx 30\ \text{K/s}$$

Starting from a 45 °C board, the board limit of 110 °C is reached in about **2.2 s**.

### 3.3 Junction overshoot above board

Per LS FET: 4.8 W + its share of switching/dead time (≈ 0.6 W) ≈ 5.4 W.

$$\Delta T_{j-board} = 5.4 \times (1.5 + 3) \approx 24\ \text{K (quasi-steady after ~100 ms)}$$
