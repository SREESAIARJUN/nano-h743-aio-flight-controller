# STM32H743 FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #32)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Findings

Two findings shape everything below. Settle them before layout.

**A. The H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller in this architecture.**
ArduPilot runs on the H743 and *emits* bidirectional DShot600. Each of the four motor channels needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and closes the commutation loop. The 48 kHz figure is therefore an ESC firmware parameter, not an H743 timer setting. The model below reflects that split.

**B. "Nano" and "80A burst ×4" are physically incompatible without a heatsink.**
The thermal model (§3) shows:
- On a bare 20×20 or 30×30 board, the stack reaches its thermal limit in roughly 2–4 s at 4×80A.
- Board copper at a total battery current of 320A adds about 20 W by itself.

My recommendation is either of these:
- Re-rate the board to "80A per channel, ≤2 s, single-motor-dominant burst", or
- Move to a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger form factor with an aluminium heat spreader.

All component values below are **design assumptions, clearly labelled**. Re-run the numbers with the datasheet values of the FETs you actually select.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses (6-step trapezoidal, complementary/synchronous PWM)

Each phase leg spends one third of the electrical period in each of three states:
- **PWM'd:** HS on for fraction D, LS on for (1−D).
- **Sink:** LS on 100 %.
- **Floating.**

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D \, I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
\qquad
P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Summing over the three legs gives a channel total that is independent of D, because exactly two FETs carry the current at every instant:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\, I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$
