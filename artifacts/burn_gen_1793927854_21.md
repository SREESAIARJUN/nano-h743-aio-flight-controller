## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Verdict

Two points need to be settled before any calculation, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight-controller board. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC board. The ESC board carries its own commutation MCU(s), which drive the gate drivers and FETs. Typical choices are an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The loss model below applies to the ESC board. The DMA model applies to the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor fails the energy balance.** Section 3 shows that with four motors at 80 A, total dissipation is roughly 90 W. On a 20×20 or 30×30 board with about 5 J/K of thermal mass, that heats the board at about 18 K/s adiabatically. A 10-second burst is therefore physically impossible without destroying the board.

The defensible rating, which is the basis of this design, is:
- **80 A single-motor burst, ≤1 s, 30.5×30.5 mm board, 2 FETs in parallel per switch.**
- **35–40 A continuous per motor.**

If marketing requires "80 A" on a 20×20 board, that label is not supportable. Typical honest 20×20 ratings are 12–35 A.

> **Validation caveat:** All device parameters below are representative values for the 40 V, sub-1 mΩ, 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. Before release, substitute the actual datasheet values (R_DS(on) max vs. T_j, Q_g, Q_gd, Q_rr, Q_oss, R_θJC, Z_θJC curves). Then verify by IR thermography under a dynamometer load. Treat the netlist as a reference design requiring DRC/ERC and layout review, not a production-released schematic.
