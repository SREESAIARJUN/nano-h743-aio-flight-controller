# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15)
## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Executive Summary

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Per-channel loss at 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz | **≈ 20.4 W** in FETs, plus ≈ 3.2 W in copper |
| Whole-board loss, all 4 channels at 80 A | **≈ 94 W** |
| Adiabatic burst budget, 50 °C → 110 °C board | **≈ 2.0–2.3 s** (all four motors) |
| Realistic continuous rating (prop-wash cooled) | **≈ 25–30 A per motor** |
| DShot timer | **TIM1 CH1–CH4** (PE9/PE11/PE13/PE14), one timer group, BIDIR |
| DMA cost of bidirectional DShot | 5 streams (TIM1_UP + 4 × TIM1_CHx), routed via DMAMUX1 |

**The 80 A figure is a burst rating of about 2 seconds, not a continuous rating.** The derivation below shows why. Marketing should not print "80A" without "burst ≤ 2 s".

**Architectural clarification.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated motor MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 is the DShot master. Switching losses therefore come from the ESC MCU's PWM settings, and the FC side is governed by ArduPilot's timer/DMA constraints. Both are modelled here.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 1.47\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Bridge conduction

With synchronous rectification in 6-step drive, exactly two switch positions carry the phase current at every instant:

- During the PWM on-time: the HS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time: the LS FET of phase A and the LS FET of phase B.

The duty cycle D therefore cancels out:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.47\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

- Each phase leg conducts for 240° of the electrical cycle: 120° of HS/LS PWM sharing and 120° of solid LS conduction.
- Average loss per FET ≈ 18.8 / 6 ≈ **3.1 W**.
- Instantaneous peak per FET = I²R = **9.4 W**.

**Low-eRPM hazard.** At high eRPM (above about 1 kHz electrical), the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) averages the 9.4 W pulses. At stall or startup, eRPM approaches 0, so one LS FET may carry the full 9.4 W continuously. ESC firmware must apply a stall-current limit (AM32: current limit + stall protection).

### 2.4 Copper loss

Assume 8-layer, 2 oz copper on the power layers. Effective path resistance per channel (bus planes + phase pad + vias + shunt) is about 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Copper resistance rises about 0.39 %/K, so this figure is optimistic when the board is hot.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching transition (HS FET only)

The LS FET turns on after dead time, with its body diode already conducting, so it switches near ZVS.

**Gate currents during the plateau:**

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_G} = \frac{6.8}{2.5} = 2.72\,\text{A}, \qquad I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_G} = 1.28\,\text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} = \frac{14\,\text{nC}}{2.72\,\text{A}} = 5.1\,\text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,off}} = \frac{14\,\text{nC}}{1.28\,\text{A}} = 10.9\,\text{ns}$$
