# Job #65: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC
## Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timing, and Netlist Specification

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Problems with the Brief

Four parts of the brief need clarification before the analysis is meaningful.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC.** ArduPilot sends DShot to ESCs and does not commutate motors. This design therefore uses the following arrangement:
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot.
   - Four ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware) commutate the motors.
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-MOSFETs.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as per-motor, ≤ 2 s, non-simultaneous.**
   - Four motors at 80 A each would draw 320 A from a battery through a nano-format board. No XT60 or 30×30 copper stack survives that.
   - Sustained continuous rating is targeted at 40 A per channel.
3. **48 kHz is the ESC PWM frequency.**
   - It is unrelated to the DShot bit rate (600 kbit/s).
   - The two are treated as independent subsystems.
4. **Component parameters are design-target values, not a specific datasheet.**
   - Every number marked † must be replaced with the values for the selected MOSFET and driver, then the model re-run.
   - The equations are the deliverable. The numbers are a worked example.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Bridge-level derivation

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. Take one 60° sector in which phase A switches with PWM and phase B low side is held on. The phase current I flows through:

- **A-high** for fraction D of each PWM period
- **A-low** (synchronous rectifier) for fraction (1 − D)
- **B-low** for 100% of the sector

Summing the three contributions:

$$
P_{cond,sector} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

**Conduction loss is independent of duty cycle.** At any instant, two R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current. This holds for every sector, so it is also the bridge average.

### 2.2 Per-FET average

By symmetry over one electrical revolution, each of the six FETs carries one third of the total:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}
$$

Within its own active sector, a single FET can momentarily dissipate the full I²R_ds. This matters only at very low eRPM, where the sector duration approaches the FET's thermal time constant. The startup and stall case is addressed in §4.4.

### 2.3 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

Because R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, the problem is solved by fixed-point iteration (§4.3). For the first pass, take T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 1.6 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 2.348\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.348\times10^{-3} = 30.05\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 10.0\ \text{W}
$$

The supply-to-motor PCB copper path adds further loss (§4.2).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low side turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at zero voltage.

### 3.1 Gate current during the transition

Turn-on (charging through the driver pull-up):

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{drv,up} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3}{8.3} = 0.843\ \text{A}
$$

Turn-off (discharging through the driver pull-down):

$$
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{drv,dn} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{6.3} = 0.476\ \text{A}
$$

### 3.2 Transition times

The transition time is the current-rise portion (Q_gs2) plus the voltage-fall portion (Q_gd):

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\ \text{nC}}{0.843} = 11.9\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
t_{off} = \frac{10\ \text{nC}}{0.476} = 21.0\ \text{ns}
$$

### 3.3 V–I overlap energy and power

$$
E_{on} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\, t_{on} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 11.9\text{n} = 12.0\ \mu\text{J}
$$

$$
E_{off} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 21.0\text{n} = 21.2\ \mu\text{J}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = (E_{on} + E_{off})\,f_{sw} = 33.2\,\mu\text{J} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.59\ \text{W}
$$
