# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Design Model

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction losses, 48 kHz switching losses, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and representative netlists.

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two premises in the brief would sink the design if left unchallenged.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller while also running ArduPilot.** ArduPilot (ChibiOS) has no BLDC commutation path, and 48 kHz six-step or FOC across 4 motors with zero-cross detection would collide with the scheduler. The correct architecture is:
   - **H743**: flight controller. It sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware). Each drives one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz.
2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is only a seconds-scale burst rating.** Section 3 shows this numerically. A 20×20 board can survive roughly a 1 s all-motor burst or a ~4 s single-motor burst. Its continuous rating is about 15–20 A per motor in prop wash. Marketing "80 A" must be specified with a duration.

All device parameters below are **representative assumptions**. Before release, substitute datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET and gate driver.

## 2. Loss Model Per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With trapezoidal six-step drive and synchronous rectification, at any instant the phase current flows through **exactly two FET channels in series**:
- either the high side of the PWM leg or its synchronous low side, and
- the continuously-on low side of the sink leg.

Ripple is small at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (I_rms ≈ I_avg), so:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{hot} = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.006 \times 85] = 1.3 \times 1.51 = 1.963\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.963\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.926\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{25.1\ W}
$$

**PCB copper loss.** Assume 2 oz copper on outer layers and 1 oz inner, about 0.15 mΩ of effective phase path:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.15\times10^{-3} \approx 0.96\ \text{W}
$$

**Thermal runaway check.** Note that P_cond depends on T_j through R_hot. The loop is stable only if

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\, Z_{th} < 1, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\alpha = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.3\times10^{-3} \times 0.006 \approx 0.10\ \text{W/K}
$$

Against the per-FET R_th(j-c) of 1.5 K/W, 0.10 × 1.5 is far below 1, so the junction-to-case loop is stable. The board level is a different story. With a sustained board resistance of ~8 K/W per channel, 0.1 × 8 = 0.8, which approaches marginal. **Sustained 80 A on this board is thermally unstable.**

### 2.2 Switching Losses (Only the PWM Leg Switches)

**Gate currents during the plateau:**

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{6.5}{5.7} = 1.14\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3.5}{5.7} = 0.61\ \text{A}
$$

**Transition times:**

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{9\ \text{nC}}{1.14} = 7.9\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{9\ \text{nC}}{0.61} = 14.7\ \text{ns}
$$

(Check the driver's actual source/sink limit. FD6288-class drivers source about 1.5 A and sink about 1.8 A, so R_g dominates here.)
