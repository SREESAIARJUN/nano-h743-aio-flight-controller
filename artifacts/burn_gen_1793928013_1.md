## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, one 3-phase bridge, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation, two phase legs conduct at any instant. Current always flows through exactly **one high-side and one low-side device in series**:

- During the PWM on-time (D), the path is the high-side FET plus the low-side FET of the return phase.
- During the off-time (1−D), the synchronous low-side FET conducts instead of the high-side FET.

The path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) elements, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on),hot} = 2(80)^2(1.28\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{16.38\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 2.73\ \text{W}$$

**Worst-case single FET.** At D ≈ 1, the conducting high-side FET dissipates I²R = 8.19 W during its 120° window. This matters only for electrical periods longer than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). That occurs at very low eRPM, such as stall or startup at full current, which the firmware must prevent.

**Copper and shunt losses (add separately):**

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 (R_{shunt} + R_{trace}) = 6400 \times (0.5 + 0.3)\,\text{mΩ} = 5.1\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss

One leg is PWM-switched at a time, so one hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
