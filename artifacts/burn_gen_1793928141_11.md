# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Finding

Three points need to be settled before the math.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated MCU, and the H743 talks to all four over bidirectional DShot600. A common choice is one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32, or one dual-motor MCU per pair. Running FOC for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported configuration.

2. **The 80 A figure.** In this model, 80 A is the **phase current per motor during a burst** on 6S (25.2 V max). That is about 2 kW per motor. On a "nano" footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), the model below shows this current is **thermally limited to bursts of roughly 1 to 1.5 s** with no heatsink. A continuous 80 A rating is not physically achievable at this size. Treat any marketing spec that claims otherwise as a red flag.

3. **Component parameters.** All values below are representative placeholders and must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values. The model is linear in these inputs, so substitution is straightforward.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step, synchronous rectification)

During any commutation sector, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the synchronous low-side FET for 1−D. The current I flows through one R_ds the whole time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is fully on.

This gives:

$$P_{cond}=I^2R_{ds}(T)\,[D+(1-D)]+I^2R_{ds}(T)=2I^2R_{ds}(T)$$

$$R_{ds}(T)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W | 46 W |
| 100 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 16.8 W | 67 W |
| 125 °C | 1.44 mΩ | 18.4 W | 74 W |

Per-FET view: each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Its instantaneous dissipation is I²R, which is 9.2 W at 125 °C. This matters for the junction-to-case rise, covered in §3.

**PCB copper loss.** Add copper resistance. A 2 oz trace that is 5 mm wide and 10 mm long, paralleled across 4 layers, is about 0.12 mΩ. At 80 A on two legs that adds roughly 1.5 W per motor. This loss is easy to forget, and it heats the same copper the FETs dump heat into.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Switching losses come from the PWM leg only: one hard-switched pair per motor.

| Term | Formula | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·20e-9·48e3 | 0.968 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 60e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.073 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (approx.) | 0.030 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·2·100e-9·48e3 | 0.614 W |
| **FET switching total** | | **≈ 1.69 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver IC) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw·2 = 60e-9·10·48e3·2 | 0.058 W |

**Total per motor at T_j = 125 °C:** 18.4 + 1.5 + 1.7 ≈ **21.6 W**. For four motors under simultaneous burst, that is **≈ 86 W**.

Design levers, in order of impact:

1. Lower R_ds. Conduction is about 85% of the loss, so this dominates everything else.
2. Reduce dead time. Going from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W
