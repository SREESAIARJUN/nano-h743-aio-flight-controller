## 0. Architecture Review: Three Problems in the Brief

These issues determine whether the design works. The math in later sections assumes they are resolved as follows.

| # | Brief assumption | Problem | Resolution adopted |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The H743 runs the ESC at 48 kHz | ArduPilot cannot do motor commutation. No ArduPilot firmware exists for driving 12 half-bridges from the FC MCU. | One commutation MCU per channel running AM32 (e.g. AT32F421K8U7 / STM32G071). The H743 talks to them over DShot600 bidirectional. This is the industry-standard 4-in-1 topology. |
| 2 | "Nano" form factor | A 20×20 mm board cannot reject approximately 110 W per 80 A burst. The battery input would be about 320 A (4 × 80 A). | 30.5×30.5 mm minimum for power (or a 20×20 FC stacked on a 30×30 ESC). The 80 A rating is limited to a time-bounded burst (about 1–2 s, see §3). |
| 3 | 80 A continuous-like rating | XT60/XT90 leads, 12 AWG pads and 2 oz copper do not support 320 A aggregate. | 80 A per channel is non-simultaneous peak. 50 A continuous per channel is the realistic aggregate design point (§3.5). |

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step drive, two phase legs carry I_ph at any instant. With complementary PWM (AM32 default), the current path during on-time is HS(a) → motor → LS(b). During off-time it is LS(a) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(b).

Either way, exactly two MOSFET channels conduct, except during dead time.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,(1 - 2t_{dt}f_{sw})$$

The current ripple adds a correction to the RMS value:

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor at full throttle (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line, D ≈ 0.9):

ΔI = 25.2·0.09 / (15e-6·48e3) ≈ 3.2 A, so the ripple term is ≈ 0.85 A² (negligible).

R_DS(on)(110 °C) = 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 = 1.6 mΩ

**P_cond = 2 × 6400 × 1.6e-3 × (1 − 0.0096) ≈ 20.3 W**

Each MOSFET conducts for 120° electrical, so per-device average loss is about P_cond/6 × 2 ≈ 6.8 W when averaged across the six devices. The asymmetry between high side and low side is handled in §2.6.

### 2.2 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

In 6-step, only one leg is PWM'd at a time, so there is one hard-switched device per PWM cycle:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 33e-9 × 48e3 = **1.60 W**

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

= 0.85 × 80 × 200e-9 × 48e3 = **0.65 W**

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw}$$

= 40e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.048 W**

The extra current spike also adds turn-on loss. Include roughly +10 % on P_sw: ≈ 0.16 W.

### 2.5 Output-capacitance and gate loss

P_oss = ½·C_oss(er)·V²·f_sw = 0.5 × 1.8e-9 × 635 × 48e3 = **0.027 W**

P_gate = 2 × Q_g × V_drv × f_sw = 2 × 55e-9 × 10 × 48e3 = **0.053 W**
(This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the MOSFET.)
