# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4‑in‑1 ESC: Design Model, Loss Budget, DShot Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I have no prior context for "Job #9", so this package uses explicit assumptions. Change any value in §0.2 and every result below can be recalculated from the formulas.

### 0.1 Architecture

An STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at 48 kHz on a nano board, and ArduPilot is not an ESC firmware. The design is therefore split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot. Generates bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 / BLHeli_32-class firmware. 48 kHz complementary PWM, 6‑step commutation. |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3 half-bridges, bootstrap) | One per motor. |
| Power stage | 24 N-ch MOSFETs, 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm | 6 per motor. |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm (M2), 8‑layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes | Stacked FC/ESC or AIO. |

### 0.3 Feasibility Flag

80 A × 4 motors on a 20×20 board is achievable only as a short burst that relies on thermal mass. §1.5 shows a sustainable continuous rating of about 45 A per motor with prop-wash airflow. Treat any "80 A continuous" claim for this form factor as marketing.

## 1. Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction Topology in 6‑Step with Synchronous Rectification

Within each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one is held static-low. The phase current I flows through:

- **PWM leg:** high side for D·T_s, low side (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_s.
- **Static leg:** low side for 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant exactly two channels carry I. The conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 0.70\,[1+0.0055\cdot85] = 1.027\ \text{mΩ}$$

PCB and solder path resistance per motor (phase pad to bus, both legs) is estimated at R_Cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ for 2 oz with stitched planes.

**At 80 A:**

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2\cdot 6400\cdot 1.027\times10^{-3} = 13.15\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 6400\cdot0.15\times10^{-3} = 0.96\ \text{W}$$

### 1.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, so there is one hard-switched device pair per motor.

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.452\ \text{W}$$

**Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode → HS turn-on):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance (C_oss) loss, 2 nodes:**

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 2.0\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.061\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction, 2 edges per period:**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\cdot 2t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

**Gate charge (dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, 2 switching FETs):**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$
