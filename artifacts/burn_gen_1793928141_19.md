## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Feasibility Warning

The STM32H743 acts as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors itself. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated motor MCU running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. This is the industry-standard partition, and the 48 kHz figure matches AM32's PWM setting.

```

 IMU/Baro ──┤  STM32H743 (480 MHz) ├──────────┬────────┬────────┬────────┐

            └──────────────────────┘     AT32F421  AT32F421 AT32F421 AT32F421
                                         (AM32)    (AM32)   (AM32)   (AM32)
                                            │ 6×PWM   ...
                                         FD6288Q 3-ph gate driver
                                            │
                                         6× N-MOSFET half-bridges → Motor
```

> ⚠ **Feasibility flag:** As derived in §2, 80 A continuous per channel on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board is not thermally sustainable. "80 A" must be specified as a time-bounded burst. The model below produces the allowable burst duration, so the marketing rating and the physics agree.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, phase current I flows through:

- **Active phase:** the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET (synchronous freewheel) for 1−D. The current is carried continuously by one FET of this leg.
- **Return phase:** its low-side FET conducts 100 % of the time.

So, per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

(The dead-time diode interval is handled separately in §2.4.)

For sinusoidal FOC, the equivalent is P_cond = 3·I_rms,ph²·R_ds. At the same torque this is close in magnitude, so the 6-step form is used as the worst case.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Couple this with the thermal path, T_j = T_a + P·R_th. Conduction-only loss is then a fixed-point problem:

$$P = \frac{2 I^2 R_{25}(1 + \alpha(T_a - 25))}{1 - 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$I_{runaway} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}}$$

With an effective steady-state R_th per channel of ≈ 12 K/W (a nano board in free air, ¼ of the board each), this gives I_runaway = 1/√(2·1e-3·0.005·12) ≈ **91 A**. That is right next to the burst rating, which reinforces that 80 A cannot be a continuous rating.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A
Use T_j = 125 °C as the design point, so R_ds = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W/channel}$$

Per-FET peak: the return-phase low-side FET carries 80²×1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**.

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction loss
Model the phase and bus copper as a trace R:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\frac{L}{W\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu,25} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m},\ \alpha_{Cu} = 0.0039$$

Example path: 2 oz (70 µm), 4 paralleled layers, 4 mm wide, 15 mm effective length, at 85 °C:

R = 1.72e-8·1.234·0.015 / (0.004·4·70e-6) ≈ **0.28 mΩ**

The current crosses about two such segments, so P_Cu ≈ 2·80²·0.28e-3 ≈ **3.6 W/channel**. This is not negligible and argues for 8-layer, 2 oz inner planes under the FETs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at zero voltage.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

Better accuracy comes from deriving t_r and t_f from gate charge: t_sw ≈ Q_gd·R_g,total / (V_drv − V_plateau). Use this once the driver and R_g are finalized.

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction
