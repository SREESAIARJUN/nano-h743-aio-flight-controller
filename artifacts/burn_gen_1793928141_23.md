# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model for Job #14

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Two Up-Front Corrections

I don't have prior context for Job #14, so this model uses stated assumptions. Each one is a parameter you can substitute.

**Correction 1: architecture.** An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A real AIO design has two kinds of processor:

- **The H743** runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600.
- **Four ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) each drive a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

The netlists below follow that split.

**Correction 2: "80 A burst" on a nano board.** On a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board, 80 A per channel is only a transient rating, valid for a few seconds. Section 3 shows that the continuous rating lands near 30–35 A per channel. That matches what commercial boards in this class can actually sustain.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.425 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, current flows in through one phase and out through another:

- **Incoming phase, high side:** conducts for fraction D.
- **Incoming phase, low side:** conducts for (1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw). Synchronous rectification is active.
- **Outgoing phase, low side:** conducts 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[D + (1-D-2t_{dt}f_{sw}) + 1\right] \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 110 °C:

- One FET in full conduction: 6400 × 1.425 mΩ = **9.12 W**
- **P_cond,ch ≈ 18.2 W**
- **All four channels: ≈ 73 W**

**Instantaneous vs averaged loss per FET.** These are different quantities and both matter:

- **Instantaneous:** at any moment, up to about 9 W is concentrated in one device. This is the input to the junction transient.
- **Averaged:** commutation rotates the duty across all six FETs, so the per-FET average is P_ch / 6 ≈ 3 W. This is the input to the board-level thermal model.

At low eRPM (e.g., stall or startup), commutation is slow. The instantaneous figure then governs, which is why 80 A at stall is far
