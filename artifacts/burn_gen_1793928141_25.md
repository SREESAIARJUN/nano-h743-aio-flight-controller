**Scope:** Electrothermal model at 80 A burst, 48 kHz switching-loss model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Three points need to be settled before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 emits DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for bidirectional DShot.
2. **"80 A" is defined here as per-channel phase current, burst.** ESC ratings conventionally refer to phase current. Battery current is roughly I_phase · D.
3. **Feasibility.** 4 × 80 A in a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~28×28 mm board) footprint is thermally marginal. Section 3 shows the board heats at about 20–25 K/s adiabatically at full burst. The burst rating must therefore be time-bounded (≤2–3 s). Alternatively, step up to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs. I recommend publishing a **burst-duration curve**, not a bare "80 A" number.
