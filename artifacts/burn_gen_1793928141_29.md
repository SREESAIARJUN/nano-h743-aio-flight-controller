# Job #44: STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC Architecture Package

**Rev A: Engineering Derivation (pre-layout)**

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design-Integrity Flags

Three issues need to be on the table before any math.

1. **Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This package assumes:
   - **FC:** STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100).
   - **ESC:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 running AM32, plus a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-FETs per channel.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80A burst conflict.** 80A per channel on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 board is not physically credible. Section 3.6 shows that a 30.5×30.5 mm board survives 80A on all four channels for only about 2–3 s with strong prop wash. I recommend one of two paths:
   - Specify **80A burst for 1 s on one channel and 55–60A on all four**, or
   - Move to a 40×40 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs.
3. **Parameter values are representative.** MOSFET and driver values are typical of the 40V, ~0.7 mΩ, 5×6 PQFN class. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute the datasheet numbers of your selected part. Part numbers in the netlist are candidates and must be verified against current datasheets and stock.

## 1. Electrical Model of One ESC Channel (Six-Step, Synchronous)

In trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. One leg is PWM-switched (high-side at duty D, low-side complementary at 1–D). The other leg has its low-side FET held on. Under synchronous rectification, every conducting leg presents exactly one R_DS(on) in the current path at all times.

### 1.1 RMS Current Including Ripple

The phase inductor current is I_L = I_avg + ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 8 µH (typical 2207-class motor, line-to-line/2), D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{8\times10^{-6}\cdot 48\times10^{3}} = 16.4\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 22.4 = 6422\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is 0.35%, which is negligible at 48 kHz. That result is itself an argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where ripple doubles and its contribution quadruples.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Conduction Loss

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Per conducting leg (one FET's resistance in path, shared between HS/LS by duty):

$$P_{cond,leg} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per channel (two legs always conducting):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Electro-thermal fixed point.** Junction temperature depends on loss, and loss depends on R_DS(on), which depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + \theta\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; P^* = \frac{P_{25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_{ref}-25)]}{1 - \alpha_R\,\theta\,P_{25}}$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion:** α_R · θ · P₂₅ < 1. Stay below 0.5 for margin. This criterion is evaluated in §3.5.

**Numbers at T_j = 110 °C** (R_DS = 0.70 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = 1.027 mΩ):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per conducting FET-path | 6422 × 1.027e-3 = **6.60 W** |
| P_cond per channel | **13.2 W** |

### 1.3 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The low-side FET turns on into a conducting body diode (ZVS-like), so the hard-switching loss sits on the high-side FET:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 1.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This loss lands on the low-side FET.
