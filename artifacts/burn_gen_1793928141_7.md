# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, DShot Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two points shape everything that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel has its own ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The loss model therefore belongs to the ESC power stage, and the timer/DMA model belongs to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-format board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a seconds-scale rating, not a thermal steady state.** The math in §2–§3 shows this explicitly. Treat 80 A as a transient limit bounded by the thermal capacitance of the board.

## 1. Assumptions (Edit These, Then Re-run the Equations)

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S LiPo, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Motor phase current, burst, per ESC | 80 A |
| f_sw | ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | MOSFET R_ds(on) at 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V | 0.8 mΩ (40 V, 5×6 PDFN class) |
| k_T | R_ds(on) temperature factor at T_j ≈ 110 °C | 1.5 |
| R_ds,hot | Hot R_ds(on) | 1.2 mΩ |
| t_r, t_f | Switching transitions (FD6288-class driver, ~1 A peak) | 20 ns each |
| t_dt | Dead time | 100 ns |
| V_f | Body diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.85 V |
| Q_rr | Reverse recovery charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss,tr | Time-related output capacitance | 1.2 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 55 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |
| R_cu | Copper path per ESC (pads, planes, vias, motor pads) | 0.4 mΩ |
| D | PWM duty (6-step, synchronous complementary) | 0.5–1.0 |

**Commutation model:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high-side leg. At any instant two phases conduct:
- The **PWM leg** switches. Its HS FET is on for D, and its LS FET is on for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- The **return leg** has its LS FET on continuously.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

The total is independent of D because of synchronous rectification. The dead-time diode term is handled separately.

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.2\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{15.36\ W}
$$

**Hot-spot FET:** the continuously-on return-leg LS FET dissipates

$$
I^2R = 6400 \times 1.2\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{7.68\ W}
$$

for one third of an electrical cycle. Averaged over rotation, each FET sees about 2.6 W from conduction. The instantaneous 7.68 W still matters, because the electrical period at low RPM is long compared with the die's thermal time constant (about 1 ms).

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.85(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{0.65\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery, C_oss, and gate drive

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.2\text{n}(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.037\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(55\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.053\ \text{W}
$$

The gate-drive term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.5 Copper loss

$$
P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{2.56\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Totals

| Term | Per ESC (W) | 4-in-1 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 15.36 | 61.4 |
| Switching | 1.94 | 7.7 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.65 | 2.6 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.10 | 0.4 |
| Gate drive | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| Copper | 2.56 | 10.2 |
| **Total** | **20.7** | **82.6** |

At 80 A per channel, the board dissipates about **83 W**. Conduction plus copper (I²R) is about 87 % of the total. The design lever is therefore R_ds(on) and copper weight, not f_sw.

**Frequency sensitivity:** f_sw-dependent losses are about 2.8 W per ESC at 48 kHz. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per ESC
