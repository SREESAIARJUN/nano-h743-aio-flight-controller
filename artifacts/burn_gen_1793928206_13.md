## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Check

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production AIO uses this split:

```
 VBAT (6S) ──┬──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
             │                                              │
       [FC section]                                  [4-in-1 ESC section]
  STM32H743 (ArduPilot)                          4 × ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32)
     TIM1 CH1..4 ── DShot600 bidir ──────────────►  4 × 3-ph gate driver (FD6288Q class)
     ADC ◄──────── VBAT sense / total current ─────  4 × 6 N-ch MOSFETs (24 FETs)
```

### 0.2 Feasibility Flags (read before layout)

1. **Battery current.** Four motors at 80 A phase current and duty D ≈ 0.9 draw about **288 A** from the battery. This exceeds what an XT60 connector or any "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) copper stack can carry. "80 A burst" must be defined as **per motor, ≤ 10 s, non-simultaneous**, or the board must grow.
2. **Steady state is impossible at 80 A.** Section 3 shows 80 A is only sustainable for seconds.
3. **48 kHz switching costs real power.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. 48 kHz is fine for smoothness, but 24 kHz recovers about 1 W per motor at burst.

## 1. Conduction Losses (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Model

Use six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side, which is AM32's default. In each 120° electrical sector:

- The **active HS** FET conducts for fraction D.
- The **complementary LS** FET in the same leg conducts for (1 − D).
- The **return LS** FET in the opposite leg conducts for 100%.
