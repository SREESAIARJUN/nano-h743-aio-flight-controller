# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Nano Form Factor): Design Model, Job #66

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These can sit on a stacked ESC board or share one PCB. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.

**Status of the numbers.** Every number below derives from the stated assumptions. Swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values before committing. Treat the netlists as reference topology, not a production-verified design. They need a DRC/ERC pass and bench validation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** Under six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases are active at any moment:
- The PWM'd leg: high-side (HS) for fraction D, low-side (LS) for fraction 1−D.
- The return leg: LS on continuously for that 60° sector.

So exactly two R_ds(on) are always in series with the phase current, independent of D.

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-FET average.** Each leg is active 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average loss per FET is:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

The worst FET (the static LS) can see up to I²R during its sector. The electrical frequency is several kHz, far faster than the die thermal time constant (around 1 ms). The average is therefore valid for junction temperature.

**Self-consistent solution.** Iterate the junction temperature until R_ds(on) and T_j agree:

| Iteration | T_j | R_ds | P_cond,ch | P_FET |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.56 W |
| 1 | 90 °C | 1.67 mΩ | 21.4 W | 3.56 W |
| 2 (converged, board at 85 °C) | ≈105 °C | 1.78 mΩ | **22.8 W** | **3.8 W** |

**Board-level conduction loss** with all four channels at 80 A: **≈91 W**.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. All terms below are per channel.

1. **Hard-switching V–I overlap:**
   $$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

2. **Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period):
   $$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_d)\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

3. **Reverse recovery:**
   $$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

4. **Output capacitance:**
   $$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

5. **Gate drive** (two FETs):
   $$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$
   This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

**Per-channel switching total: ≈2.2 W.**

**Design levers:**
- Dead time is the second-largest term. Cutting t_d to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- At 24 kHz, switching loss halves, at the cost of more current ripple.
- At 80 A, switching is under 10 % of total loss. Conduction dominates, so R_ds(on) is the primary selection metric.

## 3. Total Loss and Efficiency

| Item | Per channel | Four channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.8 W | 91.2 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-channel) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper traces and planes (≈0.15 mΩ effective path) | ≈1.0 W | 4 W |
| **Total** | **≈27.6 W** | **≈110 W** |
