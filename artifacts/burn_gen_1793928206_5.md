# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Systems Architecture Model (Job #55)

## 0. Scope and Two Findings to Resolve First

**Finding 1: Architecture.** The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. It has no motor-control-optimised analog front end, and ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The defensible partition is:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600.
- **ESC:** 4 × AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32. Each drives one 3-phase bridge through a gate driver and returns eRPM telemetry on the same wire.

Everything below assumes this partition.

**Finding 2: Physics.** "Nano" (20 × 20 mm) combined with 80 A burst on four channels at once is not thermally credible. Section 3 quantifies this. It shows that:

- 80 A is reachable as a single-channel burst for less than about 1 s.
- 4 × 80 A simultaneously needs at least a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board with a heatsink, or a 6–8 layer, 3 oz-internal stackup.

I give you the model so you can set the burst rating honestly.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel = one 6-FET bridge)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct per 60° sector:

- The **PWM leg** passes current through the high-side FET for duty D and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D. That leg therefore conducts I for 100 % of the sector.
- The **return leg** low-side FET is on for 100 % of the sector.

This gives a total bridge conduction loss that is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\kappa(T_j-25)]$$

With κ ≈ 0.006 /K, the value at 100 °C is R = 1.9 × 1.45 = **2.76 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(2.76\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{35.3\ W}$$

Each sector's dissipation lands on 2–3 FETs. Over a revolution, each FET carries about 1/3 of the total, so roughly **11.8 W per FET while it is active**. The per-FET peak inside its own sector is higher. This peak is what matters if the burst is shorter than the electrical period, which happens at low RPM or stall.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so solve the following iteratively. It diverges (thermal runaway) if Z_th·∂P/∂T ≥ 1.

$$T_j = T_a + P_{cond}(T_j)\,Z_{th}$$

**Copper losses must be added.** A 2 oz (70 µm) trace, 5 mm wide, 10 mm long:

$$R = \rho L/(wt) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.01/(0.005\cdot70\times10^{-6}) = 0.49\ \text{mΩ}$$

At 80 A that is about 3.1 W per such segment. Per channel, budget around 3 such segments (phase and bus paths), about **9 W** at 25 °C. Use ×1.3 at 100 °C, which gives about **12 W**.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Each term below uses the Section 1 values.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k | 0.97 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Coss charge | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 ns · 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.7 W** |

**Conclusion:** at 48 kHz this design is about 95 % conduction-dominated. Reducing f_sw buys little. Lower R_ds(on), paralleled FETs, and copper are the levers.

**Dead time** is the second-largest switching term. Trimming it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, but only after verifying shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Per channel (35.3 + 1.7 + 12 copper) | **≈ 49 W** |
| 4 channels simultaneous | **≈ 196 W** |

The efficiency figure looks fine. The problem is power density: about 196 W into 4 cm².

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Transient (burst) model: Cauer ladder

$$T_j(t)=T_a+\sum_i P\,R_i\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

| Node | R (K/W) | C (J/K) | τ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Junction → case (die + clip) | 1.2 | 0.02 | ~25 ms |
| Case → PCB thermal pad / vias | 2.5 (per FET, 12 × 0.3 mm vias, filled) | 0.15 | ~0.4 s |
| PCB (20 × 20, 6 L, ~3.5 g) → spread | — | ≈ 3.0 (Cu 0.385 J/gK, FR4 1.1 J/gK, mixed) | — |
| Board → air (prop wash ~5–10 m/s, h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K, 2 × 4 cm²) | ≈ 15–20 | — | ~50 s |

### 3.2 Burst endurance (adiabatic board approximation for t ≪ 50 s)

- **4 channels at 80 A (196 W):** dT/dt = P/C = 196 / 3.0 ≈ **65 K/s**.
- **Board budget:** from T_a = 45 °C to a board limit of about 105 °C (keeping T_j ≤ 125 °C with the ~20 K junction-to-board rise) gives 60 K.
- **Board-limited burst:** t_burst ≈ **0.9 s**.
- **Single-channel FET hotspot:** the FETs see a local ΔT_jb ≈ 11.8 W × (1.2 + 2.5) ≈ **44 K** within about 1 s. The board is not isothermal, and local spreading resistance dominates.
- **Realistic single-FET T_j ceiling at 1 s:** 45 + 44 + bulk rise ≈ **115–130 °C**.
