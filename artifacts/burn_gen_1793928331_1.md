# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #4)

## 0. Feasibility Issues to Resolve First

Three points in the brief conflict with physics or architecture. The model below accounts for them rather than ignoring them.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. Alternatively, one MCU per 4-in-1 runs Bluejay on an EFM8, but that is limited to 24/48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM therefore lives in the ESC firmware, not in the H743's timers.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) and 80 A burst per channel are incompatible.** The worked numbers in §2 show a 20×20 board reaching thermal runaway in under 2 s at 4×80 A. The model is presented parametrically. My recommendation is a **30.5×30.5 mm** layout (or a 25.5 mm whoop with burst derated to about 45 A).
3. **Total current and battery limits.** 4×80 A = 320 A from the pack. This exceeds XT60 ratings and most 6S packs. A burst on a *single* motor (yaw/recovery transients) is the realistic case, and the thermal model treats both cases.

**Assumptions (change these and re-run the equations):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full), 22.2 V (nominal) |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 40 V, R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss,eff ≈ 2 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Package | PDFN 5×6. A 3.3×3.3 package is about 2 mΩ, so double the conduction terms. |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase, V_GS = 10 V, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns (with R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, dead time t_dt = 100 ns |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.55 mΩ. This term creates the positive feedback loop in §2.4.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation, two phases carry current I_ph at any instant:
- **Switching leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D). The sum is 1, so this leg is always one R_ds.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is held on for the whole 60° sector, which adds one R_ds.

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) \quad(\text{independent of } D \text{ with sync rectification})$$

Add the copper path. For a 2 oz, 6-layer pour the phase-to-bus trace resistance is R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path, with α_Cu = 0.0039:

$$P_{cu} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{cu}(T)$$

### 1.3 Switching loss (hard-switched leg only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

### 1.4 Secondary terms

| Term | Equation |
|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | $P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ |
| Dead-time body diode | $P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw}$ |
| Output capacitance | $P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}$ |
| Gate drive (2 FETs toggling) | $P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ (dissipated mainly in driver/R_g) |

### 1.5 Worked numbers at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond | 2·80²·1.55 mΩ | **19.8 W** |
| P_cu | 2·80²·0.39 mΩ (at ~100 °C) | **5.0 W** |
| P_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| P_dt | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| P_rr | 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| P_oss | 0.5·2 nF·25.2²·48 k | 0.03 W |
| P_g | 2·60 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.06 W (driver) |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 27.5 W** |

Efficiency for this channel: P_out ≈ 22.2 V·80 A·D. At full throttle this is about 1.78 kW, so efficiency is around 98.5%. That looks good on paper, but 27.5 W in about 4 cm² of copper is the problem.

**Observations:**
- At 48 kHz, conduction losses dominate (~90%). Halving f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. The main benefit of 48 kHz is lower current ripple and acoustic noise, not efficiency.
- Ripple check: ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw). For a typical 5–15 µH motor at D = 0.5, ΔI ≈ 9–26 A pk-pk at 48 kHz, versus double that at 24 kHz. This is the real justification for 48 kHz on low-inductance motors.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (Cauer, per channel, lumped to board)

```
P_ch ──[R_jc=1.0 K/W per FET]──┬── T_case ──[R_cb≈0.5 K/W, vias]── T_board
                               C_FET≈0.15 J/K                       │
                                               C_board ─────────────┤
                                               R_ba (airflow) ─── T_amb
```

### 2.2 Board thermal capacity

| Board | Copper + FR4 mass | c_eff | C_board |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20×20 mm, 6L 2 oz | ≈ 4 g | 0.6 J/g·K | **≈ 2.4 J/K** |
| 30.5×30.5 mm, 8L 2 oz | ≈ 9 g | 0.6 J/g·K | **≈ 5.4 J/K** |

Board-to-ambient resistance under prop wash (~5 m/s): R_ba ≈ 4 K/W (20×20) and ≈ 2.2 K/W (30×30).

### 2.3 Transient response

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P\,R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right),\quad \tau = R_{ba} C_{board}$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + \frac{P_{FET}}{1}\,(R_{jc} + R_{cb}),\quad P_{FET} \approx P_{ch}/2 \text{ (two FETs share conduction)}$$
