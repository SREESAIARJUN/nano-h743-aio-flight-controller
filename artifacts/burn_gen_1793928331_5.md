# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #36): System Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

Three points need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot running on the H743 sends DShot commands. Each of the four motors needs its own ESC MCU running firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does the 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection and DShot telemetry. A single-H743 design that also commutated motors would not be supported by ArduPilot. This document therefore models an **H743 flight controller plus 4 ESC MCUs** on one stack or AIO.

2. **"Nano" with an 80 A burst on all four channels is thermally marginal.** The results below show that the 80 A rating must be time-limited to seconds. It must not be quoted as a continuous figure.

3. **Component values are representative assumptions, not datasheet citations.** Replace every value in the parameter table with figures from your selected MOSFET and driver datasheets before you release the design.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel = one 3-phase bridge, 6 MOSFETs)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two** MOSFETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- **PWM on-time (D):** the high side of phase X and the low side of phase Y conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** the low side of phase X (synchronous rectification) and the low side of phase Y conduct.

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on),25}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The loss is independent of duty cycle, but it is split differently between devices:

- The phase-Y low side always carries ~10.2 W.
- Phase X's two devices share the other ~10.2 W in proportion to D.

**The hottest device dissipates about 10.2 W plus its share of switching loss.**

Copper trace loss must be added on top. Assuming 0.3 mΩ of effective path from the bus to the motor pad:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ W$$

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz (hard-switched high side)

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** of the synchronous-rectifier body diode:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive**, dissipated mostly in the driver, for 2 actively switched FETs per channel:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ W$$

### 1.3 Totals

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (MOSFET) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Qrr + Qoss + Qg | 0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At full throttle that is about 2 kW per channel, so efficiency is roughly 98.7%. The 100 W of loss is the problem regardless of efficiency.

**Design levers, in order of impact:**

1. **R_ds(on).** Conduction is about 80% of the loss. Paralleling 2 FETs per switch halves P_cond to about 10 W per channel but doubles board area.
2. **Dead time.** Reducing 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel.
3. **f_sw.** Only about 12% of the loss scales with frequency. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.6 W per channel at the cost of acoustic noise and current ripple.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Junction rise over board (hottest FET)

Worst-case hottest-device power:

$$P_{FET} \approx 10.2 + 1.94 + 0.6 \approx 12.7\ W$$

$$\Delta T_{j-board} = P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,pad\to board}) = 12.7 \times 4 \approx \mathbf{51\ K}$$

**Board temperature must therefore stay below ~100 °C to hold T_j ≤ 150 °C.**
