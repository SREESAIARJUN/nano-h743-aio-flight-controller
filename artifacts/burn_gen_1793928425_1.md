# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #34): Design Reference Model

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**What the H743 does and doesn't do.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, which in practice means four AT32F421 or STM32G071 chips running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs. The H743 only sends throttle frames and receives eRPM telemetry.

**Baseline assumptions.** Every number below depends on these. Substitute your actual BOM values.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A (DC-equivalent per channel) |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 class |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ per die (single FET per switch) |
| R_DS(on) tempco | α | ≈ 0.5 %/K (R₁₀₀ ≈ 1.5 × R₂₅) |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Rise + fall time at 80 A | t_r + t_f | 20 ns (driver-limited) |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Commutation | — | Trapezoidal six-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step operation with complementary PWM, current flows through one high-side FET and one low-side FET at any instant:

- **PWM-ing leg:** The high-side conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side of the same leg conducts for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** The low-side is on 100% of the sector.

So, per channel:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot 1 = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty cycle** with synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple, with motor inductance L_ph (assume 15 µH line-to-line for a large 5–7" motor):

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

Worst case is around D = 0.5 with V_bemf ≈ V_bus/2:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \cdot 0.5}{15\times10^{-6}\cdot 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\text{ A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple correction is negligible (+0.1%). Use I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel (2·I²·R) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 75 °C | 1.25 mΩ | 16.0 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W |

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

This term is often bigger than designers expect. Take a 3 oz (105 µm) inner and outer phase trace, 6 mm wide and 15 mm long, using ρ_Cu(100 °C) = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m:

$$
R_{trace} = \frac{\rho L}{W t} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.006\cdot105\times10^{-6}} = 0.52\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Assume two layers in parallel, so 0.26 mΩ. Add the bus and ground path, roughly 0.3 mΩ shared by the four channels.

- Per-channel copper: 80²·0.26 m ≈ **1.7 W**
- Shared bus copper at 320 A total: 320²·0.3 m ≈ **30 W** (all four channels at burst)

The ground and bus return is a first-order loss term on a nano board. Use 4–6 layers of 2–3 oz copper and very short battery-pad-to-FET paths.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

All values are per channel, with one leg actively hard-switching.

### 2.1 V·I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\times10^{-9}\cdot48\times10^{3} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

Note that Q_rr rises with di/dt and T_j. Derate to roughly 2× at 125 °C, giving 0.12 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

This is significant. Reducing t_dt to 50 ns halves it, but you must verify shoot-through margin against your driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET die. Two FETs switch per active leg:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per channel (hot) |
|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.97 |
| Q_rr | 0.12 |
| Q_oss | 0.04 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Gate (driver) | 0.05 |
| **Total switching** | **≈ 1.8 W** |

### 2.7 Combined loss at 80A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.8 W | 7.2 W |
| Phase copper | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| Shared bus copper | — | ~30 W |
| **Total** | **~22.7 W** | **~121 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full burst is 121 W lost against 4·80·22.2 ≈ 7.1 kW of input, roughly **98.3%**. The efficiency is good. The problem is power density.

### 2.8 Frequency sensitivity

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the switching losses fall to about 0.9 W per channel, but ripple doubles. At 48 kHz, switching is less than 10% of total loss, so **conduction dominates**. The best lever is lower R_DS(on), either through paralleled FETs or a better die. Lowering frequency helps far less.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state check

Assume a nano form factor, roughly 30×30 mm ESC. A realistic θ_board-to-ambient with prop wash is about 3–5 K/W for the whole board.

$$
\Delta T_{ss} = 121\text{ W}\cdot 4\text{ K/W} \approx 480\text{ K}
$$

**This is physically impossible.** 80A is strictly a transient rating on this board size. Continuous rating must be derived from the thermal limit (see §3.4).

### 3.2 Transient (burst) model: two-pole Foster network

```
P_die ──[θjc=1.0 K/W]──┬──[θcb=0.8 K/W]──┬──[θba=4 K/W]── T_amb
                     C_j≈0.05 J/K      C_board≈7 J/K
```

**Board thermal capacitance.** The board carries about 4 g of copper (c = 0.385 J/g·K) and 3 g of FR4 (c ≈ 1.1 J/g·K), plus 8 FET packages at roughly 0.15 J/K each.

$$
C_{board} \approx 1.54 + 3.3 + 1.2 \approx 6\text{–}7\ \text{J/K}
$$

**Board time constant.** τ_b = θ_ba · C = 4 · 6.5 ≈ 26 s, so a burst of a few seconds is quasi-adiabatic:

$$
\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C} = \frac{121}{6.5} \approx 18.6\ \text{K/s}
$$

**Junction-to-board offset.** The two FETs conducting in a channel share about 19 W, so roughly 9.6 W each. At θ_jc + θ_cb ≈ 1.8 K/W, that gives ΔT_j-b ≈ 17 K, settling with τ_j of tens of ms.
