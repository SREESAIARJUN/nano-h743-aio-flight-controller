## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Design Review Flags

The H743 cannot commutate four BLDC motors directly with adequate timing resolution and still run ArduPilot. The standard and assumed architecture is therefore:

| Block | Assumed implementation |
|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz, ArduPilot |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) running AM32 firmware, one per motor |
| Gate drivers | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, 250 V, bootstrap) |
| MOSFETs | 24× 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 class (parameters in §1) |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal |
| Form factor | "Nano" stack, 20×20 mm (M2) or 25.5×25.5 mm whoop pattern |
| Rating | 80 A burst per channel, interpreted as the industry convention of roughly 1 s, not continuous |

> **Design-review flags (must be resolved before tape-out)**
> 1. **80 A on four channels simultaneously means 320 A of battery current.** No nano-class XT30/XT60 lead or pad stack supports this. The realistic case is 80 A on one channel during a transient, or about 40–55 A on all four.
> 2. **The thermal result in §3 shows the burst rating is time-limited to about 1–2 s** at full four-channel load on a 20×20 board. Marketing must specify the burst duration explicitly.
> 3. **3.3×3.3 mm MOSFETs physically cannot carry 80 A** (package and clip limits). PDFN 5×6 is required, which constrains the board to 25.5 mm or larger with 12 FETs per side.

All device parameters below are representative values for a modern 40 V trench MOSFET. Replace them with the actual datasheet values of the chosen part.

## 2. Loss Model (per Channel)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- One phase leg is **PWM-switched**: the high side H conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low side L conducts for (1−D).
- One leg is **statically ON (low side)** for the full 60° sector.
- One leg is floating, used for BEMF sensing.

The current path therefore always passes through exactly **two conducting FETs in series**, independent of D.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left( R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + R_{DS,hot} \right) = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS,hot}
$$

With phase ripple neglected (high motor inductance relative to 48 kHz), I_rms ≈ I_DC = 80 A.

The hot on-resistance is:

$$
R_{DS,hot} = 0.80\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.28\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}
$$

**Copper path loss.** Phase pour plus bus via stack is roughly 0.3 mΩ per channel (2 oz inner layers, via array):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. With synchronous rectification, the low-side FET turns on with zero voltage (ZVS, after body-diode conduction). Hard-switching loss therefore occurs only in the **high-side FET**.

**(a) V–I overlap.** The plateau voltage is V_pl ≈ 4.5 V.

Turn-on rise time:

$$
t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv} - V_{pl})/R_G} = \frac{16\,\text{nC}}{(10-4.5)/4.7} \approx 13.7\,\text{ns}
$$

Turn-off fall time (diode-bypassed R_G of about 1 Ω, sink-limited):

$$
t_f \approx \frac{16\,\text{nC}}{1.8\,\text{A}} \approx 8.9\,\text{ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22.6\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.09\ W}
$$

Add parasitic loop inductance (about 3 nH) overshoot margin: **use 1.4 W**.

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and temperature. Budget **0.1 W**.

**(d) Output capacitance**, high side plus low side C_oss charge exchange:

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate charge** (dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET die):

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst, 25.2 V

| Loss term | W per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, hot) | 16.4 | 65.6 |
| Copper path | 1.9 | 7.6 |
| V–I switching | 1.4 | 5.6 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.15 | 0.6 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 20.5 W** | **≈ 82 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 = 8.06 kW, so η ≈ 99.0 %. The electrical efficiency is fine. **The problem is purely thermal density.**
