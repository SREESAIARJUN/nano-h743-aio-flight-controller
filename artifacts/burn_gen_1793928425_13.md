# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture & Derivations (Job #15)

## 0. Architecture Clarification

An STM32H743 does not commutate MOSFETs directly. The partitioning is:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz, ArduPilot) | Generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32 or BLHeli_32) | 6-step or FOC commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drive | 4× FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V bootstrap) | Drives 24 N-MOSFETs |
| Power stage | 24× 40 V, ~0.7–1.0 mΩ, PDFN 3.3×3.3 / 5×6 | 3 half-bridges per motor |

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your real BOM values.
- Battery: 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge)
- Burst current: 80 A per motor (phase RMS ≈ DC-link equivalent for 6-step at high duty)
- f_sw = 48 kHz
- R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, with temperature coefficient ×1.5 at T_j ≈ 110 °C, giving 1.5 mΩ
- t_r + t_f = 20 ns (effective, at gate-drive strength ~1.5 A)
- Q_g = 50 nC, Q_oss = 30 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V
- Dead time t_dt = 50 ns per edge

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, at any instant the current flows through:
- the PWM leg: the high-side for duty D and the synchronous low-side for 1−D (exactly one FET conducting at a time), and
- the return leg: one low-side FET conducting continuously.

So at every instant, two FETs carry the full current in series.

### 1.2 Equations
$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\,/\text{K} \Rightarrow R_{DS}(110^\circ C) \approx 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

Per conducting FET: **9.6 W** (worst-case FET in the return leg).

The three phases rotate through these roles, so each FET is active for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The time-averaged per-FET loss is therefore about 19.2 W × 2/6 ≈ 6.4 W. The instantaneous peak is 9.6 W, and because commutation periods (~100 µs) are short compared with the FET thermal time constant (~ms), junction temperature tracks the **average** (≈6.4 W).

### 1.3 Copper / PCB loss
For a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 4 oz (140 µm) copper, assume a phase trace length of ~8 mm and an effective width of 4 mm:
$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times 0.008}{0.004\times140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega
$$
Two traces in the current path plus the shunt (0.5 mΩ, if used) give about 1.0 mΩ, so P_Cu ≈ 80² × 1.0 mΩ ≈ **6.4 W per motor**. This term is frequently omitted from datasheet-based estimates.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg is static between commutations.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5×25.2×80×20 ns×48 k | **0.968 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 30 nC×25.2×48 k | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC×25.2×48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8×80×100 ns×48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (from 12 V LDO, 2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2×50 nC×10×48 k | 0.048 W (in the driver/LDO) |
| **Total P_sw per motor** | | **≈1.41 W** |

Sensitivity: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it is ≈2.8 W, and at 24 kHz ≈0.7 W. At 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise because conduction losses dominate by ~14:1.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Item | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.41 W | 5.6 W |
| PCB copper / shunt | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Driver + MCU + LDO | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **27.3 W** | **≈109 W** |

The DC bus would carry 4 × 80 A × D. At full throttle this is about **320 A** through the XT60 and battery pads. XT60 is rated ~60 A continuous, so an "80 A ×4" burst is physically limited by the connector, battery C-rating and pad copper, not the FETs alone. In practice these ratings mean 80 A on *one* channel for ≤10 s.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state (shows why "80A" can only be a burst rating)
Thermal path: Junction → case (R_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W) → board via thermal vias (R_cb ≈ 2.0 K/W per FET with a 3×3 via array, 0.3 mm vias) → board → air.

For a 30×30 mm board with two sides convecting under prop wash, h ≈ 80–150 W/m²K:
$$
R_{ba} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{100 \times 2\times0.0009} \approx 5.6\,\text{K/W}
$$
At the 4-motor burst: ΔT_board = 109 × 5.6 ≈ **610 K**. Continuous operation is impossible.
For a single motor at 80 A: 27.3 × 5.6 ≈ 153 K, which also exceeds limits. **Sustainable continuous** current with T_board ≤ 100 °C at 40 °C ambient:
$$
P_{max} = \frac{60}{5.6} \approx 10.7\,\text{W (total)} \Rightarrow I_{cont,4ch} \approx \sqrt{\frac{10.7/4}{2R_{DS}+R_{Cu}}} \approx 30\,\text{A/ch}
$$
This is consistent with typical "35A continuous / 80A burst" nano ESC labelling.

### 4.2 Transient (burst) model: first-order lumped RC
Board thermal capacitance:
- FR4 + Cu, ≈5 g: C ≈ 5 g × ~0.9 J/g·K ≈ 4.5 J/K
- 24 FETs: ≈0.5 J/K
- So C_th ≈ 5 J/K, and τ = R_ba·C_th = 5.6 × 5 ≈ 28 s

$$
T_{board}(t) = T_a + P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)
$$

Junction temperature includes the per-FET rise (6.4 W average × 3.2 K/W ≈ 20 K; this local stage settles in ~50 ms):
$$
T_j(t) \approx T_{board}(t) + 20\,\text{K}
$$

Time to T_j = 125 °C (board 105 °C) from T_a = 40 °C:
$$
t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{65}{P R_{ba}}\right)
$$

| Scenario | P | t_burst |
|---|---|---|
| 1 motor at 80 A, others at 20 A | 27.3 + 3×~2.2 = 34 W | −28·ln(1−65/190) ≈ **11.7 s** |
| All 4 at 80 A | 109 W | −28·ln(1−65/610) ≈ **3.2
