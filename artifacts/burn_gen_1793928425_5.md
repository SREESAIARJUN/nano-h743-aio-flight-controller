## 0. Architectural Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change the results.

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 should be the **flight controller**, not the commutation MCU. ArduPilot cannot drive DShot into itself. The architecture is therefore:

```
[STM32H743 – ArduPilot] --DShot600 bidir x4--> [4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071), AM32] --> [3x FD6288Q drivers + 24 MOSFETs]
```

The alternative is one dual-quad ESC MCU. Either way, the 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

### 0.2 80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint

80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm footprint is **not a continuous rating**. Section 2 shows it is a roughly 1-second thermal-mass-limited burst. I model it honestly as a transient, not a steady state.

If "80A burst" means the **total** across four motors (20 A/motor), divide all conduction losses by 16.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, which is AM32's default.

At any instant, the phase current flows through exactly **two conducting switches**:
- one on the static leg, and
- either the HS or the LS of the PWM leg.

Because the PWM leg always has one switch on, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the key simplification.

### 1.2 Conduction losses

The RMS current includes the PWM ripple Δi:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right),\qquad r = \frac{\Delta i}{I}$$

With motor inductance L_ph ≈ 15 µH at 48 kHz, r ≈ 0.1, so the ripple correction is +0.08% and negligible.

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

R_ds depends on T_j, so the solution is **self-consistent**:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\cdot I^2 R_{25}\,k(T_j)$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}(1 - 0.1375)}{1 - 0.0055\,R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}}$$

**Worked example.** I = 80 A, T_c = 90 °C:

- I²·R₂₅ = 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = 7.68 W
- Denominator: 1 − 0.0055 × 1.5 × 7.68 = 0.9366
- Numerator: 90 + 1.5 × 7.68 × 0.8625 = 99.94
- **T_j ≈ 106.7 °C**, giving k = 1.45 and **R_ds ≈ 1.74 mΩ**

The resulting conduction losses are:

- Per conducting FET: 6400 × 1.74 mΩ = **11.1 W**
- Per motor: P_cond = **22.3 W**

Additional copper losses:
- Phase-pad and bus-trace copper ≈ 0.3 mΩ path → 1.9 W
- Shunt, if fitted (0.25 mΩ) → 1.6 W

### 1.3 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That leg has one hard-switched transition pair per period.

**(a) V-I overlap**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery.** The body diode of the complementary FET recovers after dead time.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) C_oss charge/discharge**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> Dead time costs almost as much as the switching transitions. Trim t_dt to the minimum safe value; AM32 exposes this setting.

**(e) Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated in the FD6288Q and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ W$$

### 1.4 Per-channel total at 80 A burst

| Term | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 22.3 |
| Copper / shunt | 3.5 |
| V-I overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.6 W** |

| Scenario | Total Loss | Efficiency (at P_out ≈ V_bus·I·D_eff) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 motors × 80 A | **~114 W** | ~97.5% at full throttle |
| 4 motors × 30 A continuous | ~17 W | — |

The 30 A continuous figure follows from conduction scaling with I² while switching scales roughly linearly with I.

### 1.5 Bus capacitor ripple current

Per motor channel:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at D = 0.5, giving **40 A_rms per channel**.

Four unsynchronized channels add in quadrature:

$$I_{C,rms,total} \approx 40\sqrt{4} = \mathbf{80\ A_{rms}}\ \text{(worst case)}$$

This is impossible to absorb on board with ceramics alone. The required strategy is:
- Fit 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 per channel, local to the half-bridges.
- Mandate an external **≥ 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR electrolytic** at the battery pads (specified in the documentation).
- Treat the board pads plus external cap as the true ripple sink.
