## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction losses

With complementary 6-step PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs in series:
- the PWM leg (high-side or its synchronous low-side), and
- the static low-side of the return phase.

Temperature-corrected on-resistance:

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/K$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_DS,hot ≈ 1.5 × 1.2 mΩ = 1.8 mΩ.

Conduction loss per ESC:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Phase-trace copper loss adds to this. For 2 oz copper (70 µm), 4 mm wide, 15 mm long, paralleled on 4 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} \approx 0.23\,m\Omega$$

The current path crosses two phase traces, so P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.23 mΩ ≈ **2.9 W**.

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{drv}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{12-4.5}{16.5} = 0.45\,A$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{16.5} = 0.27\,A$$

**Transition times**

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{12\,nC}{0.45} = 27\,ns$$

$$t_f = \frac{12\,nC}{0.27} = 44\,ns$$

**Loss components**

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 71 ns × 48k | **3.44 W** |
| Body-diode conduction in dead time | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss charge/discharge | 2 · ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Gate charge (mostly dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 4.8 W** |

### 1.3 Totals

| Item | Per ESC | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Phase copper | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| Current shunt (0.1 mΩ, I_bat ≈ 320 A shared) | — | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 133 W** |

**Design levers, in order of payoff:**
1. **Conduction dominates (~70%).** R_DS(on) and copper are the key levers; f_sw is secondary.
2. **Dead time.** Dropping t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 2.5 W total.
3. **Switching frequency.** Moving 48 kHz → 24 kHz saves about 9.6 W total, but raises current ripple and acoustic noise.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Per-FET average dissipation

There are 24 FETs, and each conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Excluding shunt and copper losses:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{92 + 19.2}{24} = 4.6\ W$$
