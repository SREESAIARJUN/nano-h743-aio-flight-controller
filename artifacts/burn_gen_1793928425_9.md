# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

### Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors.

- **FC (STM32H743, ArduPilot):** sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (AM32-class on AT32F421 or STM32G071), one per motor, each running 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM at 48 kHz.

This is the only viable partitioning. One H743 cannot simultaneously run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops and the flight stack at 48 kHz with acceptable jitter.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 120 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 95) = **1.43 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-Step with Complementary PWM

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly **two** conducting channels: the PWM leg (HS or synchronous LS) and the return-leg LS. Ripple at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (~10–20 µH) is small, so I_rms ≈ I_pk under burst:

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2\cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$
$$=80^2\times 2\times 1.43\times10^{-3}= \mathbf{18.3\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper Loss

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R_{Cu}=6400\times0.5\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution (Hottest Device)

Each LS FET carries full current for 120° of the electrical cycle as the return leg, plus a (1−D) share during its leg's PWM period. Worst case (D → 1, full throttle burst) gives ≈ 1/3 duty at full current:

$$P_{FET,max}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS} + P_{sw,share}\approx \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.43\text{m}) + 0.6 \approx \mathbf{3.65\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz (Per Channel)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. There is one commutating pair per channel at any time.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(24\text{ns})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

Check t_r against the driver: t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g, where I_g = (V_drv − V_plateau)/R_g,tot = (10 − 3.2)/4 Ω = 1.7 A. That gives 4.7 ns intrinsic. The 12 ns figure includes loop inductance and Miller re-trigger margin, so it is conservative.

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\tfrac12(50\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times300\text{ns}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.92\ W}$$

Dead time is the second-largest switching term. Reducing t_dt from 150 ns to 80 ns saves ~0.43 W per channel. The limit is driver propagation mismatch: verify shoot-through margin on a scope before tuning.

### 2.5 Gate Drive (2 Switching FETs)

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

This loss is mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

**P_sw,ch ≈ 2.20 W**

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per Channel (W) | 4 Channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 18.30 | 73.2 |
| Copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| V-I overlap | 1.16 | 4.6 |
| Dead time | 0.92 | 3.7 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.12 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **23.7** | **≈ 94.8 W** |

Efficiency at burst is 1 − 23.7/(25.2 × 80 × D_eff). At D_eff ≈ 0.95 this is **≈ 98.8 %**. The efficiency is fine. **Heat removal is the problem.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-State Feasibility

Board-to-air resistance for 20×20 mm, both sides, in prop wash (h ≈ 50 W/m²K):

$$R_{\theta,BA}=\frac{1}{hA}=\frac{1}{50\times(2\times0.02^2)}=25\ \text{K/W}$$

Steady-state rise at 94.8 W would be ≈ 2,370 K.
