## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design-Level Findings

**The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation or FOC firmware for the H7, so a working "H743 4-in-1" is an all-in-one (AIO) board:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600 on four timer channels.
- **ESC section:** four dedicated ESC MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32). Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs, with PWM at **48 kHz**.

The 48 kHz switching frequency therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

**The 80 A burst figure has a large thermal consequence.** I interpret it as 80 A phase current per channel. On a nano (≤30×30 mm) board, the model in §3 shows:

- About 32 W per channel and about 128 W total.
- Allowable burst of roughly 1.5–2 s.
- Realistic continuous rating of roughly 20–25 A per channel, even with prop wash.

This is consistent with commercial nano AIOs, which are typically labeled 25–35 A. A "nano 80 A" rating is only defensible as a short burst rating, and the documentation should say so.
