# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Feasibility Findings

The model below shows that **80 A per channel on four channels at once cannot be sustained on a nano-format board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm)**. It can be met only as a **time-limited burst of about 1–2 s**, set by the board's thermal mass. A product claim should read something like "80 A burst ≤1 s, single-channel-dominant, 35 A continuous with airflow". The full derivation follows so the numbers can be re-run against your actual MOSFET datasheet.

Two architectural clarifications:

- **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM.
- **The netlist is a reference design**, not a validated layout. Every part value must be checked against current datasheets, and the design needs DRC/ERC plus bench validation.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega\,(1+0.005\cdot100) = 2.10\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Current path

In each commutation sector, current flows through one high-side leg (PWM'd) and one low-side leg (on). With synchronous rectification, the PWM'd leg shares current between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its low-side FET (for 1−D):

$$P_{HS}=I^2R_{ds}D,\qquad P_{LS,sync}=I^2R_{ds}(1-D)$$

So the PWM'd leg dissipates I²R_ds regardless of D, and the static leg adds another I²R_ds:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

For a flat-top trapezoidal phase current, I_rms ≈ I_pk during the 120° conduction window. That window rotates among the six FETs, but at the **board level** two conduction paths are always active per channel.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.10\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

Peak per FET is 13.4 W while it is conducting. Averaged over commutation, each FET carries about one third of that, roughly 4.5 W.

### 2.4 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds rises with T_j, so the loss must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{cond}(T_j)\cdot R_{th}\;\Rightarrow\; T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+2I^2R_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1-2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{th,crit}=\frac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha}=\frac{1}{2\cdot6400\cdot1.4\text{e-}3\cdot0.005}=11.2\ \text{K/W}$$

The effective channel R_th must be well below 11 K/W at 80 A. A nano PCB in still air is typically 15–25 K/W, so **continuous 80 A is unstable, not merely hot.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase hard-switches. Each term is per channel.

**(a) V–I overlap (high-side, hard-switched):**
$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance, both FETs in the leg:**
$$P_{oss}=2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f = 1\text{e-}9\cdot635\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction, two edges per period:**
$$P_{dt}=V_f I\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f = 40\text{e-}9\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive, two FETs per leg. This loss lands in the driver, not the FET:**
$$P_g = 2Q_gV_{drv}f = 2\cdot40\text{e-}9\cdot10\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,ch}\approx \mathbf{2.37\ W}$$

At this current, **conduction dominates by about 11:1**. Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.2 W per channel and raises current ripple. Spend design effort on R_ds(on) and copper instead.

### 3.1 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 26.9 W | 107.6 W |
| Switching (total) | 2.37 W | 9.5 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ: 80²·0.25 m) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ path) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈32.8 W** | **≈131 W** |

Efficiency check: output power is about 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 7.3 kW, so 131 W of loss gives roughly 98% efficiency. Efficiency is fine. **Thermal density is the problem.**
