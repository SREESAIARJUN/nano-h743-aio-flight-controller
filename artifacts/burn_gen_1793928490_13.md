## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

Two points need settling before the math, because the numbers depend on them.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any production-viable AIO design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
- The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware.
- The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- Running FOC/6-step for four motors directly on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot and is not proposed here.

**2. 80A burst on a "nano" board is a thermal edge case.** The model below assumes:
- 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, board about 36 × 36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper.
- A 20 × 20 mm board cannot carry 4 × 80A, even as a burst. The numbers in §3 show why.

**MOSFET parameters are assumed, representative of a 40V 3.3×3.3 mm class part.** Replace them with your chosen part's datasheet values. The equations hold; the outputs scale with the parameters.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation pattern (6-step trapezoidal with complementary PWM / synchronous rectification):**
- In every 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd high, one is held low, and one floats.
- At any instant, current flows through exactly two FET channels:
  - the high-side FET (during D) or the low-side FET (during 1−D) of the PWM leg, and
  - the low-side FET of the sink leg.

**Total conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

**Temperature dependence**, with α ≈ 0.0055 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.55 at 125 °C:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**At the burst point (T_j = 125 °C):**
- FET channels: 2 · 80² · 1.40 mΩ = **17.9 W**
- Copper: 2 · 80² · 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W** (lumped PCB plus pad; this heats the board, not the junction)

**Per-FET distribution** (each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I^2 R$$

At D → 1 (burst):
- each FET sees ≈ 1/3 · 80² · 1.40 mΩ ≈ **3.0 W**
- low-side FETs at D = 0.5 see ≈ **4.5 W**, which is the worst case.

**Self-consistency.** Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve T_j = T_board + P(T_j) · R_th,jb by fixed-point iteration. It converges in 3–4 iterations; §3 uses the converged values.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor:

**(a) V·I overlap loss in the high-side FET.**

Gate current during each transition:
- Turn-on: I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl)/R_g = 7/5 = 1.4 A
- Turn-off: I_g,off = V_pl/R_g = 0.6 A. In practice the driver sink is stronger; with R_g,off = 2 Ω this becomes 1.5 A.

Transition times:
- t_r = (Q_gs2 + Q_gd)/I_g,on = 12 nC / 1.4 A ≈ 8.6 ns
- t_f ≈ 12 nC / 1.5 A ≈ 8.0 ns

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 16.6\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 0.80\text{ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$
