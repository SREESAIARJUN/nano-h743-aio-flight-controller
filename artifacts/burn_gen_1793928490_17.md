# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #82): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot DMA Map

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These constraints shape every number below.

1. **Commutation MCU split.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each motor channel gets its own small ESC MCU running AM32: AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32, 4×4 mm) or STM32G071. The FC sends bi-directional DShot600 to these ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot and would require custom firmware.
2. **"Nano" + 80 A burst.** A 20×20 mm two-board stack *can* survive an 80 A per-channel burst for about 1–2 s. It *cannot* sustain 4×80 A. Section 3 shows the board heats at roughly 35–40 K/s at full 4-channel burst. I specify the design as **80 A burst (≤1 s, ≤25 % duty cycle), 35–40 A continuous with prop-wash airflow**. If the customer requires 80 A continuous, this becomes a 30.5×30.5 mm design. That is a requirements conversation, not an engineering fix.
3. **Component values.** Values are representative of the class of 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 5×6 mm PQFN MOSFETs (BSC010N04LS-class). **Re-run the model with the final datasheet values**: Rds(on)(T), Qgd, Qgs2, Qrr, Qoss, and Zth curves. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers are a first-pass budget.

**Operating envelope assumed:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal), f_sw = 48 kHz, AM32 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the high side, dead-time 200 ns, ambient 40 °C.
