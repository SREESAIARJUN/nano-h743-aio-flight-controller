# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Corrections

Three issues affect every number below, so they come first.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It has no six-step or FOC motor-commutation code. The real architecture is:

- The **H743 flight controller** produces bi-directional DShot600.
- A **4-in-1 ESC** contains per-motor ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). These MCUs drive the 3-phase gate drivers and MOSFETs.

All loss modelling below applies to the ESC power stage. All DMA and timer modelling applies to the H743.

**2. "Nano" and 80 A burst conflict.**
- A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4 × 80 A. That is 320 A combined, or about 8 kW from 6S.
- I have modelled a **30.5×30.5 mm** board.
- Even at that size, 80 A is a **seconds-long burst** rating, not a continuous one. Section 3 proves this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R_ds = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step, complementary PWM, one commutation sector

In each sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is the return path. With duty D and phase current I:

- High-side A: P_HA = D · I² · R_ds
- Low-side A (synchronous rectification): P_LA = (1 − D) · I² · R_ds
- Low-side B (always on): P_LB = I² · R_ds

The total per motor is **independent of D**:

**P_cond,motor = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)**
