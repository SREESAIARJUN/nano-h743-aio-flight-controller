# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Architecture & Analysis Package

## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Design-Review Flags

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 48 kHz, 6-step or FOC power stage with bi-directional DShot needs a dedicated motor MCU per channel, or one MCU running 4 instances. The baseline architecture is:

```
LiPo 6S ──┬── 4× [ESC MCU (AM32/BLHeli_32-class, e.g. STM32G071/AT32F421)
          │        → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET → motor]
          │              ▲  bidir DShot600 (single wire, open-drain-tolerant)
          │              │
          └── Buck 5V/3.3V → STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── TIM1 CH1–4
```

### 0.2 Red flags to resolve before layout

These come straight out of the math below.

| # | Issue | Quantified impact |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | 80 A/motor burst on a "nano" (≤30.5 mm) board | ~100 W total dissipation at burst. Steady-state board rise with prop-wash cooling is about 550 K, so 80 A is only viable as a **≤2 s** burst. |
| R2 | Battery-side current | 4 × 80 A × D(≈0.9) ≈ **290 A** input. XT60 is rated ~60 A continuous. Lead and pad sizing does not close at nano scale. |
| R3 | 48 kHz PWM on 6S | Switching loss is small (~1 W/ESC). Dead-time body-diode loss is comparable and must be minimized (≤100 ns). |
| R4 | Rds(on) at temperature | Conduction loss is about 93% of the total. FET selection dominates everything. |

**Recommendation:** Rate the product at **50–55 A continuous-with-airflow, 80 A for ≤2 s**, on a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2–3 oz board. A true 20×20 nano cannot support this. All numbers below are derived so you can re-run them with final datasheet values.
