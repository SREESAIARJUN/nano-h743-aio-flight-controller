### 0.1 Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In a production FC+4in1 stack (or a single-PCB AIO), the partitioning is:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (or VIT6), 400/480 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCU ×4 (or 1 multi-channel) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 ⚙ | AM32 (or BLHeli_32) with bi-dir DShot + EDT |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 4.5–20 V) ⚙ | n/a |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 / 5×6 ⚙ | n/a |

The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over **DShot600 bi-directional** signalling and, optionally, a KISS-style serial telemetry UART.

### 0.5 Feasibility Findings

> ⚠ **Finding 1: "80 A" must be defined as phase current per channel, burst.** Four channels at 80 A simultaneously is about 4 × 0.95 × 80 ≈ 304 A DC, or roughly 7.3 kW from 6S. On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 nano PCB this is limited by **heat capacity, not by MOSFET ratings**. Section 3 quantifies this: all four channels at 80 A are sustainable for only about 1 s, while a single channel at 80 A (others at cruise) is sustainable for about 3–4 s.

> ⚠ **Finding 2: Connector and wiring limits.** XT60 / 12 AWG and the board's input pads cannot carry 300 A for more than a fraction of a second. The board-level burst rating must be reconciled with the harness.
