# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture

**Job #12, Rev A (engineering baseline)**

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

Three points need settling before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the ESC side uses one dedicated MCU per motor, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 to those ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back over the same wire (bidirectional DShot). Commutating four motors from the H743 at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot is not viable: there are not enough advanced-control timers, and the ISR jitter budget would be blown. This document assumes the standard split architecture.

2. **"Nano" and "80A burst" conflict.** A 20×20 mm board cannot sink 4 × 80 A. This document uses a **30.5×30.5 mm** board and treats 80 A as a **≤ 5 s burst per channel**, with 45–55 A continuous. Section 3 shows the numbers.

3. **The netlists here are reference netlists.** They give the topology and nets for the critical blocks. They are not a fab-ready, DRC-verified design. Every part value must be checked against current datasheets and validated on hardware.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel = 1 motor = 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two FET channels: one high-side and one low-side. During the PWM off-time it freewheels through the complementary FET of the same leg.

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.45 \times 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.02\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Instantaneous bridge conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.02\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.0\,W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 sourcing and 1/3 sinking. Within the sourcing interval, the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side for (1−D).

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS,hot}}{3}\left[D + (1-D)\right]_{\text{sink-interval, LS fully on}} \;\;\Rightarrow$$

A cleaner form for the worst-case FET, which is the low-side FET at D → 1:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{DS,hot}}{3}(1+(1-D)) \;\le\; \frac{2}{3}I^2R_{DS,hot} = 4.35\,\text{W (worst LS FET, low D)}$$

$$P_{FET,avg,typ} \approx \frac{13.0}{6} \approx 2.2\,\text{W average}$$

The electrical frequency is f_e = pole-pairs × RPM / 60. For 7 pole pairs at 30,000 RPM, f_e = 3.5 kHz, giving a 286 µs period. That is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so averaged per-FET power is valid for junction temperature.

**Copper and interconnect conduction:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace}, \quad R_{trace} \approx 0.4\text{–}0.6\,\text{m}\Omega\ \text{(2 oz, inner + outer paralleled, ~15 mm path)}$$

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\,W}$$

**Current shunt** (if fitted, 0.25 mΩ on the common return; this is shared by all four channels):

$$P_{shunt} = (4 I_{avg,bat})^2 R_{sh}$$

At an 80 A battery-side burst this is 1.6 W.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (hard-switched high-side edge)

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches. The synchronous low side switches nearly zero-voltage because the body diode conducts during dead time.

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.97\,W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the complementary FET):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.060\,W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\times10^{-9}\times635\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.023\,W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 FETs switching in the PWM leg; this power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times40\times10^{-9}\times10\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.038\,W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f \, I \, (2t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\,W}$$

Dead time is the second-largest "switching-related" loss. Cutting t_d from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Do not reduce it below the driver propagation mismatch plus the FET t_d(off) margin, or you risk shoot-through.

### 2.3 Loss Summary at 80 A Burst

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | ×4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 13.0 | 52.0 |
| PCB copper | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.12 | 0.5 |
| Shunt (shared) | — | 1.6 |
| **Total** | **~17.9** | **~73** |

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 7.26 kW, so η ≈ 99.0% for the bridge alone. The loss is small relative to throughput but very large relative to the board area.

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \tfrac12 V I(t_r+t_f) + V_fI(2t_d) + Q_{rr}V + \ldots \approx 33\,\mu\text{W/Hz per channel}$$

Raising PWM from 24 to 48 kHz therefore costs about 0.8 W per channel. Conduction dominates, so 48 kHz is justified by smoother low-throttle response and lower motor current ripple.
