# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Nano: Power-Stage Model, DShot Timer Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architectural Corrections Before the Math

Three points change the design and need to be settled first.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz inverter PWM.** In every production AIO, each ESC channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and commutation live in ESC firmware. Putting 4× three-phase commutation on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor (20×20 mm, M2/M3) is outside physically credible limits.** Typical 20×20 AIOs are rated 12–25 A continuous and 30–45 A burst per ESC. The math below shows why. I model 80 A per channel as requested, but treat it as a stress case, not a rating to print on the box.
3. **Total input current is 4 × 80 = 320 A worst case.** No nano-class battery pad, XT60 (≈60 A continuous), or 20×20 copper stack can carry that. The realistic system cap is set by the battery lead, not the FETs.

All component parameters below are representative assumptions for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm (DFN/PDFN) FET class. Substitute your chosen part's datasheet values.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd and one phase is held low.

- During the on-time D: current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the off-time (1 − D): current freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B).

Integrating over a PWM period, ignoring ripple (L_motor is large relative to 1/f_sw):

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is the key property of synchronous rectification.

**Numbers at I = 80 A:**

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.30 m = **16.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.15 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 2.15 m = **27.5 W** |

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS rises with T_j, so solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}, \qquad P_{FET}(T_j) = \tfrac{1}{6}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This converges as long as $\tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC} < 1$:

$$\tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 6400 \cdot 1.3\text{m}\cdot 0.0065\cdot 1.5 = 0.027 \ll 1$$

The junction does not run away. The board-level problem in Section 5 is the binding constraint.

**Per-FET loss.** Each FET conducts about 1/3 of an electrical period, so the per-FET average is about P_cond/6. The instantaneous peak for a conducting FET is:

$$I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \cdot 2.15\text{m} = 13.8\ \text{W}$$
