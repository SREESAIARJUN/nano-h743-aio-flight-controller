# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #11)

## 0. Feasibility Summary

**An 80 A burst rating on a nano-class board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) is not physically supportable.** The thermal model in §3 shows a burst budget of roughly 1–2 s at 80 A per motor even on a 36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board. Continuous capability is about 35–45 A per motor with propwash airflow.

I recommend three changes:
- Specify **"80 A, 1 s burst / 40 A continuous"** with telemetry-based derating.
- Use a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern (≈36×36 mm board)**, not 20×20.
- Treat every figure here as a model prediction to be validated by thermocouple and IR testing. The device parameters are representative assumptions, not a specific BOM commitment.

### Architecture

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. The architecture is partitioned as follows:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot |
| Motor commutation (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | n/a |
| Power stage (×24) | 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 5×6 | n/a |
| FC↔ESC link | Bidirectional DShot600 | n/a |

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In trapezoidal six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous complementary PWM:
- The high-side FET of the PWM leg conducts for fraction D.
- Its low-side partner conducts for fraction 1 − D.
- The opposite leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$R(110^\circ\text{C}) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.51 = 1.51\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.51\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

There is an electro-thermal feedback loop: R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which raises loss. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,Z_{th}$$

The design is stable only when $2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1$.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches at zero voltage via its body diode.

**Voltage/current overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing the dead time to 100 ns halves this term. Verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.

**Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, 2 switching gates):**

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Copper and shunt loss

Assume the bus→FET→phase→FET→return path is 0.3 mΩ (2 oz, 4 parallel planes) and the shunt is 0.25 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.55\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.5\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Totals

| Term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.3 W | 77.2 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **25.6 W** | **≈102 W** |

At 80 A per motor, input power is about 4 × 80 × 25.2 ≈ 8.1 kW. Efficiency is about 98.7%, but 102 W of heat into a 4 cm² board is the binding constraint.

**Conduction loss dominates (≈75%).** Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per channel. Spending money on lower R_DS(on) is the effective lever.
