# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #27): Power-Stage, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Design Basis

**Revision A (engineering derivation; values marked [VERIFY] must be confirmed against final datasheets and bench data)**

---

## 0. Architecture Findings

Two constraints shape the rest of this document.

**Finding 1: The H743 cannot commutate the motors directly.**
The STM32H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1, TIM8). That gives 6 complementary pairs, enough for 2 three-phase bridges, not 4. HRTIM has 10 outputs, also insufficient with dead-time-safe pairs. The design therefore uses:

- **FC domain:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot. It emits bi-directional DShot600.
- **ESC domain:** 4 × dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Each drives one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz PWM.

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in **ESC firmware**, not ArduPilot.

**Finding 2: 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is a transient rating, not a continuous one.**
The derivation in §3 shows roughly 22 W dissipated per motor at 80 A. A 20×20 board with prop-wash cooling cannot reject 88 W. The defensible spec is **80 A for ≤1–2 s bursts** (single motor) with firmware thermal derating, and roughly 35–45 A continuous per motor at 6S. Marketing labels should reflect this.

**Working assumptions:**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) |
| Burst current I | 80 A (phase, peak burst) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α (T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.006 K⁻¹

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, phase A is the PWM-switching leg and phase B is the low-side return leg. Then:

- **During D·T_s:** current flows through HS_A, the motor, then LS_B.
- **During (1−D)·T_s:** current flows through LS_A (synchronous rectification), the motor, then LS_B.

Leg A always has exactly one FET conducting, and LS_B conducts continuously. Therefore:

P_cond,motor = I²_rms · R_DS(on) · [D + (1−D)] + I²_rms · R_DS(on) = **2 · I²_rms · R_DS(on)**

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property for burst analysis.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase current ripple at 48 kHz:

ΔI_pp ≈ V_bus · D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5:
ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 8.75 A

I²_rms = I² + ΔI²_pp / 12 = 6400 + 6.4 ≈ 6406 A² (0.1 % correction, negligible at 80 A)

### 1.4 Conduction results at 80 A

| T_j | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 12.8 W | 51 W |
| 100 °C | 18.6 W | 74 W |
| 125 °C | **20.5 W** | **82 W** |

### 1.5 Copper path loss

Estimated path: pad → 2 oz plane → motor pad, about 0.4 mΩ round trip per motor.

P_Cu = 80² × 0.4e-3 = **2.6 W per motor**

This heat goes into the board, not the junction.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate-current-limited transition times

Use the Miller plateau V_pl ≈ 3.0 V.

| Transition | Gate current | Time (t = Q_sw / I_g, Q_sw = Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 12 nC) |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-on | I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl)/(R_src + R_g,ext + R_g,int) = 7/(6+0+1) = 1.0 A | t_on ≈ 12 ns |
| Turn-off | I_g,off = V_pl/(R_sink + R_g,int) = 3/(3+1) = 0.75 A | t_off ≈ 16 ns |

### 2.2 Hard-switched V–I overlap

Only the HS of the PWM leg hard-switches. The LS synchronous FET turns on into body-diode conduction, which is effectively ZVS.

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 28e-9 × 48e3
= **1.35 W per motor**

### 2.3 Secondary switching terms (per motor)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8×80×100e-9×48e3 | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC) | 0.05 W |
| C_oss charge | ½ C_oss,er · V² · f_sw ×2 (C_oss ≈ 1.2 nF) | 0.04 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g · V_drv · f_sw × 2 FETs | 0.05 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **0.45 W** |
