# Job #53: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst, 48 kHz, Bi-directional DShot600)

**Document type:** Architecture and analysis baseline (Rev A, preliminary)
**Status:** Analytical model only. All component values are design assumptions to be confirmed against final datasheets and bench data.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points change the model and should be resolved at the design review.

1. **The H743 does not switch the bridges at 48 kHz.** ArduPilot on the H743 generates DShot600 commands. The 48 kHz complementary PWM is produced by four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document assumes AT32F421 or STM32G071 ESC MCUs with three-phase gate drivers. Switching losses are therefore an ESC-side model, and DMA allocation is an FC-side model.

2. **"80 A burst" must be scoped.** The baseline here is 80 A per motor for ≤1 s, with a 40 A continuous rating per motor. If the requirement is 4 × 80 A simultaneous, the board sinks about 135 W. Section 3 shows this is thermally impossible beyond about 1 s at the 20×20 mm form factor.

3. **Bus voltage and copper limits.** At 6S, 4 × 80 A means 320 A and about 8 kW of input power through 20×20 mm battery pads. This is outside physical reality for pads and wire, regardless of the FETs. The analysis below evaluates per-motor 80 A bursts. It also includes an "all four" worst case for transparency.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Bridge)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.6 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **2.3 mΩ** (2.4 mΩ is used below for margin).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, the phase current always flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high-side of phase A and the static low-side of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time, current flows through the complementary low-side of phase A and the static low-side of phase B.

Total bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk during conduction. The 120° conduction per phase is already captured by the "always two channels" argument.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each phase leg conducts for 240° of 360°. The high-side carries duty fraction D of its 120° interval, and the low-side carries (1−D) plus its full static 120°.

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R\left(\tfrac{1}{3}D\right),\qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R\left(\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)+\tfrac{1}{3}\right)$$

At full throttle (D → 0.95) with I²R = 15.4 W:

| Device | Loss |
|---|---|
| High-side FET | ≈ 4.9 W |
| Low-side FET | ≈ 5.4 W |

The low-side is the hot spot. Place low-side FETs with the best copper and via access.

### 2.3 Switching loss

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per bridge.

**Overlap (V·I crossover):**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**C_oss:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs per switching leg, dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,bridge} \approx \mathbf{2.67\ W}$$

**Design lever:** At 48 kHz, switching is about 8% of the total. Dead-time optimization (100 → 50 ns saves about 0.3 W) matters more than the f_sw choice. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per bridge but worsens current ripple and motor iron loss.

### 2.4 Other bridge-local losses

| Element | Model | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if used) | 0.25 mΩ × 80² | 1.6 W |
| Phase copper and pads (≈0.3 mΩ path) | I²R | ≈ 1.9 W |

|---|---|---|---|---|
| One motor @ 80 A | 30.7 W | 2.7 W | 3.7 W | **≈ 37 W** |
