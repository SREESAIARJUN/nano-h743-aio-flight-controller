## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held on (its low side conducts for the whole sector). Take sector A+ → B− as the example:

- **Phase A high side** conducts for duty fraction D.
- **Phase A low side** conducts the freewheeling current synchronously for (1 − D).
- **Phase B low side** conducts for the whole sector.

So, ignoring dead time, the instantaneous resistive loss is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The result is **independent of duty**. This is why synchronous rectification is mandatory at 80 A.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.80 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00080 = **10.24 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.07 mΩ | **13.70 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.16 mΩ | **14.85 W** |

**Hottest single FET.** This is the continuously-on low side in a given sector:

$$
I^2 R = 6400 \times 1.16\,\text{m}\Omega = 7.4\ \text{W}
$$

Commutation rotates this duty across all six FETs, so the time-averaged per-FET loss is about P_total/6. However, at low eRPM (heavy load, slow rotation) a single FET can carry it long enough to heat its junction.
