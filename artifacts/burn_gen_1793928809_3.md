# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): 80 A Burst Power-Stage and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot does not run field-oriented or six-step control for four motors at 48 kHz. The realistic architecture is:

```
[STM32H743 FC, ArduPilot] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)]
                                                       |-- 3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q)
                                                       |-- 6x N-MOSFET per motor (24 total)
```

The 48 kHz PWM and all switching and conduction losses live in the ESC section. The H743 owns DShot generation and eRPM telemetry capture.

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with your actual BOM datasheet values. All results scale as shown in the equations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path

In 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM, two switch positions are always in series with the phase current:

- the PWM'd leg (high-side for D, its low-side synchronous partner for 1−D);
- the continuously-on return-leg FET.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty, a key property of synchronous six-step.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | **30.7 W** | **122.9 W** |

The loss is spread over 6 FETs, each conducting about 1/3 of the electrical period. The hottest-FET estimate is **P_FET ≈ 5.1 W**.

### 1.4 Additional I²R terms

- **Current shunt** (0.25 mΩ per motor, if per-motor sensing): 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W.
- **PCB copper.** One 2 oz layer has sheet resistance R□ ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C. A phase path of about 3 squares over 3 paralleled layers gives ≈0.25 mΩ, which is **≈1.6 W per motor**. Use all inner layers as parallel power planes and via-stitch heavily.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, one leg is hard-switched per PWM cycle.

**(a) V-I overlap**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 switching FETs; dissipated in the driver and gate resistors)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{nC})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Total switching per motor ≈ 2.19 W. Across 4 motors ≈ 8.8 W.**

Design implications:

- Dead time is the second-largest term. Trimming from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Validate shoot-through margin at temperature first.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz the total is about 1.1 W, but expect more motor current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| PCB copper | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **36.1 W** | **≈145 W** |

Efficiency check at 80 A, D ≈ 0.85: motor-side electrical power ≈ 25.2 × 0.85 × 80 ≈ 1.71 kW per motor. ESC loss is 36 W, so efficiency ≈ 97.9%. That is high efficiency, but it is still 145 W into a 9.3 cm² board.
