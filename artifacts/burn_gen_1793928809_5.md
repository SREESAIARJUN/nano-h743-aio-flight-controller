# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

**Document scope:** Power stage (conduction, switching, thermal), FC↔ESC signalling (bi-directional DShot600), H743 timer/DMA allocation under ArduPilot, and reference netlists.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. All component values are design targets. Verify every number against the datasheets of your selected parts and against thermal test data before release.

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Three issues need to be settled before the math, because they change the model.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* DShot. Each motor (or all four) is commutated by dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, which generate the 48 kHz PWM. Typical ESC MCUs are the STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230.

- Letting the H743 commutate four sensorless BLDC motors while also running EKF3 is not supported by ArduPilot.
- The model below therefore uses **1 × H743 (FC) + 4 × ESC MCUs**.

### 0.2 "Nano" and "80 A burst" are in direct thermal conflict

- A 20×20 mm board cannot carry four 80 A channels; there is not enough copper area or FET footprint.
- I model a **30.5×30.5 mm** board.
- Section 3.4 shows that even this size gives roughly **24 A continuous / 80 A for about 3–5 s** per channel without a heatsink.

### 0.3 Supply voltage

The model assumes **6S**:

- V_bus = 25.2 V maximum.
- FETs must be rated 40 V minimum. Prefer 60 V for margin against 6S regen and inductive spikes.

## 1. Power Stage Topology and Parameters

The per-channel bridge is a 3-phase half-bridge built from 6 N-MOSFETs. It uses synchronous rectification and 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant, the current path is: one high-side FET (switching at duty D) → motor → one low-side FET (on for the full 60° sector). During PWM off-time, the opposite FET on the PWM phase conducts synchronously.

|---|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge |
| Burst phase current | I_pk | 80 A | Per channel |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz | AM32 configurable |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS=10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ | 40–60 V PDFN 5×6 class |

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction loss

Over one PWM period with synchronous rectification:

- The **PWM-phase high-side FET** conducts for time D·T_sw.
- The **PWM-phase low-side FET** conducts for time (1−D)·T_sw.
- The **sector low-side FET** conducts for 100 % of the sector.

So two FET channels are always in series with the motor current:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.6 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Worst single FET.** The sector low-side FET carries the full current continuously within its sector:

$$
P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = 10.2\ \mathrm{W}
$$

This peak falls on each low-side FET for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the cycle, the per-FET load is lower.

**Copper loss.** Plane, via and pad resistance between the battery pads and the FETs adds about 0.3–0.5 mΩ of path resistance per channel:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} \approx 2.6\ \mathrm{W\ per\ channel}
$$

This is not negligible, and it heats the same PCB that sinks the FETs.

**Convergence.** Because R_DS depends on T_j, iterate T_j → R_DS → P → T_j. With R_thJ-PCB ≈ 1.2 K/W and P = 10.2 W, the junction-to-board rise is ΔT ≈ 12 K. The model converges in 2–3 iterations.

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM FET)

The voltage–current overlap time per edge is governed by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\ \mathrm{nC}}{1\ \mathrm{A}} = 12\ \mathrm{ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{1.16\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{Coss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 1.5\times10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.046\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery.** The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and then hard-recovers when the high-side turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}
$$

The induced Q_rr loss in the opposing FET typically adds about the same again, so budget **0.12 W**.

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F I \,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$
P_{gate} = 6 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \approx 2 \times 50\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\times10^3 = 0.05\ \mathrm{W}
$$

The factor of 1/3 reflects that in 6-step operation only one leg is PWM-switched at a time.
