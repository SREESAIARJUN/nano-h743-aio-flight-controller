# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #65): Design Model and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 cannot directly commutate 4 motors.** Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs with hardware dead-time. The H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 as advanced-control timers, giving 6 complementary pairs. Bidirectional DShot also only makes sense when a separate ESC MCU reports eRPM back. The architecture is therefore:
   - **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot, sending bidirectional DShot600 to
   - **ESC:** 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32. Each generates 48 kHz complementary PWM for its own bridge.
2. **"80 A burst" means per motor channel, for ≤ 10 s.** That is the industry convention.
3. **Form factor is 30.5 × 30.5 mm.** A true 20 × 20 board cannot carry 80 A per channel, even in bursts (see §3.4).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology
Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phase legs carry current I:
- **Leg A (PWM'd):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D; the low-side FET conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B:** the low-side FET is held on.

### 1.2 Equations

$$
R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\cdot\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right] \approx 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.95\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

Leg A, high plus low side:

$$
P_{A} = I^2 R_{DS,hot} D + I^2 R_{DS,hot}(1-D) = I^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

Leg B, low side:

$$
P_{B} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

Per channel:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

Copper loss, assuming 2 oz copper and an effective trace/plane resistance per channel R_pcb ≈ 0.4 mΩ:

$$
P_{pcb} = I^2 R_{pcb}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| I²R_DS,hot per conducting path | 6400 × 1.95 m = **12.5 W** |
| P_cond per channel | **25.0 W** |
| P_pcb per channel | 6400 × 0.4 m = **2.6 W** |

Per-FET worst case is 12.5 W, giving ΔT_jc = 12.5 K. That is acceptable only if the case is held below about 110 °C.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Conditions: V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full), I = 80 A, t_r + t_f = 40 ns (FD6288-class driver, R_g ≈ 10 Ω), dead time t_dt = 100 ns per edge.

Only Leg A switches.

| Loss | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | **1.94 W** |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw = 0.5·60e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·60e-9·10·48e3 | 0.06 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### 2.1 Channel and Board Totals at 80 A

$$
P_{ch} = 25.0 + 2.6 + 2.7 \approx \mathbf{30.3\,W}, \qquad P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{121\,W}
$$

### 2.2 Design Levers for 48 kHz
Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A, but raises current ripple and acoustic noise. AM32 supports variable PWM frequency, which lets you trade these off.
