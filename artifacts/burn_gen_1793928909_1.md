# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power Stage and Timer Architecture (Job #13)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

These points change the design, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO board, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs (STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. Driving 12 half-bridges with sensorless BEMF commutation from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not a viable design. This document models **H743 (FC) → 4× ESC MCU → 4× 3-phase bridge**.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" footprint is a burst rating of seconds, not a continuous rating.** The thermal model in Section 3 shows this quantitatively. Labelling the board "80 A" without a duration is a marketing number. Specify it as **"80 A / 2 s burst, X A continuous at defined airflow."**
3. **Part parameters below are representative class values.** They describe a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, DFN5x6-class MOSFET and an FD6288-class driver. Every number marked ⚠ must be replaced with values from the selected datasheet before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel = 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

The model assumes trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, exactly two phases carry current.
- The PWM leg conducts through either its high-side FET (during D) or its low-side FET (during 1−D).
- The opposite leg's low-side FET is held on.

The current path therefore always crosses **two R_ds(on)**, regardless of duty:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence of R_ds(on) is approximated as:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}
$$

At 80 A:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 6400 × 2 × 1.0 m = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 6400 × 2 × 1.5 m = **19.2 W** |

**Copper and interconnect loss.** Phase traces, plus the pad and solder path, add roughly 0.3–0.5 mΩ per path on a 2 oz × 8-layer stack. At 0.4 mΩ:

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m} \approx 2.6\ \text{W per channel}
$$

**Positive feedback risk.** Because P_cond rises with T_j and T_j rises with P_cond, the coupled system must be solved iteratively (Section 3.3). If the effective thermal resistance R_θ satisfies

$$
R_\theta \cdot \alpha \cdot P_{cond,25} > 1
$$

the loop diverges, and the result is thermal runaway.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

**V–I overlap (hard-switched leg only).**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery.** In synchronous mode, the body diode conducts only during dead time, but Q_rr is still incurred:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance.**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction.** There are two dead-time intervals per period.

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.84\ \text{W}
$$

This term is significant, and it is the main argument for keeping dead time minimal (≤ 200 ns if the driver and layout allow).

**Gate drive.** This is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET. Only the PWM leg's two FETs switch.

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}
$$

**Total switching-related loss per channel:**

$$
P_{sw,tot} \approx 0.97 + 0.05 + 0.06 + 1.84 + 0.06 \approx 2.98\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper / interconnect | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| Switching + dead time | 3.0 W | 11.9 W |
| DC-link cap ESR ripple (est.) | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Efficiency is 1 − 100 / (4 × 25.2 × 80) ≈ **98.8 %**. The efficiency is fine. Absolute heat flux into a roughly 30 × 30 mm board is the problem.

**Design insight:** Conduction dominates by about 6.5:1. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.5 W per channel. Lowering R_ds(on) by paralleling FETs or moving to a larger package buys far more than switching optimization.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Foster/Cauer Lumped)

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc 0.8 K/W]──●──[Rθ_spread 3 K/W]──●──[Rθ_ca (airflow)]── T_amb
       (per FET)        │ C_pkg≈0.1 J/K      │ C_board
                     T_case               T_board
```

### 3.2 Adiabatic Burst Limit

For short bursts, convection is negligible and the board behaves as a single heat capacitance. For a 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board:

- Copper mass ≈ 8 × (9.3 cm² × 70 µm × 8.96 g/cm³) × ~0.7 fill ≈ 3.3 g, giving 3.3 × 0.385 = **1.27 J/K**
- FR4 ≈ 1.6 g at 1.1 J/g·K, giving **1.8 J/K**
- 24 FETs plus solder ≈ **0.6 J/K**
- **C_board ≈ 3.7 J/K**

The temperature ramp at 100 W is:

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C} = \frac{100}{3.7} \approx 27\ \text{K/s}
$$

Starting from a board temperature of 60 °C, with a 105 °C board limit (FR4 and capacitor derating):

$$
t_{burst,max} \approx \frac{105 - 60}{27} \approx \mathbf{1.7\ s}
$$

### 3.3 Junction Excursion (Per FET, Worst Case)

The worst-case FET is the always-on low-side FET in a commutation sector, carrying the full 80 A.

$$
P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m} = 9.6\ \text{W}
$$

$$
\Delta T_{jc} = 9.6 \times 0.8 = 7.7\ \text{K}
$$

Then T_j = T_board + 7.7 + ΔT_spread, where ΔT_spread ≈ 9.6 × 3 × Z_th(t)/R_th. This settles in roughly 100 ms, so T_j ≈ T_board + 25 to 35 K.

At the end of the 1.7 s burst, T_j ≈ 105 + 30 = **135 °C**. This is below the 150/175 °C rating but leaves little margin.

Commutation rotates the full-current role among the FETs, which averages per-FET dissipation to roughly ⅓ to ½ over an electrical cycle. The steady sector-hold case (stall or low RPM) is the true worst case and must be covered by AM32 stall protection.

### 3.4 Continuous Rating

Steady state requires P = (T_board − T_amb) / R_θ,ca. With R_θ,ca ≈ 2.5 K/W (prop-wash airflow, nano board) and ΔT = 105 − 40 = 65 K:

- P_max ≈ 26 W total for the board
- Solving 4 × [I² × (2 × 1.4 m + 0.4 m) + ~1.5 W] = 26 W gives I ≈ **37 A continuous per motor**

**Recommended rating label:** "40 A continuous / 80 A 1.5 s burst (6S, airflow)." Enforce it with an ESC-side I²t limiter or ArduPilot `BATT_*` current limiting.
