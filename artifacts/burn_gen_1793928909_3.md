## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

Several points need to be settled before the math, because they change the numbers.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and has no ESC firmware. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs (one per motor) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Suitable parts include the AT32F421K8U7, STM32G071GBU6, or GD32E230. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them and receives eRPM telemetry back. This is the only architecture in which "ArduPilot bi-directional DShot" is meaningful.

2. **"80 A burst" on a "nano" board is thermally marginal.** Section 3.4 shows that a 20×20 mm board survives roughly 1–1.5 s at 4×80 A before the copper/FET junction limit is reached. I recommend one of two options:
   - a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor for an 80 A / 2 s burst rating, or
   - keeping 20×20 mm and rating it 80 A for ≤1 s with a 45 A continuous rating.

   The model below lets you choose.

## 1. Commutation Model

The model uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

In each 60° electrical sector:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side (HS) conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw. The LS acts as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the LS is fully ON.
- **Leg C:** floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period: 120° in the PWM role or the fully-on role.

## 2. Conduction Losses (Derivation)

### 2.1 Hot R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds,hot}=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Instantaneous conduction loss per motor

The current path at any instant always crosses exactly two FET channels, one in leg A and one in leg B:

$$P_{cond,A}=I^2R_{ds,hot}\,D + I^2R_{ds,hot}(1-D) = I^2R_{ds,hot}$$
$$P_{cond,B}=I^2R_{ds,hot}$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond}=2I_{ph}^2R_{ds,hot}}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = 19.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

The result is independent of D. Synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence, apart from the dead-time term below.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

During two dead-time windows per PWM period, current freewheels through the LS body diode:

$$P_{dt}=2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}=2(0.8)(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Copper and PCB path loss

Assume 2 oz copper, 4 parallel layers, about 15 mm effective path per phase, and roughly 0.25 mΩ of interconnect including the pads:

$$P_{Cu}=I^2R_{Cu}=80^2(0.25\text{m}\Omega)=1.6\ \text{W per motor}$$

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

Only leg A switches. HS turn-on and turn-off are hard-switched, and the LS is soft-switched (ZVS via its body diode):

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2(80\text{nC})(10)(48\text{k})=0.077\ \text{W}$$

### 3.5 Frequency sensitivity

Switching-related loss (3.1–3.3 plus dead time) scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw + P_oss + P_rr + P_dt (per motor) |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.64 W |
| 48 kHz | 3.28 W |
| 96 kHz | 6.56 W |

At 80 A, 48 kHz costs about 1.6 W per motor more than 24 kHz. In exchange, current ripple is lower, which reduces motor copper and iron loss. 48 kHz is the right compromise for low-inductance 2xxx/3xxx-class quad motors.

## 4. Loss Budget and Thermal Model

### 4.1 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W / motor | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Channel conduction | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Hard switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Coss + Qrr | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| PCB copper | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.08 | 0.3 |
| **Total** | **24.2** | **96.6** |

At D ≈ 0.9 the electrical output is about 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 × 4 ≈ 7.26 kW, so the ESC stage efficiency is about 98.7%. That figure is excellent, but 97 W is still being dumped into a board of a few grams.

### 4.2 Per-FET loss (time-averaged over the electrical cycle)

The electrical frequency is well above 100 Hz at burst RPM, while the FET thermal τ is on the order of 10 ms or more, so averaging is valid.

- **HS, leg-A role (1/3 of cycle):** (I²R·D + P_sw + P_oss) / 3 → at D = 0.9: (8.64 + 1.94 + 0.05) / 3 ≈ **3.54 W**
- **LS, leg-A role (1/3):** (I²R·(1−D) + P_dt + P_rr) / 3 = (0.96 + 1.23 + 0.06) / 3 = 0.75 W
- **LS, leg-B role (1/3):** I²R / 3 = 9.6 / 3 = 3.2 W
- **Worst-case LS total** (both roles over a cycle): ≈ **3.95 W**
