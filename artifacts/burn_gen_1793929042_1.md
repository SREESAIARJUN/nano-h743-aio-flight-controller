# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #19): System Model and Design Package

---

## 0. Architecture Review: Corrections Before Detail Design

Three issues in the brief would invalidate the design if left unaddressed.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|------------|
| A1 | **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware and contains no ESC commutation code. | The board uses a split architecture: the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. |
| A2 | **An 80 A continuous rating is physically impossible on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board.** Section 3 shows a steady-state rise in the hundreds of kelvin. | 80 A is treated strictly as a **time-limited burst**. The model derives the maximum burst duration (about 1.5–3 s) and a firmware current/thermal limiter. |
| A3 | **The H7 DMA cannot reach DTCM.** DShot buffers placed there fail silently. | All DShot and telemetry buffers go in D2 SRAM1/SRAM2, marked non-cacheable or with cache maintenance. ArduPilot's ChibiOS HAL handles this when the hwdef memory map is correct. |

All component parameters below are **stated assumptions** representative of 30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs used in 4-in-1 ESCs. Substitute your final datasheet values and re-run the equations; the structure of the model is what matters.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

**Temperature dependence of on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85) = 2.07\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Current path.** The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). The motor current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs in series:

- the HS or LS device of the PWM'd phase, and
- the LS device of the return phase.

This holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.07\text{m} = \mathbf{26.5\ W}$$

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.8\text{m} = \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

**Electro-thermal coupling.** Because R_ds depends on T_j, solve for the operating point iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{\theta}(t)\,\big[P_{sw} + I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,25}(1+\alpha(T_j^{(n)}-25))\big]/N_{active}$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. Thermal runaway occurs if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot Z_\theta \ge 1$$

That is:

$$I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\, Z_\theta \ge 1$$

For 80 A, this gives runaway when Z_θ ≥ 23 K/W. This is a real risk on an unheatsinked nano board in steady state, which is another argument for a burst-only rating.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches hard.

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

**Body-diode conduction during dead time (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the MOSFET):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.66\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (MOSFETs) | 26.5 W | 106 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.66 W | 6.6 W |
| Copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| **Total** | **33.3 W** | **133 W** |

**Efficiency at burst.** Output power is roughly V·I·D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 1814 W per channel, so η ≈ 98.2 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is the heat **density**.

**Design levers, in order of impact:**

1. **Lower R_ds.** Conduction dominates, at about 80 % of the total.
2. **Parallel MOSFETs.** Doubling devices per position halves P_cond, but there is no space for this on a nano board.
3. **Reduce copper loss.** Use 2 oz or heavier copper and stitched internal planes.
4. **Lower the PWM frequency.** Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel, so this is **not** the primary lever.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-MOSFET Average Dissipation

Over one electrical period, each of the 6 devices carries the loss for about 1/3 of the time. The PWM'd device additionally carries the switching loss. The average is:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond} + P_{sw}}{6} \approx 4.7\ \text{W}$$

The peak device in a 60° segment sees about 13 W instantaneously. The thermal time constant of the die and package (about 10–50 ms) is much longer than one electrical period at high eRPM, so averaging is valid above roughly 1000 eRPM.

**Low-speed caveat.** At stall or very low RPM, a single FET pair can carry the full current for a long time. The firmware stall-current limit covers this case.

### 3.2 Thermal Network (Cauer/Foster, Lumped)

```
 Tj ──R_θJC(1.5)── Tcase ──R_θCB(1.0, thermal vias + solder)── T_board ──R_θBA── T_amb
  │                  │                                           │
 C_j(~0.02 J/K)     C_pkg(~0.1 J/K)                             C_board
```

**Board-to-ambient resistance (convection in prop wash).** Assume both sides of a 30.5×30.5 mm board, so A ≈ 1.86×10⁻³ m², and h ≈ 100 W/m²K under prop wash:

$$R_{\theta BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{100 \cdot 1.86\times10^{-3}}
