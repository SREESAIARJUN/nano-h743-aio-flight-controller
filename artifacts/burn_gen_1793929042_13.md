# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #24)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

This model assumes a standard AIO architecture. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AM32-class, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071), which perform commutation at 48 kHz.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C with the max-spec part: R = 1.3 × 1.47 ≈ **1.91 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Trapezoidal 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases carry current I:
- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D. The low side (sync rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** the low side conducts 100%.

There are therefore always exactly two FET channels in series with the current:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per-FET average: each phase is active 2/3 of the electrical period, split between its high-side and low-side switch:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2I^2R}{6}$$

The worst-case instantaneous single-FET loss is I²R, which occurs on the static low side.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(80)^2(1.91\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.4\ W}$$

- Per-FET average: 4.1 W
- Per-FET peak (static sector): 12.2 W

### 1.4 Parasitic copper losses (not optional at 80 A)

| Path | Est. R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace, per phase (×2 active) | 0.25 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Current shunt (if per-ESC, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Battery pad to bus (shared, 320 A total if all 4 burst) | 0.1 mΩ | 10.2 W |

**ESC total conduction, including copper: ≈ 29 W per channel.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per ESC.

### 2.1 Voltage-current overlap

Rise and fall times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_{g,on}}{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_{g,off}}{V_{pl}}$$

With R_g,on = 5 Ω, R_g,off = 2 Ω and V_pl = 4.5 V: t_r ≈ 8 ns, t_f ≈ 4 ns. Add package and layout inductance, and use a realistic **t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns**.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Remaining switching terms

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time body diode | $2V_F I\,t_{dt}f_{sw}$ = 2(0.8)(80)(50n)(48k) | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | $V_{bus}Q_{rr}f_{sw}$ | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | $\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw}$ | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | $2Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}$ | 0.05 W (dissipated in driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.9 W** |

**Insight:** at 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 15:1. Raising PWM from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per ESC. Paralleling FETs or using lower-R_DS parts buys far more than slowing the PWM.
