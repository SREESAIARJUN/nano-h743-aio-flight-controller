## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

### 0.1 What the H743 does and does not do
The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any production "H743 + 4-in-1" AIO, each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, usually an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The two layers split as follows:

| Layer | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIH6 / VIT6 @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot, generates bi-directional DShot600, decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC control (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN-32) | 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF commutation, DShot decode, GCR eRPM reply |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver, QFN-24) | Bootstrap high-side, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink |
| Power stage (×24 FETs) | 40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3.3 | 6 FETs per motor |

The 48 kHz switching analysis below therefore applies to the ESC MCUs. The DShot/DMA analysis applies to the H743.

> **Feasibility warning:** "80 A" on a nano AIO is a **burst** rating, valid for seconds. Section 3 shows that four channels at 80 A simultaneously is a ~95 W heat load on a board with roughly 6 J/K of thermal mass. The board reaches its limit in about 4 seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology
Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM):

- Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period: 120° sourcing and 120° sinking.
- During the PWM'd 120° interval, the high side (HS) conducts for duty D and the low side (LS) conducts for (1 − D).
- The current path therefore always contains **exactly two conducting FETs**.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At a design T_j of 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.005 \times 85) = 1.71\ \text{mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss
Sum over the three legs. Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the period, with one FET carrying I_ph at any instant:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 3 \cdot \tfrac{2}{3} \cdot I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.71\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.9\ W}
$$

This result is independent of D, which is a property of synchronous rectification.

### 1.4 Per-FET conduction loss
The worst case is a high side at D → 1, conducting for its full 1/3 of the period:

$$
P_{cond,FET,max} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = \mathbf{3.65\ W}
$$

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses
The shunt carries chopped DC-link current, so

$$
I_{shunt,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{D}
$$

$$
P_{shunt} = I_{ph}^2 D \, R_{shunt} \;\xrightarrow{D=1}\; 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{mΩ} = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

PCB phase and bus copper is estimated at about 0.3 mΩ of effective path resistance. With 3 oz outer layers paralleled through via arrays to the inner planes:

$$
P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 \times 0.3\,\text{mΩ} \approx 1.9\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

In six-step drive, only **one high-side FET per motor hard-switches** at any time. The low side commutates softly: its body diode conducts during dead time, giving near-zero-voltage turn-on.
