# Job #29: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC)
## Electrothermal Model, Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not drive the power stage.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole ESC) uses a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as the STM32G071 or AT32F421. The MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The architecture below follows that split.
2. **"80 A burst" must be qualified.** If 80 A is per motor on all four motors at once, battery current is about 320 A. No nano-format board, XT60 connector, or battery lead supports that. The math below shows **all-four 80 A operation is limited to about 2–3 s** on a 30×30 mm board, and less on 20×20 mm. A sustainable spec is roughly **35–45 A continuous / 55–65 A burst (10 s) per motor** on 30×30 mm.
3. **All component values are representative assumptions.** Replace them with the selected MOSFET's datasheet values and re-run the model before tape-out.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At the design junction temperature T_j = 110 °C:
$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times(1+0.006\times85) = 1.81\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss
In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly **two** FET channels in series:
- one high-side FET, or its complementary low-side FET during PWM off-time, and
- one low-side return FET.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\times 80^2\times 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET conduction loss
Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical period. That load is shared with its complement according to duty cycle. Averaged over one electrical cycle:
$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,motor}/6 \approx 3.9\ \text{W}$$

The worst case is about 7.7 W on a high-side FET at 100% duty. Use this value for the junction-gradient check.

### 2.4 Electrothermal feedback (fixed-point iteration)
R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\!\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\right)\cdot R_{\theta,jb}$$

Convergence requires:
$$\frac{\partial T_j}{\partial T_j} = I_{rms,FET}^2\,R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,jb} < 1$$

At 80 A, per-FET RMS ≈ 46 A and R_θ,jb ≈ 5 K/W:
$$46^2 \times 1.2\text{e-3} \times 0.006 \times 5 = 0.076$$

The loop is stable, so no runaway occurs at the FET level. Board-level runaway is a separate issue, covered in §4.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

At any instant, only one leg is PWM-switched per motor. That means one hard-switched FET plus its complement.

| Term | Formula | Value / motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw = 0.5·30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.018 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.35 W** |

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it drops to about 1.2 W. At 96 kHz it rises to about 4.7 W. At 80 A the design is **conduction-dominated (≈ 91%)**. Lowering R_DS(on), by paralleling or using a better die, matters far more than lowering frequency.

| **Worst case** | | **≈ 117 W** |

### 4.2 Steady-state check (fails)
Board-to-air thermal resistance for a 30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board:
- about 8 K/W in still air
- about 3–4 K/W in prop wash

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 117\ \text{W} \times 3.5\ \text{K/W} \approx 410\ \text{K}$$

Continuous all-motor 80 A is **impossible**. The burst rating rests entirely on thermal capacitance.
