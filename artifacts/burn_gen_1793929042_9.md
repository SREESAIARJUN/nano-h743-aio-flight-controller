## 0. Architectural Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, which closes the 48 kHz PWM and commutation loop. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In trapezoidal (6-step) synchronous PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- the PWM leg's high-side **or** low-side FET (complementary switching), and
- the static leg's FET.

The duty cycle D therefore cancels out of the conduction term. With D = PWM duty:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

For sinusoidal FOC with peak phase current Î, the equivalent result is:

$$P_{cond,FOC} = \tfrac{3}{2}\hat I^{2} R_{ds}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

Conduction loss raises junction temperature, which raises R_ds(on), which raises loss. This is solved by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(n)})\right)\cdot(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB})$$

The loop is stable while:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta < 1 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\cdot 0.005\cdot R_\theta \ll 1$$

Evaluated here: 12.8 W × 0.005 × 2.5 ≈ 0.16, so the iteration is stable and there is no thermal runaway at the device level.

### 1.3 Numbers at T_j ≈ 125 °C

R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**

P_cond = 2 × 80² × 0.0015 = **19.2 W per ESC channel**

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

These are frequently forgotten and are not small.

**Phase and bus copper.** Assume 2 oz copper, 4 parallel layers, about 8 mm effective length × 6 mm width per path.

- Sheet resistance for 2 oz copper: ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.
- Squares per layer: 8/6 = 1.33.
- Four layers in parallel: R_cu ≈ 0.083 mΩ. This rises to about 0.11 mΩ hot.
- P_cu ≈ 80² × 0.11 mΩ × 2 paths ≈ **1.4 W**

**Shunt.** A 0.5 mΩ shunt on the low-side return carries current only part of the cycle.

- P_shunt ≈ I²R·(1 − D)_avg, approximately 80² × 0.0005 × 0.5 ≈ **1.6 W**
- This is a worst case. A low-side shunt in 6-step sees current mostly during the off-time.
