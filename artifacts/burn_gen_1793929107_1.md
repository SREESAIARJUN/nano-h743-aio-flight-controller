## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Flag

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V full, 22.2 V nominal | 40 V-class FETs required |
| Burst current | 80 A **per motor channel** | Peak phase/DC-link current |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz | ESC firmware (AM32/BLHeli_32 class) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification, complementary PWM | |
| Flight controller | STM32H743 @ 480 MHz, timer clock 240 MHz | Sends DShot600; does **not** drive the bridges |
| ESC MCU | One per channel (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) | Runs the commutation loop |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers | |

**Feasibility flag.** "Nano" boards (20 × 20 mm) cannot physically conduct or dissipate 80 A per channel. The thermal mass alone limits bursts to around 1 s, and copper cross-section limits the DC bus. This document therefore models a **30.5 × 30.5 mm** stack. If 20 × 20 mm is a hard requirement, derate to roughly 45 A burst per channel (see §3.5).

**Architecture note.** The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel runs its own MCU and gate driver. The H743 is responsible for the DShot600 command and telemetry link (§4). Losses in §1–§3 belong to the ESC half of the stack.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

During any commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B low-side is held on:

- **PWM on-time (D):** current flows through Q_AH and Q_BL.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous) and Q_BL.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two MOSFET channels, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left[ D\cdot R_{AH} + (1-D)\cdot R_{AL} + R_{BL} \right] \approx 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\,/^\circ\text{C (typ. for 40 V trench)}$$

Candidate FET: 40 V, 5 × 6 mm DFN, R₂₅ = 1.2 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V.

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.80 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.00 mΩ |

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

$$P_{FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.8\times10^{-3} = 23.0\ \text{W}$$

**PCB copper loss.** Assume 0.3 mΩ effective bus-plus-phase path per channel:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

**Shunt loss.** Assume 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt; current flows through it during the low-side path:

$$P_{shunt} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

**Total conduction per channel: ≈ 26.5 W**

### 1.4 Thermal runaway check

R_DS(on) rises with T_j, and P_cond rises with R_DS(on). The loop is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\alpha\cdot R_{th} < 1$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·1.2 mΩ·0.0067 = 0.103 W/K. The loop stays stable only if R_th(j-amb, effective) < 9.7 K/W.

This is why **continuous** 80 A per FET pair is unstable on a small PCB, and why 80 A must be specified as a burst rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Values: V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, t_r = t_f = 15 ns (gate driver ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink with 2.2 Ω gate resistor), t_dt = 100 ns dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (active switch only)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-times per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

Note that Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A it may be 2–3× the datasheet value, giving about 0.1–0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance (Q_oss ≈ 60 nC)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (Q_g ≈ 60 nC, two switching FETs per channel)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{n}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.
