## 0. Architecture Corrections

These issues change the analysis and should be resolved before layout.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/BlueJay, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421 or EFM8, plus a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288Q. The H743 only emits DShot600 and receives bi-directional eRPM telemetry. This document models it that way.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) stack is at the edge of physical feasibility.**
- Nano 4-in-1 ESCs are normally rated 20–45 A per channel.
- The math below shows that 80 A on 3×3 mm DFN MOSFETs gives a burst window of **about 1–3 seconds**, not a continuous rating.
- Four channels at 80 A simultaneously means 320 A of battery current at 25 V (8 kW). No nano-scale battery connector, pad or trace can carry that.

**I model 80 A as a single-channel burst, with the other three channels at 40 A.** Treat the outputs as a parametric model. Plug in the datasheet values of your chosen FETs and re-run the numbers.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases carry current:
- one leg is held fixed (one FET fully ON);
- the other leg is PWM'd complementarily (high-side for D, low-side for 1−D).

At every instant, therefore, exactly **two FETs** carry I_ph. The diode-conduction intervals are handled separately in §3.4.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; 2\,I_{rms,ripple}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

where

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

The ripple term is computed with L_ph = 10 µH, a typical value for a 2207-class motor. Peak-to-peak ripple is at most V_bus/(4·L·f_sw) ≈ 13 A, giving 3.8 A RMS. This is <0.3% of the DC term, so I neglect it.

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period.
- The fixed-leg FET conducts for the whole of that third.
- The PWM leg splits conduction D : (1−D) between its high-side and low-side FETs.

Averaged over an electrical revolution:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

This holds for a symmetric FET layout.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_DS rises with T_j, and T_j rises with loss. The closed-form fixed point is:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$
