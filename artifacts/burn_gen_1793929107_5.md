# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package: Job #64

## 0. Architectural Clarification

The 48 kHz switching loop and the DShot timer/DMA loop run on **different processors**:

| Domain | Processor | Firmware | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Bi-directional DShot600 TX/RX, timers, DMA, RPM notch filter |
| ESC (×4, or 1 MCU per motor) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class | AM32 or BLHeli_32 | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, dead-time, eRPM telemetry encoding |

ArduPilot never sets the 48 kHz PWM. That is an ESC firmware parameter. The two models below are therefore independent. The DShot link is the only coupling between them.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.18 mΩ**

### 1.2 Per-FET loss under 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

Per phase, over one electrical period:

- **High-side FET:** conducts for the 120° "high" segment at duty D.
- **Low-side FET:** conducts for the full 120° "low" segment, plus (1−D) of the "high" segment (synchronous freewheeling).

$$P_{HS} = I^2R\cdot\frac{D}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = I^2R\cdot\frac{1+(1-D)}{3}=I^2R\cdot\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Summing per phase gives:

$$P_{HS}+P_{LS} = \tfrac{2}{3}I^2R$$

Per motor (three phases):

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

This result is physically consistent: at every instant exactly two FETs carry the current in series.

### 1.3 Numbers

| Case | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor at 125 °C | 2 × 80² × 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** |
| Worst single FET (low side, D → 0) | 80² × 2.18 mΩ × 2/3 | 9.3 W |
| Single FET at full throttle (D = 1) | 80² × 2.18 mΩ / 3 | 4.65 W |
| Four motors | 4 × 27.9 W | **111.6 W** |

### 1.4 Electro-thermal iteration

R_DS(on) rises with T_j and loss rises with R_DS(on), so solve for a fixed point:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)})\,R_{th,ja}$$

The loop converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_{th} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{th} < 1$$

With per-FET R_th,ja around 25 K/W on a nano board, the product is 0.86 × 25 ≈ 21 ≫ 1. **There is no steady-state solution at 80 A.** This is thermal runaway, so 80 A is strictly a time-limited burst rating (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With high-side PWM, the high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET commutates at near-zero voltage after dead time, so its own V·I overlap loss is negligible but it contributes Q_rr and dead-time diode loss.

| Term | Formula | Per motor at 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (two active FETs, mostly dissipated in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

### Total loss budget at 80 A burst

| Scope | Breakdown | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 27.9 W conduction + 2.7 W switching | **30.6 W** |
| Board (×4) | | **≈ 122 W** |
| Board incl. copper, shunts, caps (estimate) | + I²R of bus/phase copper and shunts, ≈ 8–12 W | **≈ 130 W** |

### Design sensitivities

- **Conduction dominates (≈ 91 %).** Lower R_DS(on) or paralleled FETs have far more impact than faster switching.
- **Frequency trade-off:** dropping f_sw from 48 → 24 kHz saves only ≈ 1.3 W per motor. Raising to 96 kHz costs ≈ 2.7 W more per motor. 48 kHz is a reasonable acoustic/efficiency compromise.
- **Optimal R_DS(on)/Q_g trade-off:** minimize P = 2I²R + k·Q_g(R)·f_sw. In practice, for f_sw < 100 kHz at 80 A, always choose the lowest R_DS(on).
