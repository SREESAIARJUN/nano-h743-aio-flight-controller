# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #24)

## 0. Architecture Correction and Baseline Assumptions

**The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate the motors in this design.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller and produces DShot frames. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, because those firmwares are what implement the 48 kHz PWM and the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The partition used throughout is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir x4──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32) ──► 4× 3-phase gate driver ──► 24 MOSFETs
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° electrical interval, two phases conduct. Take the case where phase A is PWM'd high and phase B is held low.

| Device | Conduction fraction | Current |
|---|---|---|
| A-high | D | I |
| A-low (complementary) | 1 − D | I |
| B-low | 1 | I |

The total instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle. Averaged over 360° electrical, the loss is shared across 6 FETs, so each FET carries:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

(This assumes a flat-top trapezoidal current. With high ripple at low inductance, use $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$.)

### 1.2 Temperature coupling and closed-form junction temperature

R_ds(on) rises with temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Junction temperature depends on dissipated power:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{th}$$

Combining the two gives a closed-form solution:

$$T_j = 25 + \frac{(T_a - 25) + P_{25} R_{th}}{1 - \alpha P_{25} R_{th}}$$

**Thermal-runaway criterion:** the system is stable only if $\alpha P_{25} R_{th} < 1$.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

- $P_{25,FET} = 6400 \times 0.001 / 3 = 2.13$ W
- $\alpha P_{25} R_{th} = 0.004 \times 2.13 \times 40 = 0.341$ (stable, but strongly amplified)
- Steady-state $T_j = 25 + (15 + 85.3)/0.659 = $ **177 °C**

**Result:** 80 A is **not** sustainable as a steady-state current. It is a burst rating only (see §3).

At a 125 °C junction, R_ds = 1.4 mΩ, and the conduction losses are:
- per channel: $2 \times 6400 \times 0.0014 = 17.9$ W
- per FET: 6.0 W instantaneous during conduction, 3.0 W averaged

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The complementary low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS).

| Loss term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Body diode during dead time | $V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$ | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | $E_{oss} f_{sw}$ | 0.5 µJ · 48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | $2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}$ | 2 · 40 nC · 10 · 48 k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Sensitivity:** both the overlap loss and the dead-time loss scale linearly with f_sw.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel.
- Raising to 96 kHz costs about 2 W per channel.
- Trimming dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
