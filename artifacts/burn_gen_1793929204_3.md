**Architecture note.** In a production 4-in-1, the H743 does *not* drive the MOSFETs directly. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs and one low-side switch stays on:

- **PWM on-time (D):** current flows through HS_A and LS_B, so 2 FETs conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) and LS_B, so 2 FETs still conduct.

Two channel-resistances therefore carry I_ph at all times, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

For 40 V trench FETs, R_ds(100 °C) ≈ 1.45×R_25 and R_ds(125 °C) ≈ 1.6×R_25.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.9 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ | **30.7 W** |

**PCB copper.** Assume a phase path of about 25 mm at an effective 8 mm width on 4 layers of 2 oz. Copper sheet resistance at 2 oz is about 0.25 mΩ/□ per layer, giving roughly 3.1 squares at 0.0625 mΩ/□ ≈ 0.2 mΩ. That adds 6400·0.0002 ≈ **1.3 W** per motor at 25 °C, rising to about 1.7 W hot (+0.39 %/K).

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each switch conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so its average loss is roughly P_motor/6. However, the instantaneous loss while conducting is I²R ≈ **15.4 W** (hot) in a single 3.3×3.3 mm package. This peak matters for junction ripple at low eRPM, because the commutation period then approaches the package thermal time constant (§3.3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg switches. The low-side switch on the conducting phase is static during the sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high side)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 40 ns (driver about 1 A source/sink, R_g ≈ 5 Ω):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

The rise and fall times can be estimated from the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}} \quad (\text{rise}), \qquad \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,total}}{V_{plateau}} \quad (\text{fall})$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (sync FET body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so treat this as 0.05–0.15 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 30\text{nC}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 40\text{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A, T_j ≈ 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| FET conduction | 30.7 |
| Copper | 1.7 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | ~0.15 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 35 W** |
| **All 4 motors at 80 A** | **≈ 140 W** |

Switching losses are only about 8 % of the total. **This design is conduction-dominated.** Going from 48 kHz down to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor. Halving R_ds (paralleled FETs or a better die) saves about 15 W.

Efficiency check at full throttle: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 7.1 kW, so η ≈ 98 %. That efficiency is fine; the problem is **power density**, not efficiency.
