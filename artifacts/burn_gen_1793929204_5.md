**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 4 × 3 phases at 48 kHz while also running ArduPilot. The architecture is therefore:

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the full phase current at every instant:

- **PWM on-time:** HS(A) and LS(B) conduct.
- **PWM off-time:** LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) and LS(B) conduct.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,\frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

where n_p is the number of paralleled FETs per switch, and the on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Burst Case at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C, n_p = 1

$$R_{ds}(125) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET is commutated in for 120° of the electrical cycle. Its loss depends on its role:

| Role | Effective current share | Loss over 120° interval |
|---|---|---|
| HS (PWM switch) | D | D · I² · R |
| LS of PWM leg (sync rect) | (1 − D) | (1 − D) · I² · R |
| LS (return leg, static on) | 1 | I² · R = 9.3 W |

The static return-leg LS FET is the hottest device. Time-averaged over the full cycle, it dissipates approximately 9.3 / 3 + PWM-leg share ≈ **4–5 W**, with 9.3 W peaks per commutation interval.

### 1.4 Copper Losses (Do Not Omit)

Trace and plane resistance on a 30 × 30 mm board, using 2 oz (70 µm) copper with 2 layers in parallel per current path:

$$R_{sheet} = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; \approx 0.12\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \ (2\ \text{layers})$$

A path of roughly 4 squares (battery pad → HS → phase pad, and back via LS) gives about 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} \approx 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

Copper loss is comparable to FET switching loss. This is why 4-in-1 boards use 3–4 oz copper and heavy power planes.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Gate Drive Transition Times

$$I_{on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{7}{5} = 1.4\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.6\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{on}} = 10.7\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{15}{0.6} = 25.0\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Loss Terms

Only one leg is hard-switching at a time.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 35.7 ns × 48 k = **1.73 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 60 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.07 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 100 n × 48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate charge (driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 2 × 60 n × 10 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw,motor** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

### 2.3 Frequency Sensitivity

The overlap term scales linearly with frequency:

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 46\ \mu\text{J/cycle}$$

- 24 kHz → about 1.1 W
- 96 kHz → about 4.4 W

At 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates switching loss by roughly 8:1. Lower R_ds,on is therefore a more effective improvement than faster switching.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per motor | All 4 motors simultaneously |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, I_bat ≈ D · I) | ~1 W | ~4 W |
| **Total** | **~25 W** | **~100 W** |

The efficiency check is sound. At D ≈ 0.8, motor input power is about 1.6 kW per motor, so a 25 W loss corresponds to about 98.4% bridge efficiency.
