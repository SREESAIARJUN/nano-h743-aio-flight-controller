**Architecture correction.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. Each ESC channel runs its own small MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. It does not commutate the motors. The design below reflects this split.

**Feasibility warning.** As shown in §3, **80 A per motor (320 A total) on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally viable only as a sub-second, single-motor-dominant transient.** It is not a sustained rating. Plan the marketing number and the firmware current limits around the §3 results.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, current enters through one phase leg and returns through another:

- **Phase X (PWM leg):** HS_X conducts during D·T_sw. LS_X conducts (synchronous rectification) during (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase Y (return leg):** LS_Y conducts for the whole sector.

So **exactly two channels carry the full phase current I at every instant** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·1.2e-3 = **15.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | 2·6400·1.92e-3 = **24.6 W** |

### 1.3 Electrothermal feedback

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself:

$$P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\,P$$

Solving gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway occurs when R_th·2I²R₂₅·α ≥ 1.** At 80 A:

- 2I²R₂₅α = 0.092 W/K
- Runaway threshold: R_th,eff ≥ 10.8 K/W

The per-motor effective path to ambient on a bare nano board is roughly 15–25 K/W. **Continuous 80 A therefore has no stable operating point. It is transient-only by physics, not just by margin.**

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

| Element | Assumed resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad → FET → bus path | ~0.4 mΩ round trip | 2.6 W |
| Current shunt (if fitted, 0.25 mΩ, on bus) | 0.25 mΩ | I_bus²·R; at D = 0.5 and 40 A avg ≈ 0.4 W (RMS-dependent) |

**Per-motor conduction subtotal (hot): ≈ 27.2 W**

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off per period, with the LS body diode commutating.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f = 0.5\cdot1\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.015\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V f = 30\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\text{ W}$$

Reverse recovery also adds to the HS turn-on current spike. Add roughly +10 % to P_VI if Q_rr is high.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor. This is the cheapest optimization available, but verify shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation skew.

### 2.5 Gate drive loss (dissipated in the driver/resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f\cdot 2 = 50\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.048\text{ W}$$
