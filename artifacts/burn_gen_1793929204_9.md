# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Power-Stage Model, DShot Timing, and Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor's commutation, 48 kHz PWM, and eRPM telemetry is handled by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This design uses 4 × AT32F421K8U7, one per channel. Running FOC/6-step for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported configuration.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm mount, roughly 26×26 mm PCB) is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal section shows it is time-limited to seconds.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step, Synchronous Complementary PWM)

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg PWMs complementarily and the other leg's low-side FET stays on.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) for (1−D)·T_sw. The current I flows through one of them at all times.
- **Static leg:** the low-side carries I for 100% of the sector.

So, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

With temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 80 A with a hot junction:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.36\ W}$$

Copper and solder-joint losses:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$

**Note on electro-thermal coupling.** Because R_ds rises with T_j and T_j rises with P, the steady state is the fixed point of T_j = T_board + P(T_j)·Z_th. Solve this iteratively in the simulator. The 1.5× factor above assumes convergence near 125 °C.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Both of its FETs see the transitions: hard switching on the high-side, and diode/dead-time events on the low-side.

**2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

A tighter estimate uses the Miller plateau: t_sw ≈ Q_gd / I_g. With Q_gd = 10 nC and a driver giving I_g ≈ 0.8 A at the plateau, t ≈ 12.5 ns, which is consistent with the assumption.

**2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The recovery current also adds to high-side turn-on loss, roughly another 0.05 W. Both are included below.

**2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle)**

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \,(2 t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**2.4 Output-capacitance loss**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 2\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.061\ W}$$

The ½ factor is dropped here because C_oss,er is energy-equivalent and charging from the bus dissipates the equal amount again.

**2.5 Gate-drive loss** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

## 3. Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

| Loss | W per channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 15.36 |
| Copper / joints | 1.92 |
| V-I switching | 1.16 |
| Q_rr (+ induced turn-on) | 0.10 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| C_oss | 0.06 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 19.3 W** |
| **Board, 4 channels simultaneously** | **≈ 77 W** |

**Efficiency at full burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. At D ≈ 0.9 this is ~1.8 kW per channel, so η ≈ 99%. The problem is power density, not efficiency.

**Per-FET distribution.** Over an electrical cycle each FET is active in roughly 1/3 of the sectors. The low-side FETs carry more, because they act as both the static leg and the synchronous rectifier.

- Average per FET: (15.36 + 1.95) / 6 ≈ 2.9 W
- Worst-case low-side FET: ≈ **3.5–4 W** during a sector

**Sensitivity rule of thumb.** P_cond scales with I², and switching loss scales with V·I·f.

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 0.9 W per channel but raises acoustic noise and current ripple.
- Each 0.1 mΩ of R_ds(on) is worth about 1.3 W per channel at 80 A.
