## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.9 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.40 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, phase current flows through two phase legs. Within the active high-side leg:
- During D·T_sw, the high-side FET conducts.
- During (1−D)·T_sw, the complementary low-side FET in the *same* leg conducts (synchronous freewheel).

The return leg's low-side FET conducts continuously for that 60° sector.

This means **two FETs are always in series with the motor current.** It holds regardless of duty cycle, as long as synchronous rectification is active and dead time is neglected:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

For flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.40\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg is active for 240° of 360° (120° sourcing, 120° sinking). Within its active window, conduction is split between the high-side and low-side FET according to D. Averaged over an electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \approx 3.0\ \text{W}$$

At high duty (D → 1), the worst-case FET carries ≈ 2/3 × I² R:

$$P_{cond,FET,max} = \tfrac{2}{3} \times 80^2 \times 1.40\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.0\ W}$$

Size for this case. Full throttle at burst is exactly when D → 1.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently the dominant hidden term)

For a 2 oz (70 µm) internal layer stack with 4 parallel layers, a 15 mm path that is 6 mm wide gives roughly:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.006 \cdot 70\times10^{-6} \cdot 4} \approx 0.15\ \text{mΩ}$$

At 100 °C, copper resistance rises ×1.3, giving ≈ 0.2 mΩ. Applying this on bus + phase paths (×2):

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.2\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching FET (one per channel at a time) incurs hard-switching V-I overlap.

### 2.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{src} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10-3}{8+2.2+1} = 0.625\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{sink} + R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{4+2.2+1} = 0.417\ \text{A}$$

### 2.2 Transition times

The overlap interval spans Q_gs2 (current rise) plus Q_gd (voltage fall):

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{0.625} = 24\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} = \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{0.417} = 36\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.3 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{pk} (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 60\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.90\ W}$$
