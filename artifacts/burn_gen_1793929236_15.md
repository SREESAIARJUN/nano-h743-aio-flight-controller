# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not switch the bridges.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot600, and four dedicated ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz commutation PWM. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Driving 12 half-bridges with BEMF zero-cross detection from the FC while also running EKF3 is not a supported ArduPilot configuration. The "48 kHz switching" therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs, and the "DShot600 DMA" belongs to the H743.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A × 4.** The thermal analysis in §3 shows that a 20×20 or 25×25 board cannot sustain 4 × 80 A for more than about 1–3 s. I treat 80 A as a **per-motor phase-current burst** and compute the real burst-time limit rather than assume one.

**Design assumptions.** Replace these with your BOM datasheet values. The model is fully parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|

| I_ph | 80 A | burst phase current per motor |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation geometry
The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). In each 60° sector, one leg PWMs and one leg's low-side is held on. Each FET is active for 120° of every 360° electrical, which is 1/3 of the time.

Within its active window:
- The PWM-leg high-side conducts for fraction D.
- The PWM-leg low-side (synchronous) conducts for fraction (1−D), minus dead time.
- The return-leg low-side conducts continuously.

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}$$

The LS expression averages the PWM-leg and return-leg roles, which alternate between sectors.

Summed over the 6 FETs of one motor, it collapses to an exact and convenient result. **Two FET channels are always in the current path:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds (solve self-consistently)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.07 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.07\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.5\ W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt
Assume a 1 oz inner layer at 0.5 mΩ/sq and an effective 2 squares per phase path in parallel across 4 layers. The current-sense shunt is 0.25 mΩ, single per ESC, carrying the DC-link current I_dc = D·I_ph.

- Copper: P_Cu ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **3.2 W**
- Shunt: P_shunt = (0.95 × 80)² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.4 W**

**Conduction subtotal per motor ≈ 31.1 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg high-side hard-switches. The sync low-side switches at near-zero V_ds (ZVS-like) but incurs dead-time diode conduction and Q_rr.

| Term | Formula | Value / motor |
|---|---|---|
| Hard switching (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 35 ns × 48 k = **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.018 W** |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_SD·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 | 40 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 = **0.04 W** |

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.4 W per motor.**
