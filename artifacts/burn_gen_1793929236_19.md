## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

**Architecture interpretation.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC uses one small ESC MCU per motor running AM32 or BLHeli_32 class firmware (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421). The H743 does not commutate motors. It drives bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Correct me if Job #20 specifies otherwise.

> **Feasibility flags. Read these first.**
> 1. **80 A × 4 on a "nano" board (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) is not thermally sustainable.** Section 3 shows roughly 95 W total dissipation and only a few seconds of burst before T_j reaches 125 °C. "80 A" products are typically ≥40×40 mm boards with 5×6 FETs, and they quote burst ratings of a few seconds.
> 2. **Input capacitor ripple dominates the design** (Section 2.4). At 80 A, the board cannot be built without a low-ESR bulk capacitor at the battery pads.
> 3. If the real FETs are 3.3×3.3 mm parts (R_ds ≈ 2–3 mΩ), conduction loss scales linearly. Burst time drops to under 1 s.
