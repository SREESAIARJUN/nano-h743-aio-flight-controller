## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]^{\,}\quad,\ \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.3 mΩ × 1.5 ≈ **1.95 mΩ** (worst case, maximum spec).
Typical design value: 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step conduction with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase A switches high-side/low-side complementarily at duty D, while phase B's low-side is held on. The phase current I passes through:

- the high-side of A for fraction D,
- the low-side of A for fraction 1−D (synchronous rectification),
- the low-side of B for 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2I^2R_{ds}$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is an important simplification. Because each FET carries current in 2 of 6 sectors on average, and electrical frequency far exceeds the die thermal time constant (ms) at flight eRPM, the per-FET average is:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}}{3}$$

At I = 80 A (I² = 6400 A²), with R = 1.5 mΩ:

- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6400 × 1.5e-3 = **19.2 W per motor** (25.0 W at maximum R_ds)
- P_cond,FET = **3.2 W average** (instantaneous peak 9.6 W during the full-on sector)

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase copper (2 oz, ~10 mm, 3 mm wide, plus vias) ×2 in path | ~0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Per-motor shunt (0.5 mΩ, 2512) | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W (consider low-side DCR sensing or 0.25 mΩ) |
| Battery pad / common bus | 0.2 mΩ (carries 4×I) | 0.2e-3 × 320² = **20.5 W at 4×80A** |

> The common bus is a hidden dominant term. Battery pads must be maximal and copper must be stacked on 4 or more layers.
