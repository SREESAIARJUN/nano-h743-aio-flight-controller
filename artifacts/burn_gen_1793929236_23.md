# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Design

This document covers:

1. The power-stage loss model (conduction at 80 A burst, switching at 48 kHz)
2. The thermal model (steady-state and transient burst)
3. The ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan on the STM32H743
4. Netlists for the FC-to-ESC interface and one ESC power channel
5. A verification plan

---

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Corrections

Three points in the request need clarifying before any numbers mean anything.

### 0.1 The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU

- **The H743 does not commutate motors.** Each 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/BB21 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay).
- **The interface between them is bidirectional DShot.** The H743 sends commands to the ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back.
- **This document models both sides of that link:** the FC timers and DMA, and the ESC power stage.

### 0.2 "Nano" form factor versus 80 A

- **20×20 mm "nano" boards** are physically limited to roughly 35–45 A burst per motor, set by copper area and connector size.
- **This analysis assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board** so that 80 A is at least plausible for short bursts.
- **It shows quantitatively where 80 A breaks down** (Section 2).

### 0.3 "80 A burst" means per motor, not all four at once

- **Four motors at 80 A each is 320 A on the battery bus** (about 8 kW at 6S).
- **That exceeds an XT60** and is beyond what any common pack can deliver.
- **Both the single-motor and the four-motor-simultaneous cases are computed** so the worst case is bounded.
