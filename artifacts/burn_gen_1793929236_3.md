# Job #35: STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC
## System Architecture, Loss Models, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Reality Check (Read First)

Three points shape every calculation below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor bridge is commutated by its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay. Common choices are the AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8BB51. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by that ESC MCU, not the H743. I have architected it this way. Driving 4×6 FETs plus BEMF sensing from the H743 is technically possible, but no supported ArduPilot firmware path exists for it.
2. **"80A per motor" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally credible**, even as a burst. I therefore model a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board (8-layer, 2 oz)** and a **20×20 or 30.5 FC**. The model is parametric, so you can substitute your real geometry.
3. **Device parameters are representative placeholders.** They are typical of 40 V/1 mΩ-class 5×6 PQFN FETs. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected part before sign-off. The equations remain valid either way.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Derivation

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, at every instant exactly one high-side path and one low-side path carry the phase current I. During PWM-on, the HS FET of phase X and the LS FET of phase Y conduct. During PWM-off, the complementary LS FET of phase X freewheels, which is synchronous and not the body diode except during dead time. The LS FET of phase Y still conducts.

The current therefore always flows through **two R_DS(on) in series**, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, \frac{R_{DS}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

With trapezoidal currents, I_rms ≈ I_pk for the DC-link current seen by the conducting pair, assuming flat-top current and ignoring commutation notches. The temperature dependence is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET conducts during a 120° window of the electrical cycle. For the HS FET, that window is split D (on) / (1−D) (handed to its LS complement).

- **High-side FET average:** $P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[D + \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 0\right] I^2 R_{DS}$ during its own drive phase, plus nothing else. In practice HS and LS losses are shared, so the per-FET average is well approximated as:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}$$

- **Instantaneous peak** while conducting is $I^2 R_{DS}$. This peak sets the junction ripple at low eRPM.

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond/motor @80 A | P_cond, 4 motors | Per-FET average | Per-FET instantaneous |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 W | 51.2 W | 2.13 W | 6.4 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper Loss

The phase/bus copper is roughly 0.25 mΩ effective per motor current path, assuming 2 oz, short planes, and parallel layers:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\text{ W/motor}$$

This loss is not negligible. It heats the same board mass as the FETs.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. The other conducting leg is static within the 60° sector. So per motor there is one hard-switched HS/LS pair at any time.

### 2.1 Loss Components

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, HS turn-on/off):**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on):**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced extra HS turn-on loss is roughly 2× this value at high di/dt. Budget **0.12 W**.

**(d) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):**
$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss Summary (per motor, 80 A, 25.2 V)

| Component | Loss |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.69 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr (incl. induced) | 0.12 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W |
| **Total in FETs** | **2.44 W** |
| Gate driver | 0.05 W |
