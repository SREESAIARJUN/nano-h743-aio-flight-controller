# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot Timer Allocation

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the MOSFETs directly. ArduPilot has no field-oriented control (FOC) or six-step motor driver. The valid architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                   │ 6× PWM (48 kHz, complementary)
                                                   ▼
                                    4× 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class)
                                                   ▼
                                    4× 6 N-MOSFET bridge (24 FETs total)
```

All loss figures below apply to each ESC channel. Every number depends on the assumed MOSFET parameters in §1. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values into the equations.

> **Feasibility flag:** "Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst ×4" conflict physically. The analysis below assumes the smallest realistic form factor, a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** board. On 20×20 mm the burst time computed in §4 shrinks by about 55%.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, at any instant exactly one high-side path and one low-side path carry the phase current:

- The **PWM leg** alternates between its HS FET (duty D) and its LS FET (1−D).
- The **static leg** stays fully on.

Each leg therefore conducts continuously, so:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds(on),T} \cdot 2 \qquad R_{ds(on),T} = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.006/\text{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 1.44\text{ m}\Omega \times 2 = \mathbf{18.43\ W/ESC}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries about 1/6 of the load over an electrical revolution, so the average is about 3.1 W per FET. The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is 9.2 W.

**Copper and shunt additions:**

- 0.5 mΩ shunt: 80² × 0.5 m = 3.2 W. Use 0.25 mΩ or a Hall sensor on the bus instead, giving **1.6 W**.
- Phase/bus copper: 8-layer 2 oz, about 0.3 mΩ effective, giving **1.9 W**.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, which feeds back into loss:

$$
P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}
$$

This converges as long as 2I²R₂₅·α·R_θ < 1. Here the loop gain is about 0.019 × 0.8 ≈ 0.015, so the system is stable with no thermal runaway at the FET level.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·27e-9·48e3 | **1.306 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw = 25.2·60e-9·48e3 | 0.073 W |
| C_oss | V·Q_oss·f_sw = 25.2·50e-9·48e3 | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200e-9·48e3 | **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.058 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.11 W/ESC** |

**Design levers:**

- **Dead time:** dead-time loss scales linearly with t_dt. Reducing to 50 ns saves 0.31 W per ESC, but shoot-through margin must be verified with the driver's propagation-delay spread.
- **Frequency:** at 24 kHz, switching loss falls to about 1.05 W. However, current ripple doubles, and ripple adds RMS conduction loss in low-inductance motors.

**Ripple RMS correction (optional term).**

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}f_{sw}}, \qquad P_{ripple} = 2R\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

Example: L = 15 µH, D = 0.5, giving ΔI = 8.75 A and P_ripple = 0.018 W, which is negligible.

### Total per ESC at 80 A burst

$$
P_{ESC} = 18.43 + 2.11 + 1.6 + 1.9 \approx \mathbf{24.0\ W}
$$

$$
P_{total,4\times} \approx \mathbf{96\ W}\quad(\eta \approx 1 - 96/(4\cdot 25.2\cdot 80 \cdot \bar D) )
$$

At full throttle (D≈1), η ≈ 98.8%. However, 96 W must be absorbed by the board.
