# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC Stack: Design Analysis (Job #10)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own motor-control MCU (assumed here: AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU owns the 48 kHz PWM, commutation and dead-time. The H743 runs ArduPilot, sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU, and receives eRPM back. An H743 directly switching four three-phase bridges would need 12 complementary PWM pairs and is not a realistic ArduPilot target.
2. **"Nano" and 80A are in tension.** 80A continuous per channel is not survivable on a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board. This document treats 80A as a **time-limited burst** and derives how long the burst can last (§2.6).
3. **Component parameters are representative placeholders.** They are typical of 40V, 3.3×3.3/5×6 DFN, ~1 mΩ trench FETs. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release.

```
NET  GYRO_SPI      : H743.SPI1 (PA5 SCK / PA6 MISO / PD7 MOSI), CS PC15 -> IMU (ICM-42688-P)
NET  +3V3          : LDO_3V3.OUT -> H743.VDD*, IMU.VDD (separate filtered LDO for IMU recommended)
```

Each signal line also gets a pull-up so the bidirectional (inverted) idle state is defined when the H743 is in reset: 10 kΩ to 3.3 V at the ESC side. The 33 Ω series resistors damp ringing on the stack connector.

**Pin choice rationale.** All four motors sit on **TIM1** (PE9/11/13/14, AF1), which gives:
- one timer group, so one shared DShot update-DMA burst and one ArduPilot output group;
- an advanced-control timer;
- no conflict with TIM2/TIM5, which can be kept for LEDs (WS2812) or extra outputs.

## 2. Power Stage Mathematical Model (per ESC channel, at 80A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction paths (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

During any commutation sector, current I flows through phase A's high-side FET and phase B's low-side FET (ON interval, duty D). During the OFF interval it flows through phase A's low-side FET (synchronous freewheel) and still through phase B's low-side FET.

That means two FET channels carry I at all times, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{HS} D + I^2 R_{LS,A}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS,B} = 2 I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

if all FETs share the same R_DS(on). Per individual FET, conduction loss averages over the commutation rotation (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle). The **worst instantaneous** FET carries the full I²R continuously within its sector.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on), solved self-consistently

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_c + P_{FET}(T_j)\,Z_{\theta JC}$$

Solving by fixed-point iteration with T_c = 90 °C and worst-sector P_FET = I²R(T_j):

| Iteration | T_j (°C) | R (mΩ) | P_FET = 80²·R (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 90 | 1.551 | 9.93 |
| 1 | 104.9 | 1.631 | 10.44 |
| 2 | 105.7 | 1.636 | 10.47 |

The iteration converges at **T_j ≈ 106 °C** and **R ≈ 1.64 mΩ**. The worst-case design point below rounds this to 1.8 mΩ (equivalent to T_j ≈ 136 °C) for margin.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,m\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$
