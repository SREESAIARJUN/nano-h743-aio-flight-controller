## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, one phase is the PWM leg and one phase is held low. Current I flows through:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET for fraction D and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for fraction 1−D, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, 100% of the time.

### 1.2 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
R_{hot} = k_T\,R_{25}\,(1 + \alpha_{pkg}) \approx 1.6 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

The result is independent of D. Synchronous rectification makes this so, apart from the dead-time term in §2.

At 80 A:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,4} = \mathbf{122.9\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect)

Each current loop is roughly 4 squares of 2 oz copper (0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, 0.35 mΩ/□ hot), across ~2 parallel layers. That gives about 0.7 mΩ per path.

| Element | Formula | Per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper | I² · 0.7 mΩ | 4.5 W |
| Shunt | I² · R_sh = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |

**Total I²R per ESC is about 38.4 W at 80 A.**

## 2. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on the high-side turn-on and turn-off. The low-side switches at near-zero voltage, after the body diode conducts during dead time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 kHz | 0.968 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48 kHz | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½ Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 kHz | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6 · Q_g · V_dr · f_sw | 0.086 W |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |
| **Total, 4 ESCs** | | **≈ 6.9 W** |

**Interpretation.** At 80 A, switching loss is about 4% of conduction loss, so 48 kHz is not the thermal problem. The dead-time term is the largest controllable one. Tightening t_dt to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per ESC, but cross-conduction margin must be verified first.

**Frequency sensitivity.**

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 36\ \mu\text{J per cycle}
$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 96 kHz changes total loss by only ±0.9 W per ESC.
