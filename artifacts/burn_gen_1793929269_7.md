# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #60)

## Loss Model, DShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, one per channel (AM32 on AT32F421 is assumed here). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The loss model applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value | Basis |

> ⚠️ **Engineering flag up front:** 80 A per channel on a ~28 mm board is a **transient-only** rating. The thermal analysis in §3 shows a continuous capability of roughly 15–25 A per channel without a heatsink. Specify "80 A burst" with a defined duration of 2–4 s. Marketing-style "80A" without that qualifier will fail field validation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, two phases carry current:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** current alternates between high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) at duty D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** LS is held on continuously for the 60° sector.

Conduction loss per leg depends only on the current through it, regardless of D, because one device in each active leg is always on:

$$
P_{cond,leg} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T \cdot R_{DS(on),25}
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
R_{DS(on)}(110^\circ C) = 1.45 \times 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.31\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,leg} = 80^2 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} = 8.35\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 8.35 = \mathbf{16.7\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.3 Copper path loss (often ignored, significant here)

Assume 15 mm of phase/bus trace path, 4 parallel 2 oz layers, 5 mm wide. Copper resistivity at 100 °C is ρ = 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{A} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{4 \times 70\times10^{-6}\times0.005} \approx 0.24\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.24\times10^{-3} \approx 1.5\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 1.4 Distribution per device

Within a 60° sector, the LS on Leg B carries 8.35 W. In the steady rotation average, each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (HS or LS role). So the average per FET is:

$$
P_{FET,avg} = \frac{16.7}{6} \approx 2.8\ \text{W}
$$

However, at **low eRPM or stall**, the sector duration approaches the junction thermal time constant (~1–10 ms). Use the **sector peak of 8.35 W per device** for junction transient checks at startup and stall.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 Turn-on/off overlap loss

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48\,000 = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times60\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48\,000 = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\times10^{-9}\times48\,000 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This term is large, so dead time should be tuned down to about 100 ns. That requires gate-drive validation for shoot-through.
