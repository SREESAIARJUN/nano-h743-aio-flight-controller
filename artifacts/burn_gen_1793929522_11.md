# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Issues

I have no specification for "Job #37", so every parameter below is an assumption. Each one is tagged **[A]**. Replace them with your actual BOM values and re-run the equations. The model is built so you only need to swap parameters.

### Architecture correction

Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between a flight controller and an ESC. A design that uses it therefore needs:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743, running ArduPilot.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs (for example, AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421). Each MCU runs its own commutation and PWM at 48 kHz.

Driving 24 gate signals for four motors directly from the H743 would make DShot pointless. It would also make ArduPilot unsuitable, because ArduPilot has no integrated ESC firmware. The rest of this document assumes the FC + 4×ESC-MCU architecture.

### Physical feasibility of "nano" with 80 A burst

Sections 2 and 3 show that 4 × 80 A on a 20×20 mm "nano" board can only be a **sub-second-to-few-second burst**, even with good airflow. Do not market it as a continuous rating. I strongly recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board for an 80 A-class rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification)

At any instant, two phase legs carry current. One leg is PWMing (HS/LS complementary). The other has its LS held on. The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 (R_{sh} + R_{cu})
$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_m\, f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

The worst case is D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ A_{pp}
$$

$$
\frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 3.6\ A^2 \ll 6400\ A^2
$$

The ripple contributes less than 0.06 %, so it is negligible at 80 A. It matters for light-load efficiency and EMI, not for burst thermals.
