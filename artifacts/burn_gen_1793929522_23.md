# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Package

---

## 0. Architecture Constraints

### 0.1 The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs
In every production 4-in-1 stack (AM32 / BLHeli_32 / Bluejay), each ESC channel has its own MCU, such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does the commutation.

The H743 runs ArduPilot and only sends DShot600 frames, then receives eRPM telemetry back. The two halves of the math therefore live on two different processors:

| Domain | Processor | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, commutation | ESC MCU (×4, or ×1 for 4-ch MCUs) | Switching losses (§3) |
| DShot600 bidir, timer/DMA | STM32H743 | §5 |
| 80 A conduction | MOSFET bridge | §2 |

### 0.2 Form factor
80 A burst per channel is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" ESC. The copper area and thermal mass do not exist; §4 shows why.

- **FC:** 20×20 mm nano.
- **ESC:** 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers.

If the ESC must also be 20×20, de-rate to roughly 45 A burst. The §4 model scales with board area, so you can recompute.

## 1. Bridge Operating Model

Trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM:

- At any instant, one phase-leg is PWM'd (HS and LS alternating) and one leg has its LS held on.
- Current path during the **on** interval: HS_a → motor → LS_b.
- Current path during the **off** interval: LS_a (sync rect) → motor → LS_b.
- Therefore **two FETs carry I_phase at all times**, regardless of duty D.

Ripple: the motor inductance (L_ph ≈ 10–30 µH for 2207-class motors) at 48 kHz keeps ΔI/I below about 10%. That adds less than 0.1% to the RMS value, using I_rms² = I²(1 + (ΔI/I)²/12), so it is neglected.

## 2. Conduction Losses (80 A Burst)

Per conducting FET:

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds(on),25} \cdot k_T = 80^2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{11.52\ W}$$

Split by duty D (the HS/LS sharing within the PWM'd leg):

- **HS_a:** P = D · 11.52 W
- **LS_a:** P = (1−D) · 11.52 W
- **LS_b:** P = 11.52 W (full time). **This is the hot spot.**

Per channel (always two FETs in path):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{ds,hot} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Commutation rotates the "full-time" role across all six FETs. Averaged over an electrical revolution, each FET sees roughly 23.0/6 × (fraction of time it is in the path) ≈ 3.8 W average. However, the electrical period at burst is about 0.5–1 ms, far shorter than the FET thermal time constant (~10–50 ms). **The junction therefore tracks the averaged value plus a small ripple**, which is favorable.

### 2.1 Copper and passive I²R (often forgotten)

| Path | R estimate | Loss @ 80 A/ch |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz × 8 L, ~10 mm) | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W/ch |
| Battery bus (4 × 80 = 320 A peak, theoretical) | ~0.15 mΩ | 15 W total |
| Current-sense shunt (0.25 mΩ, total-current) | 0.25 mΩ | 25 W @ 320 A (**unacceptable**) |

Do not use a single total-current shunt at 320 A. Use either:
- a 0.1 mΩ shunt, rated for 10 W pulse, or
- a Hall sensor (e.g., ACS770 class) off-board.

Realistically, all four motors at 80 A simultaneously equals about 8 kW on 6S. Size the bus for that, or accept that the battery/XT90 limits it first.
