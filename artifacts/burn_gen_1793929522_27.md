# STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #10)

**Scope:** This is an architecture-level reference model. All MOSFET parameters are representative 40 V-class values, not tied to a specific part. Substitute datasheet values before layout. The netlists are design-intent connectivity for schematic capture. They are not fab-ready and require EE review, a DRC/ERC pass, and bench validation.

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings (Read First)

1. **80 A burst on a 20×20 mm nano stack is thermally marginal.** Section 2 shows that roughly 90 W of board dissipation is reached at 4×80 A. On a nano footprint this is sustainable only for about 2–4 s, with about 25–30 A per channel continuous. **The "80 A" figure is defensible only as a time-limited burst rating, and the burst duration must be specified alongside it.** If true 80 A capability is required, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC with 5×6 mm FETs.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** Each ESC channel, or one shared MCU in a 4-in-1, runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on a small MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 over 4 signal lines. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware setting, not an H743 timer function.
3. **Co-locating the IMU with 90 W of FETs** causes gyro thermal drift and EMI coupling. Section 5 lists the required mitigations.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology assumptions
- **Motor drive:** 3-phase BLDC, trapezoidal six-step commutation.
- **PWM scheme:** synchronous (complementary) PWM, the AM32 default.
- **Conduction path:** at any instant two phases conduct and one floats. Current path: battery+ → high-side FET (phase A, duty D) → motor → low-side FET (phase B, 100%) → battery−. During the PWM off-time, A's low-side FET carries freewheel current for the remaining (1−D).

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,\big[\underbrace{D}_{HS_A} + \underbrace{(1-D)}_{LS_A\ sync} + \underbrace{1}_{LS_B}\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle under synchronous rectification. It is a useful property: worst-case conduction loss does not depend on throttle position for a given phase current.

**Ripple correction.** Motor-current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp}=\frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example values: 6S full charge (25.2 V), D = 0.5, back-EMF ≈ 12 V, and L_ph = 10 µH (typical 2207 motor phase-to-phase / 2).

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{13.2\cdot 0.5}{10\mu\cdot 48k}=13.75\,\text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; \Delta I^2/12 = 15.8\,\text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against 6400 A² (0.25%). **At 48 kHz, ripple can be ignored in the conduction term.** This is one benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$
