# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Electrical, Thermal and DShot Timer Model

Before the derivations, three architecture constraints shape everything that follows.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32). The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1 and TIM8). That is enough for two 3-phase bridges, not four, and ArduPilot has no in-core BLDC commutation anyway.

**2. "80A burst" and "nano" are in tension.** On a 20×20 mm board, 4×80A is a transient rating of a few seconds at most, set by board heat capacity rather than MOSFET limits (see §3). I model a 30.5×30.5 mm stacked ESC and a 20×20 mm variant, and flag where the 20×20 variant fails.

**3. All component values are stated assumptions.** Substitute real datasheet values (R_DS(on) vs. T_j curve, Q_rr, Z_th(j-c)) before tape-out. The model is parameterized so this is mechanical.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel, 6 FETs)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In any 60° commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{DS,25}$$

With R = 0.975 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.975\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{12.5\ W}$$

The copper path adds:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The copper loss is distributed across the board and is not trivial. The design implications are 2 oz or heavier copper, and stitched inner-layer power planes.

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) rises with temperature and temperature rises with loss, so solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This converges only if R_th · 2I² R_25 α < 1. With α ≈ 0.006 /K, that requires an effective R_th < 1/(2·6400·0.65e-3·0.006) ≈ **20 K/W per channel**. This is easily met at the FET level. It is only violated if the board saturates.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switch V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48k | 1.69 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs, dissipated in the driver plus R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.048 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.13 W** |

Commutation events at the electrical frequency (~1–3 kHz) add under 0.1 W and are neglected.

The overlap term dominates. Each 5 ns cut from t_r + t_f saves about 0.24 W per motor, so a stronger gate drive is the main switching-loss lever. Lowering f_sw to 24 kHz halves P_sw but raises current ripple and acoustic noise; 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance racing motors.

### 2.3 Per-motor and total burst loss

$$P_{motor} = 12.5 + 2.13 + 3.2 \approx \mathbf{17.8\ W}$$

$$P_{ESC,total} = 4 \times 17.8 \approx \mathbf{71\ W}\ \text{(plus ~1 W for the 4 ESC MCUs, LDOs and drivers)}$$

At 80A × 4 = 320A from 25.2 V (~8 kW electrical, assuming high duty), this is about 99% efficiency. The absolute heat is the problem, not the percentage.

### 2.4 Per-FET loss (for junction temperature)

Each FET conducts for one-third of the electrical cycle, so the 6 FETs share the conduction loss. The switching loss falls on whichever high-side FET is active, averaged over three high-side FETs.

- Conduction per FET: 12.5 / 6 ≈ **2.1 W**
- Switching per high-side FET: 2.1 / 3 ≈ **0.7 W**
- Worst-case FET (high side): **≈ 2.8 W average**
- Within a sector, the instantaneous load on the worst FET reaches about I²R + P_sw ≈ 8.4 W for 1/6 of the electrical period.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer approximation)

```
 P_FET ─► [Rth_jc 1.2 K/W] ─► T_case ─► [Rth_c-pcb 2–4 K/W per FET via-array] ─► T_board
                                                                                    │
                                     C_board (lumped) ── [Rth_b-a] ── T_ambient (+ prop wash)
```

### 3.2 Junction rise above board

The junction time constant is milliseconds, so it reaches quasi-steady state immediately:

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}b} \approx 2.8\ \text{W} \times (1.2 + 3) \approx \mathbf{12\ K}$$

Instantaneous ripple peaks add about 5 K on top. The FET itself is not the limit.

### 3.3 Board thermal mass (the actual limit)

$$C_{board} = \sum m_i c_i$$

| Variant | Mass breakdown | C_board |
|---|---|---|
| 30.5×30.5, 6-layer 2 oz, with aluminum heat spreader | PCB ~6 g (FR4/Cu mix, ~0.75 J/g·K) + 4 g Al (0.9 J/g·K) | ≈ 8.1 J/K |
| 20×20, 6-layer 2 oz, no spreader | PCB ~2.6 g | ≈ 2.0 J/K |

**Adiabatic heating rate at 72 W:**

- 30.5 mm: dT/dt ≈ **8.9 K/s**
- 20 mm: dT/dt ≈ **36 K/s**

**Cooling.** With R_th(b-a) set by prop wash, an effective h ≈ 100 W/m²K over about 2×(30.5 mm)² gives R_th ≈ 5.4 K/W. Continuous dissipation capacity is then only about **10–15 W**, which corresponds to about 30A continuous per motor (P ∝ I²).

**Burst limit** (from a 50 °C board, with a 110 °C board limit to keep T_j < 125 °C):

$$t_{burst} = C\,R\,\ln\!\left(\frac{P R - (T_0 - T_a)}{P R - (T_{max} - T_a)}\right)$$
