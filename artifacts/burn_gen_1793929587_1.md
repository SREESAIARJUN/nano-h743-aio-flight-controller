# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Architecture, Loss Model, and DShot Allocation

This is a reference design and first-order analysis. Silicon parameters are representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm power-MOSFET values, not a specific part. Before committing to layout, replace them with your selected part's datasheet values and validate on hardware.

---

## 0. Architectural Clarification

On a 4-in-1 AIO, the H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. The partitioning is:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot. Generates DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC control (×4) | AT32F421 / STM32G071 class MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 | Commutation and **48 kHz** complementary PWM. |
| Gate drive (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class) | Bootstrap high-side drive and dead time. |
| Power stage (×4) | 6 × N-MOSFET, 40 V | Three half-bridges per motor (24 FETs total). |

The 48 kHz switching loss therefore belongs to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA and timer problem belongs to the H743 domain.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.5 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 2.27\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Current path

Assume six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At every instant, two FETs are in series with the motor current:

- the static FET of the non-PWM phase, and
- either the high-side or low-side FET of the PWM phase, depending on duty.

The duty cycle D cancels out of the total, giving:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.27\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{29.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 2.3 Self-consistency (thermal runaway check)

Because R_DS rises with T_j, solve the coupled system:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j)$$

The system is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_\theta < 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1$$

Per FET (approximately one-third duty share) with R_θ ≈ 5 K/W:

$$\tfrac{1}{3}\times 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3}\times0.006\times5 = 0.19 < 1$$

The system is stable, with margin.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM half-bridge switches.

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W per motor** |

### 3.1 Total loss per motor

$$P_{motor}(I) = 2R_{DS}I^2 + k_{sw}I,\qquad k_{sw}\approx 0.0276\ \text{W/A}$$

At I = 80 A:

- **P_motor ≈ 31.3 W**
- **P_total (all 4 motors) ≈ 125 W**
- Average per FET ≈ 31.3 / 6 ≈ **5.2 W**

### 3.2 f_sw trade-off

Switching loss is only about 7 % of the total at 80 A. Dropping to 24 kHz saves roughly 1 W per motor but increases current ripple and acoustic noise. At burst current, **conduction loss dominates**, so R_DS(on) and copper are the levers that matter.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board (per FET)

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = P_{FET}(R_{\theta jc} + R_{\theta,via}) = 5.2 \times (1.2 + 4.0) \approx \mathbf{27\ K}$$

R_θ,via assumes a via array of about 20 vias (0.3 mm drill, filled) under the drain pad.

### 4.2 Board thermal mass (adiabatic burst)

| Element | Mass | c_p | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper (6 × 70 µm × 650 mm²) | 2.45 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.94 J/K |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm) | 1.9 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 2.1 J/K |
| Components | — | — | ~1.0 J/K |
| **Total C_board** | | | **≈ 4 J/K** |

Board heating rate with all four motors at 80 A:

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} = \frac{125\ \text{W}}{4\ \text{J/K}} \approx 31\ \text{K/s}$$

From 40 °C board temperature to an 83 °C board limit (so that T_j ≈ 110 °C):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{83-40}{31} \approx \mathbf{1.4\ s}$$

With all four motors at 80 A simultaneously, the burst is limited to about 1.4 s. With one motor at 80 A, it extends to about 5.5 s.
