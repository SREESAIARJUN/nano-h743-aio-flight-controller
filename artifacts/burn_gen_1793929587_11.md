**Important architectural clarification:** the H743 does not commutate the motors. ArduPilot cannot run BLDC commutation loops. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to four local ESC MCUs, and each MCU drives one 3-phase bridge. Both live on the same PCB. This is how every production "AIO" with an H7 works, and it decouples flight-control timing from commutation timing.

**Form-factor reality check:** 24 MOSFETs plus 4 gate drivers plus 4 ESC MCUs plus the H743 is physically achievable on a 30.5×30.5 mm mount (≈ 36×36 mm board) with stacked or two-sided placement. It is not achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" mount at 80 A. The thermal math in §3 assumes the 30.5 mm class. At 20×20 mm, divide the burst duration by roughly 2.3, the ratio of board areas.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET is ON for duty D. The low-side FET carries the freewheel current for (1 − D) minus dead time. Synchronous rectification means the channel resistance, not the body diode, carries the current outside dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is ON for the full sector.
- **Floating phase:** carries no current; this is where the BEMF sensing happens.

The motor phase current I_ph flows through exactly two channel resistances at all times, outside dead time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left[ D \cdot R_{HS} + (1-D) \cdot R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] \approx 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This assumes identical high- and low-side FETs.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25} \cdot \left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}, \quad \alpha \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3 \text{ (silicon trench)}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives a factor of about 1.5–1.6. I use **1.5 mΩ hot** for design. Iterate this value with the thermal result in §3; the solution converges in 2–3 passes.
