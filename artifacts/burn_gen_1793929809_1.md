## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. In any production 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU that runs the commutation firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32) and generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

```
 STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (×4)──► 4× AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q ──► 24× N-MOSFET ──► motors
        ▲                                                                                     │
        └──────────── ADC: VBAT divider, shunt current amp ◄──────────────────────────────────┘
```

**All numbers below depend on these assumed parameters.** Substitute your actual BOM datasheet values before you sign off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, two phases carry current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET stays fully on.

Instantaneous conduction loss per channel:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, because synchronous rectification always presents one R_DS(on) in the PWM leg.

$$
R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{25} = 1.6 \times 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.08\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.08\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.6\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

$$
P_{cond,PCB} = I^2 R_{pcb} = 6400 \times 1.0\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.4\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

The PCB loss is spread over copper, not concentrated at the junctions.

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Each high-side FET only conducts with duty D inside that window.

- The worst-case FET is a low-side device. It carries the full I²R loss (13.3 W) during its 120° window.
- Its average loss over the electrical cycle is about 4.4–5 W.
- The electrical frequency is roughly 1–3 kHz, i.e., a period of about 0.3–1 ms. The die thermal time constant is on the order of ms, so the junction partially averages these pulses. Use the instantaneous 13.3 W for ripple checks and the average for steady rise.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap

Switching time per edge:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{11\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 11\,\text{ns}
$$

Overlap loss:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time, then is recovered by high-side turn-on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}
$$

The induced extra turn-on current spike is captured to first order by the overlap term. Add 20–50 % margin if the measured Q_rr at your di/dt is higher.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This term is larger than most designers expect. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but only if the shoot-through margin is validated on the scope.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

There are two switching FETs per channel. This power is mostly dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$
P_g = 2 \times Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$
P_{sw,total} \approx 1.06 + 0.073 + 0.024 + 0.61 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{1.82\ W\ per\ channel}
$$
