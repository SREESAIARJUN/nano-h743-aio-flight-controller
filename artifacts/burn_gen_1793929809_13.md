# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching Loss, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

**Job #20, Rev A (engineering draft)**

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings to Resolve First

Four findings change the design before any detailed math matters.

1. **Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. That gives 1× H743 plus 4× ESC MCUs. Everything below assumes this split.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor.**
   - On a 20×20 mm board this is not physically achievable. 24 MOSFETs rated for 80A pulses need 5×6 mm or at least 3.3×3.3 mm packages, plus copper area.
   - The minimum viable footprint is **30.5×30.5 mm** with 3.3×3.3 mm FETs, and even then only for bursts of a few seconds (see §3).
   - I treat "80A" as **per-channel peak phase current**, not battery current.

3. **Battery current.** Battery current equals duty × phase current (I_bat = D·I_ph). Four channels at 80A and full duty would draw **320A** from the pack. That far exceeds an XT60 (~60A continuous) and any nano-class pack. Realistic simultaneous burst is ~120–160A total battery current. The power entry and bus design use that figure.

4. **Assumptions to verify.** All device parameters below are representative values for 40V, ~1 mΩ-class trench MOSFETs. Replace them with the datasheet values of the FET you actually select, and validate against IR thermography on a prototype.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:
- **PWM on-time:** one high-side FET plus the opposite phase's low-side FET.
- **PWM off-time:** two low-side FETs (one of them synchronous).

Total bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

**Distribution across devices.** Each phase is active for 120° of every 360°:
- **High-side FET:** conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period at duty D, so $P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R$.
- **Low-side FET:** carries the fixed (non-PWM) conduction plus the complementary interval, so $P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1 + (1-D))\, I^2 R$ on average.

Summed over all six devices, these terms give back $2I^2R$.

**Electro-thermal fixed point at 80A.** Because R_ds(on) depends on junction temperature, solve iteratively:

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_ds(on) | P_cond,bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 2 | 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | 23.8 W |
| 3 (converged) | ~150 °C | 2.03 mΩ | **26.0 W** |

**Copper losses.** Add the phase and bus copper. Assume about 0.3 mΩ effective path resistance at 2 oz, inner and outer layers paralleled:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\,\text{W}$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated half-bridge hard-switches, once on and once off per PWM cycle.

**Gate currents:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3}{6.2} = 1.13\,\text{A} \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{6.2} = 0.48\,\text{A}$$

**Voltage/current crossover times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.9\,\text{ns} \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 20.7\,\text{ns}$$

**Loss components:**

| Mechanism | Expression | Value @ 80A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·29.6 ns·48k | 1.43 W |
| Body diode reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.073 W |
| Output capacitance (C_oss) | Q_oss·V·f_sw (hard-switched charge loss) | 0.048 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Notes:**
- The turn-off asymmetry dominates. Adding a turn-off diode with R_off ≈ 2 Ω cuts the overlap loss by about 35%.
- Shortening dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W. Only do this after confirming shoot-through margin with the scope.
- At 48 kHz, switching loss is under 10% of total, so **conduction dominates.** That justifies spending board area on R_ds(on) and copper rather than on faster switching.
