# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three problems in the brief would cause a design failure if left unaddressed.

| Issue | Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 does not commutate the motors** | ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It cannot run BLDC commutation, and the H743 has no spare timer or ADC budget for 4 FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz. | Use a split architecture. The H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. |
| **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board** | Board-level dissipation reaches about 70 W (shown in §2–§3). A 20×20 board has about 2.5 J/K of heat capacity, so it overheats in a few seconds. | Specify burst duration explicitly. The recommended form factor is 30.5×30.5 mm. If 20×20 is mandatory, derate to about 40 A burst / 25 A continuous. |
| **Part numbers** | I cannot guarantee current datasheet values or availability. | All component values below are **representative placeholders**. Substitute your chosen parts' datasheet parameters into the equations. |

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

Assumed example FET: 40 V, R_DS,25 = 0.70 mΩ (5×6 or 3.3×3.3 package).

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.006, the multiplier is 1.6, giving **R = 1.12 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry the phase current I:

- **Low-commutated leg:** the LS FET is on 100%.
- **PWM leg:** the HS FET is on for D, and the LS FET (synchronous rectifier) is on for 1−D.

Including PWM ripple ΔI (triangular):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Total bridge conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, because the HS and LS FETs share the PWM leg.

Per-FET time averages over one electrical cycle (each leg is active for 1/3 of the cycle in each polarity):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R$$

The **low-side FETs are the hottest** at low duty. Place them on the best copper.

### 2.3 Numerical result (I = 80 A, ΔI = 10 A)

- I_rms² = 6400 + 8.3 = 6408 A²
- P_cond,bridge = 2 × 6408 × 1.12 mΩ = **14.35 W per motor**
- Worst LS FET at D = 0.5: (1/3)(1.5)(6400)(1.12e-3) = **3.58 W**

### 2.4 PCB copper and shunt losses (often ignored, usually significant)

$$R_{sq}(\text{1 oz}) \approx 0.49\ \mathrm{m\Omega/sq},\quad R_{sq}(\text{2 oz}) \approx 0.25\ \mathrm{m\Omega/sq}\ (25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}),\quad \times[1+0.0039(T-25)]$$

Assume the current path (VBAT → HS → phase pad → motor → phase pad → LS → GND) has an effective R_Cu of about 0.30 mΩ at 100 °C. This assumes multiple paralleled 2 oz layers.

- P_Cu = 80² × 0.30 mΩ = **1.92 W per motor**
- Shunt (if per-motor, 0.25 mΩ): **1.6 W**
- Prefer a single common shunt or a Hall sensor on the VBAT input.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz, V_bus = 25.2 V)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The loss terms are:

| Term | Equation | Assumed values | Loss / motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | t_r = t_f = 15 ns (Q_sw = 15 nC / I_g = 1 A) | 0.5·25.2·80·30e-9·48e3 = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | V_F = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | Q_rr = 50 nC | **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | Q_oss = 40 nC | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 FETs | Q_g = 60 nC, 10 V | **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | | **≈ 2.23 W** |

Ringing sensitivity: overlap loss scales with t_sw. Loop inductance L_loop raises overshoot by ΔV = L·di/dt. With 2 nH and di/dt = 80 A/15 ns, ΔV ≈ 10.7 V, giving a peak of about 36 V on 40 V FETs.

- This is marginal. Either use 60 V FETs for 6S or slow t_sw to about 25 ns.
- Slowing to 25 ns costs about 1 W more of switching loss.
