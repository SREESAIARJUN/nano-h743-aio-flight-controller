# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #26): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture Note

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (for example AT32F421 or EFM8BB21 running AM32 or BLHeli_S/Bluejay). Each one drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and speaks bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The model below follows that partition:

```
[H743 FC] --4x BDShot600--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [4x 3-ph gate driver] --> [4x 6-FET bridge] --> motors
```

All component values below are **design assumptions** that you must confirm against the datasheets of the parts you actually select. Each formula is written so you can substitute real values.

## 2. Conduction Losses at 80 A

### 2.1 Topology Model

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification, two phase legs are active at every instant:

- The PWM leg alternates current between its high-side FET (on-time D) and its low-side FET (off-time 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The return leg's low-side FET conducts for 100% of the 60° sector.

As a result, the motor current always flows through exactly **two R_ds(on)** in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 2.2 Electrothermal Solution

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the loss, so the solution is found by iteration:

1. Assume T_j.
2. Compute R_ds(T_j).
3. Compute P_cond.
4. Compute the new T_j from the thermal network in §4.
5. Repeat until T_j converges.

At the design limit of T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 85) = 2.20\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.20\text{m} = 28.2\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{m} = 1.6\ \text{W}
$$

**Per-FET RMS current.** In 6-step operation, each phase position carries current for 120° of the electrical cycle. So the loss averaged over one electrical revolution is 28.2 W / 6 ≈ **4.7 W per FET**.

The instantaneous loss in the FET that is conducting is much higher. For example, the return-leg low-side FET dissipates I²R = 14.1 W during its sector. The FET thermal time constant (around 10 ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM. Therefore, check **Z_th,jc(t_p)** at the minimum operating eRPM, not just the average.

**Paralleling option.** Using 2 FETs per position (48 FETs) halves R_ds, giving P_cond,FET ≈ 14.1 W. This is usually not feasible on a nano footprint.
