**Red flags to settle up front:**
1. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 nano board is physically aggressive.** The model below shows it is survivable for a few seconds at most, not as a sustained rating.
2. **Real 3×3 mm FETs that fit nano boards are typically 2–3 mΩ, not 1 mΩ.** Re-run the numbers with your actual part. Losses scale linearly with R_ds.
3. **An H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) is very tight on a 20×20 board.** Consider the TFBGA100 (8×8 mm), or a stacked FC/ESC pair.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Instantaneous conduction path
In 6-step drive, the phase current I_ph always flows through exactly two conducting devices: one in the PWM leg (HS during D, LS synchronous rectification during 1−D) and the static LS of the return leg.

$$P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 74 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W | 82 W |

- **Per-FET average:** 6 FETs share the load, so ≈ 18.6 / 6 ≈ 3.1 W average per FET.
- **Per-FET peak:** about 9.3 W while conducting.
- **Why averaging is valid:** the die thermal time constant (~ms) is comparable to or longer than the electrical period at flight RPM.
