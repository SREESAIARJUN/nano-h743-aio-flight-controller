## 0. Scope Corrections

These architectural constraints must be settled before any math.

1. **ArduPilot does not commutate motors.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each motor's 6-step or FOC commutation runs on a dedicated ESC MCU. This design uses four AT32F421 running AM32, which is common and supports bi-directional DShot (BDShot).
   - **Option A (selected):** H743 FC plus 4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs on one PCB stack.
   - **Option B:** H743 performing commutation. This is not supportable on ArduPilot and is rejected.
2. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** On a nano (20×20 mm) or even a 30.5×30.5 mm board, steady-state dissipation at 80 A/motor is physically impossible (§3). The deliverable is therefore a **time-limited burst model**.
3. **The device parameters are design assumptions, not a specific datasheet.** Substitute your selected MOSFET's datasheet values into the equations. The model structure is what matters.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in Synchronous 6-Step Commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** The high-side (HS) MOSFET is on for duty fraction D. The low-side (LS) MOSFET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D). Diode conduction occurs during dead time.
- **Return leg:** The LS MOSFET is on continuously.

The motor current therefore always passes through exactly two channel resistances in series, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D \cdot R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,return} \right] = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 0.80\,(1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.106 \text{ mΩ} \approx 1.1 \text{ mΩ}
$$

### 1.3 Per-Motor Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.1\times10^{-3} = 14.1 \text{ W}
$$

### 1.4 PCB Copper Loss

The current path traverses two phase paths:

$$
P_{pcb} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{pcb} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.30\times10^{-3} = 3.84 \text{ W}
$$

> Copper loss is roughly 27% of FET conduction loss. **Phase copper is a first-order design variable on a nano board, not a parasitic.**

### 1.5 Electro-Thermal Positive Feedback

Because R_DS rises with T_j, the junction temperature must be solved by iteration:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + R_{\theta JC} \cdot P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

The loop converges only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta JC} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta JC} < 1
$$

For a single conducting FET: 6400 × 0.8×10⁻³ × 0.0045 × 1.2 ≈ 0.028 ≪ 1. **The junction-level loop is stable. Runaway, if it occurs, happens at board level (R_θBA), not at the junction.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return-leg LS MOSFET commutates at electrical frequency, not f_sw, so its switching loss is negligible.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap Loss (PWM-Leg High Side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.45 \text{ W}
$$

To sanity-check the overlap time, estimate t_sw ≈ Q_gd / I_g. For a gate driver delivering about 1 A at the Miller plateau, this gives roughly 10 ns per edge before layout inductance.
