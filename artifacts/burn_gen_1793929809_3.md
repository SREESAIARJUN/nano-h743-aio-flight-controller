# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Architecture Correction

**Architecture correction:** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071.

- The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600.
- The models below follow that split.

> **Engineering reality check:** No nano-class 4-in-1 can sustain 80 A per motor. The math below shows 80 A is a **thermal-capacitance-limited burst of about 3 s**. The continuous rating is about 30 A. Spec the product that way.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification.

- At any instant, two phases carry I_ph: one high-side path and one low-side path.
- During t_on, the PWM leg's high-side FET conducts.
- During t_off, the same leg's low-side FET conducts. The diode only conducts during dead time.

As a result, exactly two R_DS(on) are in series at all times, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the six FETs carries the loss for an average share of 1/6:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

The worst-case instantaneous load at low eRPM is different. At standstill or near-stall, current sits in one FET pair. Use P = I²·R_DS on a single FET for stall analysis.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per ESC | P per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.85 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** | 3.10 W |

### 1.4 Additional copper loss

For a 2 oz trace, about 0.5 mΩ per phase path including vias:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{trace} \approx 2(6400)(0.25\text{ m}\Omega) = 3.2\text{ W}$$

This assumes 0.25 mΩ effective per path.
