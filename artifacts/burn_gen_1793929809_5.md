**Critical architecture flags:**

1. **ArduPilot does not run the ESC.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each motor is commutated by a separate ESC MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 never touches gate drivers. The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **An 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is physically marginal.** The calculation below shows roughly 70 W of dissipation at full burst. That exceeds what a nano footprint can sink for more than a few seconds. Small-format AIOs typically use 3.3×3.3 mm dual or half-bridge FETs with higher R_DS(on) than assumed here, which would make the losses worse. Treat 80 A as a **≤ 2–5 s transient rating** or move to a 30×30 mm format.
3. All values are **first-order engineering estimates**. Validate with the actual FET's transient thermal impedance curve (Z_thJC) and a thermal-camera bring-up test.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET conducts continuously.
- **Phase C:** floating (BEMF sense).

So **exactly two FET channels are in series with the motor current at every instant** (dead time excluded). This holds regardless of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}$$

At 125 °C:
$$R_{DS(on)}(125) = 0.7 \times 1.6 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_J) + I^2 R_{Cu} + I^2 R_{shunt}$$

**Silicon only:**
$$P_{cond,Si} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.12\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 2.24\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

**Copper and shunt.** Assume 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner pours, giving R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for the phase-plus-return path. Assume a shared 0.25 mΩ battery shunt that sees the total current.

- Copper per motor: 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**
- Shunt: (4 × 80)² × 0.25 mΩ = 102400 × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**

The shunt figure would only occur if all four motors drew 80 A simultaneously. It also argues for a ≤ 0.1 mΩ shunt (≈ 10 W), or for per-phase low-side sensing.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (for junction-temperature analysis)

Over one electrical revolution, each leg spends:
- 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg (HS: D, LS: 1−D),
- 1/3 as the return leg (LS on 100%),
- 1/3 floating.

Average conduction loss per FET over an electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$$

At burst (D ≈ 0.95, I²R = 6400 × 1.12 mΩ = 7.17 W):
- P_HS ≈ 2.27 W
- **P_LS ≈ 2.51 W** (the low side is the conduction-limited device)

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. There is one turn-on and one turn-off per period.

### 2.1 Switching times from gate charge

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drv}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}, \quad t_f \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

Add roughly 5 ns each for the Q_gs2 interval. Use t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns.

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse-recovery loss

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time, then the high side turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Add roughly the same again inside the high-side FET from the recovery current spike.

### 2.4 Output-capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 60\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 2.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is significant. Cutting dead time to 100 ns (if the driver and FET allow it) saves about 0.6 W per motor.

### 2.6 Gate-drive loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and the gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_{gate} = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 100\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.096\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Switching subtotal per motor

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.94 + 0.12 + 0.04 + 1.23 \approx \mathbf{3.3\ W}$$

The switching loss is shared over three legs
