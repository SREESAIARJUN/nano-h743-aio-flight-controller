## 0. Architecture Decision

Before the math, one architectural point needs settling. The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. The standard and robust partition is:

| Function | Device | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, EKF, scheduling | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) or VIH6 (TFBGA100) | ArduPilot target, 480 MHz, DMAMUX |
| Motor commutation, 48 kHz PWM, back-EMF sensing | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32, or 1× EFM8/AT32 per motor | Hard real-time 6-step with comparator zero-cross detection |
| Gate drive | 3-phase driver per motor (e.g. FD6288Q class, ~1.5 A/1.8 A) | Bootstrap high-side, integrated dead time |
| Link | Bi-directional DShot600, 1 wire per motor | eRPM telemetry for ArduPilot harmonic notch |

The H743 sees each ESC only as a DShot endpoint. The 48 kHz switching losses belong to the ESC MCU's PWM.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 75) = **1.375 mΩ (typ)**, or 1.79 mΩ (max).

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

In 6-step synchronous PWM, current always flows through two channels in series. During the on-time these are high-side x and low-side y. During the off-time, the current freewheels through the synchronous low-side x and low-side y. The loss is therefore independent of D to first order:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.375\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.6\ W}\ \text{(typ)},\quad 22.9\text{ W (max } R_{ds})$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg is active 2/3 of the electrical period (4 of 6 sectors). Split by device:

- **High-side FET:** conducts D × (1/3) of the period:
  $$P_{HS} = I^2 R\,\tfrac{D}{3} = 6400 \times 1.375\text{m} \times 0.267 = 2.35\text{ W}$$
- **Low-side FET:** conducts (1/3) as the return path plus (1−D)(1/3) as the synchronous rectifier:
  $$P_{LS} = I^2 R\,\tfrac{1+(1-D)}{3} = 6400 \times 1.375\text{m} \times 0.40 = 3.52\text{ W}$$

Check: 3 × (2.35 + 3.52) = 17.6 W ✓

**Conclusion:** the low-side FETs are the thermal hot spot. Place them on the best copper.

### 2.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

Because R_ds rises with temperature and loss rises with R_ds, solve the coupled pair:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,R_{th}$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + P_{25}R_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - P_{25}R_{th}\,\alpha}$$

This system has a thermal-runaway condition at **P₂₅ · R_th · α ≥ 1**. For the low-side FET, P₂₅ = 2.56 W. Runaway would require R_th ≥ 78 K/W, so the static case is stable. The real limit is the board's thermal capacitance (Section 4).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switching at a time, with one hard-switched transition pair per cycle.

| Loss term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 25 ns × 48 k | **1.21 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw = 40 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.06 W (dissipated mostly in driver and gate resistors) |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 2.0 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 41.6\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 24 to 48 kHz adds about 1 W per motor. Shaving dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns recovers about 0.3 W.

## 4. Thermal Model and the Feasibility Problem

### 4.1 Steady state is impossible on a nano board

A 20×20 mm board has about 8 cm² two-sided area. With heavy prop-wash convection (h ≈ 100–150 W/m²K):

$$R_{th,board\text{-}air} \approx \frac{1}{h A} \approx \frac{1}{125 \times 8\times10^{-4}} \approx 10\ \text{K/W}$$

At 85 W this implies a steady-state rise of about 850 K. **80 A continuous on all four motors is physically impossible.** Only a time-limited burst is viable.
