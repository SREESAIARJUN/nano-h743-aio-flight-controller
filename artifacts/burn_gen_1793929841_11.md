## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three issues affect every calculation below, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCUs (AT32F421 in this design). The DShot analysis applies to the H743.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a thermal-capacitance problem, not a steady-state rating.** The model below shows roughly 100 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. The design can survive this only for about 0.5–1.5 s, and only from a cool start. I quantify this rather than assume it.
3. **At 80 A the duty cycle is usually near 100%,** so switching loss collapses and conduction dominates. Worst-case switching loss occurs at mid-duty with high current, for example punch-outs from a hover throttle setting. Both corners are modeled.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step / trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

In each 60° sector:
- **PWM leg:** HS on for D, synchronous LS on for (1–D).
- **Low leg:** LS fully on.
- **Third leg:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

Each phase spends 120° (1/3 of the electrical period) as the PWM leg, 120° as the low leg, and 120° floating.
