- **Battery current:** 4 × 80 A at high duty is roughly 320 A from the battery, about 8 kW. An XT60 cannot carry this, and a 30.5 mm board cannot dissipate the losses.

- **Specification language:** "80 A burst" must be defined as a time-limited rating with firmware thermal derating. Sections 1–3 prove this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds is about 1.6 mΩ. This is the design value.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal operation with complementary PWM

Consider one commutation step (phase A high, phase B low, C floating) with phase current I and duty D:

- Q_AH conducts for D·T. Q_AL synchronously freewheels for (1−D)·T.
- Q_BL conducts for 100% of the step.

So two FET channels are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D, which is why complementary PWM is used. Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}$$
