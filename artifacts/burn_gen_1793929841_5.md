# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #39): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

Several points in the request need clarifying before the math. Otherwise the numbers will be internally consistent but physically wrong.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU, not the ESC MCU.** The H743 runs ArduPilot. The 48 kHz commutation PWM comes from four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware), such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071. One H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/trapezoidal loops *and* ArduPilot. This document models both sides.
2. **The "nano" form factor and 80 A are in tension.** A single 20×20 mm board cannot hold an H743 (TFBGA100, 8×8 mm), an IMU, regulators, 24 power MOSFETs and 4 ESC MCUs. The baseline here is a **two-board 20×20 mm stack**: FC on top, 4-in-1 ESC below. The thermal analysis (§3) shows that 80 A is a **≤1 s burst rating**, not a sustained one.
3. **Supply voltage:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum. The FETs are 40 V class, which leaves thin margin; §2.6 covers the 60 V option.
4. **Reference MOSFET parameters** (generic 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class; substitute datasheet values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @25 °C, V_GS=10 V (max) | R₀ | 1.7 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K (≈1.45× at 125 °C) |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 35 nC |
| Gate-drain charge | Q_gd | 8 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_SD | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-PCB thermal resistance | R_θJB | 2.5 K/W |

5. **"80 A burst"** means 80 A **phase (motor) current** per ESC channel, which is the conventional ESC rating.

## 1. Power-Stage Topology per Channel

Each channel is a 3-phase half-bridge (6 FETs) with complementary (synchronous) PWM and trapezoidal 6-step commutation.

During any 60° commutation sector, phase X is PWM-switched and phase Y is held low:

- **PWM ON (duty D):** current flows V_bus → HS_X → motor → LS_Y → GND
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_X (synchronous rectification, after dead time) → motor → LS_Y

So **two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current**, independent of duty. This simplifies the conduction model.

The relationship between battery and phase current is:

$$I_{dc} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

80 A of phase current at D = 0.6 draws about 48 A per channel from the battery.

## 2. Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

### 2.1 Conduction Loss with Temperature Feedback

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With trapezoidal drive and moderate motor inductance, the current ripple is small, so I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph.

Because R_DS depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P, the operating point must be solved by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\big(T_j^{(k)}\big)\cdot R_{\theta JB}$$

The loss is shared unevenly across FETs:

| Device | Conduction share |
|---|---|
| LS_Y (held on) | I² R for 100% of the sector |
| HS_X | I² R · D |
| LS_X | I² R · (1−D) |

Averaged over 6 sectors, each of the 6 FETs carries 1/3 of the total, but the **instantaneous worst-case FET (LS_Y) carries I²R continuously for 1/3 of the electrical cycle**. For the bursts considered here (> 1 electrical period), use the average for board heating and the per-sector peak for junction rise.

**Example at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**

- R_DS = 1.7 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.35 mΩ**
- P_cond = 80² × 2 × 2.35 mΩ = **30.1 W per channel**

### 2.2 Switching (V-I Overlap) Loss

Hard switching applies on the HS_X edges. With gate driver source/sink resistance R_g,tot and plateau voltage V_pl:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_{g,on}}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_{g,off}}{V_{pl}}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Using V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3.5 V, R_g,on = 10 Ω and R_g,off = 5 Ω:

- t_r ≈ 8 nC·10/6.5 = **12.3 ns**
- t_f ≈ 8 nC·5/3.5 = **11.4 ns**

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 23.7\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.15\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery, Output Capacitance, and Gate Drive

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = 30n × 25.2 × 48k | 0.036 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 2 × 35n × 10 × 48k | 0.034 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |

At this current level, Q_rr is small but **scales with di/dt**. Watch it if you cut R_g,on to speed up edges.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$$

With t_dt = 200 ns (a typical AM32 setting at this gate charge):

$$P_{dt} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
