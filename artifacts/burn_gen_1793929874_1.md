# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Stack (Job #55): Design Model and Feasibility Analysis

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three points need correcting before any numbers are meaningful:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and *emits* DShot600. Commutation and the 48 kHz PWM run on four separate ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/AT32 running BlueJay/BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware, not ArduPilot. The model below uses this split: an FC board (H743) plus an ESC board with 4 MCUs and 24 MOSFETs.
2. **80 A is a burst rating, not continuous.** The thermal model shows a "nano" 20×20 mm ESC cannot hold 80 A for more than about 1–2 s. Use a 30.5×30.5 mm format for any realistic 80 A burst claim.
3. **Component values are representative, not datasheet-verified.** Every value marked **[A]** is an assumption. Replace it with your selected MOSFET and driver datasheet values before release. The netlists are reference topologies, not a production-verified BOM.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per MOSFET, complementary PWM

In synchronous (complementary) PWM, one leg conducts continuously through either the high-side or low-side FET:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds,hot} D,\qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}(1-D)$$

$$P_{leg} = P_{HS}+P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

The leg loss is independent of duty cycle. This is why synchronous rectification matters.

### 2.2 Per motor channel, 6-step trapezoidal

At any instant, two of the three phases carry current, so two legs conduct:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,25}\,k_T$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.0\text{ m}\Omega)(1.4) = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

For FOC/sinusoidal drive, use $I_{rms,phase}$ across three legs: $P = 3 I_{rms}^2 R$. With $I_{rms} = I_{pk}/\sqrt2$, this gives about 1.5·I_pk²·R, slightly lower.

### 2.3 Copper loss

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{Cu} = 2(6400)(0.5\text{ m}\Omega) = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

This term is often ignored, and it is not negligible. Copper resistance also rises about 0.39%/K.

### 2.4 Worst single-FET instantaneous loss

$$P_{FET,pk} \approx I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 6400 \times 1.4\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{9.0\ W}$$

Commutation rotates this loss across the six FETs, so the time-averaged loss per FET is lower. The junction still sees the peak during each conduction interval (see §4.3).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs these losses; the other conducting leg is static during a sector.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance (two FETs per leg)
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 1.5\text{n}(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors)
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-channel switching total
$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W}$$

### 3.7 Design trade-off

At 80 A, switching loss is about 11% of conduction loss. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles the switching term (+1.35 W/ch). In exchange, it reduces current ripple, motor iron/copper ripple loss, and audible noise. Keep 48 kHz, but make dead time as short as the driver's propagation skew safely allows.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Total burst dissipation (all four channels at 80 A)

| Term | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 17.9 W | 71.6 W |
| Copper | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| **Total** | **27.0 W** | **≈108 W** |

### 4.2 Lumped board thermal capacitance

The model is $C_{th}\,\frac{dT}{dt} = P - hA(T-T_a)$.

| Board | Cu (6L, 2 oz) | FR4 1.6 mm | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30.5×30.5 mm | 3.4 g × 0.385 = 1.3 J/K | 2.7 g × 1.1 = 3.0 J/K | **≈4.3 J/K** (plus about 1 J/K for FETs/caps → ~5 J/K) |
| 20×20 mm | 0.56 J/K | 1.3 J/K | **≈2.2 J/K** |

Convection under prop wash: h ≈ 100 W/m²K **[A]**. For 30.5 mm, A ≈ 1.9×10⁻³ m², so hA ≈ 0.19 W/K. That is negligible over seconds; the burst is essentially adiabatic.

### 4.3 Burst duration limit

Limit: T_board ≤ 100 °C (keeping T_j ≤ ~125 °C), starting at T₀ = 40 °C, so ΔT = 60 K.

Junction headroom above the board: $\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET,pk} R_{th,jc} = 9.0 \times 1.5 = 13.5$ K, plus about 10 K across the pad/vias. This leaves roughly 60 K of board rise before T_j hits ~125 °C.

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T}{P}$$

| Format | 4-motor 80 A burst | 1-motor 80 A (others at 20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 30.5×30.5 | 5 × 60 / 108 ≈ **2.8 s** | ≈ 8 s |
| 20×20 | 2.2 × 60 / 108 ≈ **1.2 s** | ≈ 3.5 s |
