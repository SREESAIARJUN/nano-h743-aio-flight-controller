## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step Synchronous PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, two legs carry current:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** The low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.
- **Leg C:** Floating, used for back-EMF sensing.

Ignoring the small dead-time fraction, phase current flows through exactly **two FET channels at every instant**, regardless of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] \approx 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This D-independence holds only with synchronous rectification enabled (AM32 default). If you use non-synchronous (diode freewheel) mode, replace the (1−D) term with V_F·I·(1−D). At low duty that is dramatically worse.

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{DS,25}\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big]
$$

Because loss rises with T_j and T_j rises with loss, solve this self-consistently (§3.4). It is positive feedback: thermal runaway occurs if

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{\theta,JA,eff} \ge 1
$$

### 2.4 Parasitic Copper and Shunt Losses

These are not negligible at 80 A.

**Shunt:**

$$
P_{shunt} = I^2 R_{sh} = 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ mΩ} = 1.6\text{ W per channel}
$$

The shunt carries return current for the whole cycle in a single low-side shunt topology.
