## 0. Assumptions and Up-Front Engineering Flags

You did not provide the MOSFET part, layer stack or thermal environment. I have therefore parameterized every equation and given a worked example with stated, typical values. **Replace these with datasheet values before sign-off.**

There are two architectural points to settle first.

**1. The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation controllers. These are normally 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or one MCU per two motors running BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. Alternatively, a single H743 could run all four FOC loops, but ArduPilot does not support that. This document assumes the standard architecture:

> **H743 (ArduPilot) → 4 × DShot600 bidir → 4 × ESC MCU (AM32, 48 kHz PWM) → 4 × 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs)**

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** The model in Section 3 shows that at 80 A the board can sustain roughly **0.5–1.5 s** before reaching the junction limit. Even that requires the burst to start from a warm-but-not-hot board. Treat it as a ≤1 s rating, not a continuous one. I recommend publishing it as "80 A / 1 s burst, 35–45 A continuous with airflow".

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology under 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, one phase high-side (HS) switches at duty *D* with its complementary low-side (LS). The third conducting phase holds its LS on for the full period.

| Device | Conduction fraction | Current |
|---|---|---|
| HS, switching phase | D | I_ph |
| LS, switching phase (sync rect.) | 1 − D − 2·t_dt·f_sw | I_ph |
| LS, return phase | 1 | I_ph |

The total conducted I²R per motor is approximately independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} \approx I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The dead-time interval, where the body diode conducts, is handled in §2.3.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

Datasheet "normalized R_DS(on) vs T_j" curves are typically 1.5–1.7× at 125–150 °C.

### 1.3 Ripple-corrected RMS current

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D\,(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

### 1.4 Worked example

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz |
| Phase current | 80 A (burst) |
| L_ph-ph | 15 µH (typical 2207–2808 class motor) |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm, R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V (illustrative) |
| PCB trace/via + solder resistance per path | 0.6 mΩ (two FETs + copper) |

- Worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5: ΔI = 25.2·0.25 / (15 µH · 48 kHz) = **8.75 A pp**.
- RMS adder: 8.75²/12 = 6.4 A². I_rms² = 6400 + 6.4 = **6406 A²**, so ripple is negligible at 80 A.
- R_DS(on) at T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005: 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 6406 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = 23.1\ \text{W per motor}
$$

$$
P_{cond,Cu} = 6406 \times 0.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.8\ \text{W per motor}
$$

**Conduction total: about 26.9 W per motor, about 108 W for four motors at 80 A.** At 25 °C the figure is about 19 W per motor. Note the positive feedback: loss rises with T_j, which is why the burst rating collapses quickly.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS device)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

The transition times depend on the gate drive:

$$
t_{r/f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}}
$$

Example values: Q_gd = 6 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, R_g,total = 6.5 Ω.

- I_g ≈ 1 A, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 6 ns (circuit parasitics typically push the total to ~20 ns).
- P_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz = **0.97 W per motor**.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery and C_oss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.2\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.018\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

With t_dt = 100 ns per edge and V_SD = 0.8 V:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g\,V_{drv}\,f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.029\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.5 ESC MCU timer resolution check (48 kHz, center-aligned)

$$
ARR = \frac{f_{TIM}}{2 f_{sw}} = \frac{64\,\text{MHz}}{96\,\text{kHz}} \approx 666\ \text{counts}
$$

This is about 9.4 bits on a G071 or AT32F421 at 64 MHz. It is adequate, but 48 kHz costs about 0.6 W per motor more in switching and dead-time loss than 24 kHz. The benefit is quieter operation and smoother low-throttle response on low-inductance motors.

### 2.6 Loss summary at 80 A, 6S, T_j = 125 °C

| Component | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.1 W | 92.4 W |
| Copper/solder | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Hard switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 W | 0.3 W |
| **Total** | **~28.6 W** | **~114 W** |

At 80 A, conduction is about 94% of the total. Lowering f_sw buys little at this current. **Lower R_DS(on) and more copper are the only levers that matter.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

The heat path is a Foster/Cauer ladder:

> Junction → (R_jc) → case/pad → (R_pad-PCB, vias) → board copper plane (C_board) → (R_conv) → ambient air

Junction rise under a step of power P is:

$$
\Delta T_j(t) = P \sum_i R_i \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right), \quad \tau_i = R_i C_i
$$

### 3.2 Parameter estimation for a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board

**Board heat capacity:**

| Element | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 6 layers × 400 mm² × 70 µm × 60% fill ≈ 0.10 cm³ → 0.9 g | × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 0.35 J/K |
| FR4 | 0.64 cm³ → 1.2 g | × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 1.3 J/K |
| 24 FETs + solder | ~1.5 g | ≈ 0.4 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 2.0 J/K** |
