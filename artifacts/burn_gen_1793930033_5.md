## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from mechanical angle:

$$\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

To compensate for angle latency, use the predicted angle $\hat\theta_e = \theta_e + \omega_e\,(1.5\,T_c)$. This covers one sample of computation delay plus half a PWM period.

### 1.2 Plant and PI design

The dq voltage equations (SPMSM, $L_d=L_q=L$) are:

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

After cross-coupling decoupling, each axis reduces to $G_i(s)=\dfrac{1}{Ls+R}$.

Use a PI controller $C_i(s)=K_p + K_i/s$ with zero/pole cancellation, $K_i/K_p = R/L$. The open loop then becomes $\omega_c/s$:

$$\boxed{K_{p,i}=L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i}=R\,\omega_c}$$

Bandwidth limit with total delay $T_d \approx 1.5\,T_c = 75\,\mu s$: choose $\omega_c \le 1/(4T_d)$. That gives $\omega_c \approx 2\pi\cdot 500$ Hz, so $K_p = 6.6$ V/A and $K_i = 17\,600$ V/(A·s).

Decoupling plus back-EMF feedforward:

$$v_d^* = \text{PI}_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = \text{PI}_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

### 1.3 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

The SVPWM linear range is $|v_{dq}| \le V_{\max} = V_{dc}/\sqrt3$. Allocate d-axis priority:

$$v_d^{sat}=\text{clamp}(v_d^*,\pm V_{\max}),\quad v_q^{sat}=\text{clamp}\Big(v_q^*,\pm\sqrt{V_{\max}^2-(v_d^{sat})^2}\Big)$$

Back-calculation anti-windup, discretized as forward Euler on the integrator:

$$x_I[k+1] = x_I[k] + T_c\Big(K_i e[k] + \tfrac{1}{T_t}(v^{sat}-v^*)\Big),\qquad T_t = \sqrt{K_p/K_i}$$

### 1.4 SVPWM (min/max injection)

Inverse Park gives $(v_\alpha, v_\beta)$, then inverse Clarke gives $(v_a,v_b,v_c)$. Add the common-mode offset:

$$v_0 = -\tfrac12(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})),\qquad d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{dc}}$$

## 2. Cascaded Stabilization Loops

For a stabilizing gimbal, the velocity loop is closed on the **IMU gyro rate projected onto the joint axis**, not on the encoder derivative. This rejects base motion directly. The encoder handles commutation and serves as fallback and auxiliary damping.
