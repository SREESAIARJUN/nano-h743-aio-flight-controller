## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. An 80A-burst "4-in-1" stack is two systems:

| Domain | Silicon | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (400–480 MHz) | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) | Generates DShot600 frames and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry |
| ESC | 4 × local MCU (e.g., STM32G071 / AT32F421) + 3-phase gate driver + 24 MOSFETs | AM32 or BLHeli_32-class | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, bidirectional DShot responder |

The loss and thermal model (§1–3) applies to the ESC power stage. The timer and DMA plan (§4) applies to the H743.

### 0.2 Realism check on "nano + 80A"

80A per channel on a 20×20 mm board is not thermally credible. The copper area cannot dump roughly 120 W. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm** ESC, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner copper. **80A is treated strictly as a burst rating (≤ 2–5 s)**. Continuous rating is derived in §3.5.

### 0.3 Baseline component parameters

These are assumed values. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; every equation below is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | DC link |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current (trapezoidal plateau) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ | @ V_gs = 10 V, 40 V-rated, 5×6 mm DFN class |
| α_R | 0.0055 /K | R_ds temperature coefficient (≈1.55× at 125 °C) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation geometry

Use 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phases conduct: one leg sources current and one sinks it.

- The **PWM leg** has its high-side FET on for duty D and its low-side FET on (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D − 2·t_dt·f_sw.
- The **return leg** has its low-side FET fully on for the 60° sector.

Each phase leg is active for 4 of the 6 sectors (240° of 360°). Over one electrical cycle, the current path always passes through exactly **two series channel resistances**, ignoring dead-time diode intervals, which are handled in §2.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction power

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^{2}\cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\left(1 - 2\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw}\right)
$$

The temperature dependence is

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\bigl[1+\alpha_R\,(T_j-25)\bigr]
$$

The dead-time correction factor is 1 − 2(150 ns)(48 kHz) = 0.9856.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so solve by fixed-point iteration (§3.3 supplies T_j). At the converged burst-end T_j ≈ 115 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(115) = 1.5\,(1+0.0055\cdot 90) = 2.24\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 6400 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.00224 \cdot 0.9856 = \mathbf{28.3\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Distribution across the 6 FETs

By symmetry over an electrical revolution, each FET carries current 1/3 of the time.

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 = \frac{28.3}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{1}\Big/2 \;\rightarrow\; \mathbf{4.7\ W\ \text{average per FET}}
$$

Here 2 FETs are conducting at any time, giving 28.3 W total across 6 FETs, or 4.7 W average.

> **Hot-spot caveat:** At low duty (D < 0.3, e.g., punch-out from low throttle), low-side FETs carry the sync-rectified current most of the time and run roughly 1.4× hotter than high-side FETs. Size the thermal design to **P_LS,max ≈ 6.0 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The high-side FET sees V–I overlap. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage (body diode conducting during dead time) but contributes Q_rr and diode losses.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (high side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot22\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side diode, dissipated mostly in high-side FET)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance (hard turn-on of high side)

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss(er)}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 1.1\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.034\ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot300\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.92\ W}
$$

This is a large term. Cutting dead time to 80 ns saves about 0.43 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin with your driver's propagation mismatch, which is typically ±25 ns.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 45\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.043\ \text{W per leg}
$$

### 2.6 Per-channel switching total

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 1.06 + 0.048 + 0.034 + 0.92 = \mathbf{2.06\ W}
$$

### 2.7 Totals at 80A burst

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 115 °C) | 28.3 W | 113.2 W |
| Switching + dead-time | 2.06 W | 8.2 W |
| Gate drive (3 legs, 1 active PWM) | ~0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side sensing) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| PCB copper I²R (est. 0.4 mΩ path) | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| **Total** | **~34.6 W** | **~138 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 85%. **R_ds(on) and copper resistance are your primary levers; f_sw is secondary.** Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 4 W total but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 2.8 Efficiency at burst

Output power ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 × ~0.9 effective duty ≈ 7.26 kW, giving η ≈ 98.1%. Losses are small relative to throughput but large relative to the board's thermal mass.
