# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model and Reference Design

**Design package: Job #31. Status: preliminary analysis, not released for fabrication.**

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

These findings affect everything downstream, so they come first.

| # | Issue | Resolution |
|---|-------|------------|
| 1 | **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCUs: four single-chip ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. | The H743 is the FC. It generates DShot and decodes bidirectional telemetry. The ESC section is a separate power domain on the same PCB (AIO) or a stacked board. |
| 2 | **80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) form factor is not thermally credible** for more than about 1 s with all four motors loaded (see §3.5). | Spec the 80 A as a per-motor, single-channel, ≤1 s transient. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint. |
| 3 | **48 kHz PWM** is valid for AM32. It roughly doubles switching loss versus 24 kHz. | It is modeled below. It is worth it only for low-inductance motors. |

All component values below are **parametric placeholders**. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected MOSFET and driver before sign-off.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

| Symbol | Meaning | Example value |
|---|---|---|

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The analysis assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. During the PWM off-time, the active leg's low-side FET carries the freewheeling current. The total current path therefore always contains **two FET positions in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond}(T_j) = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

This loss is distributed over the 6 FET positions. Each position carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so per-position loss averages to:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The hottest position is somewhat higher. At low duty, the low-side FETs carry more of the RMS current.

**Numeric result at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_ds = 1.0 × [1 + 0.0055 × 85] = **1.47 mΩ**
- P_cond = 6400 × 2 × 1.47e-3 = **18.8 W per motor**

### 2.3 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg, 48 kHz)

Only the actively PWM'd leg switches hard, so there is one hard-switching transition pair per PWM period.

**(a) V-I overlap loss:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5\times25.2\times80\times22\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery loss** (low-side body diode, recovered at high-side turn-on):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_ph. At 80 A, derate by roughly 2× to be safe, giving **≈0.07 W**.

**(c) Output capacitance loss:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}
$$

This is a conservative full-charge form.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive loss:**

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

Gate loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET die.

**Total switching-related loss per motor:**

$$
P_{sw,tot} \approx 1.06 + 0.07 + 0.05 + 0.61 + 0.06 \approx \mathbf{1.85\ W}
$$

At 24 kHz this would be about 0.93 W, so 48 kHz costs **about 0.9 W per motor** at 80 A. Conduction loss dominates by about 10×, so 48 kHz is affordable. Dead-time loss is the largest switching term. Tune AM32 dead time to the minimum safe value measured on the real gate driver.

### 2.4 Copper / PCB Loss (Frequently Ignored)

Sheet resistance for 2 oz (70 µm) copper is:

$$
R_\square = \rho/t = 1.72\times10^{-8}/70\times10^{-6} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \text{ (at 25 °C, +0.39 %/K)}
$$

Consider a bus/phase path of about 6 squares per layer, with 3 parallel layers carrying current. This gives about 0.5 mΩ per path at 25 °C and about 0.6 mΩ hot. Twice that for the round trip yields roughly 1.2 mΩ.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2\times1.2\text{m} \approx \mathbf{7.7\ W per motor}
$$

**Copper loss is comparable to FET loss on a 20×20 board.** This is the single strongest argument against 80 A on nano. Mitigations:
- Use a 4 oz outer / 2 oz inner stackup.
- Add solder-filled bus bars.
- Pad out battery leads directly to the bridge.
