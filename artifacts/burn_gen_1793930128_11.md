## 0. Assumptions and Scope Corrections

The brief contains three implicit assumptions that need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** No ArduPilot build performs FOC or six-step commutation of four motors on the flight controller MCU. The realistic architecture is:
   - **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware) each drive a 3-phase gate driver and a 6-FET bridge.

   The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency, set in AM32/BLHeli config, not an H743 parameter.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a seconds-scale rating, not a sustained one.** The math below quantifies exactly how many seconds.

3. **Component values are representative placeholders.** Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values into the equations. Every equation is written parametrically for that reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology Basis

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, phase current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path is the complementary FET's channel rather than its body diode, apart from dead time. The conduction loss per motor bridge is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, split by duty between its high and low partners. The average per-FET share is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6}$$

The commutation frequency is several hundred Hz to kHz, far faster than the junction thermal time constant (ms), so averaging is valid.

### 1.3 PCB Copper Conduction (Not Negligible)

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \quad (\times1.3 \text{ at } 100\,°C)$$
