# STM32H743 Nano AIO (4-in-1 ESC + FC): Design Model, Job #14

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

I don't have prior Job #14 documents, so this analysis builds a self-contained baseline. Every parameter marked **[A]** is an assumption. Replace each one with datasheet or measured values before you sign off.

**Architecture decision.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel uses its own commutation MCU running AM32 (AT32F421K8U7, **[A]**), driven by bidirectional DShot600 from the H743. Do not commutate four sensorless BLDC motors directly from the H743:
- The H743 has only two advanced-control timers with complementary outputs (TIM1 and TIM8). HRTIM could cover more, but this is non-standard.
- Four channels of zero-crossing back-EMF detection would compete with the flight stack's real-time budget.
- The ArduPilot and AM32 ecosystems assume this split architecture.

**Critical feasibility flag.** The math below shows that 80 A per channel on a nano (≈27×27 mm, 20×20 mount) board is a short-burst rating only, on the order of 1–2 s with all four channels loaded simultaneously. It has no thermal steady state. Treat any "80 A" marketing figure as a burst number, and set ESC current limiting accordingly. A 30×30 or larger layout, or 35–45 A burst, is the realistic target for this footprint.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through one high-side (HS) leg and one low-side (LS) leg. With complementary PWM:
- **Driven phase:** the HS FET conducts for duty D, and its complementary LS FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return phase:** the LS FET conducts 100% of the sector.
