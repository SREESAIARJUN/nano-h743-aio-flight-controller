## 0. Architecture Correction

The architecture needs to be stated precisely, because it drives everything else.

- **The STM32H743 does not drive the bridges.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. It sends DShot frames to the ESC.
- **The 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCUs.** You need four ESC MCUs (for example 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), or one dual-motor MCU per two motors.
- **The 48 kHz PWM is an ESC firmware parameter**, not an H743 timer function. AM32 supports 24/48 kHz.
- **The H743's role:** generate DShot600 on 4 pins, receive the bidirectional eRPM replies, and run the flight stack.

```
6S LiPo ─┬─► 4× 3-phase bridge (24 FETs) ◄── 4× gate driver ◄── 4× ESC MCU (AM32, 48 kHz)
         │                                                              ▲ DShot600 bidir (1-wire each)
         └─► 5V/3V3 BEC ─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ───────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at all times:

- **Non-PWM leg:** one FET (for example the low-side FET of phase B) is on for the full 60° sector.
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D).

The current always flows through exactly two channels, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At I = 80 A and R = 2.08 mΩ (hot):

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.08\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.6\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-device stress:** the steady-on FET dissipates I²R = **13.3 W** while active. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the time, giving roughly **4.4 W average per FET**. However, the per-sector instantaneous value is what heats the junction at low eRPM, and at stall/low-speed burst the sector time is long relative to the junction time constant.

**Electrothermal feedback:** because R rises with T_j, iterate the following until it converges (or fails to converge, which indicates runaway):

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)})\,R_{th,ja}$$

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM leg switches hard. One hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | **0.06 W** |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (dissipated in the hard-switching FET) | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g·V_drv·f_sw × 2 FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver/R_g | 0.04 W |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

Observations:

- **Dead time dominates switching loss** at 80 A. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. AM32's dead-time setting and the driver's propagation matching matter more than edge speed.
- **48 kHz vs 24 kHz:** switching loss scales linearly with frequency, so going to 48 kHz costs about +1.15 W per motor. In exchange you get lower current ripple and quieter motors. At 80 A, conduction loss is about 11× switching loss, so 48 kHz is affordable.

### Total burst dissipation

| Scope | Calculation | Dissipation |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 26.6 + 2.3 | **≈ 29 W** |
| 4-in-1, all motors at 80 A | 4 × 29 | **≈ 116 W** |
| Plus copper, shunts, capacitor ESR, connectors | estimate | **≈ 125–135 W** |

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction steady state (single PDFN3.3 FET)

The thermal resistance path is R_th,j-a = R_th,jc + R_th,c-pcb (vias/solder) + R_th,pcb-a(spread).

| Element | Value, K/W |
|---|---|
| R_th,jc (bottom pad) | 1.5 |
| Solder + via array (thermal-via field under the pad, filled vias) | 4–8 |
| Local spreading into a crowded nano board | ≥ 10 |

The FET stays on at 13.3 W for the whole sector. Even with only the first two terms (about 6–10 K/W):

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} \approx 13.3 \times (6\text{–}10) \approx 80\text{–}133\ \mathrm{K}$$

**Single PDFN3.3 FETs per switch position cannot sustain 80 A for more than a few hundred milliseconds.** Junction rise to the PCB is 80–130 K before the board itself heats.
