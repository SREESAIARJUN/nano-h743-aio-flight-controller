# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation, and Reference Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Issues to Resolve First

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC). It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 does not commutate the motors. Running ArduPilot plus four sensorless commutation loops on one core is not supported, so this split is the design basis.

| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge |

**Issues to resolve first.**

1. **Total current is not credible for a nano board.** "80 A burst ×4" means 320 A drawn from a battery connector on a ~26 × 26 mm PCB. The math below shows this is a few-second capability at best. Before layout, negotiate the rating to either:
   - 80 A per channel, non-simultaneous; or
   - 4 × 45 A continuous / 4 × 60 A burst.
2. **The netlists are reference topologies, not fab-ready.** Pin numbers must be checked against the exact package datasheets, and the design needs ERC, DRC and SI review before release.
3. **ArduPilot hwdef syntax evolves.** Verify the BIDIR/DMA conventions against `libraries/AP_HAL_ChibiOS/hwdef/scripts/` and the hwdef README at the ArduPilot version you target.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step, synchronous rectification)

### 1.1 Current paths

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase sources and one phase sinks. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- The active leg's high-side switch (HS) conducts for a fraction D.
- That leg's low-side switch (LS) conducts for a fraction 1 − D (synchronous freewheeling).
- The return leg's LS conducts for 100% of the sector.

So **exactly two channels are always in series with the phase current I_ph**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \qquad (1)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{typ. 40 V trench})$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 75) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Battery current versus phase current

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At part throttle, the phase current exceeds the battery current. The design case is therefore D → 1 with I_ph = 80 A.

### 1.4 Numbers

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel (eq. 1) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00100 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.00145 = **18.6 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | **20.5 W** |

### 1.5 PCB copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect)

- **Per-channel phase and bus copper**, R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ total (2 oz outer and inner layers, 26 mm board):
  80² × 0.4 mΩ = **2.6 W per channel**.
- **Shared battery entry plus a 0.25 mΩ current shunt** at 4 × 80 A = 320 A:
  320² × (0.25 + 0.15) mΩ = **41 W**.

That 41 W alone is a design-breaking number. Use a Hall-effect sensor (e.g. a 200 A-range Allegro/ACS-class part) or per-channel shunts, and run the battery current through thick copper and pads.
