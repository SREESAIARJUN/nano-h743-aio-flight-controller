# Job #6: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction model, 48 kHz switching model, thermal model, bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation for ArduPilot, and reference netlists.

---

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design, so they come before the derivations.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It cannot run the ESC commutation loops. Every production AM32/BLHeli_32 4-in-1 uses one ESC MCU per motor. | The architecture is H743 (FC) driving DShot600 to 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 (AM32) ESC MCUs. |
| F2 | On a nano footprint (20×20 mm holes, ~26×26 mm board) with a single 3×3 mm DFN MOSFET per switch, the numbers below show that **80 A per motor is a seconds-scale transient, not a rating.** | Spec it as "80 A burst ≤ 1 s, single-motor." Sustained capability lands around 35–45 A. |
| F3 | 4 × 80 A from 6S is 320 A of battery current. That exceeds any XT60 pigtail or nano power-plane design. | The burst spec must be per-motor and non-simultaneous. Set the AM32 current limit accordingly. |

**Parameter status:** Every device parameter below is a stated assumption representative of a 40 V DFN3333 MOSFET class. Before release, re-run Section 1–3 with the chosen part's datasheet values. The spreadsheet form of these equations is in Section 7.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.175 mΩ**.

### 2.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

The worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple contributes under 0.06%, so it is neglected from here on.

### 2.3 Per-FET current distribution in 6-step complementary PWM

Consider a 120° sector with phase A PWM-high and phase B held low:

- **HS_A** conducts for a fraction D of the sector.
- **LS_A** conducts for 1 − D (synchronous freewheel).
- **LS_B** conducts for 100% of the sector.

Each FET plays each role once per electrical cycle, i.e., in one-third of the cycle. Therefore:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}\,D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{I^2R_{ds}\,(2-D)}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2I^2R_{ds}$$

The total is independent of D: two FETs are always in the current path.
