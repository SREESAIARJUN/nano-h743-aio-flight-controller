# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC "Nano": Thermal, Switching & DShot Architecture (Job #50)

## 0. Architectural Assumptions

Every number below follows from these assumptions. Change any one and the results scale as shown in the equations.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | AIO board. The **H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only**. Each motor is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or similar). ArduPilot cannot commutate BLDC motors directly from the H743. |
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V (design/derate at 26 V) |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mount, ~36×36 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner (equivalent to 6×2 oz in the mass model) |
| MOSFET (representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN class) | R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ 10 V; Q_g = 50 nC; t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns (driven); Q_rr = 40 nC; V_SD = 0.8 V; C_oss(er) = 1.0 nF; R_θJC(bottom) = 1.5 K/W |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), V_GS = 10–12 V, dead time 100 ns |
| Commutation | Six-step trapezoidal, synchronous (complementary) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Burst | 80 A per motor, all four motors simultaneously (worst case), duty ≈ 0.95 |

Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values into the Section 1–2 equations. The structure of the model holds, but the magnitudes will move.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In any 60° sector, one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) device carry the phase current. With synchronous PWM, the PWM-ing leg's HS conducts for D and its LS (sync rectifier) for (1–D). So at every instant, exactly two R_DS(on) elements are in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle. The average per FET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\ \to\ \frac{19.2}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

During the conducting window itself, the instantaneous per-FET loss is 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**. This matters for the transient Z_th analysis at low electrical frequency (startup or stall).

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

Phase and bus path: ~0.5 mΩ total (bus planes, vias, motor pads).

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Current-sense shunt (if per-channel)

With a 0.5 mΩ shunt carrying I_bus ≈ D·I:

$$P_{shunt} \approx (0.95 \times 80)^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m} \approx 2.9\ W$$

Recommendation: use one 0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt (≈ 0.25 m × (4×76)² ≈ 23 W at full 4-motor burst). That is still severe. A Hall-effect sensor, or a lower-value 4-terminal shunt read by the H743 ADC, is preferable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg's HS hard-switches. The LS commutates through the body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48k | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48k | **0.61 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss(er)·V²·f_sw | **0.015 W** |
| Gate charge (2 FETs, mostly in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·50 n·10·48k | **0.048 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.66 W** |

The switching duty rotates across three HS devices, so each FET averages about 0.65 W of switching loss plus a share of the diode loss.

**Sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel. The cost is higher motor current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}$$
