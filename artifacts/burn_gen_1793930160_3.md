# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #59): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Three points need settling before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The usual choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture is therefore H743 → 4× DShot lines → 4× ESC MCU → 4× three-phase gate driver → 24 MOSFETs.

2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally transient-limited.** Sections 2–4 show the real limit is board heat capacity, not MOSFET ratings. 80A on all four channels at once is survivable for roughly 1–2 s. Treat the rating as a per-motor, short-burst specification, not a simultaneous one.

3. **System current is extreme.** 4 × 80A = 320A from 6S (25.2 V) is about 8 kW. Battery leads, the XT60/XT90 connector and the bulk capacitors must be sized accordingly. An XT60 is inadequate.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### Operating assumption

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. The motor current always flows through exactly two half-bridge legs. In each leg it flows through either the high-side or low-side FET, depending on PWM state.

Because one of the two FETs in the switching leg is always conducting, the current path resistance is independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I^2 \cdot R_{Cu}
$$

### Hot R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25} = 1.4 \times 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.82\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### Per-channel result at 80 A

$$
P_{FET,cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.82\times10^{-3} = 23.3\ \text{W}
$$

### PCB copper path

R_Cu is the bus plane, phase pads and vias. For a 2 oz, 8-layer stack it is about 0.4–0.6 mΩ per channel effective. Using 0.5 mΩ:

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

### Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries roughly an equal share:

$$
P_{cond,FET} \approx 23.3 / 6 \approx 3.9\ \text{W (average)}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs switching loss. That is one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 Gate current during the Miller plateau

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{src} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3}{11} = 0.64\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{3}{6} = 0.50\ \text{A}
$$

### 2.2 Transition times

The switching charge is Q_sw = Q_gs2 + Q_gd. Taking Q_gs2 ≈ Q_gs/2 = 4 nC gives Q_sw = 10 nC.

$$
t_{on} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.64} = 15.6\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.50} = 20.0\ \text{ns}
$$

### 2.3 Loss components per channel

**Voltage–current overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_{on} + t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35.6\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.72\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the complementary FET):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance** (both FETs in the leg):

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 900\,\text{pF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.027\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time windows per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$
