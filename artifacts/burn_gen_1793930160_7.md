# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #56): Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Design Package

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

An STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors and run ArduPilot at the same time. On every commercial AIO, each motor bridge has its own dedicated ESC MCU. This package therefore assumes:

| Block | Assumed Part | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | ArduPilot. Sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM. |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | Runs AM32 or BLHeli_32. Generates the 48 kHz PWM. |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Drives the half-bridges. |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R<sub>DS(on)</sub> = 1.3 mΩ max at 25 °C, Q<sub>g</sub> = 40 nC, Q<sub>gd</sub> = 8 nC, Q<sub>rr</sub> = 40 nC, R<sub>θJC</sub> = 1.2 K/W | One FET per switch position. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V full charge, 22.2 V nominal | |
| Burst rating | 80 A per motor phase current, 4 motors simultaneously | Worst case. |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in the ESC firmware (AM32 `PWM frequency` or BLHeli_32 setting), not in ArduPilot. ArduPilot only controls the DShot command link.

**Before tape-out:** replace every bracketed parameter with the datasheet value for your actual MOSFET at your actual gate-drive voltage. The structure of the model is general; the numbers below are only representative.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At a design junction temperature of T<sub>j</sub> = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.3 \times 1.4675 = 1.91\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows through two switch positions:

- **High-side position:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. Its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Opposite low-side FET:** conducts for 100% of the sector.

Ignoring dead time, the total conduction loss per motor is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.91\text{e-}3 = 24.4\ \text{W}$$

Per FET, the worst-case instantaneous loss is I²R = 12.2 W. Averaged over commutation, each FET carries about 1/3 of the time, but the instantaneous figure is what matters for junction-temperature excursion.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt losses (per motor)

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase shunt (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Bus copper, 2 oz, ~15 mm × 4 mm, both rails | ~0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Motor pad and solder | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.6 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 4.8 W** |

Use 3 oz or 4 oz inner layers with stitched copper pours on the power planes. At 80 A, copper losses are not negligible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Conditions: V<sub>bus</sub> = 25.2 V, gate drive ≈ 1 A source / 1.5 A sink.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side PWM FET only)

Switching times from the Miller charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} \approx 8\ \text{ns}, \quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,sink}} \approx 6\ \text{ns}$$

Add Q<sub>gs2</sub> time and layout inductance, giving a realistic t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>f</sub> ≈ 40 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (synchronous FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

This assumes t<sub>dt</sub> = 100 ns.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

With C<sub>oss,eff</sub> = 1.5 nF:

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eff} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{e-}9 \times 635 \times 48\text{e}3 = 0.02\ \text{W}$$
