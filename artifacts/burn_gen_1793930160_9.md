## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Stated Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. That is the job of each ESC's own MCU, running AM32 or BLHeli_32.

| Function | Owner |
|---|---|
| Flight control and DShot600 generation | STM32H743 running ArduPilot |
| 48 kHz PWM and 6-step/FOC commutation | ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per channel or one shared) |
| Power stage | 4 × 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs) |

The 48 kHz figure is an ESC firmware setting (`PWM_FREQUENCY` in AM32). The H743 only sets the command rate and receives eRPM.

### 0.3 Feasibility Flag

Read this before committing the design. On a 20×20 mm (nano) 4-in-1, **80 A per channel is only achievable as a short burst, on the order of 1 s.** Section 3 shows the board's thermal mass cannot sustain it. Commercial 20×20 4-in-1s typically rate 40–60 A burst. Treat 80 A as a burst rating with an enforced time limit, not as a continuous capability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths (6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In any commutation state, one phase is PWM'd high-side, one phase is held low-side ON, and one phase floats.

- During D·T_sw, current flows through HS(x) → motor → LS(y).
- During (1−D)·T_sw, current freewheels through LS(x) (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS(y).

The current therefore always traverses exactly **two** FETs:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot 1 = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is why burst current, not throttle, sets conduction loss.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS factor | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond @ 80 A, per ESC channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 | 1.20 | 15.4 W |
| 85 °C | 1.45 | 1.74 | 22.3 W |
| 110 °C | 1.64 | 1.97 | 25.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.75 | 2.10 | 26.9 W |

Example at 110 °C: P = 2 × 6400 × 1.97 mΩ = 25.2 W.
