## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071.

```
VBAT(6S) ──┬── 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET → motor]
           ├── BEC 5V/3A ── BEC 3V3 ── STM32H743 + IMU/baro/flash
           └── VBAT sense divider, shunt/hall current sense → H743 ADC
H743 TIM1 CH1–4 ── bidir DShot600 ──► ESC1–4 signal pins
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

Six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM is assumed. At any instant one phase is high, one is low, and one floats.

- The **PWM'd leg** conducts through the high-side FET for D·T and the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T.
- The **static leg** conducts through one FET for 100% of T.

Per motor, therefore, there is effectively **2 FET resistances in series at all times**, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{trace}$$

### 1.2 Thermal runaway coupling

Conduction loss raises temperature, which raises resistance, so the two must be solved together:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,Z_\theta$$

Substituting and solving gives a closed form:

$$P_{FET}=\frac{I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_{ref}-25)]}{1-I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_\theta}$$

The model is stable only if **I²·R₂₅·α·Z_θ < 1**.
- At 80 A: I²R₂₅α = 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.006 = 0.0384 K⁻¹·W.
- So runaway occurs when **Z_θ > 26 K/W**. Steady state on a small board without airflow approaches this bound.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P per conducting FET (I²R) | P_cond per motor (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 6.4 W | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 9.3 W | 18.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 10.2 W | 20.5 W |

PCB trace and via resistance must also be included. With 0.3 mΩ of total phase-path copper per motor, add 6400 × 0.3e-3 = **1.9 W per motor**. This number is often forgotten and is not small.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the static leg does not. Per motor:

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side)

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}= 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}= 0.04\text{ W}$$

Q_rr rises steeply with di/dt and temperature, so derate this term ×2–3.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term. Reduce dead time to the minimum the gate driver and FETs allow (≈100 ns), which cuts it to 0.6 W.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 20.5 |
| Copper | 1.9 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 1.23 |
| Coss + Qrr (derated ×2) | 0.18 |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W per motor** |
| **All 4 motors at 80 A** | **≈ 99 W** |

The ESC's efficiency at this point is 1 − 24.8/(25.2 × 80) ≈ 98.8%. Efficiency looks good, but **100 W into roughly 10 cm² of PCB is the actual problem.**

### 2.7 The 48 kHz trade-off

Switching plus dead-time loss scales linearly with frequency, at about 2.4 W per motor at 48 kHz. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.2 W per motor. The cost is more current ripple and audible noise on low-inductance motors.

Ripple check:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor with L ≈ 20 µH phase-to-phase at D = 0.5:
- 48 kHz: ΔI ≈ 6.6 A p-p
- 24 kHz: ΔI ≈ 13 A p-p
