# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop and Dual ICM-42688-P Sensor Fusion

I don't have the original Job #5 specification, so this design assumes a common configuration. Every parameter is a named constant so you can substitute your real values.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Motors | 3 × gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (7 pole pairs), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Position sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder per axis (AS5048/MT6701 class) |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7 class) with single-precision FPU |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, separate SPI buses, shared CLKIN |
| Loop rates | Current 20 kHz, velocity 4 kHz, angle/attitude 1–2 kHz, IMU ODR 8 kHz |

## 1. Control Architecture

```
            ┌──────────── 1 kHz ────────────┐   ┌──── 4 kHz ────┐   ┌──── 20 kHz ────┐
 θ_ref ──►(Σ)─► Angle P ─►(Σ)─► ω_ref ──►(Σ)─► Velocity PI ─►(Σ)─► i_q* ─► Current PI ─► SVPWM ─► Motor
           ▲               ▲ ω_ff         ▲                 ▲ i_cog,ff     ▲ (d-q decoupled)
           │                              │                 │              │
     θ̂ (Mahony/IMU)                ω̂ (fused gyro)    Anti-cogging     i_a,i_b (ADC)
           │                              │          (θ_mech, LMS)         │
           └─────── Dual IMU fusion + vibration rejection ◄────────────────┘ encoder θ
```

Two design decisions follow from this structure:

- **Velocity feedback comes from the gyro, not encoder differentiation.** The gyro measures inertial rate, which is the quantity being stabilized. It is also noise-free compared with a differentiated 14-bit encoder.
- **Electrical commutation always uses the encoder.**

## 2. Plant Models

### 2.1 Electrical model (d-q frame, surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L\frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

- $\omega_e = p\,\omega_m$, where p is the number of pole pairs.
- $\lambda_m$ is the PM flux linkage.
- The torque constant is $K_t = \tfrac{3}{2} p \lambda_m$.

### 2.2 Mechanical model per axis

$$
J\dot\omega_m = K_t i_q - B\omega_m - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{ext}
$$

### 2.3 Clarke and Park transforms (amplitude-invariant, two-shunt)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 2.4 SVPWM by min-max zero-sequence injection

$$
v_{a,b,c}^{0} = \text{inverse Clarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta)
$$

$$
v_{off} = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}^{0}) + \min(v_{abc}^{0})\right)
$$

$$
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x^{0} + v_{off}}{V_{bus}}
$$

The linear range is $|v_{dq}| \le V_{bus}/\sqrt3$.

## 3. Cascaded Loop Design

### 3.1 Current loop (PI with pole-zero cancellation)

The plant from $v_q$ to $i_q$ is $G_i(s) = \dfrac{1}{Ls + R}$. Choose the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole:

$$
C_i(s) = K_{p,i} + \frac{K_{i,i}}{s},\qquad K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}
$$

The closed loop then becomes first order:

$$
\frac{i_q}{i_q^*} = \frac{\omega_{ci}}{s + \omega_{ci}}
$$

Choose $\omega_{ci} \le 2\pi f_{PWM}/20$. At 20 kHz this gives $\omega_{ci} \approx 2\pi \cdot 1000$ rad/s.
