**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (80 A burst, 48 kHz), transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

## 0. Architecture Clarification (read first)

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot cannot run four 3-phase sensorless BLDC controllers at 48 kHz. The correct partition for a "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (or VIT6), 480 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) | Attitude control, DShot600 bidir master |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), 120 MHz | AM32 | 6-step sensorless commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge driver, bootstrap) | — | 6 gate outputs per motor |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | — | 6 per motor |

**Form-factor warning.** "Nano" usually means a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm mount. Sustained 80 A per channel is physically impossible at that size. Section 3 proves that 80 A is a short burst rating of about 2 to 4 s. This document assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board**. At 20×20 mm, derate the burst by roughly 50% or more.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

Assumptions:
- Complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step trapezoidal commutation.
- In every 60° sector, two legs carry I_ph.
  - The PWM leg alternates between the high-side (HS) FET (D) and the low-side (LS) FET (1−D). Either way, one R_DS(on) is in path.
  - The return leg has one LS FET fully on.
- So the instantaneous current path always contains **exactly two R_DS(on)**.

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Ripple correction.** With phase inductance L_ph ≈ 10 µH, peak-to-peak ripple is ΔI ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw), which is at most 13 A at D = 0.5:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 14 \approx 6414\ \text{A}^2$$

This adds 0.2%, so it is ignored below.

**Temperature dependence (closed form).** Because R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, solve the coupled equation:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\,P(T_j), \qquad P(T_j) = P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0 (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

The model is stable only if **αR_θP₀ < 1**. This is the thermal-runaway criterion; use it as a design rule check.

**Numbers at 80 A:**

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | **17.1 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |

**Per-FET conduction share.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° of 360°):

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\times\frac{\text{2 FETs conducting}}{\ldots}\;\Rightarrow\; \approx 3.1\ \text{W @ }125\,°\text{C}$$

Within a sector, the instantaneous load on the always-on LS FET is up to 9.3 W. This matters for the thermal transient below the commutation period, but at 80 A eRPM is high and the commutation period is short (< 200 µs). The FET thermal time constant (~ms) therefore averages it out.
