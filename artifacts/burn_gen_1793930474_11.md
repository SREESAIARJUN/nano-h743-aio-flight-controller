## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

These points shape every number below.

1. **The H743 does not run the ESC.** In every production 4‑in‑1 design, the 48 kHz commutation PWM is generated by four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and the STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. This document follows that partition.
2. **"80 A" is a burst rating per motor channel,** not continuous. Section 3 shows that a nano‑class board cannot sustain it. The board is thermally limited to roughly 35 A continuous per channel, and to seconds‑long bursts at 80 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes six‑step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. During each 60° electrical sector:

- one phase is the **PWM leg**: high side and low side alternate with duty D;
- one phase is the **static leg**: its low side is held on;
- the third phase floats and is used for BEMF zero‑cross detection.

The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **two conducting FETs in series**.

### 1.2 RMS Current Including Ripple

Peak‑to‑peak ripple current is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L\,f_{sw}} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

The resulting RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds less than 0.06 % at 80 A, so it is negligible here. It becomes significant only for high‑kV, low‑L motors (L < 5 µH).
