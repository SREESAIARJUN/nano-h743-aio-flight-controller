# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #3)

**Scope:** Power-stage loss model (conduction at 80 A burst, switching at 48 kHz), transient thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

**Note on parameters:** Every device parameter below is a stated assumption, chosen to be representative of a 3.3 × 3.3 mm 40 V MOSFET-class nano stack. Before release, substitute datasheet values for your selected parts. The equations are structured so you only need to replace the inputs.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the MOSFETs. Doing so would require hard-real-time 6-step or FOC control on four motors, which conflicts with ArduPilot's scheduler. The architecture is split into two layers:

| Layer | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 @ 400 MHz, ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 master, eRPM telemetry consumer, harmonic notch |
| ESC (×4) | STM32G071 / AT32F421 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot slave |
| Gate drive (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver (e.g. FD6288-class, 6–12 V gate drive) | Drives 6 N-FETs per motor |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V | 4 motors × 3 half-bridges |

The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an ESC-firmware setting. The H743 only sees DShot.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), exactly **two phase legs** carry I_ph at every instant:
- One leg is static (high-side or low-side held ON).
- One leg is PWM-modulated, with current alternating between its high-side FET (duty D) and its low-side FET (1 − D).

The total conducting resistance is therefore always 2·R_ds(on), independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence and Fixed-Point Solution

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + 0.005(T_j - 25)]$$

Because T_j depends on loss and loss depends on R_ds(T_j), the operating point is the solution of:

$$T_j = T_b + R_{th,jb}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

Iterate starting from T_j = T_b. It converges in 3–4 steps, because the loop gain R_th·∂P/∂T is much less than 1. The model diverges (thermal runaway) only if R_th,jb · 2I²·R_ds,25·0.005 ≥ 1.

### 2.3 Numerical Result at T_j = 110 °C

- R_ds(110) = 1.2 mΩ × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ**
- P_cond,motor = 2 × 80² × 1.71 m = **21.9 W**

### 2.4 Distribution Across the 6 FETs (One Electrical Revolution)

Each FET is in the conduction path for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). Within that window, the split depends on whether the FET is the static or PWM member of the pair.

Average per FET:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2}{6}P_{cond,motor} = 7.3\ \text{W}$$

**Worst case:** firmware that always PWMs the high side, at D → 1. The high-side FETs then carry I² over their full 120° window: 80² × 1.71 m × 1/3 ≈ 3.65 W from that window, plus their static share. Use **7.3 W** as the design average and apply a 1.2× hotspot factor for the PWM-side device.
