## 0. Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It does not commutate the motors. A working 4-in-1 AIO uses this split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (TFBGA100) or VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, sensor fusion, DShot master |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6, AM32 firmware | 6-step / sinusoidal commutation, BDShot slave |
| Gate drivers (×4) | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap, 250 V/µs class) | Half-bridge drive |
| Power stage | 24× 40 V N-MOSFET, PQFN 3.3×3.3 | 4 motors × 3 legs × 2 FETs |

This split drives the DShot allocation (Section 4). The H743 timers transmit DShot600 to four on-board ESC MCUs and capture their GCR-encoded eRPM replies.

**Form-factor warning (key finding).** A "nano" board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) cannot dissipate 80 A continuous per motor. The analysis below shows that 80 A is valid only as a **burst of about 1–3 s** and is thermally limited by board heat capacity. Marketing should state the burst duration and its conditions, not a bare "80 A."

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Topology-level derivation

The motor runs with complementary (synchronous) PWM and 6-step commutation. During any 60° electrical sector, current flows through two legs:

- **PWM leg:** high-side for time D·T_s, low-side (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_s. Neglecting dead time, the current always flows through exactly one R_DS(on) of this leg.
- **Static leg:** low-side FET held fully ON.

So the instantaneous conduction path always contains exactly two R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{pcb}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,k(T_j)
$$
