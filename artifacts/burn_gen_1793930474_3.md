# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Job #49: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

The models below rest on stated assumptions. Each one marked **[VERIFY]** must be replaced with the actual datasheet value before design release.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor (phase current, 6-step trapezoidal) |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, synchronous rectification |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W **[VERIFY]** |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm mounting (≈36×36 mm outline), 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers |

**Architecture corrections. Please read these before using the numbers.**

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** It has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1, TIM8). HRTIM cannot cleanly serve 12 half-bridges with independent BEMF timing. The standard and correct partition is:
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
   - **4× dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) receive bidirectional DShot600.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs 80 A.** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A, even as a burst. Section 3 shows why. The model below uses 30.5×30.5 mm. Treat 80 A as a **≤1–2 s burst rating**, not continuous.
3. **Current sensing.** Use **one shared bus shunt**, not per-phase shunts. At 320 A aggregate, even 0.1 mΩ dissipates 10 W. Two to three 0.1 mΩ 2512 shunts in parallel, or a Hall sensor such as the ACS772 class, is the realistic choice.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 ≈ **1.45 mΩ** (use 1.5 mΩ with margin).

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly **two** FET channels:

- **Commutating leg:** the high side conducts during D, and the synchronous low side conducts during (1−D). One channel is always on.
- **Static leg:** its low side is on for the full 60° sector.

Body-diode conduction is confined to dead time, which is handled in §2.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

Ripple correction for inductor current ripple ΔI (triangular):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

With motor L ≈ 15 µH and worst case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} \approx 8.75\ \text{A} \Rightarrow +6.4\ \text{A}^2 \ \text{(negligible, } +0.1\%)$$

### 1.3 Numbers

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per ESC channel (hot) | 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** |
| P_cond per conducting FET | **9.6 W** |
| P_cond, 4 motors | **76.8 W** |
| Copper trace/plane loss (estimate: 0.3 mΩ path per motor) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ × 4 = **7.7 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per switching event pair (one commutating leg per motor):

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns (driver ≈ 1 A source/sink, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode → high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals per period, t_dt = 50 ns, V_F = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_F I\,t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Summary per motor

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 19.20 |
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total per ESC** | **≈ 20.6 W** |
| **×4 + copper** | **≈ 90 W** |

**Design insight:** conduction loss is about 93% of the total. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only ≈0.7 W per motor. Spend effort on R_DS(on) and copper, not slower switching. Note that 48 kHz does have a real cost: it raises the minimum on-time and dead-time fraction at low throttle.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state junction rise (single conducting FET)

$$T_j = T_{PCB} + P_{FET}(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,vias})$$

With R_θ,vias ≈ 1.5 K/W (an array of about 20 × 0.3 mm filled vias under the drain pad):

$$\Delta T_{j-PCB} = 9.6 \times (1.2 + 1.5) \approx \mathbf{26\ K}$$

### 3.2 Board as lumped thermal capacitance (governs burst duration)

| Element | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (8 layers × 70 µm, ~85% fill, 36×36 mm) | 7.7×10⁻⁷ m³ × 8960 × 385 | 2.66 J/K |
| FR4 (1.6 mm) | 2.07×10⁻⁶ m³ × 1850 × 1100 | 4.2 J/K |
| FETs, caps, connectors (estimate) | | ~1.5 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 8.4 J/K** |

Board-to-air resistance with prop wash (≈5–10 m/s): R_θ,BA ≈ 4 K/W, so τ = R·C ≈ 34 s.

$$T_{PCB}(t) = T_a + P R_{\theta}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau})$$

Steady state would be 90 W × 4 K/W = 360 K rise, which is impossible. **80 A is therefore strictly a burst rating.**

Allowed burst, with T_a = 40 °C, starting from a 60 °C cruise, and T_PCB,max = 100 °C (giving T_j ≈ 126 °C):

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{P R_\theta - (T_0 - T_a)}\right) = -34 \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{40}{340}\right) \approx \mathbf{4.2\ s}$$

The adiabatic approximation (ΔT·C/P = 40 × 8.4 / 90 ≈ 3.7 s) agrees.
