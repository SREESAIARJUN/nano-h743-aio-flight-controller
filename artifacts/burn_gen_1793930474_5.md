# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #24): System Architecture Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Findings

Three architectural points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an ArduPilot AIO, the H743 runs the autopilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 frames. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The DMA and timer model therefore applies to the H743, and the power-stage model applies to the ESC section.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A RMS motor phase current per channel**, for a duration of seconds. Battery current is lower by roughly the duty cycle (I_bat ≈ D·I_ph).
3. **No MOSFET was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Replace the parameters in Table 1 with your BOM part's datasheet values; every equation below is parametric.

The key thermal finding, derived in Section 3, is this: **80 A per channel on a nano-format board is only survivable as a burst of about 1.5–2 s.** The continuous rating is set by the board's convective path, not by the silicon. Your marketing and ArduPilot current limits should reflect this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry the full phase current I:
- **The PWM'd leg** conducts through the high-side FET for a fraction D of each period and through the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1−D. Over a full period this leg therefore always presents one R_ds in the path.
- **The static low-side leg** presents a second R_ds.

The total conduction loss per channel is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.005\,/\mathrm{K}$$

Adding PCB copper, a phase-node trace R_Cu of about 0.3 mΩ round-trip:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{ds}+R_{Cu})$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_ds | P_FET (W) | P_Cu (W) | Total per channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold, T_j = 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 | 1.9 | 14.7 W |
| Hot, T_j = 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 | 1.9 | **21.1 W** |

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each of the six FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time. Its time-averaged conduction loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{6}P_{cond,FETs} = 3.2\ \mathrm{W}\ (\text{hot})$$

The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R_ds = 9.6 W. At low electrical frequency (startup or stall), the die sees close to that peak. Use the transient Z_θ curve, not the average.

**Thermal runaway check.** The R_ds positive feedback converges if:

$$\frac{dP}{dT}\cdot R_{\theta} < 1$$

Here dP/dT = 2I²R_ds,25·α = 0.064 W/K per channel. Against R_θ,JC of 1.5 K/W this is stable. Against a poor board-to-ambient path it is not stable in steady state, which is consistent with the burst-only conclusion.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per switching period, one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off occur in the high-side FET. The low-side FET switches at near zero volts (ZVS via the body diode) but incurs dead-time and reverse-recovery losses.
