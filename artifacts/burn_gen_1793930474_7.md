# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #20)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Several constraints shape the rest of this design. Each is quantified in the sections below.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 does not drive the gates. A 4-in-1 ESC uses one ESC MCU per channel (or a dual-motor MCU), running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The FC's job is to send DShot and receive eRPM. | 48 kHz is the ESC-side PWM frequency. It is independent of the FC timer configuration. |
| F2 | 80A burst on all four channels draws 320A from the battery bus. At 6S that is roughly 7–8 kW. | Battery pads, XT90-class leads and bus capacitance dominate the design, not the FETs. |
| F3 | Total loss at full burst is about 86 W (4 × 21.5 W) on a small PCB with roughly 4–5 J/K of thermal mass. | Simultaneous 80A on all channels is limited to about 2–4 s. A realistic per-channel continuous rating is 40–50A with airflow. |
| F4 | The smallest H743 packages are LQFP100 (14×14 mm) and TFBGA100 (8×8 mm). | A "nano" 20×20 mm stack needs the TFBGA100 (STM32H743VIH6) with 6–8 layer HDI. 80A-class FETs realistically require a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger ESC board. |

All numbers below use stated assumptions so you can substitute datasheet values for the actual parts.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In any 6-step commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (HS at duty D, LS synchronous at 1−D), one phase has its LS fully on, and one phase floats.

**Per-sector, per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,D + I^2 R_{hot}(1-D) + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

The loss is independent of D because synchronous rectification keeps one FET pair always conducting.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Per-FET loss, which sets the thermal design.** Over an electrical revolution, each phase leg is PWM-active for 1/3 of the time, LS-solid for 1/3, and floating for 1/3. For a single FET:

- HS FET: $I^2R(D/3)$
- LS FET: $I^2R[(1-D)/3 + 1/3]$

The worst case is the LS FET at D → 0 (low-throttle, high-current braking or prop-wash cases):

$$P_{LS,max} = 80^2 \times 1.5\text{m} \times \tfrac{2}{3} = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

At D = 0.5, P_HS = 1.6 W and P_LS = 4.8 W.

With **two paralleled FETs per position**, R_eff halves and every figure above halves (9.6 W per channel). This is strongly recommended for 80A.

**Copper (PCB) loss.** Assume 2 oz copper (0.25 mΩ/□ at hot temperature) with 4 paralleled power layers, giving 0.0625 mΩ/□. With about 3 □ from the battery pad to each FET leg and the same again to the motor pad:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 0.0625\text{m} \approx \mathbf{2.4\ W\ per\ channel}$$

At D = 0.5 the shared battery bus also carries 4 × 80 × 0.5 = 160 A average. Size the bus planes accordingly and consider soldered bus bars.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Per channel, one HS FET hard-switches and one LS FET soft-switches (ZVS via the body diode) per PWM period.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap (HS) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | ½ · 1 nF · 635 · 48k = **0.015 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 · V_f · I · t_dt · f_sw | 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48k = **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in R_g and driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 2 · 50 nC · 10 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.33 W per channel** |

**Design lever:** dead-time loss is the largest switching term.
