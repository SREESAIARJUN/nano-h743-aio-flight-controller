# STM32H743 AIO + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Form Factor): Engineering Design Package, Job #50

## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Stated Assumptions

Several points in the brief need resolving before any math is meaningful.

1. **Which MCU does what.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The **48 kHz switching** is performed by the ESC's own per-channel MCUs (one per motor, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), driving the gate drivers. The H743 never touches the bridge PWM. The design below reflects this split.
2. **80 A on a nano board is a burst rating, not continuous.** The math in Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four channels produces more than 130 W in a board of about 20×20 to 25×25 mm. That is only survivable for roughly 1 to 3 s. I size it honestly rather than to the marketing number.
3. **All component parameters are representative placeholders.** They are typical of the class of part, not quoted from a specific datasheet. Substitute your actual BOM values into the equations; the model is the deliverable.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

For any commutation sector (e.g. A+ / B−), the current path depends on the PWM state:

- **PWM ON:** High-side A (Q_AH) and low-side B (Q_BL) conduct.
- **PWM OFF (synchronous rectification):** Low-side A (Q_AL) and low-side B (Q_BL) conduct.

So **two FETs are always in series with the phase current**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{AH} + (1-D)\,R_{AL} + R_{BL} \right] \approx 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0071\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(110\,°C) = 1.5 \times 1.6 = 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

This is a positive feedback loop: losses raise T_j, which raises R, which raises losses. The fixed point exists only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\, R_{\theta} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha\, R_\theta < 1
$$

$$
2 (6400)(0.0015)(0.0071)(2.0) = 0.27 < 1 \quad \checkmark \text{ (no thermal runaway at FET level)}
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Instantaneous dissipation in each conducting FET is **15.4 W**.

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for roughly 1/3 of the cycle in a given position, so the mean is about **5.1 W per FET**. This averaging is valid because the electrical frequency (typically above 1 kHz at load) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (about 1 to 5 ms).

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, should not be)

Phase pad / trace resistance, assuming 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, ~0.3 mΩ per phase path:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \times R_{Cu} \times 2\ \text{phases active} = 6400 \times 0.0006 = 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

Bus current shunt (0.25 mΩ). Battery current is approximately the sum over channels of D × I_ph. At 80 A phase and D ≈ 0.85, that is about 68 A per channel, or 272 A total. This is not a realistic battery draw on a nano, so in practice the battery and its sag limit this burst. At a realistic 160 A total:

$$
P_{shunt} = 160^2 \times 0.00025 = 6.4\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge (phase A in the example sector) commutates hard. That is one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per PWM period.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

This is a conservative upper bound; the exact value is $\tfrac12 C_{oss,eff}V^2 f$.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}
$$
