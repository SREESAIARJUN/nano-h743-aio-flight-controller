## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Up-Front Engineering Flags

**Architecture assumed.** This is an AIO (all-in-one) board. The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Four independent ESC MCUs (AM32/BLHeli_32-class, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071) each drive one three-phase bridge. The H743 does **not** switch the FETs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not by the H743.

**Representative component parameters.** Replace these with your actual datasheet values. Every equation below is parameterized so you can substitute directly.

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I_pk | Burst phase current per motor | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_DS(on),25 | FET on-resistance at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V (40 V, PDFN5x6 class) | 1.2 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.006 /K |
| t_r + t_f | Effective V–I overlap (driver ~1 A, R_G ≈ 4.7 Ω) | 20 ns |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 40 nC |
| Q_oss | Output charge | 40 nC |
| Q_rr | Reverse-recovery charge | 30 nC |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | Dead time per edge | 50 ns |
| V_GS | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |

**Flag 1: 80 A on a "nano" footprint.** On a 20×20 or 30×30 mm board, 80 A per motor can only be a **seconds-scale burst** rating, not a continuous one. Section 3 proves this quantitatively. If the 80 A figure is meant as the *total* for all four motors (20 A each), the losses fall by ~16× and the design becomes comfortable. Confirm which definition the spec intends before layout freeze.

**Flag 2: FET package.** A 3.3×3.3 mm DFN FET typically has R_DS(on) ≈ 2–3 mΩ, which roughly doubles conduction loss. The model assumes 5×6 mm-class parts. That is only feasible on a 30×30 mm board with FETs on both sides.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

During any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET carries I for fraction D of each period. The complementary low-side FET carries I for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on and carries I for 100% of the sector.
- **Phase C:** floating; used for back-EMF sensing.

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used (dead-time diode conduction is handled in §2.4). The I² factor is exact for flat-top current; for ripple, use $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$.

### 1.3 Temperature coupling and closed-form fixed point

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta} \cdot P(T_j)$$

Solving the loop gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta (P_0 + 2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha))}{1 - 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta}$$

where P_0 is the temperature-independent loss (switching plus diode). This exposes a **thermal-runaway criterion**:

$$2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_\theta < 1$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ × 0.006 = 0.092 W/K. Runaway therefore occurs if R_θ(junction→reference) exceeds about **10.8 K/W**. Steady-state burst operation thus requires a thermal path below ~10 K/W per motor. That is achievable for the FET-to-board path but **not** for board-to-air on a nano board (see §3).

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 89.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.92 mΩ | 24.6 W | 98.3 W |

**Distribution within one motor.** The return-leg low-side FET is the hottest device, at I²R ≈ 11.1 W at 100 °C. The PWM-leg pair shares the other ~11.1 W in the ratio D : (1−D). Six-step commutation rotates the roles, so the time-averaged loss per FET is ≈ 22.3/6 × 2 ≈ 7.4 W during the burst. The *instantaneous* worst FET still sees 11.1 W for one sector, which lasts tens to hundreds of µs.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg (one FET pair per motor at a time).

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The ½CV² formulation underestimates this loss for nonlinear C_oss, so the charge-based form is used.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode → high-side turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so treat this as a lower bound at 80 A.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \,(2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$
