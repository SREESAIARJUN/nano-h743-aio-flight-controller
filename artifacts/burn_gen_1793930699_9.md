## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Use the amplitude-invariant Clarke transform, followed by the Park transform with electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt3}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.2 PMSM dq model

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

Here λ_m = K_t / (1.5 p) for a surface-mount PMSM, and electromagnetic torque is τ = 1.5 p λ_m i_q = K_t i_q.

### 1.3 Current PI by pole-zero cancellation

Place the PI zero on the electrical pole R/L. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_{p,i} = \omega_c L,\qquad K_{i,i} = \omega_c R
\quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{i}{i^*}=\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

With the assumed parameters: K_p,i = 2π·1500·0.0021 ≈ 19.8 V/A and K_i,i = 2π·1500·5.6 ≈ 52 780 V/(A·s).

Keep ω_c ≤ ω_PWM / 15. This leaves margin for the 1.5-sample delay (computation plus PWM update) of digital control. That delay contributes a phase lag of ω_c · 1.5 T_s ≈ 20° at 1.5 kHz.

### 1.4 Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

$$
v_d^* = PI_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = PI_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

### 1.5 Voltage limiting and SVPWM

Limit the voltage vector to the inscribed hexagon circle, |v| ≤ V_bus/√3. Give priority to the d-axis:

$$
v_q^{max}=\sqrt{(V_{bus}/\sqrt3)^2 - v_d^{*2}}
$$

SVPWM uses min-max zero-sequence injection, which is equivalent to classic sector-based SVPWM. Starting from the inverse-Clarke phase voltages:

$$
v_{0} = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_a,v_b,v_c)+\min(v_a,v_b,v_c)\right),\qquad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

### 1.6 Anti-windup by back-calculation

For a PI with output u = sat(u_unsat):

$$
\dot x_I = K_i e + K_{aw}(u - u_{unsat}),\qquad K_{aw}=\frac{1}{T_t},\quad T_t=\sqrt{T_iT_d}\ \text{or}\ T_t\approx \frac{K_p}{K_i}\ \text{for PI}
$$
