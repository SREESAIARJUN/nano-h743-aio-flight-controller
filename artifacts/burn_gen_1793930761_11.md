## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

An STM32H743 does not commutate the motors directly. It has no hardware path suited to 4× independent 6-step or FOC control with BEMF zero-crossing comparators, and ArduPilot does not run ESC firmware. The system therefore splits into two parts:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, BDShot600 master |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | — | Drives 6 N-FETs per motor |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | — | 4 × 3 half-bridges |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification
At any commutation sector, two phases conduct. One leg is PWM'd (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D) and the other leg's LS is held on. The current I therefore always flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} \right] + I^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

The approximation is independent of D when R_HS ≈ R_LS.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R(125^\circ\text{C}) = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss
Assume 0.5 mΩ effective trace and pad resistance per motor current loop (6-layer, 2 oz outer and inner):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

### 1.5 Self-consistency (electro-thermal iteration)
R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Solve iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}\big(R(T_j^{(n)})\big)\cdot R_{\theta JC}
$$

Commutation period at high eRPM (~100 µs) ≪ FET thermal τ (~ms). Each motor's loss is therefore time-averaged over its 6 FETs: P_FET ≈ 22 W / 6 ≈ 3.7 W. This gives ΔT_JC ≈ 5.6 K, so the iteration converges in 2 steps. **T_j is dominated by board temperature, not junction rise.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.97 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw = 25.2·50 nC·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | V·Q_oss·f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·50 nC·10·48 k | 0.05 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.74 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity.** Overlap and dead-time losses scale linearly with f_sw, at ≈ 33 mW/kHz per motor. Moving 24 → 48 kHz therefore costs ≈ 0.8 W per motor. That cost is acceptable because 48 kHz reduces current ripple, motor iron loss, and audible noise on small low-inductance motors.

**Dead-time trade-off.** Halving t_dt to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. The risk is shoot-through across the FD6288 propagation mismatch (±~20 ns), so verify on the bench with a current probe on the DC link.

## 3. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (All 4 Motors)

| Item | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 7.0 W |
| Gate drivers + LDO + MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × D. At D ≈ 0.9 this is ≈ 7.3 kW, so η ≈ 98.6%. The efficiency is fine; the **power density** is the problem.
