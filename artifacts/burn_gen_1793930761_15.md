## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three issues must be settled before the math is meaningful.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and does not implement BLDC commutation. The architecture is therefore an AIO board with:

- **STM32H743** running ArduPilot. It is the DShot600 bidirectional master.
- **4 × ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, one per motor. An example is the AT32F421 or STM32G071 class.
- **4 × three-phase bridges** of 6 MOSFETs each, giving 24 FETs total.

The 48 kHz figure is the ESC bridge PWM frequency. It is set by the ESC firmware, not by the H743.

### 0.2 "80 A burst" on a nano board is a short-duration rating

A 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot reject approximately 100 W continuously. Section 3 shows that the realistic rating is roughly **1–2 s with all four channels at 80 A**, or longer if only one channel bursts. The spec sheet should state burst duration and duty explicitly.

All device parameters below are representative values. Verify them against the datasheets of the parts you actually select. The equations are parametric, so you can substitute real values directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous complementary PWM on the active leg. At any instant, one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current. Either the active FET or its complementary FET conducts during each PWM sub-interval.

At burst conditions, D → ~1, and the current path is always exactly two R_ds,on in series.

### 1.2 RMS Current With Ripple

The peak-to-peak ripple is:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\, f_{sw}}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\Delta I_{pp,max}\big|_{D=0.5} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}
$$

The RMS current is:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contribution is 0.06%, so it can be neglected at burst.

### 1.3 Temperature-Dependent R_ds,on and Electrothermal Feedback

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.
