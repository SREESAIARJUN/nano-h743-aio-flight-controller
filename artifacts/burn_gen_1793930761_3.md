# STM32H743 Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #24)
## Design Model: 80 A Burst Thermals, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Warnings

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. The standard and recommended topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (×4)──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                   └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

The H743 is too far from the power stage, and too busy, to do commutation, zero-crossing detection, or FOC for four motors in ArduPilot. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU.

### 0.2 Feasibility warning on "nano" and 80 A

On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per motor is not physically achievable. The constraints are copper cross-section, FET footprint, and heat capacity. My recommendations:

| Form factor | Realistic burst (≤5 s) | Continuous |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 mm "nano" | 35–45 A | 25–30 A |
| 25.5×25.5 mm | 50–60 A | 35–40 A |
| 30.5×30.5 mm, 8 L / 2 oz outer | 70–80 A | 45–55 A |

The math below is fully parameterized, so you can plug in your board. Worked numbers assume a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer board with 2 oz outer and 1 oz inner copper. All MOSFET values are placeholders. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This is coupled to junction temperature, so the system is solved iteratively in §4.

### 1.2 Trapezoidal (6-step) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct:
- One high-side FET (HS), PWM duty D.
- One low-side FET (LS) of another phase, held on.
- During PWM off-time, the complementary LS in the HS leg conducts after dead time.

The current path therefore always passes through exactly two FET channels. The total bridge conduction loss is:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The loss is independent of D under synchronous rectification. Per FET over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts 1/3 of the time:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right] I^2 R_{ds}$$

The low-side FETs run hotter. This matters for layout: give the LS FETs the better thermal path.

### 1.3 Worked numbers (80 A, T_j = 110 °C → R_ds = 1.2 × 1.38 = 1.66 mΩ)

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.66\text{ m}\Omega = 21.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

Per-FET values at D = 0.9 (full-throttle burst):

| FET | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| HS | ⅓ · 0.9 · 6400 · 1.66 m | 3.19 W |
| LS | ⅓ · 1.1 · 6400 · 1.66 m | 3.90 W |

The instantaneous dissipation while conducting is 80² × 1.66 m = 10.6 W. This sets the per-pulse junction rise in §4.3.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper (often larger than the FETs)

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t}, \quad \rho_{Cu,25} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m},\ \alpha_{Cu} = 0.0039$$

Example: a 2 oz (70 µm) trace, 4 mm wide and 10 mm long, at 25 °C:

$$R = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.01}{0.004 \times 70\times10^{-6}} = 0.61\text{ m}\Omega$$

That is half of one FET. The phase and bus paths must be poured on multiple layers in parallel with via stitching (§6.3). Target ≤0.3 mΩ total per current path, which adds about 2 W per motor at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In trapezoidal control, only one leg switches at a time (HS PWM, LS complement). Each loss term below is per motor.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

The effective rise time from the gate driver is:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} \cdot (R_g + R_{drv})}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_total = 4 Ω, and a 10 V drive against a 4.5 V plateau, t_r ≈ 8.7 ns. This is consistent with the 20 ns sum.

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.042\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr scales up. Budget 2× in practice, giving 0.1 W.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Design lever:** cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. Verify against the driver's propagation mismatch first.

### 2.5 Gate drive

The loss is mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss summary per motor (80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.97 |
| Coss | 0.04 |
| Qrr (2× margin) | 0.10 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total switching** | **1.76** |

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) = 1.76 \times \frac{f}{48\text{k}}$$

| f_sw | 24 kHz | 48 kHz | 96 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| P_sw per motor | 0.88 W | 1.76 W | 3.52 W |

At 80 A, conduction dominates (21 W vs 1.8 W). 48 kHz is a reasonable choice: it gives low acoustic noise and low current ripple on low-inductance motors, with little thermal cost.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Item | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j 110 °C) | 21.2 W | 84.8 W |
| Copper/PCB conduction (0.3 mΩ) | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching | 1.8 W | 7.0 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, total current path) | — | ~1.6 W* |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, MCUs | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~25 W** | **~102 W** |

*The shunt sits on the common return. Its loss is I_total² × R. At a realistic 4 × 50 A average = 200 A, this would be 10 W, so use 0.1 mΩ or Hall/per-phase sensing. Note that 4 × 80 A simultaneous equals 320 A from the pack, beyond any reasonable XT60/XT90 and pack combination. The thermal model below conservatively assumes all four motors at 80 A.

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 4 × 22.2 V × 80 A × 0.9 ≈ 6.4 kW, so η_ESC ≈ 98.4%.
