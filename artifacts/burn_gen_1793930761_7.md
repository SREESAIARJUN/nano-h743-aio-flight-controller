## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Check

### 0.1 Who switches the FETs

The STM32H743 does **not** switch the MOSFETs. In a 4-in-1 stack:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (4 wires)──► 4× ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 / AM32)
                                                        │ 48 kHz complementary PWM
                                                        ▼
                                                   Gate driver ─► 6 FETs per channel (24 total)
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an **ESC firmware setting** (AM32 supports up to 48 kHz). The H743 only sets the throttle and reads eRPM telemetry.

### 0.2 Interpreting "80A burst"

The current interpretation changes the outcome completely:

| Interpretation | Battery current (4 motors) | Verdict for nano form factor (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) |
|---|---|---|
| **A: 80A per channel** | 320 A | **Not physically viable.** XT60 and 12 AWG leads, bus copper, and FET packages all exceed limits within milliseconds to seconds. |
| **B: 80A aggregate** (20A/ch) | 80 A | Viable for bursts of about 5–10 s with airflow. |

Both cases are computed below. Case A is retained to show quantitatively why it fails.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology state analysis (trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (HS/LS complementary), one is held low (LS on), and one floats.

- **ON state (duty D):** HS(PWM leg) → motor → LS(return leg)
- **OFF state (1−D), synchronous:** LS(PWM leg) → motor → LS(return leg)

So **two FETs always carry the phase current I**, regardless of duty:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25}
$$

### 2.2 Ripple correction

Ripple adds a small term. With phase inductance L_ph and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI ≈ V_bus·D(1−D)/(2 L_ph f_sw):

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}
$$

For L_ph = 15 µH and D = 0.5: ΔI = 25.2·0.25/(2·15e-6·48e3) = 4.4 A, so ΔI²/12 = 1.6 A². This is negligible at 80 A and about 0.4 % at 20 A.

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Over a full electrical cycle each FET is active about 1/3 of the time in the role above:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx P_{ch}/3, \qquad \text{but } P_{FET,peak\,(sector)} \approx I^2 R_{ds}
$$

At low eRPM (stall or punch-out), the sector dwell time approaches the thermal time constant of the die. You must then design for **peak**, not average.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one HS/LS pair per channel at a time.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of HS)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then the HS turns on into it:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}
$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\quad(\text{using }E_{oss}\approx\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V)
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two edges per period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET. Two FETs switch on the PWM leg:

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw}
$$

| Term | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| P_VI = ½·25.2·I·20n·48k | 0.968 W | 0.242 W |
| P_rr = 30n·25.2·48k | 0.036 W | 0.036 W |
| P_oss = ½·40n·25.2·48k | 0.024 W | 0.024 W |
| P_dt = 0.8·I·200n·48k | 0.614 W | 0.154 W |
| P_gate = 2·40n·10·48k | 0.038 W | 0.038 W |
| **P_sw per channel** | **1.68 W** | **0.49 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 24 to 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss. The benefit is lower current ripple and acoustic noise.

## 4. Copper and Interconnect Losses (Often the Real Limiter)

### 4.1 Bus plane

Trace resistance is R = ρL/(w·t), with ρ_Cu = 1.72e-8 Ω·m and 2 oz copper (t = 70 µm). For a 15 mm long, 8 mm wide bus on two parallel layers:

$$
R = \frac{1.72\text{e-}8 \cdot 0.015}{0.008\cdot 70\text{e-}6 \cdot 2} \approx 0.23\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

| | Case A (320 A total) | Case B (80 A total) |
|---|---|---|
| Bus plane, ×2 for (+)/(−) | 2·320²·0.23m = **47 W** | 2·80²·0.23m = **2.9 W** |
| Phase pads and motor lead joints, about 0.3 mΩ per phase × 2 conducting | 3.8 W/ch | 0.24 W/ch |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, if present) | 1.6 W/ch | 0.1 W/ch |

### 4.2 Bus capacitor ripple

$$
I_{C,rms}\approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{at }D=0.5,\; I_{C,rms}=I/2\ \text{per channel}
$$

Case A therefore needs about 40 A_rms per channel of ripple capability, which **exceeds any nano-board cap bank by roughly 10×**. Case B needs about 10 A_rms per channel. Interleaving across four ESC MCUs helps, but this is not guaranteed because AM32 PWM is not phase-synchronized.
