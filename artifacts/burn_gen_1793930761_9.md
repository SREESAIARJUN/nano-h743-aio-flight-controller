# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #3)

## 0. Feasibility Problems to Resolve First

Several assumptions in the brief need correcting before the math is useful.

| Issue | Reality | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** | In every production AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8BB21) running AM32 or BLHeli. | The 48 kHz PWM is set in AM32/BLHeli. It is not an ArduPilot or H743 timer parameter. |
| **"80 A burst" on a nano board** | A nano board is 20×20 mm (or a 25.5×25.5 mm whoop). That leaves roughly 3×3 mm FET packages and about 4–5 J/K total thermal mass. | 80 A per channel is only viable for under 1–2 s, and only on a full-size 30.5 mm board with 5×6 mm FETs. Section 2 quantifies this. |
| **Unspecified parameters** | Battery voltage, FET part number and burst duration were not given. | I fix explicit assumptions below. Substitute your datasheet values into the formulas. |

**Baseline assumptions**

- 6S pack, V_bus = 25.2 V.
- 40 V N-ch FETs, 5×6 mm PQFN class:
  - R_ds(on) = 0.9 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V.
  - Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 80 nC.
  - V_SD = 0.8 V.
  - R_th(j-pcb) ≈ 2 K/W.
- Gate driver FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink) at V_gs ≈ 10 V.
- Effective t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at 80 A. Dead time t_dt = 200 ns per edge.
- Complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step trapezoidal commutation, as AM32 does by default.
- I_ph = 80 A motor phase current (the burst rating).

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · (T_j / 298 K)^α, with α ≈ 2.0–2.3 for modern trench FETs.

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 0.9 mΩ × (398/298)^2.2 ≈ **1.70 × 0.9 = 1.44 mΩ**.

This creates a positive feedback loop: losses raise T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises losses. Section 2.3 checks for runaway.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In any 60° commutation sector of 6-step drive:

- The **PWM leg** conducts I_ph through the HS FET for duty D, and through the LS FET for (1−D) via synchronous rectification.
- The **static leg** conducts I_ph through its LS (or HS) FET for 100 % of the sector.

P_cond,ch = I_ph² · R_ds · [D + (1−D)] + I_ph² · R_ds = **2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T_j)**

This is independent of duty, which is a useful property of synchronous PWM.

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond / channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W | 46 W |
| 100 °C | 1.28 mΩ | 16.4 W | 66 W |
| 125 °C | 1.44 mΩ | **18.4 W** | **74 W** |

Add the PCB copper path. A phase trace of 6 layers × 2 oz, ~5 mm wide × 8 mm long has R ≈ 0.25 mΩ per layer-set at 25 °C:

P_Cu ≈ 2 · 80² · 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W/ch** (hot).

### 1.3 Switching loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one hard-switched HS turn-on/turn-off pair per period.

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching)**

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
= 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 kHz = **1.94 W**

**(b) Body-diode conduction during dead time** (two edges per period)

P_dt = V_SD · I_ph · 2 · t_dt · f_sw
= 0.8 · 80 · 2 · 200 ns · 48 kHz = **1.23 W**

This term is the reason to minimize dead time. AM32 lets you set it; aim for 100–150 ns if the driver and FET permit.

**(c) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovered at HS turn-on)

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.06 W**

A Q_rr-induced HS turn-on loss term adds roughly 0.05 W.

**(d) Output capacitance** (one HS and one LS device per period)

P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw · 2 = 0.5 · 80 nC · 25.2 · 48 k · 2 = **0.10 W**

**(e) Gate charge** (dissipated mostly in the FD6288 and gate resistors)

P_g = Q_g · V_gs · f_sw · 2 = 60 nC · 10 · 48 k · 2 = **0.058 W/ch**

The driver supply current is Q_g·f·2 ≈ 5.8 mA per channel. This is small, but it matters for the 10–12 V gate rail LDO when summed over 4 channels.

### 1.4 Loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W/ch | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 18.4 | 73.7 |
| PCB copper | 3.8 | 15.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.21 | 0.8 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.6 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

Efficiency check: delivered motor power is roughly D·V·I. At D = 0.8 that is about 1.6 kW per channel, so the 25.6 W loss is about 1.6 %. The efficiency is fine. **Heat density is the problem**, not efficiency.

**Frequency sensitivity.** The switching-related terms scale linearly with f_sw.

- At 24 kHz they total about 1.7 W/ch.
- At 96 kHz they total about 6.8 W/ch.

At 80 A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between acoustic noise and current ripple. Ripple is ΔI = V/(4·L·f), with phase L ≈ 20 µH for a typical 2207 motor.
