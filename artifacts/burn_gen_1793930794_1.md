## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Assumptions

### 0.1 What the H743 drives

In an ArduPilot AIO, the **STM32H743 does not commutate the motors**. It sends DShot600 frames to four ESC MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. Each ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32-class part) generates the 48 kHz 6-step PWM.

| Domain | Device | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot, attitude control, DShot600 output, bdshot eRPM capture, harmonic notch |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or equivalent) ×4 | Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, BEMF sensing, GCR telemetry |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | High/low-side gate drive |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× N-MOSFET per channel (24 total) | 3-phase bridge |

### 0.3 Feasibility flag (please read)

Four channels × 80 A = **320 A aggregate burst**. In a nano form factor, the following are physically limiting, not the MOSFETs:
- the battery pads and XT60 connector (rated ~60 A continuous),
- the main shunt,
- the board's thermal mass.

The model below shows that 80 A is achievable as a **per-channel, time-limited** rating. It is not a simultaneous 4×80 A sustained condition. Treat "80 A" as a datasheet burst figure with a defined duration and ambient temperature.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, one high-side FET of phase *x* and one low-side FET of phase *y* carry the phase current I. With synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the off-interval current freewheels through the complementary low-side FET instead of a body diode. So the current always flows through **exactly two R_DS(on)** elements, apart from dead-time.

**Bridge conduction loss (one ESC channel):**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I^2 R_{Cu}$$

**Per-FET split** (each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\left[(1-D) + 1\right] I^2 R_{DS}$$

- At full throttle (D → 1), losses are symmetric between high-side and low-side.
- At mid-throttle, the low-side FETs run hotter. Place the low-side FETs on the best copper.
