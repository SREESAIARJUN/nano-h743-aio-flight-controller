# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

This is an engineering baseline. Every number below follows from stated assumptions. Replace the assumed parameters with datasheet values for your chosen FETs and board stack-up before committing to layout.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

In a practical H743 AIO, **the H743 does not commutate the motors**. The partitioning is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                              │
                                    3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q)
                                              │
                                     6× N-MOSFET half-bridges per motor
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency. The H743 only generates DShot frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary, synchronous PWM. At any instant, motor current flows through **exactly two conducting devices in series**:

- the PWM leg: its high-side FET for duration D, then its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D;
- the opposite leg's low-side FET, which is on continuously during that 60° sector.

Treating the phase current I as flat-topped:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2\left[R_{DS}(T_j)\,D + R_{DS}(T_j)(1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This total is independent of D. The loss is distributed across the bridge as the sectors rotate.

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.5 \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 2.07\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 2.3 Copper loss

Assume 0.5 mΩ of effective phase-loop copper per motor:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 2.07 mΩ | **26.5 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Per motor** | | **29.7 W** |

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one FET per motor hard-switches at any instant (the PWM-leg high side). Its complement soft-switches after the dead time.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I \cdot 2\,t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.6 Switching total

| Term | Result |
|---|---|
| P_VI | 1.94 W |
| P_oss | 0.023 W |
| P_rr | 0.060 W |
| P_dt | 0.61 W |
| P_g | 0.038 W |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 2.67 W** |

Scaling: P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A, at the cost of higher current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 3.7 Total power-stage loss at 80 A burst

$$
P_{motor} = 29.7 + 2.7 \approx 32.4\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{board} = 4 \times 32.4 \approx \mathbf{130\ W}
$$

That is about 130 W dissipated in a 25 × 25 mm board.

## 4. Thermal Analysis

### 4.1 Steady-state (continuous) capability

- **Heat-transfer coefficient.** Prop-wash forced convection gives h ≈ 80 W/m²K.
- **Effective area.** Both sides: A = 2 × 0.025² = 1.25 × 10⁻³ m².
- **Board-to-ambient resistance:**

$$
\theta_{BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80 \times 1.25\times10^{-3}} = 10\ \text{K/W}
$$

- **Allowed dissipation.** For ΔT = 60 K (40 °C ambient → 100 °C board):

$$
P_{max} = \frac{60}{10} = 6\ \text{W total} = 1.5\ \text{W per motor}
$$

- **Sustainable current.** Solving the per-motor loss equation:

$$
I^2(2R_{DS} + R_{Cu}) + k_{sw} I \approx 1.5\ \text{W}
$$

  where k_sw ≈ 0.033 W/A. This gives **I_cont ≈ 15–17 A per motor**.

### 4.2 Burst (adiabatic) capability

For short bursts, thermal mass dominates rather than convection.

Board thermal capacitance:

| Contributor | Value |
|---|---|
| PCB (~4 g, effective c_p ≈ 0.9 J/g·K) | ≈ 3.6 J/K |
| FETs, capacitors, connectors | ≈ 1.5 J/K |
| **C_th** | **≈ 5 J/K** |
