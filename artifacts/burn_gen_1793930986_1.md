# FOC Motor Control and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

This document gives the equations and C++ code for one gimbal axis. The other two axes are identical instances, and the IMU fusion block is shared by all three.

I have no specification for "Job #42", so the design rests on the assumptions in §0. Every hardware-dependent number is a named constant, so you can substitute your real values.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Loop Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (e.g. GBM2804-class). Pole pairs p = 7. R_s ≈ 5.6 Ω, L_s ≈ 1.8 mH, K_t ≈ 0.07 N·m/A |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A or MT6701 class), read over SPI at 20 kHz |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class with FPU. Center-aligned PWM at 20 kHz. Inline or low-side current sensing |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera/payload frame. Mounted symmetrically about the payload pivot (r₂ = −r₁) |
| Bus | 12–16 V |

**Loop rates and bandwidths**

```
                                     ┌──────── 20 kHz (PWM ISR) ─────────┐
θ_ref ─►[Angle P+FF]─ω_ref─►[Vel PI]─iq_ref─►(+)─►[Current PI d/q]─►[SVPWM]─► Motor
 1 kHz    ▲  BW ≈ 15 Hz      ▲ 4 kHz          ▲    BW ≈ 1.5 kHz        │
          │                  │ BW ≈ 80 Hz     │ iq_cog(θm)             │
          │                  │                └─[Anti-cogging LUT]◄─θm─┤ encoder
    θ_imu (attitude)    ω_gyro·axis                                    │
          └──[Mahony/ESKF]◄──[Dual-IMU fusion + notch/LPF]◄── 2×ICM-42688-P @ 8 kHz
```

**Inertial stabilization.** For a gimbal, the velocity loop is closed on the *inertial* rate of the camera, not on the encoder rate. That rate is the fused gyro rate projected onto the joint axis. This is what rejects the base (drone) motion.

The encoder-PLL rate is still used for:
- FOC commutation,
- cogging compensation,
- a damping blend when the gyro is saturated or faulty.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Current Loop, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

The electrical angle is computed from the mechanical angle, the pole-pair count and a calibrated zero offset:

$$
\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{e,0} \pmod{2\pi}
$$

**Clarke transform** (amplitude-invariant, assumes $i_a+i_b+i_c=0$):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park transform:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad
v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant Model and Current PI Design

The PMSM in the rotor (dq) frame is:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Decoupling feedforward.** Adding these terms leaves each axis as a pure first-order plant $1/(Ls+R)$:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m,\qquad \lambda_m = \frac{2K_t}{3p}
$$
