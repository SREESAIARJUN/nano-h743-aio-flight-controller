# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop, Anti-Cogging Feedforward, and Dual ICM-42688-P Fusion

**Assumptions.** I don't have the Job #49 specification, so I assumed a typical setup:

- Brushless gimbal motors (e.g., GM4108-class, 24N/22P).
- 14-bit absolute encoder per joint.
- Two ICM-42688-P IMUs rigidly mounted on the camera plate.
- An STM32G4/H7-class MCU with an FPU.

**Before you deploy:**

- Replace every parameter marked `// CONFIG` with your hardware values.
- Check the ICM-42688-P anti-aliasing filter (AAF) register values against the datasheet revision you have.

## 1. Loop Architecture and Rates

```
 Attitude ref ──► [Angle loop 1 kHz] ──ω_ref──► [Velocity loop 4 kHz] ──iq_fb──┐
        ▲              ▲                             ▲                         ▼
        │        q̂ (camera)                    ω_j (gyro→joint)        (+)◄── iq_ff (anti-cogging, LMS)
        │              │                             │                         ▼
 Dual IMU 8 kHz ─► [vibration filters + fusion] ─────┘          [FOC current PI 20 kHz] ─► SVPWM
                                                                         ▲
                                                Encoder θ_m ─► θ_e ──────┘
```

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q PI) | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz |
| Velocity (PI) | 4 kHz | ω_v ≈ 2π·150 Hz |
| Angle (P + velocity feedforward) | 1 kHz | ω_a ≈ 2π·30 Hz |
| IMU ODR | 8 kHz (decimated to 4 kHz) | — |

The rule of thumb is roughly 5–10× bandwidth separation between cascaded loops.

**Design choice: what feeds the velocity loop.** The velocity loop is closed on **gyro-measured inertial rate**, projected onto the joint axis. It is *not* closed on the encoder derivative. This is what gives inertial stabilization. The encoder is used only for commutation and for cogging phase.
