# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot DMA Plan, Reference Netlist

## 0. Ground Rules and Three Architectural Findings

Every device parameter below is a **stated assumption**, marked [A]. Swap in datasheet values from your actual MOSFET and driver. The equations stay valid; the numbers will move.

Three architectural facts shape everything that follows:

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel (or all four) is driven by the ESC-side MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421, STM32G071 or STM32F051. The H743 only emits DShot600 frames and captures the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The two timing domains are independent.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The thermal section shows the board heats at roughly 100 K/s at full 4×80 A load.
3. **Phase current is not battery current.** At high duty, four motors at 80 A phase each can approach **~300 A of battery current**. No XT60, pigtail or 20×20 bus plane survives that. Realistic burst is 80 A on one or two channels, or a short whole-craft punch limited by battery sag.

**Commutation model.** Six-step trapezoidal operation with complementary PWM. At any instant two phases conduct: one leg is PWM-switching and one leg's low side (or high side) is held on. Current always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series** (HS/LS pair per active leg).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per motor channel (instantaneous, during conduction)

With synchronous rectification, the switching leg's current alternates between its HS and LS FET. Either way, one FET in that leg carries I_ph. So:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on),hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

### 2.2 Per FET (averaged over an electrical revolution)

Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (4 of 6 sectors). Within that time, the current is shared between the HS and LS of that leg, depending on duty D and sector type. Averaged, each FET carries the I² load about 1/3 of the time:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(0.0024) = \mathbf{5.1\ W}$$

The check closes: 6 FETs × 5.1 W ≈ 30.7 W.

### 2.3 Self-consistency (R_ds depends on T_j)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ [A]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET} \cdot R_{\theta jc}$$

Iterate the two equations. With R_θjc ≈ 1.5 K/W [A], the junction sits about 9 to 10 K above the pad. Conduction loss therefore tracks *board* temperature. That makes the PCB, not the die, the thermal bottleneck.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses (usually forgotten)

- **Phase pad/trace:** about 0.3 mΩ per phase path [A]. Two paths in series per channel gives 80² × 0.6 mΩ = **3.8 W per channel**.
- **Shunt:** typically on the shared bus. At a 0.25 mΩ shunt and I_bus = 200 A, that is **10 W** in one tiny resistor. Use per-channel shunts, or accept that current sensing saturates during burst.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One HS/LS pair per channel commutates per PWM period.

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (Q_rr)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

The Q_rr current also adds to HS turn-on current. Multiply P_sw by about 1.1 if Q_rr is large.

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

### 3.6 Switching subtotal

$$P_{sw,total,ch} \approx 0.97 + 0.05 + 0.04 + 0.61 = \mathbf{1.67\ W}$$

**Design insight.** At 80 A, conduction dominates by about 18:1. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel but raises current ripple and acoustic noise. The better lever is R_ds(on) and copper. Dead time is the largest "switching" term, so tighten it to the driver's minimum safe value. This must be verified on a scope at full V_bus.

### 3.7 Current-ripple sanity check (motor-dependent)

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D (1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH [A], D = 0.5 gives ΔI ≈ 4.4 A_pp. This is negligible against 80 A, so the RMS ≈ DC assumption holds.
