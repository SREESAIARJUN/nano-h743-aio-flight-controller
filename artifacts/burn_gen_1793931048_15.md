# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4‑in‑1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Findings

I have no Job #37 design files, so this design is built from stated assumptions. **Every parameter marked [A] is an assumption. Replace it with the value from your chosen MOSFET or driver datasheet before freezing the design.**

**Three findings that drive the architecture:**

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** Each ESC channel needs its own small MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32, or Bluejay. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 commands those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.
2. **80 A per motor on a nano board is a short-transient rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model in §3 shows a 20×20 mm board heats roughly 2–4× faster than a 30.5×30.5 mm board. Specify the burst as a duration, for example "80 A for ≤ 2 s at 40 °C ambient with airflow," not just as a current.
3. **The H743's own temperature limit constrains the board.** On an AIO board, the MCU, IMU, and barometer share copper with roughly 100 W of switching loss. The H743 is rated to 85 °C or 125 °C ambient depending on temperature-grade suffix. IMU bias drift becomes the practical limit well before that.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

During any 60° commutation sector of 6-step drive, exactly two legs carry the phase current:

- **The PWM'd leg:** the high-side MOSFET conducts for fraction D. The synchronous low-side MOSFET conducts for fraction (1−D). Dead time is small and handled separately.
- **The static leg:** the low-side MOSFET conducts continuously.

At any instant, the current therefore passes through exactly two R_DS(on) in series:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.74\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W}$$

**Per-FET share.** Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so averaged over a rotation each of the 6 FETs carries about 1/3 of the I²R. That gives about 3.7 W per FET. The high-side FET of a leg at D ≈ 1 carries the most and is the hottest device.

**Ripple correction (minor).** Phase inductance smooths the current, so I_rms ≈ I_avg × √(1 + r²/12). With 30 % peak-to-peak ripple (r = 0.3), the correction is +0.4 %, which is negligible.

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only one leg is PWM'd at a time, giving one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_{SD}\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery, Output Capacitance, and Gate Drive

| Loss | Formula | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.036 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48k | 0.058 W (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |

### 2.5 Shunt and Copper

| Loss | Assumption | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current-sense shunt | 0.25 mΩ [A] in the DC return path, carrying ≈ I·D | 80² × 0.25 m | ≤ 1.6 W per channel |
| PCB copper, battery pads to FETs | ≈ 0.3 mΩ [A] effective, shared by 4 channels carrying 4 × 80 × D A | — | Up to ≈ 30 W total at D = 1 |

The copper term is a major and often overlooked contributor. It argues for 2–3 oz copper on the outer layers and the inner power planes.

### 2.6 Totals at the 80 A Burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Loss term | Per channel | Four channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.3 W | 89.2 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Reverse recovery, output capacitance, gate | 0.14 W | 0.6 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Subtotal (silicon + shunt)** | **26.6 W** | **≈ 106 W** |
| Plus bus copper (D ≈ 1) | — | 10–30 W |

**Conclusion: conduction loss dominates at about 84 %.** The highest-leverage design levers are:

- **Lower R_DS(on).** Use the lowest available, and consider paralleling FETs if area allows.
- **Lower T_j.** Because of the temperature coefficient, cooling the junction directly reduces loss.

Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.2 W per channel and costs motor ripple and audible noise. Keep 48 kHz.
