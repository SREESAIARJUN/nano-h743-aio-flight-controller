## 0. Architecture Clarification (Read First)

Three assumptions need to be fixed before the math means anything.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz commutation PWM.**
   - In a combined FC + 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames.
   - Each ESC channel has its own small MCU that runs the 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. Typical choices are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware.
   - The switching-loss model in §2 therefore applies to the ESC MCU's PWM, not to H743 timers.

2. **The "80A burst" is taken as per motor channel, at 6S.**
   - Supply is 25.2 V at full charge, which is the worst case for switching loss.
   - If 80A is meant as the total board current, divide the per-channel results by roughly 16 (since I²/4² per channel).

3. **A "nano" form factor (20×20 mm mounting) with 80A per channel is near or beyond physical limits.**
   - The thermal section quantifies this.
   - The conclusion is that 80A is a burst rating of seconds, not a continuous rating.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM.

- At any instant, two phase legs carry the motor current I_M.
- In each active leg, exactly one FET conducts (HS or LS), except during dead time.
- The conduction path therefore always contains **two FETs in series**.

Instantaneous conduction power:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2\,I_M^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_M^2 R_{pcb}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-FET RMS current (for per-device thermal analysis).** Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the static leg.

- High-side FET of the PWM leg conducts for duty D over 1/3 of the cycle:

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_M\sqrt{D/3}$$

- Low-side FET carries the freewheel current (1−D) over 1/3 of the cycle, plus the static on-state over 1/3 of the cycle:

$$I_{LS,rms} = I_M\sqrt{(1-D)/3 + 1/3}$$

Worst case for the low-side FET is D → 0 (low throttle at high current, e.g. during a stall or desync), which gives I_LS,rms = 0.816·I_M.

### 2.2 Electro-Thermal Fixed Point

Because R_ds depends on T_j, the junction temperature must be solved self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,Z_{th}(t)$$

Substituting the loss model:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + I^2 R_{ds25} Z_{th}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th}}$$

The denominator defines a **thermal runaway criterion**:

$$I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\, Z_{th} < 1$$

If the denominator reaches zero, no stable junction temperature exists.

**Numeric result at 80A (per channel, T_j taken as 125 °C):**

- R_ds = 1.6 mΩ × [1 + 0.005 × 100] = **2.4 mΩ**
- FET conduction: P_cond,FET = 2 × 80² × 2.4 mΩ = **30.7 W**
- Copper: P_pcb = 80² × 0.6 mΩ = **3.84 W**
