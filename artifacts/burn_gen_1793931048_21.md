**Finding 1: The architecture split is mandatory.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM. In every DShot 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole stack) is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The switching-loss model applies to the ESC firmware's PWM. The DMA model applies to the H743.

**Finding 2: 80A burst is not credible on a 20×20 mm nano board.** The analysis in §2 shows about 120 W of total dissipation at burst. A 20×20 mm board lacks the copper cross-section and thermal mass to absorb it. My recommendation:
- Use a 20×20 FC stacked on a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC, or
- Derate to roughly 45–55A burst if a true 20×20 ESC is required.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With trapezoidal or 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. Current therefore flows through one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) FET. With synchronous (complementary) PWM:

- During the on-time D, current flows through HS(phase A), then the motor, then LS(phase B).
- During the off-time (1 − D), current freewheels through the synchronous LS(A) and LS(B).

The current always traverses exactly two channels. To first order, conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

This creates positive feedback, which must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

At a converged T_j of about 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)}(110) = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}\times[1 + 0.005\times 85] = 2.14\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.14\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Additional resistive losses (per motor)

| Source | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| PCB copper, phase plus bus (about 0.3 mΩ effective) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| **Total I²R** | | **32.5 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET loss

Each FET conducts during 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° commutation). The average per-FET loss is therefore about P_cond / 6 × 2 ≈ **9.1 W**.

The instantaneous loss during a FET's conduction window is **I²R ≈ 13.7 W**. This value drives the junction transient, because the electrical period at high eRPM (about 0.1–1 ms) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant. Use the average loss for board temperature and the instantaneous loss for the junction ripple check.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses occur per switching leg, meaning the one HS/LS pair being PWM'd.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{ns}\times48\text{kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\times 2 = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\times 2 = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

**P_sw ≈ 2.67 W per motor.** Dead time is the second-largest term. Cutting t_d to 50 ns with a matched driver saves about 0.3 W per motor.

**Sensitivity to frequency.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw: about 1.3 W at 24 kHz and about 5.3 W at 96 kHz. At 80A, conduction loss is 92% of the total. Raising f_sw for smoother low-throttle control is therefore cheap, but R_DS(on) is the lever that matters at burst.

## 3. Total Dissipation

| | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| I²R (FET, shunt, copper) | 32.5 W | 130 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| **Total burst** | **35.2 W** | **≈ 141 W** |

At 25.2 V × 80 A = 2016 W input per motor, this corresponds to about **98.3% electrical efficiency**. Thermally, however, 141 W on a 30×30 mm board is the problem to solve.

Note: all four motors at 80A simultaneously is the worst case. Real bursts such as punch-outs approach it, so design for it.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Network (Cauer approximation)

```
P_FET ─[Zth_jc]─ T_case ─[R_solder+via]─ T_board_local ─[R_spread]─ T_board_bulk ─[R_conv]─ T_air
                                                                         │
                                                                     C_board
```

### 4.2 Board thermal capacitance (30×30 mm, 6L/2oz, 1.6 mm)

- **Copper:** 900 mm² × 6 × 0.070 mm = 378 mm³, which is 3.38 g. At 0.385 J/g·K this gives **1.30 J/K**.
- **FR-4:** 900 × 1.6 = 1440 mm³, which is 2.66 g. At about 1.1 J/g·K this gives **2.93 J/K**.
- **FETs and components:** about **0.6 J/K**.
- **C_board ≈ 4.8 J/K.**

### 4.3 Bulk temperature rise during burst (near-adiabatic)

Convection with prop wash is about h ≈ 60 W/m²K over 1800 mm² of two-sided area, giving R_conv ≈ 9 K/W. The bulk time constant is τ = 9 × 4.8 ≈ 43 s. That is much longer than any burst, so the board behaves adiabatically:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{141}{4.8} \approx 29\ \text{K/s}$$

| Burst duration | Board ΔT | T_board (from 40 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 15 K | 55 °C |
| 1.0 s | 29 K | 69 °C |
| 2.0 s | 59 K | 99 °C |
| 3.0 s | 88 K | 128 °C ✗ |

### 4.4 Junction rise over local board

The values below use:
- 13.7 W instantaneous per conducting FET,
- Z_th(j-c) at 1 s of about 1.0 K/W, and
- a thermal-via array under the drain pad (≥ 9 × 0.3 mm filled vias) of about 2.5 K/W local.

$$\Delta T_{j-b} \approx 13.7 \times 3.5 \approx 48\ \text{K (worst-case in-window)};\quad \text{average} \approx 9.1\times3.5 = 32\ \text{K}$$
