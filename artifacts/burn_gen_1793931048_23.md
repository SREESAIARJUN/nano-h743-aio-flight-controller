# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal and Timing Design Model

**Scope:** 6S (25.2 V max) nano stack (20×20 mm mount), 80 A per-motor burst, 48 kHz ESC PWM, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 MCU partitioning

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.

| Function | Device | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, EKF, DShot600 generation, eRPM decode | STM32H743 (480 MHz, ArduPilot runs it at 400 MHz) | ArduPilot target |
| 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, BEMF zero-cross, eRPM telemetry | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 (one per motor) | Each motor needs a dedicated hard-real-time commutation loop |
| Gate drive | 4× 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q-class, bootstrap) | Fits a 20×20 board |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.6 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **2.35 mΩ** (factor 1.47).

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

Each phase leg spends one third of the electrical cycle in each of three states:

- **High-side window:** the HS FET conducts for fraction D of each PWM period. The LS FET carries the freewheel current (synchronous rectification) for the remaining 1 − D.
- **Low-side window:** the LS FET conducts 100 %.
- **Floating window:** neither FET conducts; BEMF is sensed on this phase.

Average conduction loss per FET:

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac{1}{3}D\,I_{rms}^2R_{ds},\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I_{rms}^2R_{ds}$$

Summing over a leg and then over three legs gives the per-motor total. It is **independent of D**, because two FETs are always in series with the winding:

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 RMS correction for PWM ripple

$$I_{rms}=I_{avg}\sqrt{1+\frac{(\Delta I/I_{avg})^2}{12}},\qquad \Delta I=\frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: a 2207-class motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH at 48 kHz and a 10 V headroom term gives ΔI ≈ 14 A at 80 A. The correction is only 0.25 %, so I_rms ≈ I_avg here. Ripple becomes significant only on very low-inductance motors or at low f_sw.
