**Two architecture flags you should review before layout:**

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel (or the whole ESC) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. This model follows that partition.
2. **"Nano" (20×20 mm) with 4 × 80 A burst is beyond realistic thermal and copper capacity.** Section 3 quantifies this. A 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board is the minimum credible platform. The figures below assume that board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant:

- **On-phase:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D) via synchronous rectification.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET is held on.

Because complementary PWM keeps one FET of the switching leg on at all times, outside the dead time the motor current always flows through **exactly two R_ds(on)** in series, independent of D.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Conduction loss per motor channel

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{pcb}]$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times (2.90 + 0.30)\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The FET-only part is 18.6 W, about 9.3 W per conducting FET pair member. Because six-step rotates which FETs carry current, the average per FET over a commutation cycle is 18.6 W × 2/6 ≈ 6.2 W. The peak instantaneous load per FET is 9.3 W.

### 1.4 Electrothermal stability
Because R_ds rises with T_j, the junction temperature must satisfy:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\, I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{ds,25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches 0:

$$I_{runaway} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{R_{\theta JC} R_{ds,25}\,\alpha}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{0.8 \times 0.001 \times 0.0045}} \approx 527\ \text{A}$$

The margin is ample at the junction level. **The real limit is the board-to-air path**, covered in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard switching, linear approximation)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery
At turn-on of the high side, the low-side body diode recovers:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The rule of thumb is to add about 50% to P_VI to account for the extra current the recovery adds during the turn-on edge.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2\ (\text{HS + LS}) = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the dominant switching-related term at 48 kHz. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it. That is safe only with a fast driver and characterized propagation delays.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor and total burst loss at 80 A

| Loss | Per motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET, 125 °C) | 18.6 | 74.4 |
| Copper | 1.9 | 7.7 |
| V–I overlap (+50% Q_rr adder) | 1.45 | 5.8 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 | 0.24 |
| C_oss | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| Dead time | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Gate | 0.05 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.3** | **≈ 93** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 per motor this gives about 1814 W, so η ≈ 98.7%. The stage is efficient, but **93 W of heat on a 9 cm² board is the design-limiting problem.**

### 2.7 Switching-frequency sensitivity

$$P_{sw}(f) = f_{sw}\,[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f)\cdot 1.5 + Q_{rr}V + C_{oss}V^2 + 2V_F I t_{dt} + 2Q_g V_{gs}] \approx 58\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$
