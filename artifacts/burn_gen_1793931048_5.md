# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #24): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three points in the spec need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware. On a correct design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example, AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Those ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM, not the H743. All the switching math below therefore applies to the ESC side.
2. **An 80 A burst on a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** Section 3 shows it is thermally limited to a few seconds.
3. **The netlists below are representative reference topologies.** They are not a verified, DRC-checked schematic. Part values must be validated against the datasheets of the specific MOSFETs and drivers you select.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification under six-step or FOC drive, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs in series (one high-side, one low-side, or a low-side FET acting as synchronous freewheel). Duty-cycle terms cancel at the motor level:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, the resistance is R_DS = 1.0 × 1.6 = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.6\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Copper loss: P_pcb = I² R_pcb = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**

**Note the positive feedback.** P_cond rises with T_j, and T_j rises with P_cond. The loop is stable only while the thermal-resistance-times-sensitivity term stays below 1:

$$R_{th}\cdot\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha < 1$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary Losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 V_f I t_dt f_sw | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw | 0.06 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½ C_oss V² f_sw | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 0.05 W (dissipated in the driver) |

### 2.4 Totals at 80 A Burst

| | Per motor | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching + secondary | 2.6 W | 10.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈26.3 W** | **≈105 W** |

The hottest individual FET is the PWM-switched high-side: roughly 10.2 W of conduction plus 2.6 W of switching, or **about 12.8 W**.

**Why 48 kHz costs little here.** Switching loss is only about 10 % of the total at 80 A. Conduction dominates, so R_DS(on) and copper weight are the levers that matter, not the PWM frequency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-Case Rise (Hottest FET)

With R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 12.8 \times 1.5 \approx \mathbf{19\ K}$$

### 3.2 Board Bulk Heating (Adiabatic, Burst Regime)

Take a 25.5×25.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with FETs and capacitors, about 10 g total. Its heat capacity is roughly C_th ≈ 8 J/K (FR-4 ≈ 1.1 J/g·K, Cu ≈ 0.385 J/g·K).

Convective cooling under prop wash is about h ≈ 100 W/m²·K over A ≈ 1.3×10⁻³ m², giving G ≈ 0.13 W/K. That is negligible next to 105 W, so the burst is effectively adiabatic:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{105}{8} \approx \mathbf{13\ K/s}$$

### 3.3 Allowable Burst Duration

Start from a 50 °C board. Allow T_j,max = 150 °C with a 20 K derating margin, then subtract the 19 K junction-to-case rise. The board can therefore rise by about 61 K:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{61}{13} \approx \mathbf{4.7\ s}$$

At the 125 °C R_DS assumption, this lands at about **3 to 5 s**. A credible datasheet should state "80 A burst (≤ 5 s)". The continuous rating, set by roughly 1 W/K of effective board-to-air conductance at best, is about **25 to 35 A per motor**.

### 3.4 Mitigations (Ranked by Effectiveness)

1. Parallel FETs (2 per switch). R_eq halves, so P_cond halves and t_burst roughly doubles.
2. Use 3 oz copper on the power layers, or add thick-copper inner planes. This lowers R_pcb and raises C_th.
3. Add a firmware current limit with an I²t integrator in AM32, set to about 30 A continuous.
4. Report ESC temperature through bidirectional DShot EDT telemetry. ArduPilot can then log it and react.

## 4. Bidirectional DShot600: ArduPilot Timer and DMA Allocation (H743)

### 4.1 Protocol Timing

- **FC → ESC:** 600 kbit/s, so t_bit = 1.667 µs. A 16-bit frame takes 26.7 µs. The signal is inverted when bidirectional mode is enabled.
- **Turnaround:** about 30 µs.
- **ESC → FC:** 21-bit GCR at 750 kbit/s (1.333 µs per bit), about 28 µs, captured by timer input capture plus DMA.
- **Minimum loop:** about 85 µs per frame plus margin. This comfortably supports 1 to 2 kHz loop rates.

### 4.2 Timer Clock

TIM1 runs at 200 MHz with the core at 480 MHz. ArduPilot picks the prescaler so that one bit spans an integer tick count. A small rate error (a few percent at most) is within ESC tolerance. Verify this on a scope.

### 4.3 Recommended Allocation

Put all four motors on one advanced timer (TIM1). This gives synchronous updates and a single UP-DMA burst.

| Motor | Pin | Timer/Ch | Output DMA | Capture DMA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | PE9 | TIM1_CH1 | TIM1_UP (shared, DMAR burst) | TIM1_CH1 |
| M2 | PE11 | TIM1_CH2 | ↑ | TIM1_CH2 |
| M3 | PE13 | TIM1_CH3 | ↑ | TIM1_CH3 |
| M4 | PE14 | TIM1_CH4 | ↑ | TIM1_CH4 |
| LED | PA8* | TIM?/separate | separate stream | — |

\*TIM1_CH1 alternate function. If you need NeoPixels, move the LED to TIM3 or TIM4 instead.
