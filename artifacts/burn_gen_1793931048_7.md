# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot/DMA Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility

I don't have prior context for "Job #42", so this document states every parameter explicitly. Replace the representative values (marked ⟨param⟩) with your BOM datasheet values. The equations are written so the numbers propagate.

**Architecture assumed.** This is a standard stacked or combined design:
- The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot.
- It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware).
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- The H743 does not switch the bridges itself.

**Feasibility flag.** "80 A burst" is interpreted as **per motor channel**. That means 320 A of DC bus current when all four channels burst together.

| Board size | 80 A/channel verdict |
|---|---|
| 20×20 mm "nano" | Physically possible only as a **~1–2 s burst** (derived in §3.4). Not continuous. |
| 30.5×30.5 mm | More defensible. |

Either way, the battery leads, XT connector, and pads become the limit before the FETs do. Treat this as a design-review finding, not a footnote.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, phase current at any instant flows through exactly **one high-side-position device and one low-side-position device**. The synchronous partner carries current during the off-time, so dead time is the only exception.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 0.8 × 1.45 = 1.16 mΩ, so

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.16\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.85\ W}$$

Distribution across devices:
- Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.
- The active high-side FET carries duty D; its low-side partner carries (1 − D).
- The time-averaged loss is ~2.5 W per FET.
- The instantaneous peak is ~7.4 W on a single FET at D → 1.

**Electrothermal stability.** The feedback loop gain is

$$G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_\theta = P_{cond}\,\frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha(T-25)}\,R_\theta$$

- Per FET: 7.4 × 0.0041 × 3 ≈ 0.09, which is stable locally.
- Board level: the effective R_θ is large because only convection removes heat (§3.3). The system is therefore **not steady-state stable at 80 A**. This is the mathematical basis of the "burst only" rating.

### 2.2 Hard-Switching Loss

Only one transitioning device per PWM edge hard-switches, so

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(24\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

### 2.3 Capacitive, Reverse-Recovery, and Dead-Time Terms

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.030\text{ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode commutated by the opposite switch):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> The 200 ns dead time costs as much as the switching edges. Tune AM32 dead time to the minimum safe value for your driver and FET. 100 ns halves this term.

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.058\text{ W}$$
