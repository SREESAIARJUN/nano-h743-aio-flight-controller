## 1. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal)

In 6-step commutation, two legs conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, exactly one FET per active leg is always on (high-side during t_on, low-side during t_off). So two R_DS(on) are always in the current path, independent of duty.

### 1.1 Conduction losses

**Temperature-corrected resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.495 ≈ **1.79 mΩ**.

**Phase RMS current with PWM ripple.** The phase current carries a triangular ripple Δi:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta i^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta i = \frac{(V_{bus}-2E_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–7 inch motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-neutral, worst case D = 0.5, low back-EMF at stall/punch-out):

$$\Delta i \approx \frac{25.2 \times 0.5}{2 \times 15\mu \times 48k} \approx 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$\frac{\Delta i^2}{12} = 6.4\ \text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

**Bridge conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6406 \times 1.79\,\text{m} = \mathbf{22.9\ W}$$

**Copper path loss.** Phase, bus, and pad traces, roughly 0.3 mΩ round trip at 2 oz:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. The other active leg is static for the full 60° sector.

**V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\,Q_{rr}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = V_{bus}\,Q_{oss}\,f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive.** Two FETs switch per period; this loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 22.9 W | 91.6 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + Q_oss | 2.66 W | 10.6 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.5 W** | **≈ 110 W** |

**Key insight:** at 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 9:1. Increasing f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.3 W per motor. Choosing a 0.8 mΩ FET instead of 1.2 mΩ saves about 7.6 W per motor. Spend the BOM budget on R_DS(on), not on switching speed.

**Efficiency check.** Electrical input is P_in ≈ V_bus × I × D. With D ≈ 0.85 at full throttle, P_in ≈ 1714 W per motor. ESC efficiency ≈ 1 − 27.5/1714 ≈ **98.4 %**. This is plausible.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Per-FET loss allocation

Each leg is active 240° of 360° electrical, and the HS/LS split depends on D. Averaged over one electrical revolution (motor electrical frequency is much faster than the die thermal τ of roughly 1–5 ms at high eRPM):

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}+P_{dt}}{6} \approx \frac{25.5}{6} \approx 4.25\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET is the high-side at D ≈ 0.85 that also takes the switching loss: about **5.5 W**.
