# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Architecture, Loss Models, DShot DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need settling first, because they change the math.

1. **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** In production 4-in-1 stacks, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 class), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. DShot600 is the link between the two. The design below follows this split.
2. **"Nano" and 4 × 80 A are physically in conflict.** On a 20×20 mm board, an 80 A burst on all four channels at once is not thermally survivable for more than about 1–2 s. I baseline a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** board and show the 20×20 numbers for comparison.

| V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | 40 V MOSFETs |
| I_burst per channel | 80 A (phase current) | target: 5 s |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step current paths with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **PWM phase.** The high side (HS) conducts for duty D. The low side (LS) conducts for 1−D (synchronous freewheel).
- **Return phase.** The LS conducts 100% of the sector.

Conduction loss per channel, with n FETs paralleled per switch position:

$$
P_{cond} = \underbrace{\frac{I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{n}\,[D + (1-D)]}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{\frac{I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{n}}_{\text{return leg}} = \frac{2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}{n}
$$

Two consequences follow:

- With complementary PWM, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**.
- If sync rectification is disabled, replace the (1−D) term with $V_f\,I\,(1-D)$, which is much worse at mid throttle.
