# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller (Job #32): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points need correcting before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not implement sensorless BLDC/FOC commutation, so it cannot drive 12 half-bridges at 48 kHz. The workable architecture is:
   - **H743 (ArduPilot)** sends bi-directional DShot600 to **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AM32-class, e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071).
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs.
   - The 48 kHz switching and the DShot DMA allocation therefore live on different chips. They are modelled separately below.

2. **"80A" on a nano form factor is a short-burst rating, not a continuous one.** The math below shows this. On a 20×20 mm board, 24 MOSFETs at 80A per channel is not physically viable. I assume a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern (about 36×36 mm board)** with **3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs**.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with **complementary PWM** (synchronous rectification, AM32 default). At any instant, current flows through one FET in the PWM leg (high side during t_on, low side during t_off) plus the low-side FET of the return leg. The current therefore always sees **two R_ds(on) in series**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$
