**Deliverables:** loss model, thermal budget, DShot600/BDShot timer and DMA plan, reference netlist.

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

Several points in the brief need clarifying before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production AIO board, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743's job is to emit DShot600 frames and capture the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. I model it this way. Commutating four motors from the H743 at 48 kHz is possible but is not supported by ArduPilot.
2. **"80A burst" means 80 A phase current per motor.** At full throttle with four motors, that is 320 A through the board.
3. **The bus voltage is 6S, with 25.2 V maximum.** I use this as the worst case for switching loss.
4. **The device parameters are representative.** They are typical of 40 V, 3×3 mm DFN ESC MOSFETs and FD6288-class gate drivers. Substitute your actual datasheet values and the equations still hold.
5. **I size the board at 30.5×30.5 mm, then scale to a 20×20 mm "nano".** As shown in §3, the 20×20 mm case supports 80 A for only about a second.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Rds(on) temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /^\circ\mathrm{C}$$

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_ds,25 (Vgs = 10 V) | 1.2 mΩ |
| T_j design point | 125 °C |
| R_ds(125 °C) | 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ** |

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, two phases carry current. One leg is PWM'd with complementary (synchronous) switching, so whether the PWM is on or off, current flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.92\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and shunt contribution

For 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner pours, about 4 layers in parallel carry the phase current. The effective path is roughly 15 mm long and 4 mm wide.

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.004\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot4}\approx 0.23\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

At operating temperature (×1.3) this is about 0.3 mΩ. With roughly two path segments in series:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2\times0.6\,\mathrm{m\Omega}\approx \mathbf{3.8\ W/motor}$$

A 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt carries the DC-link return, about $I_{ph}\cdot D$. At D ≈ 0.85 its loss is about **1.2 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

The transition times are set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r,f}\approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g\approx\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{g,int}}=\frac{10-3.5}{4.7+1.3}\approx1.1\ \mathrm{A}$$

With Q_gd ≈ 14 nC, each transition takes about 13 ns, plus about 2 ns for loop inductance. That gives t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm{kHz}=\mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Only the PWM'd leg switches, so this applies once per motor.

### 2.2 Secondary terms (per motor)

| Term | Equation | Values | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body-diode reverse recovery | $Q_{rr}V f$ | 40 nC | 0.048 W |
| Coss charge | $\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f$ | 30 nC | 0.018 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $2V_f I\,t_{dt} f$ | 0.8 V, 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g) | $2Q_g V_{drv} f$ | 50 nC | 0.048 W |

The dead-time term dominates the secondary losses. AM32 lets you adjust dead time. Every 50 ns you can safely remove saves about 0.3 W per motor at 80 A.

### 2.3 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + 2V_f I t_{dt} + Q_{rr}V \approx 43\ \mu\mathrm{J/cycle}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about **1.0 W per motor** at 80 A. This is small next to conduction loss, so 48 kHz is justified if it buys you smoother low-throttle behavior and quieter motors.

### 2.4 Total loss at 80 A burst

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Shunt | 1.2 W | 4.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.2 W | 8.7 W |
| ESC MCU, LDOs, drivers | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~32 W** | **~128 W** |

At this operating point, efficiency is about 93–95% of output power. Conduction loss accounts for **about 77%** of the total. That is the term to attack: use a lower-Rds FET, or parallel two FETs per switch, which halves conduction loss.
