## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Review Flags

### Architecture

The H743 cannot drive the MOSFET gates directly with field-oriented or 6-step commutation for four motors while also running ArduPilot. The architecture is therefore:

- **FC side:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It emits bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **ESC side:** Four AT32F421 (or STM32G071) MCUs running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-FETs.

### ⚠ Design Review Flags (read first)

1. **80 A per channel × 4 channels on a 30×30 board is thermally transient-only.** The math below shows roughly 3–5 s at a full 4×80 A burst. Marketing-style "80A burst" ratings typically assume 1–2 motors and ~1 s. Specify the burst profile explicitly in the requirements.
2. **The total-current shunt dissipates ~26 W at 320 A.** Either reduce it to 0.1–0.15 mΩ or use per-ESC low-side shunts.
3. **Part numbers in the netlist are representative.** Pin numbers must be validated against the specific datasheet and package before layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, one phase is PWM'd (HS ON for D, LS ON for 1−D), one phase is held LS-ON, and one phase floats. The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{HS} D + I^2 R_{LS,a}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS,b} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This holds independently of duty D, assuming synchronous rectification and equal R_DS.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per bridge @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.5 W |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | **16.8 W** |

### 1.3 Thermal-Runaway Fixed Point

Conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS, which raises loss. Write the thermal coupling as T_j = T_b + θ·P_FET. A stable fixed point exists only if:

$$
\theta \cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \theta \cdot I^2 R_{25}\alpha < 1
$$

For the worst-case FET (the static low-side), with θ ≈ 10 K/W junction-to-board: 10 × 6400 × 0.9e-3 × 0.0045 = **0.26 < 1**. The device is stable locally. The runaway risk is at **board level** (see §4), not die level.

### 1.4 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET's average share is:

- **HS:** 1/3 of the time at duty D
- **LS (sync rect):** 1/3 of the time at (1−D)
- **LS (static):** 1/3 of the time at 100%

$$
\bar P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R D,\qquad \bar P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R (2 - D)
$$

At an 80 A burst, D ≈ 0.9 (near full throttle), with R = 1.31 mΩ:

- P̄_HS = 1/3 × 8.38 × 0.9 = **2.5 W**
- P̄_LS = 1/3 × 8.38 × 1.1 = **3.1 W** (the low-side FETs run hotter)

The electrical sector period at typical burst eRPM (≈ 1–2 kHz electrical) is 80–170 µs. That is far below the die thermal time constant (ms range), so the average values above are valid for junction estimation. Add ~10% ripple margin.
