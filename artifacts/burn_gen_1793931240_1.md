# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #11): Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Key findings:**

1. **An 80 A per-motor burst on a nano board is a transient-thermal problem, not a steady-state one.** The power stage dissipates about 98 W at 80 A × 4 motors. The lumped board heat capacity is about 4.3 J/K, so the board warms at about 19 K/s. The burst limit is roughly **2.5–3.5 s** from a 45 °C start. Firmware current limiting must enforce this, and the 80 A figure should be published as a burst rating.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, which handle commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. Typical ESC MCUs are AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The model keeps these two domains separate.
3. **DMA on the H743 is not the constraint.** DMAMUX allows any request on any stream. The real constraints are timer/pin grouping and the ArduPilot BIDIR channel-pairing convention.

All component values below are **reference assumptions** for modelling. Substitute values from your selected parts' datasheets before freezing the design.

- One leg is PWM-switching (high side, with the complementary low side).
- One leg is static low side.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Instantaneous Path

During any 60° commutation sector, the current flows through two conducting devices in series:

- **PWM leg:** the high side for duty D, then the synchronous low side for (1−D). One device conducts at all times.
- **Return leg:** the static low side, conducting 100% of the time.

So the instantaneous conduction loss per motor is:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \big[R_{DS,HS}D + R_{DS,LS}(1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,LS} \;\approx\; 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

The approximation is exact when the high-side and low-side parts are identical.

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 2.3 Copper and Interconnect Loss

The PCB path runs battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad. Assume about 0.35 mΩ per conducting path with 2 oz copper, vias, and solder joints:

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.35\text{ m}\Omega = 2.24\text{ W}
$$

### 2.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per motor | × 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.1 W | 72.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W | 79.4 W |

### 2.5 Per-Device Distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical per cycle, so each motor's loss is spread across 6 FETs. At electrical frequencies above about 1 kHz (high RPM), the die thermal time constant (around 1–5 ms) averages this out:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 3.0\text{–}3.3\text{ W}
$$

At low electrical frequency (stall or startup), use the instantaneous worst case instead: **one FET carries I²R ≈ 9.9 W at 125 °C.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Per motor, one high-side/low-side pair switches at f_sw.

### 3.1 Hard-Switching V-I Overlap (High Side)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\text{ W}}
$$

### 3.2 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.073\text{ W}
$$

$$
