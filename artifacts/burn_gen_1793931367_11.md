# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #44)

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Stated Assumptions

No MOSFET, driver, or board stack-up was specified. Every number below comes from the **declared baseline parameters** in §0.2. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers are worked examples.

### 0.1 Architecture Corrections

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any production 4-in-1, each phase bridge is run by a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 is the flight controller. It *emits* DShot600 and *receives* bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is an **ESC-firmware parameter**, not an H743 timer.
2. **"Nano" vs. 80A.** A 20×20 mm stack cannot sustain 4×80A. The thermal model in §3 shows this explicitly. 80A is treated as a **time-limited burst rating**, and the model computes the allowable burst duration. A 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board is assumed, stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.80 × 1.60 = **1.28 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-Step Synchronous Commutation

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd (complementary, synchronous rectification) and one leg is held on.

- The **PWM leg** carries current through the high-side FET for duty D and the low-side FET for (1−D). The I²R loss is duty-independent.
- The **static leg** carries current through one FET for 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.28\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{16.38\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period on average, so the 16.38 W is shared across 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 2.73\text{ W}$$

The **instantaneous** peak per FET is 8.19 W. This peak drives the junction transient (§3.3).

### 1.3 Copper Path Loss

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This loss is per motor, deposited in the board rather than the FETs.

### 1.4 Thermal-Electrical Coupling

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\right)$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. A runaway condition exists if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}R_{\theta JA} \geq 1, \quad \text{i.e.} \quad 2I^2 R_{ds,25}\,\alpha_R\,R_{\theta JA} \geq 1$$

At 80 A, the threshold is R_θJA ≥ 16 K/W. A properly heat-sunk PQFN does not reach this. A starved one can.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per PWM leg (one hard-switched pair per motor at any instant).

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k | **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.060 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V · f_sw | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k | **1.229 W** |
| Gate drive (driver loss, 2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.058 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.37 W** |

**Key insight:** At 48 kHz, **dead-time diode loss exceeds V–I overlap loss**. Two ways to recover efficiency:

- Cut t_dt from 200 ns to 80 ns. This saves about 0.74 W per motor. Verify against driver propagation skew first.
- Choose a FET with a low-V_SD integrated Schottky body diode.

### 2.1 Frequency Trade

$$P_{sw}(f) = (0.968 + 0.12 + 1.229)\frac{f}{48\text{k}}$$

At 24 kHz, switching loss halves (about 1.2 W saved per motor). The cost is higher current ripple and audible and torque ripple on low-inductance motors. 48 kHz is justified only for motors with phase inductance below about 15 µH.
