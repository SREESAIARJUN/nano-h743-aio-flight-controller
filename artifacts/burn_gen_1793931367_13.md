## 0. Architecture and Governing Assumptions

**Why there are five MCUs.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, because the H743 cannot also commutate four motors. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600, one signal line per motor.

| Burst phase current, per motor | I_B | 80 A | Duration-limited (see §3) |

**Feasibility flag.** At these figures, 80 A per motor is a *burst-only* rating on a nano-class board. The math below shows a sustained 80 A would exceed the junction limit within a few seconds. The marketing rating must be stated as a burst current with a duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, two phase legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each PWM period. The low-side FET conducts synchronously, as a rectifier, for the remaining (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the whole sector.

So at every instant the phase current passes through exactly **two R_DS(on)** in series. This ignores dead time, which is handled in §2.3.

Conduction loss per motor channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

where the on-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current carries a triangular ripple. Its RMS value adds to the DC current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - E)\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

**Worst case** occurs at D = 0.5, where E ≈ V_bus/2 for a near-stall burst. For a typical 5-inch motor, L_ph is about 20 µH line-to-line:

- ΔI_pp ≈ (12.6 × 0.5) / (20e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **6.6 A**
- Ripple term = 6.6² / 12 ≈ 3.6 A²
- This is negligible against 80² = 6400 A² (+0.06 %).
