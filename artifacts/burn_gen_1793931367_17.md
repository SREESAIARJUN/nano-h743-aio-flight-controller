## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

No part numbers were specified, so I fixed a reference design. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute your selected datasheet values. The numbers shown are worked examples, not guarantees.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | H743 flight controller sends DShot600 to 4× independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware). The FC does not commutate motors. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V (design to 30 V transients) |
| PWM | 48 kHz, synchronous rectification, trapezoidal six-step |
| MOSFET (ref.) | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class: R_ds(on),25°C = 1.0 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap). V_CC = 10–12 V from a dedicated buck. |
| Burst spec | 80 A per channel, all four channels simultaneously (worst case) |
| Ambient | 40 °C, with prop-wash convection |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag up front:** 80 A *continuous* per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not physically achievable. The model below shows the honest result: roughly **80 A for about 5–8 s, and about 40–45 A continuous**. Marketing "80 A burst" figures should carry a duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in Six-Step Commutation

In any 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- **Leg X (PWM leg):** HS on for duty D, then the complementary LS conducts for (1−D). Synchronous rectification applies outside dead time.
- **Leg Y (return leg):** LS on continuously.

Instantaneous conducting devices in the current path:

$$
\text{During } D:\; HS_X + LS_Y \qquad \text{During } (1-D):\; LS_X + LS_Y
$$

There are **always exactly two R_ds(on) in series**, independent of D. With phase current I_ph (≈ DC motor current under trapezoidal drive, ripple neglected):

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)}
$$

Including ripple, replace I² with I_RMS² = I² + ΔI²/12. For low-inductance quad motors at 48 kHz, ΔI is typically 5–15 % of I, which adds less than 0.2 % and is neglected.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**. Use 1.5 mΩ to include package and solder.

### 1.3 Worked Values at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per ESC | P per FET (avg over 6) | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.85 W | 68.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **3.2 W** | **76.8 W** |

The per-FET average assumes even distribution over an electrical revolution. The LS devices carry more (they conduct in both "return" and "freewheel" roles):

$$
P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R\,\left[1 + \tfrac{(1-D)}{2}\right],\qquad P_{HS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{6}I^2R\,D
$$

At D = 0.9, I = 80 A, R = 1.5 mΩ: P_LS ≈ 3.36 W and P_HS ≈ 1.44 W. **LS FETs are the thermal hotspot.** Place them nearest the copper pours and thermal vias.

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase/bus trace:** 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner, 4 mm effective width, 15 mm path gives ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path at 25 °C. Two paths in series yield P = 2 × 80² × 0.3 m = **3.8 W per ESC**.
- **Shunt:** 0.25 mΩ, which is common on 4-in-1 boards and shared on the bus return. Total 4×80 = 320 A on a shared shunt gives 25.6 W, which is impossible. **Use per-ESC shunts or a 0.1 mΩ shunt with an amplifier.** Per-ESC 0.25 mΩ at 80 A gives 1.6 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in each sector, so one HS/LS pair per ESC switches at any time.
