## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is high-side PWM'd, one phase is low-side held ON, and one phase floats.

- **PWM ON (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a and then LS_b.
- **PWM OFF (fraction 1−D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous rectification) and then LS_b.

In both states the motor current sees **two R_DS(on) in series**. Conduction loss is therefore essentially independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Use the datasheet normalized R_DS(on)-vs-T_j curve, since α is device-specific.

At T_j = 125 °C with R_max = 1.0 mΩ: R(T_j) = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A (flat-top trapezoidal, I_rms ≈ I_peak)

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Per conducting FET | 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **9.6 W** |
| Per motor, FETs (2 in series) | 2 × 9.6 W | **19.2 W** |
| Copper, phase + bus (R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ effective, [ASSUMED]) | 6400 × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Per motor conduction** | | **≈ 20.8 W** |
| **All 4 motors** | | **≈ 83 W** |

Thermal sharing among the six FETs of one ESC is non-uniform. The low-side FET of the held phase carries 100 % of the current for its sector. Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time. At low eRPM or stall, however, a single FET can sit in one sector for milliseconds, so **worst case is stall/low-RPM burst**, not hover.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal stability (runaway criterion)

Because P depends on T_j and T_j depends on P:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,I^2 R_{25}\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th} I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

**Stability requires** R_th · I² · R_25 · α < 1.

Per FET with I = 80 A, R_25 = 1 mΩ, α = 0.005: the limit is **R_th(j-a) < 31 K/W**.

A nano board has a steady-state R_th(j-a) per FET of roughly 20–40 K/W, which is at or beyond the runaway boundary. **80 A is thermally viable only as a time-limited burst.** Section 4 quantifies how long.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET commutates at roughly zero volts but incurs dead-time diode conduction and reverse recovery.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

Switching time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g}$$

With Q_sw ≈ 15 nC and I_g ≈ 1.5 A: t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 10 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot k, \qquad k \approx 0.5\text{–}1$$

Upper bound: 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.073 W**.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode is recovered by the high-side turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$
