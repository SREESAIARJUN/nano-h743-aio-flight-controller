# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Design Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Warnings

This model uses representative component values, listed below. Replace them with your selected part datasheets before release.

**Feasibility warnings:**

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** Bidirectional DShot means the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four separate ESC MCUs. The ESC MCUs (assumed AT32F421K8U7 running AM32) run the 48 kHz PWM and the commutation.
2. **"80 A" per channel is a burst-only rating on a nano board.** Four channels at 80 A draw 320 A from the battery. That exceeds XT60 (60 A continuous) and XT90 ratings, and exceeds what most 6S packs can deliver. The thermal model in §2.6 shows a burst budget of about 3 seconds, not a continuous rating.
3. All MOSFET, driver, and PCB parameters below are assumed. They must be verified against datasheets.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25 = 1.5 mΩ max, Q_g = 30 nC, C_oss ≈ 1 nF, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC, R_θJC = 1.5 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap), 10 V gate drive |
| Timing | t_r = t_f = 15 ns, dead time = 100 ns, body diode V_f = 0.8 V |
| PWM | 48 kHz, synchronous rectification (complementary) |
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz power layers |

## 2. Thermal and Loss Model (per ESC channel, I = 80 A)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × 1.45 ≈ **2.2 mΩ** (worst case used below).

### 2.2 Conduction loss

With six-step commutation and synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs in series. These are the high-side or low-side FET of the PWM leg, plus the low-side FET of the sinking leg.

Inductor ripple adds to the RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is D = 0.5. With L_ph = 20 µH:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (20 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 6.6 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A²

$$P_{cond}=I_{rms}^2\cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)=6404\times2\times2.2\,\text{m}\Omega\approx\mathbf{28.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Switching losses (one hard-switched node per channel)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f × 2 | 0.03 W |
| Gate charge | Q_g·V_g·f × 2 FETs (mostly dissipated in driver/R_g) | 0.03 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

### 2.4 Totals

- **P_channel ≈ 28.2 + 2.2 = 30.4 W**
- 4 channels ≈ 121.6 W
- Shunt (single 0.1 mΩ bus shunt at 320 A): I²R = **10.2 W**. This is a hotspot; use a 2512 or 3920 metal strip, or switch to per-phase low-side sensing.
- Copper (≈0.1 mΩ effective path per channel): ≈ 0.64 W × 4 ≈ 2.6 W

**P_total ≈ 134 W at full burst.**

### 2.5 Per-FET junction rise

The electrical frequency is much faster than the thermal time constant, so heating is averaged across the six FETs in a channel:

- Average per-FET power: P_FET ≈ 30.4 / 6 ≈ 5.1 W
- Junction-to-board path: R_θJ-B = R_θJC (1.5) + via array/TIM (≈4.0) = 5.5 K/W
- **ΔT_J-B ≈ 28 K**
