# STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Correction

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly. Running 4 × 3-phase, 48 kHz complementary PWM with back-EMF zero-cross detection while also running the ArduPilot EKF3 at a 1–2 kHz loop is not a supported ArduPilot configuration. The production-standard partition is:

| Block | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400 MHz | ArduPilot (ChibiOS) |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), 120 MHz | AM32 (supports bidir DShot and 48 kHz PWM) |
| Gate drivers (×4) | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase half-bridge, QFN24) | — |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 | — |

The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over **bi-directional DShot600**. This is the interface derived in §4.

### 0.3 Feasibility Warning

The numbers below show that **4 × 80 A on a nano-format (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board is a burst rating measured in seconds, not a continuous rating**. The model quantifies exactly how many seconds. Treat the "80 A" label as a ~1–3 s rating unless the airframe provides forced cooling.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D. The low side synchronously rectifies for (1−D), minus dead time.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side is static ON for the whole sector.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big]_{\text{leg A}} + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)_{\text{leg B}} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The loss is **independent of duty** when synchronous rectification is active, ignoring the dead-time fraction handled in §2.4.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Self-Consistent Solution)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × (1 + 0.45) = **2.175 mΩ**, rounded to 2.2 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.2\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{28.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Because R_ds rises with T_j, the junction temperature is the fixed point of:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P_{tot}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta}
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta\,(2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw})}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_\theta}
$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator ≤ 0, i.e. when:

$$
R_\theta \ge \frac{1}{2 I^2 R_{25}\alpha} = \frac{1}{2(6400)(1.5\text{e-}3)(0.0045)} = 11.6\ \text{K/W (motor-level)}
$$

A nano board in still air sits around 15–25 K/W. **It is therefore thermally unstable at 80 A continuous**, which confirms the burst-only rating.
