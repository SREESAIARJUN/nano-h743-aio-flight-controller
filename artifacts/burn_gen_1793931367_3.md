# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Allocation and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Findings

These issues affect the rest of the design, so they come first.

1. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot, and ArduPilot's scheduler is not designed for 4× sensorless 6-step commutation at 48 kHz PWM. The standard architecture is used here:
   - STM32H743 runs ArduPilot.
   - Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421, running AM32) each drive one bridge.
   - The two are linked by bi-directional DShot600.

   The "48 kHz switching" therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs. The "DShot DMA" belongs to the H743.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The model in §3 shows about 21 W per channel at 80 A. With all four channels at burst, that is about 85 W into a board of about 25×25 mm. The thermally limited burst is about 2–4 s, not the 10 s often printed on marketing sheets. The board should be specified as **"80 A burst ≤2 s single channel, 45–50 A continuous with airflow."**

3. **Assumed device parameters are listed in §1.** Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure. Every equation is parametric.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.305 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any commutation sector, current flows through two phases:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET conducts 100 % of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R\,D + I^2R\,(1-D) + I^2R = 2I^2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty because synchronous rectification removes the diode term (apart from dead time, §3.4).

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.305\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{16.7\ W}$$

### 2.3 Iterative junction temperature (R_DS(on) self-heating)

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on dissipation, so the two must be solved together:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)\cdot R_{\theta,J\text{-}PCB}$$

- R_θ,J-PCB ≈ 1.2 (JC) + 1.8 (solder, vias, 2 oz Cu spreading) = 3.0 K/W.

**Worst-case FET:** the return-phase low side carries I²R for its full 120° electrical conduction, which is 1/3 of the electrical period. At commutation rates of about 1–3 kHz, the junction thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms) only partially averages this. The conservative design uses the peak sector value:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2R = 6400 \times 1.305\text{ mΩ} = 8.35\ \text{W}$$

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}pcb} = 8.35 \times 3.0 \approx 25\ \text{K}$$

Iteration from T_pcb = 100 °C:

| Iteration | T_j | R_DS(on) | P | ΔT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 125 °C | 1.305 mΩ | 8.35 W | 25.1 K |
| 2 | 125.1 °C | — | — | — |

The iteration converges at **T_j ≈ 125 °C**. Thermal runaway is avoided while R_θ·α·I²R₂₅ < 1. Here that product is 3.0 × 0.0045 × 5.76 = 0.078, so the design is stable.

### 2.4 Copper and shunt conduction

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-phase hard-switched FET incurs V·I overlap loss. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
