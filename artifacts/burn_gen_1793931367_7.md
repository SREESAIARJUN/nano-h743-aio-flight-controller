## 0. Architecture Findings You Need Before Layout

**1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.**
- In an ArduPilot system, the H743 runs the flight stack and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the STM32G071 and AT32F421.
- The 48 kHz PWM is generated on the ESC MCU, not the H743.
- The design is therefore partitioned as FC (H743) → DShot → 4× ESC MCU → gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs.

**2. 80A per motor will not fit a 20×20 nano form factor.**
- Twenty-four FETs capable of 80A burst need PDFN 5×6 packages or paralleled 3.3×3.3 packages.
- They also need about 2 J/K or more of thermal mass per channel.
- **Recommendation:** use a 20×20 H743 FC stacked on a 30.5×30.5 (or 36×36 pad) ESC.
- The thermal model in Section 3 shows that even this board is burst-limited to a few seconds.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.2\,(1 + 0.0045 \times 85) = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step current paths

During any 60° sector, phase A is driven high, phase B is held low, and phase C floats.

| Device | Conduction fraction of the sector |
|---|---|
| Q_AH (PWM high side) | D |
| Q_AL (synchronous rectifier) | 1 − D, minus dead time |
| Q_BL (static low side) | 1 |

The motor current I is continuous because winding inductance ≫ PWM period. The sector loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

**Key result:** with synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of throttle and depends only on phase current.

### 1.3 Adding parasitic copper

Add the phase trace resistance R_tr (≈0.25 mΩ for 2 oz, 4 layers paralleled, ~8 mm path) and the shunt resistance R_sh (0.25 mΩ, if inline):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 I^2 (R_{ds} + R_{tr}) + I^2 R_{sh}$$

At 80 A:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| FETs | 2 · 6400 · 1.66 mΩ | 21.2 W |
| Traces | 2 · 6400 · 0.25 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Shunt | 6400 · 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Per channel** | | **≈ 26.0 W** |

### 1.4 Per-device average

Each of the 6 FETs carries current in 2 of 6 sectors, so the per-FET average is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx \frac{21.2}{6}\cdot 1 \approx 3.5\ \text{W}$$

Instantaneous peak in the static low-side FET:

$$P_{pk} = I^2 R_{ds} = 10.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The static low side switches at commutation frequency, which is negligible.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (two FETs per PWM leg; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw} \approx 2.73\ \text{W per channel}$$

**Sensitivity:**
- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per channel.
- Raising gate drive to 2 A source / 3 A sink cuts t_r + t_f by roughly half.

### 2.7 Channel and board totals at 80A burst

| Item | Per channel | ×4 board |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu + shunt) | 26.0 W | 104 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.9 W |
| Gate driver + LDO + MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **29.0 W** | **≈ 116 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 22.2 V × 4 = 7.1 kW electrical is about 98.4%. However, 116 W into a 36×36 mm board is a pure thermal-capacity problem.
