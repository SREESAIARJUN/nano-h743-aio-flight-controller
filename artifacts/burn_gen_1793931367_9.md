# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Corrections

Several premises need adjusting before the model is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel runs its own MCU, such as an STM32G071, AT32F421 or EFM8, with AM32 or BLHeli firmware. That MCU generates the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only emits bidirectional DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry. I model both layers and keep them separate.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor phase current, for a few seconds.** This is how ESC marketing defines it.
3. **"Nano" is not physically compatible with 80 A.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A is not thermally survivable for more than about 1 s; section 2.6 derives this. I size the model for a **30.5×30.5 mm** board and show the 20×20 result for comparison.
4. **All component values are representative.** Every value is stated as a parameter so you can substitute your actual BOM. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready, DRC-verified design.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,\text{mΩ}\times(1 + 0.005\times75) = 2.06\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM

At any instant, current flows through one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, during PWM off-time the freewheel current flows through the complementary FET of the PWM'd leg rather than its body diode. Either way, two channel resistances are always in series.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Under burst at full throttle (D → ~1), take I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.06\,\text{mΩ} = 26.4\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period (120° conduction):

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(2.06\,\text{mΩ}) = 4.4\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is 13.2 W. The commutation period is about 1 ms at typical eRPM, which is much shorter than the die-to-case time constant. The **average** value therefore governs T_j.

### 1.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

Because R_ds depends on T_j, the conduction loss and the junction temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + \theta\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + \theta P_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - \theta P_{25}\alpha}$$

This is **thermal runaway-stable only if θ·P₂₅·α < 1**.

- With P₂₅ = 19.2 W per motor, the board-level stability limit is θ < 10.4 K/W.
- This is easily met, but it shows why a poorly heatsunk board degrades nonlinearly.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only **one leg is hard-switched per commutation sector**, so you count one switching pair at f_sw.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

This term is small because synchronous rectification limits body-diode conduction to the dead time.

### 2.3 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = 2 V_f I\, t_{dt} f_{sw} = 2\times0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 \text{ (switching FETs)} = 40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Loss summary at 80 A burst, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss term | Per motor | Board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 26.4 W | 105.6 W |
| V-I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, I²R) | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈31.3 W** | **≈125 W** |

**Key insight:** at 48 kHz versus 24 kHz, switching loss roughly doubles from about 0.8 W to about 1.6 W per motor. That is still only about 5% of total loss. **Conduction dominates by roughly 15:1**, so R_ds selection and copper matter far more than frequency at burst. The 48 kHz choice is justified by lower current ripple and acoustic and efficiency benefits at partial throttle.

To cut shunt loss, use phase-current sensing with a 0.25 mΩ shunt, or bus-current sensing. Bus sensing carries D·I rather than I.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-case (per FET)

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET}\,R_{\theta jc} = (4.4 + 0.27)\times1.2\,\text{K/W} \approx 5.6\ \text{K}$$

This is negligible. **The board is the bottleneck, not the die.**
