## 0. Architecture Decisions

These need to be settled first, because two of them affect everything else.

| # | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | The H743 is the **flight controller only**. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware). | ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors itself. Bidirectional DShot implies an ESC MCU on the far end of each signal line. |
| A2 | Battery: **6S Li-ion/LiPo**, V_bus,max = 25.2 V, design V_bus = 22.2 V. | Sets the MOSFET voltage class and the switching-loss voltage. |
| A3 | MOSFETs: **40 V class** (6S practice). Use 60 V if motor leads are long or caps are marginal. | At 80 A, L·di/dt ringing can exceed 40 V. See §2.6. |
| A4 | "80 A burst" means **80 A per channel for ≤ 2–5 s**. Continuous rating is a separate number (§3). | A nano-format board cannot reject 4 × 30 W continuously. |
| A5 | The 48 kHz PWM is the **ESC MCU's** center-aligned PWM, with synchronous rectification (complementary) and dead time t_d. | Synchronous rectification makes conduction loss nearly independent of duty (§1.2). |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx 0.004\!-\!0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Using the common datasheet form, R_DS(on) at 125 °C is roughly 1.5–1.7 × R_DS,25.

Reference device ⚠: 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS,25 = 1.5 mΩ, Q_g = 45 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 35 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W.

At an assumed T_j = 110 °C with α = 0.005:

$$R_{DS}(110)=1.5\,\text{m}\Omega\times1.425=2.14\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM-ed and phase B is held low.

| Device | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| Phase-A high-side | D |
| Phase-A low-side (sync rect) | 1 − D − 2t_d·f_sw |
| Phase-B low-side | 1 (whole sector) |

There are always **two MOSFET channels in series** with the winding:

$$P_{cond,ch}=I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot 2$$

Ripple correction for triangular ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2=I_{avg}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Motor ripple estimate at duty D with phase-phase inductance L_pp:

$$\Delta I=\frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{pp}\,f_{sw}}\approx\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{pp}f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5. For a typical 2207/2306 motor, L_pp ≈ 20 µH ⚠:

$$\Delta I=\frac{22.2\times0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}}=5.8\ \text{A}\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}=2.8\ \text{A}^2\ (\text{negligible vs } 6400)$$

**Burst conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond,ch}=80^2\times2.14\,\text{m}\Omega\times2=\mathbf{27.4\ W}$$

The hottest single device is the one conducting 100% of the sector. Averaged over the electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the time. In a single sector, however, it carries the full 13.7 W. Electrical periods are sub-millisecond, much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so use the cycle average for T_j:

$$P_{FET,avg}\approx\frac{27.4}{6}\times... \;\to\; \text{per-FET average}=\frac{2\times I^2R}{6}\cdot... $$

Clean form: total channel loss is shared over 6 devices, so

$$P_{FET,avg}=\frac{P_{ch}}{6}=4.6\ \text{W (conduction only)}$$

### 1.3 Shunt and copper

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Per-channel shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Board copper, bus to phase pad (~0.3 mΩ, 2 oz × 4 layers) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
