## 0. Architecture Review Findings

Before presenting the models, three findings change the design direction. The math in later sections supports each one.

| # | Finding | Severity | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**, not the ESC controller. Each motor needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. | Architectural | The design uses 1× H743 plus 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071), with DShot600 between them over PCB traces. |
| F2 | **80 A continuous per motor is not achievable on a 20×20 mm board.** About 87 W of loss per motor (§3) against a board that sheds roughly 12 W total steady-state. | Critical | "80 A" must be specified as a **time-limited burst** (≤ about 1.5–2 s, §4), or the form factor must move to 30.5×30.5 mm. |
| F3 | Placement: 24 MOSFETs in DFN5×6 occupy 720 mm², which exceeds the 400 mm² board area. | Critical | Use DFN3.3×3.3 (24 × ~11 mm² ≈ 260 mm², one side). This raises R<sub>DS(on)</sub>, which worsens F2. |

**Recommendation:** Rate the product at **45 A continuous / 80 A burst (≤ 1.5 s, duty-limited)** on 20×20, or **55 A / 80 A burst** on 30.5×30.5. All numbers below are parametric, so they can be re-run for the final BOM.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 120° commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the PWM phase routes current through either its high side (during D) or its low side (during 1−D). The return phase's low side conducts continuously during that 60° sector. **Exactly two FETs are always in series with the motor current.**

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 100 °C: R<sub>DS</sub> = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0065 × 75) = **1.49 mΩ**

### 2.3 Per-motor conduction loss

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.49\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.1\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{6.4\ W}$$

### 2.4 Per-device distribution

Over one electrical revolution, the 2I²R loss is shared across 6 FETs:

- **Average per FET:** I²R/3 = **3.18 W**
- **Instantaneous peak per FET:** I²R = **9.5 W**

The peak lasts one 60° sector. This matters for low-eRPM, high-current events such as punch-outs from hover.

### 2.5 Electrothermal coupling

Because R<sub>DS</sub> depends on T<sub>j</sub>, which in turn depends on P, the solution is a fixed point:
