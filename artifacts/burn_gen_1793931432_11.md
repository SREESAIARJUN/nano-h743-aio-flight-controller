# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #66): Design Basis Document

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Three findings drive the rest of this document.

1. **"Nano" form factor and 80A burst conflict.** At 80A per channel, the board dissipates about 88W total (§2–3). A 20×20 mm board has roughly 1.4 J/K of thermal mass and reaches MOSFET junction limits in under 1 s. **The minimum credible footprint is a 30.5×30.5 mm mount on a ~36×36 mm board with 2 oz copper on 8 layers.** Even then, 80A is a **~3 s burst rating**, not a continuous rating. I have modelled that geometry.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per channel, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Each MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. "48 kHz" is therefore the **ESC MCU PWM frequency**, not an H743 timer.
3. **Component parameters below are representative placeholders.** Re-run the model with the datasheet values of your chosen MOSFET before release. At 6S, a 40V MOSFET leaves little margin for switching overshoot. I recommend 40V parts only with a tight layout and snubbers, and 60V parts if you must support 6S HV (4.35V/cell).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly **two FET positions carry I_ph at all times**:
- During PWM-on, the current path is the high side of the PWM'd phase plus the low side of the sinking phase.
- During PWM-off, the low side of the PWM'd phase (synchronous rectifier) carries the current, plus the same sinking low side.

The channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110°C, the factor is 1.51:
- Typical: R = 0.9 × 1.51 = **1.36 mΩ**
- Worst case: R = 1.2 × 1.51 = **1.81 mΩ**
