# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #60): Architecture and Mathematical Model

## 0. Feasibility Summary

The model below shows that **80 A simultaneous on all four channels cannot be sustained thermally on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board.** Running the numbers:

- At 4 × 80 A and 48 kHz, the power stage dissipates about **100 W**.
- A nano board has a heat capacity of about **4 J/K**.
- The board therefore heats at roughly **24 K/s** before cooling is counted.
- With realistic cooling, it reaches its derating limit in about **3 s**.

I have completed the design and stated the rating honestly. Section 4.5 gives three options:

- **Nano board:** 80 A burst for ≤1 s per channel, staggered across channels; 45 A continuous.
- **Same rating, larger board:** move to 30.5×30.5 mm.
- **Lower frequency:** drop to 24 kHz.

**Architecture clarification.** The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends them bidirectional DShot600. The **48 kHz PWM is set in ESC firmware, not on the FC.**

**Component values.** MOSFET and driver parameters are representative values for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 5×6 DFN part. Re-derive them from the selected datasheets before release.

Two board layouts are possible:

- **Stacked (recommended):** FC and ESC as separate PCBs on a 2-pin plus 8-pin harness. This keeps the H743 and IMU out of the MOSFET heat and noise. All thermal numbers below assume the ESC board only.
- **Single board:** stated in the job, but subtract about 10 °C of headroom for the IMU.

### 2.1 Commutation Model

- The high-side (HS) switch of the PWM phase conducts for fraction D.
- That phase's low-side (LS) switch conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- The LS switch of the return phase is fully ON.
