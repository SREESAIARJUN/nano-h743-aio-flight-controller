# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #57): Engineering Model

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before any equations are valid.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every ArduPilot 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The thermal and switching model (§1–3) therefore applies to the ESC power stage, and the timer and DMA model (§4) applies to the H743.
2. **80 A on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The math below shows that a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot reject about 100 W. The deliverable here is the allowable burst duration, not a steady-state claim.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In any commutation sector, two legs carry the motor current I:

- The **PWM leg**: the high-side FET (HS) conducts for duty D, and the complementary low-side FET (LS) conducts for 1−D.
- The **static leg**: one FET is on for the whole sector.

The instantaneous conduction path is therefore always two R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch}(T_j)=2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Including current ripple, with ΔI_pp = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L_ph·f_sw):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2+\frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

The ripple term is under 0.5 % for typical 5–15 µH phase inductance at 48 kHz, so it is neglected below.

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries one sixth of the total:

$$P_{cond,FET}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

While a FET is conducting, its instantaneous dissipation is I²R_DS.
