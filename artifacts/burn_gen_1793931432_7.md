### Architecture

The STM32H743 runs **ArduPilot** as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate motors. ArduPilot has no motor-commutation firmware, and running 12 half-bridges plus EKF3 on one core is not a sane design.

Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU: **4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32**. The H743 talks to them over **bi-directional DShot600**.

```
          ┌───────────────── STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) ──────────────────┐
 IMU/Baro │ TIM3_CH3  TIM3_CH4  TIM5_CH1  TIM5_CH2   (bidir DShot600)     │
          └────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────────┬───────────────────────────┘
               │33Ω      │33Ω      │33Ω      │33Ω
           ┌───▼──┐  ┌───▼──┐  ┌───▼──┐  ┌───▼──┐
           │ ESC1 │  │ ESC2 │  │ ESC3 │  │ ESC4 │  AT32F421 + 3-ph driver + 6 FETs
           └──┬───┘  └──┬───┘  └──┬───┘  └──┬───┘
             M1        M2        M3        M4
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Phase current vs. battery current

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct and one floats. The average battery current is:

$$
I_{batt} = D \cdot I_{ph}
$$

At full-throttle burst, D → 0.95–1.0, so I_batt ≈ I_ph. At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current. **FET losses scale with phase current.** This is why "80 A rating" claims based on battery current understate FET stress at mid-throttle.

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}
= \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{15\times10^{-6}\cdot 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \text{A}_{pp}\ (\text{worst at } D=0.5)
$$

RMS current:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\ \text{A}^2
$$

Ripple adds 0.1%. It is negligible at 80 A but matters at light load.

### 1.3 Per-FET conduction distribution

Each phase leg is active for 120° (1/3) of the electrical period as the PWM leg, and for another 120° as the return leg. With high-side PWM and complementary low-side synchronous rectification:

| FET | Conducts | Average loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side (PWM leg) | D × 1/3 period | $P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$ |
| Low-side (same leg, sync rect) | (1−D) × 1/3 | $\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R$ |
| Low-side (return leg) | full 1/3 | $\tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$ |
| **Low-side total** | | $P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D) I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$ |

Sum over 3 legs:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 3\left[\tfrac{1}{3}D + \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\right] I^2 R = 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This is the expected result: at every instant, exactly two FETs are in series with the motor current.

**Worst-case FET:** the low-side at low D, which approaches ⅔·I²R. At burst (D ≈ 1), high-side and low-side split evenly at ⅓·I²R each.

> Some AM32 builds PWM the low side instead. The equations mirror; the worst FET becomes the high side.

### 1.4 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.
