# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Architecture, Loss Model and DMA Plan

## 0. Ground Rules and Feasibility Check

These architectural constraints come before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot outputs DShot. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The FC and ESC are co-designed as a stack or single board, but they are separate control domains.
2. **80 A per channel is a burst rating, not continuous, at nano scale.** At 4 × 80 A the stack dissipates about 80 W, and a 20×20 or 30×30 board cannot reject that in steady state. Section 3 shows the result: about 5–8 s burst at 40 °C ambient with prop wash. The continuous rating is about 35–40 A per channel. A 20×20 mount is not recommended for this current class, so this design assumes a 30.5×30.5 mount on a 36×36 mm outline.
3. **Parameters are representative.** The MOSFET, driver and copper values below come from typical 40 V, 5×6 mm ESC-class parts. Re-run the model with your selected part's datasheet values: R_DS(on)(T), Q_g, Q_gd, Q_rr, Z_th(jc). The model is parameterized for that.

**Design basis**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V (40 V FETs give 1.6× margin for regen and ringing) |
| Burst current | 80 A phase current per ESC; 4 ESCs simultaneous = 320 A worst case |
| PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal; FOC changes the numbers by about ±10 % |
| Ambient | 40 °C; airflow from props ≈ 5–8 m/s over the stack |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Representative FET: R_DS,25 = 0.90 mΩ at V_GS = 10 V. At T_j ≈ 100 °C (the converged value from §3.4), R_DS ≈ 0.90 × 1.34 ≈ **1.20 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, phase X carries +I through its high side (HS) and phase Y returns it through its low side (LS). PWM is applied to the X leg:

- **PWM on (D):** HS_X + LS_Y conduct.
- **PWM off (1−D):** LS_X (sync rect.) + LS_Y conduct.

Two FETs are therefore always in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.20\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

Each FET conducts in 2 of 6 sectors (the HS/LS share depends on D). The average per FET is 15.4 W / 6 ≈ **2.6 W**. The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R = 7.7 W. This matters at low eRPM (see §3.5).

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction

Sheet resistance of 2 oz (70 µm) copper is ρ/t = 1.72e-8 / 70e-6 ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, or about 0.31 mΩ/□
