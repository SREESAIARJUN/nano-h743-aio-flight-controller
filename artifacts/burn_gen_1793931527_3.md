**Key finding:** 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is a **~2-second adiabatic burst rating** on this form factor. It is not a continuous rating. The realistic continuous rating is **~23–25 A per motor in prop-wash cooling**. Section 4 derives both numbers. Label the product accordingly, or move up to a 30×30 mounting / 5×6 FET design.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths in six-step synchronous commutation

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held fully on for the whole sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore the same for every duty cycle:

$$P_{bridge}(t) = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

The loss is independent of D. **Two FET resistances are always in series with the motor current.**

### 2.2 Temperature-corrected R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C, R<sub>DS</sub> = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

### 2.4 Copper path loss

Path: battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad. Each path is about 0.35 mΩ with 2 oz copper plus a 4-layer paralleled bus. The current crosses two path segments:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times I^2 \times 0.35\,\text{m}\Omega = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.00035 = \mathbf{4.5\ W}$$

### 2.5 Worst single FET

The return-leg low-side FET conducts for a full 120° of every 360°:

$$P_{LS,max} = I^2 R_{DS} \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} = 6400 \times 0.0018 / 3 = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

The high-side FETs carry roughly D/3 of that, plus the switching loss.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one high-side and one low-side FET at a time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

Gate driver: 1 A source/sink, R<sub>g,ext</sub> = 4.7 Ω, plateau about 3 V. The plateau gate current is I<sub>g</sub> ≈ (10 − 3)/(4.7 + 1.5) ≈ 1.1 A.

$$t_{r} \approx t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} + t_{ir} \approx \frac{7\,\text{nC}}{1.1\,\text{A}} + 8\,\text{ns} \approx 15\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.5 \times 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$
