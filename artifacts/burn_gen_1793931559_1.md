## 1. Field-Oriented Current Control

### 1.1 Electrical angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

- θ_off is found at commissioning: apply v_d > 0 with θ_e = 0, then read θ_m.
- Latency compensation: θ_e,comp = θ_e + p·ω_m·(T_SPI + 1.5·Ts). The 1.5·Ts term is one sample of computation delay plus half a PWM period.

### 1.2 Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)
$$

### 1.3 Park transform

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.4 Plant model in the dq frame (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

The resulting torque is

$$
\tau_m=\tfrac32 p\lambda_m i_q = K_t i_q.
$$

### 1.5 PI current control with decoupling (pole-zero cancellation)

Choosing K_i/K_p = R/L cancels the plant pole at −R/L. The closed loop then becomes first order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p^{(i)} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i^{(i)} = R\,\omega_c
$$

Example: ω_c = 2π·1500 rad/s gives K_p = 33 V/A and K_i = 94 kV/(A·s).

Discrete form (backward Euler), applied identically to the d and q axes:

$$
e_k = i^*_k - i_k,\qquad
I_k = I_{k-1} + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw}(v_{k-1}^{sat}-v_{k-1}^{unsat})
$$

$$
v_k^{unsat} = K_p e_k + I_k + v^{ff}_k
$$

The decoupling feed-forward terms remove the cross-coupling and back-EMF from 1.4:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

**Voltage limiting.** The vector is limited to the circle that SVPWM can produce linearly, with d-axis priority:

$$
|v_d|\le V_{max},\qquad |v_q|\le\sqrt{V_{max}^2-v_d^2},\qquad V_{max}=\frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt3}\cdot m_{max}
$$

Use m_max ≈ 0.95 to leave headroom for the current-sampling window.

### 1.6 Inverse Park and SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

$$
v_a=v_\alpha,\quad v_b=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta,\quad v_c=-\tfrac12 v_\alpha-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_a,v_b,v_c)+\min(v_a,v_b,v_c)\big),\qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

## 2. Cascaded Stabilization Control

### 2.1 Mapping camera rates to joint rates

The joint order is assumed to be yaw (ψ, base) → roll (φ) → pitch (θ, camera). The camera angular velocity in the camera frame is

$$
\boldsymbol\omega_c = R_y(\theta)^T\Big(R_x(\phi)^T\big(\boldsymbol\omega_{base,\psi} + \dot\psi\hat z\big) + \dot\phi\hat x\Big) + \dot\theta\hat y
$$

For stabilization, each motor is driven on its own joint rate. That rate is estimated by subtracting the base contribution, or, more robustly, the camera-frame rate error is mapped to joint rates through the inverse Jacobian:

$$
\begin{bmatrix}\dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\\ \dot\psi\end{bmatrix}_{err} = J^{-1}(\phi,\theta)\,\boldsymbol\omega_{c,err},\qquad
J = \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & 0 & -\cos\phi\sin\theta\\
0 & 1 & \sin\phi\\
\sin\theta & 0 & \cos\phi\cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}
$$

Here det J = cos φ. The singularity at φ = ±90° is handled by clamping 1/cos φ and saturating the yaw command.
