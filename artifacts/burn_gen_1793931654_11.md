## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before any math:

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 AIO design the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. A typical choice is AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, runs the gate driver, and returns eRPM telemetry. So the "48 kHz switching" and "DShot DMA" domains live on different silicon. They are modeled separately below.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally capacitance-limited, not steady-state.** Section 3 shows it is sustainable for roughly 2–4 s. Any spec sheet must state the burst duration.

**Assumptions.** These are parameterized. Replace them with values from your chosen MOSFET datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.605 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM phase:** high-side conducts for D, complementary low-side conducts for 1−D. One R_ds is always in the path.
- **Grounded phase:** low-side conducts 100 % of the sector. A second R_ds is in the path.

So the motor current always sees **2·R_ds**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With ripple, the phase RMS current is:

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

At full burst, D → 1 and V_bemf ≈ V_bus·D, so ΔI is small and the ripple term is <1 %. It is neglected here.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.605\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ motor}$$
