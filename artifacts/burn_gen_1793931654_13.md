## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Finding

Before any math, two points determine everything that follows.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses one AT32F421 per channel.

**2. 80A burst on a nano footprint (20×20 mm) is thermally marginal.**
- The math in Section 3 shows 80A on one channel is sustainable for about 6–7 s.
- 80A on all four channels at once is sustainable for under 2 s.
- The "80A burst" rating should be specified as a **≤1 s, single-event, per-channel** burst. Otherwise, the board must move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint.

### Design Basis (Assumptions)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_max = 25.2 V, V_nom = 22.2 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]^{\,k},\qquad \text{linearised: } R_{DS}(T_j)\approx R_{25}\,(1+0.006\,(T_j-25))$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS,hot} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.60 = 1.60\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with complementary PWM

Exactly two phase legs are active in every commutation sector:

- **PWM-on interval (duty D):** current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** current freewheels through LS(A) (synchronous) → motor → LS(B).

In both intervals the current passes through **two conducting FETs in series**. The channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each leg's HS FET conducts for D·(1/3) of the cycle. The LS FET conducts for (1/3) + (1−D)(1/3).

| FET | Loss expression | D = 0.5 | D = 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| HS (each) | I²·R·D/3 | 1.71 W | 3.07 W |
| LS (each) | I²·R·(2−D)/3 | 5.12 W | 3.75 W |

**Design rule:** low-side FETs are the worst case at mid-throttle and high current. Give them the larger copper pour and more thermal vias.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Assumptions:
- 2 oz copper: R_□ = 0.245 mΩ/□.
- Phase path ≈ 2.5 squares, split across 3 paralleled layers, giving ≈ 0.20 mΩ.
- Return and bus path adds another ≈ 0.10 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.30\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 1.5 Current-shunt loss (critical)

A single battery-current shunt carries the **sum** of all four channel currents.

- At 4×80 A × D_eff ≈ 0.9, the DC-link current is about 290 A.
- With a 0.25 mΩ shunt: P = 290² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **21 W in one 2512 part**. This is not survivable.

**Mitigation:** use two 0.5 mΩ shunts in parallel per bus half, or a Hall-effect sensor. Alternatively, rate the current sensor at ≤ 200 A and accept saturation during bursts.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like), but incurs dead-time and reverse-recovery effects.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12 (25.2)(80)(27\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = \tfrac12(60\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = (50\,\text{nC})(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (LS FET)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\,\text{ns}\times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead-time loss is almost as large as switching loss. Reducing t_dt to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Validate shoot-through margin with a double-pulse test before doing so.

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}\ (\text{per switching FET pair}) = 2 \times 70\,\text{nC}\times10\times48\,\text{kHz} = 0.067\ \text{W}$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 2.6 Channel loss summary at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 20.50 |
| Hard switching | 1.31 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.10 |
| Gate drive | 0.07 |
| PCB copper | 1.90 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 25.1 W** |
| **Four channels simultaneously** | **≈ 100 W** (excluding shunt) |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × 0.9 ≈ 1600 W per channel. Loss of 25 W gives ≈ 98.4% efficiency. This is consistent with good-quality 4-in-1 ESCs.

**Effect of changing f_sw:**
- Switching-related losses scale linearly: P ∝ f_sw.
- Going to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel.
- Going to 96 kHz adds about 2.6 W per channel.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable balance between loss and motor current ripple / acoustic noise.
