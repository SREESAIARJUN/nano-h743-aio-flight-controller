## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal / 6-Step, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct in each 60° sector:
- **Phase A (PWM'd):** high side conducts for D·T_sw. The low side synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (static):** low side is on for the whole sector.

At any instant, exactly two FETs carry the full motor current I. The current path is either HS_A + LS_B or LS_A + LS_B. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_J)$$

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 20 µH, worst case D = 0.5, back-EMF E ≈ 12 V.
- ΔI = 13.2 × 0.5 / (20e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 6.9 A
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 4 A²
- Ripple is negligible against I² = 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Numbers at T_J = 125 °C [A3, A5]

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.12\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

Per-FET loss (each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle) ≈ 14.3 / 6 = **2.39 W average**. The instantaneous loss while conducting is 7.2 W.

### 1.4 Shunt and copper (often forgotten)

- **Phase/low-side shunt, 0.25 mΩ:** P = 80² × 0.25e-3 = **1.6 W/motor**.
- **Copper:** a 35 mm × 2 mm trace on 2 oz copper is ≈ 0.25 mΩ per layer. With 3 paralleled power layers, budget ≈ **0.5 W/motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd phase switches. Per motor there is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching of the HS FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the LS body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current. Check the datasheet curve at 80 A.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{DT} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

> This term is nearly as large as P_VI. Cutting dead time to 100 ns (AM32 setting, if the gate driver propagation matching allows) saves about 0.6 W/motor.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver/regulator, not the FET)

$$P_G = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 1.45 + 0.05 + 0.03 + 1.23 = \mathbf{2.76\ W}$$

## 3. Total Dissipation Budget

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 14.3 W | 57.2 W |
| FET switching + dead time | 2.76 W | 11.0 W |
| Shunts | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Copper | 0.5 W | 2.0 W |
| Gate drive + ESC MCU + LDO | ~0.15 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈19.3 W** | **≈77 W** |

The H743 itself (≈0.4–0.6 W at 400 MHz) is negligible by comparison, but it shares the copper. Keep the IMU thermally isolated (see §6).
