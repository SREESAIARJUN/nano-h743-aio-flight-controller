# Job #49: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC

## Engineering Model: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Stated Assumptions

These decisions drive every number below, so they come first.

### 0.1 The H743 does not commutate the motors

In every production 4-in-1 design:

- The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot. It emits DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry.
- **Four ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and perform the 48 kHz commutation PWM. Typical parts are AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230.

The 48 kHz switching-loss model therefore applies to the ESC power stage, not to H743 timers.

### 0.2 Reality check on "80 A in a nano form factor"

The math in §3 shows that 80 A on all four motors simultaneously is a **sub-2-second thermal event** on a 20×20 or 30×30 board. It is not a continuous rating. I state this explicitly so the spec sheet doesn't promise something physics won't deliver.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current paths

In each 60° commutation sector, two legs conduct:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D. The complementary low side conducts the freewheel current for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts continuously.

Each MOSFET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period, because each device participates in 2 of 6 sectors.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}\ \Rightarrow\ k_T(110^\circ\text{C})\approx1.5$$

$$R_{ds,hot}=1.5\ \text{m}\Omega\times1.5=2.25\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-device average losses

With I as the phase (DC-link equivalent) current:

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac13 I^2R_{ds}D,\qquad P_{LS,sync}=\tfrac13 I^2R_{ds}(1-D),\qquad P_{LS,return}=\tfrac13 I^2R_{ds}$$

### 1.4 Per-motor bridge total

The duty terms cancel. There are always exactly two R_ds in series:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2I^2R_{ds}$$

At 80 A:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2(80)^2(2.25\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{28.8\ W/motor}$$

Per-FET average: 28.8 / 6 = **4.8 W**. The worst-case low side carries the return-leg term plus some sync time, giving up to **≈ 9.6 W during its active third**. This peak matters for junction transients (§3.3).

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (not negligible at 80 A)

Phase trace, bus plane and solder pads total roughly R_cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per path on 2 oz copper:

$$P_{cu}=I^2R_{cu}=6400\times0.5\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{3.2\ W/motor}$$

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The return leg is static within a sector.

$$I_g\approx\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{10-3.5}{4.7+1.0}\approx1.14\ \text{A}$$
