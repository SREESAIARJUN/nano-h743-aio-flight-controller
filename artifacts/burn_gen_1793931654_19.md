# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Design Model

This document covers the conduction and switching loss model, the thermal model, the ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and a reference netlist.

## 0. Feasibility Verdict

**An 80 A per-motor burst is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board.** With 4 motors at 80 A, the stack dissipates about 90–100 W (Section 3). A 20×20 board has roughly 2–3 J/K of thermal mass and cannot reject that heat or carry the current in copper.

- **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor (still compact) with 8-layer 2 oz copper.
- **Specification:** define the rating as **80 A for ≤5 s, 55 A continuous with prop-wash airflow**.
- **Architecture:** the H743 runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on its own small MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 does not commutate motors. The 48 kHz switching frequency is the ESC-MCU PWM frequency.

All numeric parameters below are marked **[A] = assumption**. Replace them with datasheet values for your chosen parts.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

A more accurate fit is R_ds,25 · (T_j/298)^2.3. Both give about 1.6× at 125 °C.

### 2.2 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

During each 60° sector, current flows through one high-side bridge leg (PWM) and one low-side FET (on).

Within the PWM leg:
- the high-side FET conducts for D,
- the low-side FET of the same leg conducts for (1−D).

So the PWM leg contributes I²R·D + I²R·(1−D) = I²R, **independent of duty**. The static low-side FET adds another I²R.

**P_cond,motor = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j)**

At T_j = 125 °C:

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| R_ds | 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 | 1.6 mΩ |
| I² | 80² | 6400 A² |
| Per conducting FET | 6400 × 1.6 mΩ | **10.24 W** |
| Per motor | 2 × 10.24 W | **20.5 W** |

Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle on average. The six FETs share 20.5 W, about 3.4 W average per FET. However, the instantaneous junction temperature ripple at low eRPM tracks the 10 W peak.

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Losses

- **Phase copper:** 2 oz (70 µm), 4 mm wide, 15 mm long, paralleled on 4 layers gives R ≈ 0.23 mΩ. P = 6400 × 0.23 mΩ × 2 paths ≈ **2.9 W per motor**.
- **Shunt:** 0.25 mΩ on the low-side return gives P = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ ≈ **1.6 W per motor**.

**Result:** P_cond,total per motor ≈ 25 W. Across 4 motors ≈ **100 W**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Each PWM edge occurs in only the one leg being modulated. The terms below are per motor.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40n × 48k = **1.94 W** |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (dissipated in the channel, approximate) | ½ × 40n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.024 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30n × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200n × 48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 FETs | 2 × 60n × 10 × 48k = **0.058 W** |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

Across 4 motors: **≈ 10.7 W**.

**Sensitivity:**
- P_sw ∝ f_sw. At 96 kHz the switching total would be about 21 W.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable trade-off between loss and current ripple / motor iron loss.
- If eRPM is high, check that 48 kHz gives at least 10 PWM periods per commutation sector.

### 3.1 Loss Totals

| Scope | Conduction | Switching | Total |

| Board (4 motors at 80 A) | 100 W | 10.7 W | **~110 W** |
