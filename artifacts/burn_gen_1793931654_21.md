## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This gives about 1.5× at 125 °C, which is typical for trench FETs. At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 2.0 × 1.375 = **2.75 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS current in 6-step with synchronous rectification

Each phase leg drives high for 120°, low for 120°, and floats for 120°. With duty D:

| Device | Conduction fraction | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side (phase driven high) | D/3 | I²·R·D/3 |
| Low-side (sync rectification, same phase) | (1−D)/3 | I²·R·(1−D)/3 |
| Low-side (phase driven low, fully on) | 1/3 | I²·R/3 |

Per leg: P = I²R·(D/3 + (1−D)/3 + 1/3) = **(2/3)·I²·R**

Per motor (3 legs):
$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{PCB}}$$

The current path always passes through exactly two FETs in series, independent of D.

The hottest FET is the low-side: P = I²R(2−D)/3. This reaches its maximum of 2I²R/3 at low duty with high current, i.e. a stalled or locked rotor.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

- Per motor, FETs: 2 × 80² × 2.75 mΩ = **35.2 W**
- PCB copper: 2 oz gives ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□. Assume an effective path of ~2 □ in parallel across 2 layers, so R_PCB ≈ 0.25–0.5 mΩ. That adds 1.6–3.2 W; use **2.5 W**.
- Per-FET at D ≈ 1 (burst at full throttle): 80² × 2.75 mΩ / 3 = **5.9 W per FET in a 3×3 mm package**

### 1.4 Self-consistent solution

Because R depends on T_j, iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges if dP/dT · Z_th < 1. Otherwise you are in **thermal runaway**. Check this explicitly in the spreadsheet for stall conditions.
