# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture and Analysis (Job #1)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

The analysis below produces three findings that affect the design before any schematic is finalized.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself.** In a 4-in-1 design, each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running BLHeli_32-class or AM32 firmware. This design uses four AT32F421 or STM32G071 devices. The H743 is the flight controller and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The architecture below reflects this.
2. **80 A on all four channels is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** At 80 A per channel, the bridges dissipate about 88 W (Section 2). A nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) can shed only about 5–10 W continuously. The math gives a **burst window of roughly 1.5–3 s** from a 40 °C start.
3. **Battery-side current needs a sanity check.** Four channels at 80 A phase current and high duty means more than 250 A from the pack. Neither an XT30/XT60 connector nor nano-board copper supports that. The 80 A figure must be specified as **per-channel phase current, burst ≤2 s, single-channel or diagonal-pair**, or the board size must increase.

### 2.2 Conduction Losses

The temperature-dependent on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \;\Rightarrow\; R_{ds}(125^\circ C)=1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current flows through exactly two FETs at every instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. Whether the high-side FET is on or its complementary low-side FET is freewheeling, the current path always crosses two R_ds(on).

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}$$

At 80 A:

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W | 51.2 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | **3.20 W** | **76.8 W** |

**PCB copper losses** must be added. Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) internal plus external copper path of about 15 mm length and 4 mm effective width per phase, on 4 parallel layers.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.004\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot4} \approx 0.23\ \text{m}\Omega$$

At hot copper (×1.3), this gives about 0.3 mΩ per path, or roughly **3.8 W per channel** with two paths in series. That is 15.4 W across four channels, which is substantial. Use 6-layer, 2 oz on every layer under the bridge.
