## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. Running four sensorless 6-step or FOC loops at 48 kHz alongside ArduPilot's scheduler is not a supported or sensible architecture. The correct partitioning is:

```
                 ┌────────────────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──────────────────────┐
                 │ TIM1 CH1..CH4 ── bidirectional DShot600 (1-wire, open-drain-ish) ──┐ │
                 └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼─┘
                                                                                      │
   ┌──────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────────────────────────┘
   ▼              ▼               ▼               ▼
 ESC MCU 1     ESC MCU 2       ESC MCU 3       ESC MCU 4    (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
   │ 48 kHz PWM   │               │               │
 3-ph driver   3-ph driver     3-ph driver     3-ph driver   (e.g. FD6288Q)
 6× MOSFET     6× MOSFET       6× MOSFET       6× MOSFET     (24 FETs total)
```

There are two clock domains, and they are analysed separately below:

- **48 kHz** is the ESC MCUs' PWM carrier. It drives the power-stage losses (Sections 1–3).
- **DShot600** is the H743→ESC command and eRPM telemetry link (Section 4).

**Form-factor warning:** "Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A burst per motor" are physically incompatible for anything beyond roughly 1–2 s bursts. The math below makes this quantitative. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm board for an 80 A burst rating. On 20×20 mm, a realistic rating is about 45 A burst and 30 A continuous.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path in 6-step commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, exactly two phase legs carry current: one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) path. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through **two conducting channels**:

- During the ON portion (duty D), it flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- During the OFF portion (1−D), it flows through LS_A (sync-rectifying) and LS_B.

The instantaneous conduction loss per motor channel is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Per-device RMS current

Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical period: 120° as the PWM leg and 120° as the static leg, then floats for 120°.

| Device role | Conduction fraction | I_rms |
|---|---|---|
| HS switch (PWM leg, ON) | (1/3)·D | I_ph·√(D/3) |
| LS sync (PWM leg, OFF) | (1/3)·(1−D) | I_ph·√((1−D)/3) |
| LS static leg | 1/3 | I_ph·√(1/3) |

The worst-case device is any LS FET. Its I_rms is bounded by I_ph·√(2/3) when it serves both roles across sectors, which gives 65.3 A at I_ph = 80 A.

### 2.3 Numerical result

R_ds at T_j = 125 °C is 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = 1.5 mΩ.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

The worst single FET dissipates (65.3)² × 1.5 mΩ ≈ **6.4 W**.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per motor channel, per PWM cycle, the losses are:

**(a) V-I overlap (hard-switched HS):**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(d) C_oss charge/discharge (HS + LS of the switching leg):**
$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET die):**
$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.1 Loss budget

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Overlap switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.9 W** | **≈ 87.6 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 88% of the total. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 2.6 W per channel.
