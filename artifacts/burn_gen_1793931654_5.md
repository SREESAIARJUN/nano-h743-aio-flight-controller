## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Commutation-state analysis

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. In each 60° sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T, and the low-side synchronous FET conducts for (1−D)·T. Leg A therefore carries I_m 100 % of the time, split between its two FETs.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side conducts continuously.
- **Leg C:** floating (BEMF sensing).

So at every instant **exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with I_m**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{ds,hot},\qquad R_{ds,hot}=k_T R_{ds,25}$$

The per-FET split is:

$$P_{HS}=D\,I_m^2R,\quad P_{LS,sync}=(1-D)\,I_m^2R,\quad P_{LS,return}=I_m^2R$$

Each FET serves as the "return" FET for one-third of the electrical cycle, so averaged over a revolution:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \tfrac{1}{6}\cdot 2 I_m^2 R = \tfrac{1}{3}I_m^2R$$

Within any single sector, however, the return FET carries the full I²R. That instantaneous value is what drives transient junction rise.

### 1.2 Numbers

$$P_{cond,ch}=2(80)^2(1.8\times10^{-3}) = 23.0\ \text{W}$$

- Instantaneous worst-case per FET: 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W**
- Revolution-averaged per FET: **3.84 W**
- Shunt loss: I²R_sh = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W/ch**
- Copper trace/plane loss: budget about 0.5 mΩ effective → **3.2 W/ch**. This term is the one most often forgotten, and it is significant.

### 1.3 Temperature–R_ds feedback (fixed point)

$$R(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.0059/\text{K}$$

$$T_j = T_c + R_{θJC}\,I^2R(T_j)\ \Rightarrow\ T_j=\frac{T_c + R_{θJC}I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{θJC}I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches zero, at R_θJC·I²·R₂₅·α ≥ 1. With the assumed values, 0.8 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 0.0059 = 0.036, so the junction-to-case loop is stable. Runaway risk lives in the case-to-ambient path, covered in §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one hard-switched FET plus one synchronous FET per channel.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched FET:**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I_m(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k})=1.94\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery.** The sync FET's body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers:
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.060\ \text{W}$$
At 80 A, Q_rr grows roughly with di/dt and I_F. Budget 2–3× this, about **0.15 W**.

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss}\approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.024\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period):
$$P_{dt}=V_fI_m(2t_{dt})f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:
$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.058\ \text{W}$$

| Loss term (per channel, 80 A) | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 × I²R_hot) | 23.0 |
| Copper planes/traces | 3.2 |
| Shunt | 1.6 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.15 |
| Coss + gate | 0.08 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈30.6 W** |
| **4 channels simultaneously** | **≈122 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Assume full throttle at D ≈ 0.95. Output power is about 0.95 × 25.2 × 80 ≈ 1.9 kW per channel, so the 30.6 W loss gives an efficiency of about 98.4 %. That is plausible.

**Sensitivity to f_sw.** Only terms (a)–(e) scale with frequency, at roughly 2.8 W/ch per 48 kHz. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W/ch. Moving to 96 kHz adds about 2.8 W/ch. At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 8:1, so **R_ds(on) and copper are the levers, not PWM frequency.**
