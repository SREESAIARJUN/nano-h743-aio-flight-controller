# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #20)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Key Finding

### Architecture assumed

This design follows the conventional AIO split:

- **Flight controller (FC):** an STM32H743 running ArduPilot generates bi-directional DShot600.
- **ESC side:** four ESC microcontrollers (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32), each driving one 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz PWM.

The H743 does not commutate the motors. A 48 kHz, 6-step drive needs dedicated MCUs with a hardware BEMF comparator.

### Operating point

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst current, per motor channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| D | 0.5 | Worst-case duty for capacitor ripple. Conduction loss is roughly D-independent with synchronous rectification. |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal | Complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active high side |

### Key finding

80 A burst on all four channels simultaneously is **not thermally sustainable** on a nano (20×20 / 25.5 mm) board. It is limited to roughly **1–2 s** by board heat capacity, not by the MOSFET junction rating. The derivation is in §1–§3.
