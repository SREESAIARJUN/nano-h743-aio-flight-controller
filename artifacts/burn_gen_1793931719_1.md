## 0. System Architecture and Assumptions

```
 6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► VBAT bus ──► 4× 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs)
                                  │
 STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421, AM32 FW)
                                              │ 48 kHz complementary PWM
                                              ▼
                                       4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers
```

The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. That is the job of each per-motor ESC MCU. The H743 owns DShot, telemetry, and the flight stack.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value (assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (worst case for switching) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_b | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | Q | 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3 class, R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ max @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (100 °C) | k_T | 1.5 |
| Rise / fall time at 80 A (with chosen R_g) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_SD | 0.8 V |
| Dead time (per edge) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Copper + solder per conduction path | R_cu | 0.3 mΩ |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology Fact
With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, exactly **two** current paths conduct at every instant:
- the PWM leg, where HS and LS share the duty cycle D and 1−D, and
- the static LS of the return leg.

The duty-cycle terms therefore cancel at the motor level:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_b^2 \left[ D \cdot R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] + 2 I_b^2 R_{cu} = 2 I_b^2 (R_{hot} + R_{cu})$$

with

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5 \times 1.0 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Numbers

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} = 19.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.3\times10^{-3} = 3.84\ \text{W per motor (dissipated in PCB)}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Average
Each of the 6 FETs carries an equal share over one electrical revolution:

$$\bar P_{cond,Q} = 19.2 / 6 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Averaging validity check:** at 30 krpm on a 14-pole motor, f_e = 30000/60 × 7 = 3.5 kHz, giving T_e ≈ 286 µs. The die thermal time constant (τ_die ≈ 0.5–2 ms) is several times T_e, so per-FET averaging is acceptable. Below roughly 5 krpm under 80 A (prop stall or desync), averaging breaks down. Peak-model it as one FET carrying the full 80 A at a time:

$$P_{peak,Q} = 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches at a time per motor.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (HS Device)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_b (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.935\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction (LS Device)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I_b \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (Dumped into HS)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance (HS Turn-On, Both Devices' C_oss)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive
This loss is dissipated mostly in the FD6288Q and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

The FD6288Q bootstrap and LDO budget is about 0.06 W per motor.

### 2.6 Switching Totals

| Term | Per motor (W) | Lands on |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 1.935 | HS |
| Dead-time diode | 0.614 | LS |
| Q_rr | 0.048 | HS |
| C_oss | 0.046 | HS |
| **Σ switching** | **2.64** | |

Each HS PWMs for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$\bar P_{sw,HS} = (1.935+0.048+0.046)/3 = 0.68\ \text{W}, \quad \bar P_{sw,LS} = 0.614/3 = 0.20\ \text{W}$$

**Design lever:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it halves (−1.3 W per motor) at the cost of more audible noise and current ripple. In AM32 you can also enable variable PWM, which drops PWM frequency at high duty cycle.
