## 0. Architecture and Feasibility Flags

Four issues shape the rest of the design.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 runs one ESC MCU per motor (AM32/BLHeli_32 class, e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421). | The 48 kHz PWM and 6-step commutation live in the ESC MCUs. The H743 only generates DShot600 and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| F2 | **80 A burst on a 20×20 "nano" board is not thermally credible.** | All math below assumes **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper**. Section 3.6 shows how much worse 20×20 is. |
| F3 | **"80 A burst" must be bounded in time.** | Section 3 derives the duration: about **5 s with all four motors at 80 A** and about **15–20 s with one motor**. |
| F4 | **H743 junction limit.** 480 MHz (VOS0) operation is rated only for Tj ≤ 105 °C on rev V. | Keep the MCU ≥ 8 mm from the FET row, or run at 400 MHz. |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}(110) = 0.70 \times 1.38 = 0.966 \text{ mΩ}\ \ (\text{use } 1.0\text{ mΩ})$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, phase A (high side) sources current and phase B (low side) sinks it. Two cases cover the whole PWM period:

- **PWM on-time (D):** current flows through Q_AH, the motor, then Q_BL.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** Q_AL is synchronously on, so current freewheels through Q_AL, the motor, then Q_BL.

Exactly **two channels conduct at all times**, regardless of duty cycle. Per motor bridge:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors (1/3 of the electrical period):

- Low side of the "sink" phase: on for the full sector, giving I² R_ds / 3 averaged.
- High side of the "source" phase: D · I² R_ds / 3.
- Low side of the "source" phase (sync freewheel): (1−D) · I² R_ds / 3.

Worst-case single FET:

$$P_{FET,max}= \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3}\cdot(1 + 1)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}... \;\Rightarrow\; \text{use } P_{FET,max}\approx \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{2}$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2(80)^2(1.0\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{12.8\ W}$$

- Per hottest FET: about **4.3 W**.
- Four bridges at simultaneous burst: **51.2 W**.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and not negligible)

2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, about 0.30 mΩ/□ at 80 °C.

Per motor path: battery pad → bus plane (about 2 effective squares, split across 3 paralleled layers) → FET → phase pad (about 1.5 squares, 2 layers). Return path is similar.

$$R_{Cu} \approx 2\left(\tfrac{2\times0.30}{3}+\tfrac{1.5\times0.30}{2}\right) \approx 0.85\text{ mΩ}$$

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.85\times10^{-3} \approx \mathbf{5.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

This is comparable to the FET loss. **Mitigation:** stitch the battery pads directly to the bus layers with via fields (≥ 40 × 0.3 mm vias per pad) and use layers 1/2/7/8 as power.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched high-side FET hard-switches. The sync low side switches near zero voltage because its body diode clamps during dead time.

### 2.1 Gate-driver transition times (Miller plateau)

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{src}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.7\,\text{A (effective, with } R_g)} \approx 14\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx 12\ \text{ns}$$
