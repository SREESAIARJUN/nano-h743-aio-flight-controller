## 1. Electrical Operating Model

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). In each 60° electrical sector:

- **Phase X (PWM leg):** high-side FET on for duty D; low-side FET on for (1 − D) minus dead time.
- **Phase Y (return leg):** low-side FET on continuously.
- **Phase Z:** floating (used for BEMF zero-cross sensing).

Phase current I flows through two FET channels at every instant (except during dead time, when it flows through a body diode). This holds regardless of D, which gives the key simplification:

$$
P_{cond}(t) = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS}\right] \;\xrightarrow{R_{HS}=R_{LS}=R}\; 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle on average. Per-FET average loss is therefore P_channel / 6 (conduction is distributed roughly uniformly across sectors).

## 2. Thermal Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS(25)}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]
$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) |
|----------|-----------|
| 25 | 2.00 |
| 80 | 2.61 |
| 100 | 2.83 |
| 125 | 3.10 |

### 2.2 Conduction loss per ESC channel at 80 A

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,(80)^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 12\,800 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j)\ \text{W}
$$

| T_j | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|-----|--------------------|-------------|
| 25 °C | 25.6 W | 102 W |
| 100 °C | 36.2 W | 145 W |
| 125 °C | 39.7 W | 159 W |

### 2.3 Parasitic conduction (often ignored, and it matters)

| Element | Assumption | Loss @ 80 A per channel |
|---------|-----------|-------------------------|
| Phase pad / trace copper | 0.3 mΩ effective, 2 oz, multilayer stitched | 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ = 3.8 W |
| Per-channel current shunt (if used) | 0.25 mΩ | 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W |
| Common DC-bus copper | 0.1 mΩ, 320 A total | 320² × 0.1 mΩ = 10.2 W (board-wide) |

**Design rule:** do not use a single common shunt. At 320 A, a 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates 25.6 W, so use per-channel shunts or a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS770-class or TMCS1100 on the DC bus).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. That is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel at any time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, high-side turn-on and turn-off)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 18\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz}
= \mathbf{0.871\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance (Coss) charge loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 25\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-diode conduction during dead time (two dead times per PWM period)

$$
P_{DT} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.653\ W}
$$

### 3.4 Reverse recovery (low-side diode recovered by high-side turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and temperature. Apply a 2× derating, which gives about 0.07 W.

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$
