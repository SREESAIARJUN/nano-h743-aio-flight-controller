# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

You did not supply some inputs, and some of the spec needs correcting. These points come first because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM.** In every production AIO or 4-in-1 design, each motor runs on its own ESC MCU executing AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32F350. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The partition below follows that architecture. Driving 12 gates and four sensorless commutation loops from the H743 would require custom ESC firmware inside ArduPilot, which ArduPilot does not support.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm) board is a burst rating only.** The math in §3 shows it is valid for seconds at most.
3. **No MOSFET part number was given.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN8 trench FET. Replace the parameters in Table 1 with your datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal operation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

At any instant, two phase legs carry current I:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side FET for (1−D). Exactly one FET is always carrying I.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on for the 120° interval.

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period. Averaged over a commutation cycle, this gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(D-independent with synchronous rectification)}$$

The per-FET average is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot\frac{6}{\,\cdot\,}\;\Rightarrow\; \bar P_{HS} = \tfrac13 I^2 R\,D,\quad \bar P_{LS} = \tfrac13 I^2 R\,(1-D) + \tfrac13 I^2 R$$

The worst-case **instantaneous** dissipation in a single FET, which is what sets the junction transient, is:

$$\hat P_{FET} = I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature coupling

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.55 = **1.55 mΩ**.

Add the copper path. A 2 oz trace that is 5 mm long and 3 mm wide, through vias, contributes roughly 0.3 mΩ per leg, so R_eff ≈ 1.85 mΩ per conducting leg.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.85\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{23.7\ W}$$

$$\hat P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 1.55\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{9.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Thermal-runaway criterion

Writing R_θ for the effective junction-to-ambient thermal resistance, the loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}R_\theta = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1$$

With 80 A: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0055 × R_θ < 1, which requires **R_θ < 28 K/W per FET**. A single DFN on a nano board has R_θJA ≈ 35–50 K/W, so there is **no steady-state equilibrium at 80 A**. This independently confirms that 80 A is a transient-only rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode recovers when the high-side FET turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

There is also an extra turn-on current spike of roughly Q_rr/t_r. This adds about 0.05 W to the V–I term.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.018\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Both FETs in the PWM leg switch each cycle. This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Totals at the 80 A burst

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot, incl. copper) | 23.7 W | 94.8 W |
| Switching, V–I + Q_rr | 1.07 W | 4.3 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| C_oss + gate | 0.07 W | 0.3 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.4 W** | **≈102 W** |

Efficiency at the burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η ≈ 98.7 %. That headline figure is good, but the absolute dissipation is what kills a small board.

**Optimisation note:** conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 20:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz costs only about 1.7 W per channel, so PWM frequency is not the thermal lever. The levers are lower R_DS(on), paralleled FETs, and heavier copper.
