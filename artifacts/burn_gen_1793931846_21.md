## 0. Assumptions and a Form-Factor Warning

### 0.1 Form-factor warning

**80 A burst per motor is not credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board.**

The current-carrying copper, MOSFET footprints and bulk capacitance do not fit. The thermal math below shows that a 20×20 board would hit FET junction limits in about 1 s.

This document is therefore sized for a **30.5×30.5 mm mounting pattern on a roughly 36×36 mm stack**:
- The H743 FC board sits above the ESC board.
- The two boards are joined by a board-to-board connector.

If "nano" is a hard requirement, the realistic rating is about 35–45 A burst.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-commutation interval

In 6-step commutation, one high-side FET (phase A) and one low-side FET (phase B) are energized at any moment. Complementary PWM splits each leg's current between its high-side and low-side FET in proportion to duty D.

- High-side switching leg: P_HS = I² · R_ds(T) · D
- Same leg, synchronous low-side: P_LS,sync = I² · R_ds(T) · (1 − D)
- Statically-on low-side of the return leg: P_LS,static = I² · R_ds(T)

**Total per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D)}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 0.9 \times 1.5 = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.28\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.2 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET conducts during 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is several kHz, which is far faster than the junction thermal time constant, so averaging is valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{17.28}{6} \approx 2.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Copper (non-FET) conduction

Assume 2 oz copper, an 8 mm wide trace, about 15 mm path length, and 2 parallel layers. That gives roughly 0.3 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} \approx I^2 R = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 1.9\ \text{W per motor (phase + return)}$$

### 1.4 Thermal runaway check

R_ds rises with T_j, so the loss and temperature must be solved together:

$$P(T_j) = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\, R_{\theta}$$

The system is stable when α · P_FET,0 · R_θ < 1.

$$0.005 \times 2.9 \times 9 = 0.13 \ll 1 \ \rightarrow \ \text{stable}$$
