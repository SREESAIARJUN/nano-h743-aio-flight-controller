## 0. Architecture Note

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel still needs its own commutation controller, such as AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421, because 48 kHz complementary PWM, zero-crossing detection and the bidirectional DShot (BDShot) response are handled per channel. The H743's role is:

- generating DShot600,
- capturing the BDShot eRPM telemetry,
- running ArduPilot's harmonic notch from that data.

This document models both sides.

**Baseline assumptions (replace with datasheet values):**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs are active in every 60° sector:

- **High-side leg:** the HS FET conducts for duty D. During the off-time (1−D), the synchronous LS FET of the same leg freewheels.
- **Low-side leg:** the LS FET of the opposite phase conducts continuously for the sector.

This means exactly two channels are always in series with the motor current I_ph. The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on),hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,k_T\,R_{25}$$

### 1.2 Per-device RMS current

Each FET is "active" for 1/3 of the electrical period. At burst, D → 1, which is the worst case for the hottest device:

$$I_{rms,FET} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}$$

$$P_{FET} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}}{3}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| R_hot | 1.0 mΩ × 1.6 | 1.6 mΩ |
| P per conducting path | 80² × 1.6 mΩ | 10.24 W |
| **P_cond per channel** | 2 × 10.24 | **20.5 W** |
| P per FET (worst) | 6400 × 1.6 m / 3 | 3.41 W |
| PCB copper (phase + bus, ~0.25 mΩ path) | 80² × 0.25 m | 1.6 W |

### 1.4 Thermal iteration

R_ds(on) depends on T_j, so the operating point must be solved iteratively:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.007\ /\mathrm{K}$$

Converge using T_j = T_board + P_FET · Z_th(j-b). See §3.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the HS and its synchronous LS form one commutating pair.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 35 ns · 48 k | **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 n · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw (hard-switched edge) | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f_sw = 2 · 0.8 · 80 · 100 n · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 · 40 n · 10 · 48 k | 0.04 W (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.45 W** |

**Totals at 80 A burst:**

$$P_{ch} \approx 20.5 + 2.45 + 1.6 \approx 24.5\ \mathrm{W}$$

$$P_{4\text{-}in\text{-}1} \approx 98\ \mathrm{W} \quad \text{(all four channels in burst simultaneously)}$$

**Sensitivity:** conduction loss scales with I², while overlap loss scales with I · f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.15 W per channel at 80 A, so conduction dominates. The best levers are lower R_ds(on), paralleled FETs, and heavier copper.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

A two-tier Cauer-style lumped model:

```
P_FET ─► [Z_th j-c ≈ 1.2 K/W] ─► [R_th c-board (vias+solder) ≈ 2 K/W] ─► NODE_BOARD
NODE_BOARD: C_board ‖ R_th board-ambient ─► T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board thermal mass (nano 4-in-1 with Al spreader)

Assumed mass:

- FR4 ≈ 3 g × 0.9 J/(g·K)
- Cu ≈ 4 g × 0.385 J/(g·K)
- Al spreader ≈ 2 g × 0.9 J/(g·K)

$$C_{th} \approx 6\ \mathrm{J/K}$$

R_th,b-a ≈ 4 K/W in prop-wash airflow, about 12 K/W in still air.

### 3.3 Transient board rise

The time constant is τ = R · C ≈ 4 × 6 = 24 s ≫ burst duration, so the adiabatic approximation holds:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P\,R\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}}$$

| Burst | 1 channel (24.5 W) | 4 channels (98 W) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | +4 K | +16 K |
| 3 s | +12 K | +45 K |
| 5 s | +19 K | +72 K |
