## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scoping Assumptions

Several premises in the brief need correcting before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1, each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AM32 on STM32G071/AT32F421, or BLHeli_32 hardware). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. Running FOC or six-step for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not a supported ArduPilot configuration.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per-motor, ≤10 s.** All four at 80 A on 6S is about 8 kW. No nano-class board or pack sustains that. The numbers below show why.
3. **"Nano" means a 30.5 × 30.5 mm (M3) or 25.5 mm whoop-pattern PCB.** A 20 × 20 board cannot physically carry 80 A-rated FETs and the required copper.
4. **The netlists are reference topology.** They show the connectivity a layout engineer works from. They are not a released, DRC-verified design. Part values must be confirmed against the datasheets of the actual FETs and driver you procure.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

With six-step trapezoidal and complementary (synchronous) PWM, the phase current always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series** in each commutation sector:

- During the on-time, it flows through the HS of phase A and the LS of phase B.
- During the off-time, it freewheels through the LS of phase A (synchronous) and the LS of phase B.

The series resistance is therefore 2·R_ds regardless of duty cycle (dead time ignored here; it is handled in §2.3):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} + I_{ph}^2 R_{shunt}$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Electro-Thermal Coupling)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Because the loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, the steady-state solution is a fixed point:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot R_{th,ja} \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) R_{th}}{1 - I^2\cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th}}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs if the denominator reaches 0, i.e. when:

$$I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja} \ge 1$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_FETs per motor (2·I²R) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** |

**Copper and shunt:**
- Phase trace plus pad is about 0.3 mΩ round trip at 2 oz. Many AM32 designs omit a per-phase shunt, but a 0.25 mΩ shunt on the shared bus is common.
- Copper loss: 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**.
- The shunt is shared by four motors and is counted in §3.

**Per-motor conduction at a hot junction ≈ 21 W.** Each conducting FET dissipates about 9.6 W. The hottest FET is the one conducting in two of three sectors.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per motor, in each PWM period, one leg hard-switches (the PWM'd phase). The other active leg is static for that sector.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard Switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \cdot 50\text{nC}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

This is understated at 80 A because Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. Budget 0.15 W.

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{ns}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V^2 f_{sw} \approx 0.5\cdot 1.5\text{nF}\cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in the Driver, Not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 60\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k}\cdot 2 = 0.058\ \text{W/motor}$$

This is trivial per motor. However, the gate-driver LDO/charge pump sees 4 × that plus quiescent current, so size the 10–12 V rail for at least 100 mA.

### 2.6 Switching Subtotal

**≈ 1.4 W per motor at 80 A.**

At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by about 15:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz (smoother on small motors) adds only about 1.4 W per motor. Lowering R_ds is the lever that matters.

## 3. Total Dissipation Budget

| Source | Per motor | ×4 (all at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Phase copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching + rr + dt + oss | 1.4 W | 5.6 W |
| Bus copper / shunt (320 A, 0.25 mΩ + 0.2 mΩ) | — | **46 W** |
| Bulk cap ESR (see §5) | ~0.5 W | 2 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 138 W** |

The bus term is a key finding: at a 320 A aggregate, the shared battery copper becomes the largest single loss. Realistic "burst" is asymmetric (one or two motors at 80 A during yaw punch-out). Design to the following envelope:
- **Design point A (sustained):** 4 × 40 A → ~26 W total
- **Design point B (burst):** 4 × 80 A for ≤ 3 s, or 2 × 80 A + 2 × 30 A for ≤ 10 s
