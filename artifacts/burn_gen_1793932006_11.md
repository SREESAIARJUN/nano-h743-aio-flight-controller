## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Up-Front Risks

The request mixes two subsystems that are normally separate, so these assumptions frame everything below:

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Flight controller MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (LQFP100 or TFBGA100), 400 MHz, running ArduPilot |
| ESC control | **Four separate ESC MCUs** (AM32 on STM32G071 or AT32F421). The H743 does **not** drive gates. ArduPilot has no motor commutation or FOC firmware, and one H743 cannot practically commutate 4 BLDC motors alongside the flight stack. |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Switching | 48 kHz complementary (synchronous) PWM, 6-step trapezoidal |
| Burst rating | 80 A per motor, simultaneously on all 4 (worst case) |
| Form factor | "Nano" read as 20×20 mm mounting (~25×25 mm board) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, ~1.0 mΩ @25 °C class in 3.3×3.3 mm DFN (the only package that fits 24 FETs in this area) |

**Key risks to resolve before layout:**

1. **Burst current vs. board size.** The thermal math in §3 shows that an 80 A burst on all four motors drives a 20×20 board from 40 °C to MOSFET limits in about **1.5–2 s**. On a nano board, "80 A burst" is only defensible as a sub-second, single-motor transient rating. Many commercial nano-ESC ratings are marketing figures. I recommend either rerating to ~35–45 A burst / 25 A continuous, or moving to 30.5×30.5 mm.
2. **Component values are representative, not datasheet-verified.** Every R_DS(on), Q_g, Q_rr, and t_r/t_f figure must be replaced with the selected part's datasheet values at the operating temperature.
3. **The DMA map in §5 is a starting proposal.** ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` output from an actual `hwdef` build is authoritative.

## 1. Power Stage Topology (per motor)

Each motor uses a 3-phase bridge of 6 N-channel MOSFETs, so the board has 24 MOSFETs total. In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, at any instant:

- One phase leg high-side is **PWM-switching** (duty D).
- During D, current flows through that high-side FET and the low-side FET of the return leg.
- During (1−D), the low-side complementary FET of the PWM leg conducts (synchronous freewheel) together with the return-leg low-side FET.

In both PWM states the motor current flows through **two FETs in series**. This is the basis of the conduction model.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ |
| T_j (design point) | 125 °C |
| R_DS(on),125 | 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ** |

Add PCB copper and solder resistance in the current path. For 2 oz copper on 4 parallel layers, a ~6 mm path is roughly 0.2 mΩ per leg. Use R_path ≈ 0.4 mΩ per motor current loop.

### 2.2 Conduction loss (per motor)

For trapezoidal drive, the phase current during a conduction interval is approximately the DC motor current I_M, so I_RMS through the conducting pair is approximately I_M:

$$P_{cond} = I_M^2\,\left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{path}\right)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2\,(2 \times 1.6 + 0.4)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Split between FETs (D ≈ 0.85 at full throttle burst):

- PWM-leg high-side: D·I²R = 0.85 × 10.24 = 8.7 W
- PWM-leg low-side (sync): (1−D)·I²R = 1.5 W
- Return-leg low-side: I²R = **10.2 W**, the hottest FET
- Copper: 2.6 W

Commutation rotates these roles every 60° electrical, so time-averaged loss is shared across the 6 FETs. Over the short thermal time constants of DFN parts, however, **peak FET dissipation is ~10 W**.

### 2.3 Switching loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched high side):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_M\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 40 ns (driver sourcing/sinking ~1–2 A into Q_g ≈ 50 nC through ~5 Ω gate resistors):

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 100 ns, two transitions per period, V_F ≈ 0.8 V):

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_M\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (Q_rr ≈ 40 nC):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9}\times 25.2 \times 48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance** (Q_oss ≈ 30 nC):

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching FETs per PWM cycle, V_GS = 10 V):

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\times10^{-9}\times10\times48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

| Switching loss term | Per motor |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W |
| Q_rr | 0.05 W |
| Q_oss | 0.02 W |
| Gate | 0.05 W |
| **Total P_sw** | **2.67 W** |

**Sensitivity:** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to ~0.97 W, but ripple current doubles. At 96 kHz it rises to ~3.9 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for low-inductance racing/cine motors.

### 2.4 Total burst dissipation

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper path | 2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| ESC MCU, gate driver, LDO | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~26 W** | **~104 W** |

Electrical efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × 0.85 ≈ 6.85 kW, so η ≈ 98.5%. The efficiency looks excellent, but **~100 W of heat on a ~6 cm² board is the binding constraint**.

At a continuous design point of **30 A per motor** (T_j ≈ 90 °C, R_DS ≈ 1.39 mΩ):
- P_cond = 900 × (2 × 1.39 + 0.4) × 10⁻³ = 2.9 W
- P_sw ≈ 1.0 W
- Per motor ≈ **3.9 W**; total ≈ **15.6 W**
