# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller: Design Model (Job #13)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 should run ArduPilot as the flight controller. It should **not** commutate the motors directly:

- ArduPilot has no native ESC firmware for the H7.
- A 48 kHz, 6-step or FOC loop for four motors on the same core as the EKF is a real-time risk.

The standard and recommended architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM)
                                              └─► 4× FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers
                                                   └─► 24× N-MOSFETs (6 per motor)
```

### 0.2 Form-factor flag

Calling 80 A per motor a "nano" rating is not physically credible on a 20×20 mm board, and section 2 proves this. The model below therefore assumes:

- **30.5×30.5 mm board, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers.**
- **80 A is a burst rating of ≤ 2 s at a time**, never continuous.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor, at 80 A)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

AM32 drives the bridge with complementary PWM and synchronous rectification. This means exactly two FETs are always in the current path: one high-side or its synchronous low-side, plus the active low-side of the return phase.

- Hot on-resistance: R_ds,hot = k_T · R_ds(on),25 = 1.35 mΩ
- Conduction loss: P_cond = 2 · I² · R_ds,hot = 2 · 6400 · 1.35×10⁻³ = **17.28 W**

Per-FET loss depends on commutation sector and duty. Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, but the worst instantaneous FET dissipates I²·R_ds,hot = **8.64 W**. That worst-case figure is what sets junction ΔT.

### 1.2 Hard-switching loss (overlap)

- P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw
- P_sw = 0.5 · 25.2 · 80 · 20×10⁻⁹ · 48,000 = **0.968 W**

### 1.3 Dead-time body-diode loss

- P_dt = V_f · I · 2 · t_dead · f_sw
- P_dt = 0.8 · 80 · 400×10⁻⁹ · 48,000 = **1.229 W**

This term is larger than the switching loss. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor, but only if your gate-drive timing margin allows it.

### 1.4 Reverse recovery, output capacitance and gate drive

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40n · 25.2 · 48k | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 0.018 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 0.144 W |

### 1.5 Shunt loss

- P_shunt = I² · R_shunt = 6400 · 0.2×10⁻³ = **1.28 W**

Use a 2512 metal-strip shunt rated for at least 3 W pulse.

### 1.6 Totals

| Component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.28 W | 69.1 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.21 W | 0.8 W |
| Shunt | 1.28 W | 5.1 W |
| **Subtotal** | **20.97 W** | **83.9 W** |
| PCB copper (estimated below) | about 2–4 W | about 10 W |
| **Total burst dissipation** | | **about 94 W** |

Copper loss estimate for a phase path of ~15 mm length, 4 mm width, 4 layers of 2 oz copper:

- R = ρL/A = 1.72×10⁻⁸ · 0.015 / (4×10⁻³ · 280×10⁻⁶) ≈ 0.23 mΩ
- P ≈ 6400 · 0.23 m ≈ 1.5 W per phase in the current path

### 1.7 Efficiency check

Input power at full burst is roughly 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × modulation. At high throttle this is on the order of 7–8 kW. The ~94 W loss therefore gives about 98.7% efficiency, consistent with real-world 6S ESCs.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Why steady state is impossible

A 30.5 mm board in prop wash has an approximate convection coefficient of h ≈ 60 W/m²K over a two-sided effective area of ~1.9×10⁻³ m².

- R_th,board-air ≈ 1 / (60 · 1.9×10⁻³) ≈ **8.8 K/W**
- Steady-state rise at 94 W ≈ 825 K

**80 A per motor is burst-only, and the design must be treated as an adiabatic thermal-mass problem.**

### 2.2 Board heat capacity

| Mass element | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (8 layers × 70 µm, ~60% fill) | 9.3×10⁻⁴ m² · 5.6×10⁻⁴ m · 0.6 · 8960 kg/m³ = 2.8 g; × 0.385 J/gK | 1.08 J/K |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm) | 2.7 g × 1.2 J/gK | 3.2 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | | ~0.6 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | **≈ 4.9 J/K** |

### 2.3 Board temperature rise during a burst

- dT/dt ≈ P / C_th = 94 / 4.9 ≈ **19 K/s** (adiabatic)
- With the 8.8 K/W convection path, the board time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 43 s. Bursts are much shorter than τ, so the adiabatic approximation is valid.

Rise from 25 °C ambient after a 2 s burst:

- ΔT_board = R · P · (1 − e^(−t/τ)) = 8.8 · 94 · (1 − e^(−2/43)) ≈ **37.5 K**, so the board reaches ~62 °C.

### 2.4 Junction temperature

Assume Z_th(j-c) at 2 s ≈ R_th(j-c) = 1.2 K/W, which is typical for a 3.3 mm DFN.

- T_j,max = T_board + P_FET,max · Z_th(j-c) = 62 + 8.64 · 1.2 ≈ **72 °C**
