**Flag 1: Who generates the 48 kHz PWM.**
In a standard design, each ESC has its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU produces the 48 kHz gate PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.

If you intend the H743 to drive all 24 gates directly, ArduPilot cannot do it, and bidirectional DShot becomes meaningless. This document assumes the standard split architecture.

**Flag 2: 80 A on a nano board is thermally infeasible beyond a few seconds.**
Section 3 shows the math. Treat 80 A as a per-motor, short-burst, non-simultaneous rating with airflow.

**Flag 3: Verify the hwdef pin and DMA tables.**
The tables in Section 4 follow ArduPilot hwdef conventions. Verify them against the `dma_resolver.py` output and the current ArduPilot `libraries/AP_HAL_ChibiOS/hwdef` documentation before tape-out.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.

With complementary PWM at duty D, current alternates between the two devices of a leg:

- The high-side (HS) device conducts for fraction D of each PWM period.
- The low-side (LS) device in that leg conducts for fraction (1−D), acting as a synchronous rectifier.

The loop current therefore always flows through exactly two channel resistances. That gives:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per device, averaged over the electrical cycle (each device is active 1/3 of the cycle):

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + \text{(its own conducting interval)}$$

The worst case is the LS device near D → 0, or the HS device at D → 1. Full throttle (D ≈ 1) is the burst case.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (closed-loop)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Using T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}(125) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 100) = 1.45\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = 18.6\ \text{W per motor}$$

The hottest device at D ≈ 1 carries the full current for its 1/3 of the cycle, plus ripple:

$$P_{cond,HS,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\times 6400 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH, the worst ripple occurs at D = 0.5 with zero back-EMF (stall or start):

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{25.2 \times 0.5}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 17.5\ \text{A}$$

This adds about 0.4 % to the conduction loss. Ripple is negligible at 80 A but significant at low current.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per commutation interval, only the PWM-switching HS/LS pair is hard-switching. The LS device switches softly, aside from diode recovery.

**1. V-I overlap (hard-switched device):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**2. C_oss discharge:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

**3. Body diode reverse recovery** (charged to the HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**4. Dead-time body diode conduction** (two dead times per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I \times 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**5. Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, 2 switching devices):

$$P_g = Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} \times 2 = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: about 1.71 W per motor at 80 A.**

### 2.1 Per-motor and board totals (80 A burst)

| Loss term | W/motor | W (×4 motors) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (125 °C) | 18.6 | 74.4 |
| Switching (V-I overlap) | 0.97 | 3.9 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr, C_oss, gate | 0.13 | 0.5 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 1 per motor) | 1.6 | 6.4 |
| Copper traces and planes (≈0.5 mΩ path) | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| **Total** | **≈25 W** | **≈100 W** |

**Design lever:** dropping f_sw to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per motor. Conduction dominates by roughly 10:1. The leverage is in R_ds(on), paralleling devices (which nano boards cannot fit), and copper weight, not in frequency.
