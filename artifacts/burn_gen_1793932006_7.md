# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #0)

## 0. Scope and Constraints

**80A per motor on a 20×20 mm board is a burst rating of about 1 second, not a continuous rating.** The math in §2 shows this. Every figure below follows from the stated assumptions, so you can substitute datasheet values for your chosen parts.

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. The standard (and only practical) topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──BDShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32) ──► 4× 3-ph gate driver ──► 24× MOSFET
```

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.
- The H743 owns the DShot/DMA timing.

## 1. Loss Model: One ESC Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal with Synchronous Rectification

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

In any commutation sector, two phases carry current.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. Its complementary low-side FET conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the full sector.

The PWM leg therefore always has one FET carrying I. The return leg also always has one FET carrying I. The result is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

Because R_DS(on) depends on temperature and temperature depends on loss, solve iteratively. Starting at T_j = 110 °C:

$$R = 2.0\,\text{m}\Omega\,(1 + 0.0045\cdot 85) = 2.77\,\text{m}\Omega$$
$$P_{cond} = 2\cdot 80^2\cdot 2.77\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{35.4\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The temperature-dependent version, for simulation, is:

$$P_{cond}(T) = 2I^2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T-25)\right]$$

This feeds back into the thermal ODE in §2.3. The positive feedback is what makes long bursts run away.

### 1.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. Each term below is per channel.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 kHz | 1.45 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 kHz | 1.23 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs per leg, dissipated mostly in driver and gate resistor) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.8 W** |

Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per channel. That is only about 4% of total loss at 80 A, so conduction dominates.

### 1.3 Totals

| Case | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| **80 A per motor (burst)** | 35.4 + 2.8 = **38.2 W** | **≈ 153 W** |
| 80 A total, i.e. 20 A per motor | 2·400·2.3 mΩ + 0.7 ≈ **2.5 W** | **≈ 10 W** |

The second row is the realistic hover-to-cruise envelope on a nano frame.

**Hottest single FET.** The return-leg low-side FET conducts for an entire 120° sector:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R = 6400 \cdot 2.77\,\text{m}\Omega = 17.7\ \text{W}$$

Over a full electrical cycle, each FET averages about P_cond/6 plus its share of switching loss, roughly 6–7 W.
