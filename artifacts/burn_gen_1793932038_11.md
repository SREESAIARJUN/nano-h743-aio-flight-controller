## 0. Architectural Ground Truths and Assumptions

Several clarifications shape everything downstream.

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU owns the 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, and the bi-directional DShot (BDShot) reply. The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The two power domains are modelled separately below.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-format board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** The model below shows the realistic continuous figure is about 25 A per channel. 80 A is sustainable for only about 2–4 s from a warm start. This matches industry practice: marketing burst ratings are adiabatic numbers.
3. **Baseline electrical assumptions** (all parametric, so substitute your BOM values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A |
| FET (40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 / 5×6) | R_ds,on(25 °C) | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_ds tempco | α | 0.0050 /K |
| Switching edge times (driver ~1–2 A) | t_r, t_f | 15 ns each |
| Output capacitance (energy-equivalent) | C_oss,er | 1.5 nF |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Total gate charge @10 V | Q_g | 60 nC |

| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| FETs per channel / total | n | 6 / 24 |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.000 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.500 mΩ |

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phases conduct, and the current always passes through exactly two FET channels:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, held on continuously.

Including inductor ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

The total conduction loss per motor channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Ripple term

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example inputs: a 2207 motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH, worst-case D = 0.5, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12.6 V.

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\times10^{-6}\times 48\times10^{3}} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

$$\frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6.4\ \text{A}^2$$

This is negligible against I² = 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.04 A.

### 1.4 Numerical results at 80 A

| T_j | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2 × 6400 × 1.0 m = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2 × 6400 × 1.375 m = **17.6 W** | 70.4 W |
| 125 °C | **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

Per FET, the loss is roughly P_ch/6 averaged over an electrical revolution, about 3 W. Within one 60° commutation sector, however, the continuously-on low-side FET carries the full I²R. This sets the peak per-device load at about 8.8 W for 1/6 of each electrical period. At eRPM greater than about 10k, that pulse is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (around 1 ms), so the average governs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return leg switches only at commutation, which is negligible. All energies are evaluated at V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.452\ W}$$

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.046\ \text{W}$$
