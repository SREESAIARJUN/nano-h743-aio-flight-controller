## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, exactly two phases conduct the phase current I. Taking the A+ / B− sector as the example:

- The high-side FET of A is PWM'd at duty D. During the off-time (1−D), the A low-side FET conducts by synchronous rectification.
- The low-side FET of B is held on for the full sector.

At every instant there are therefore exactly **two FET channels in series** with the motor current. This gives:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per-device conduction losses over one electrical revolution (each FET is in-sector for 1/3 of the time):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} D,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}\big[1 + (1-D)\big]$$

The low-side FETs run hottest at low duty with high current. That combination occurs at punch-outs from a low-throttle state with high prop load.

### 1.2 Temperature feedback (self-consistent solution)

On-resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Junction temperature in turn depends on losses:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, Z_{\theta}(t)$$

Solve by fixed-point iteration, or in closed form for the conduction-dominated case:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + k Z_\theta R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k Z_\theta R_{25}\alpha},\quad k = \text{(current-squared share for that FET)}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when $k Z_\theta R_{25}\alpha \ge 1$. This must be checked for the hottest low-side FET at steady state:

- k ≈ (2/3)·I²
- Z_θ → R_θJA ≈ 40 K/W
- At I = 40 A: k Z R α = (2/3)(1600)(40)(1e-3)(0.0055) = **0.23**. Stable.
- At I = 80 A steady: = **0.94**. Marginal, near runaway.

This is the mathematical reason 80 A must be a time-limited burst rating.

### 1.3 Numerical values at 80 A

Assume T_j ≈ 110 °C, so R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.47\text{e-}3 = \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the low-side FET held on does not. All terms below are per channel, with V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f (hard-switched edge) | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f = 0.8·80·400 ns·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver/R_g, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f = 2·45n·10·48k | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.8 W** |

Two design observations follow from this table:

- **Dead-time diode loss is almost as large as switching overlap.** Reducing t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Validate the shorter dead time against shoot-through on the bench using a current probe on the bus.
- **Switching loss scales linearly with frequency.** At 24 kHz the total halves to about 1.4 W, but the motor has more ripple. 48 kHz is justified mainly for low-inductance (low-kV, high-pole) motors.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

Two-level Foster-style lumped model:

```
Pj ──[R_θJC 1.5K/W]── pad ──[R_spread ≈ 3 K/W (per FET, 2oz, via array)]── board node
                                                                              │
                                                                         C_board
                                                                              │
                                                     [R_θ,board-amb ≈ 3.5 K/W prop-wash; 8 K/W still air]
                                                                              │
                                                                            T_amb
```

### 3.2 Board heat capacity (30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner)

| Material | Mass × specific heat | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Cu | ≈ 3.0 g × 0.385 J/gK | 1.16 J/K |
| FR4 | ≈ 2.2 g × 1.1 J/gK | 2.42 J/K |
| FETs, capacitors, solder | | ≈ 0.6 J/K |
| **C_board** | | **≈ 4.2 J/K** |

Board time constant in prop-wash: τ = R·C = 3.5 × 4.2 ≈ **15 s**. Any burst much shorter than τ is therefore governed by heat capacity, not by R_θ.

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P_{tot} R_{BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx T_{amb} + \frac{P_{tot}}{C} t \quad (t \ll \tau)$$
