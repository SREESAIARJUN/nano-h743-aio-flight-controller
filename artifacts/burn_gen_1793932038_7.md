# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Thermal, Switching and DShot Timer Design

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

**Architecture correction.** The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller. A 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCU per motor (or one dual-motor MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document therefore models:
- **FC side:** H743 running ArduPilot, sending bidirectional DShot600 to 4 ESC MCUs.
- **ESC side:** 4× ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 with AM32), 3-phase gate driver, and 24× N-MOSFET.

**Parameters.** Values marked † are placeholders. Replace them with datasheet values before release.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge (design at 8S = 33.6 V for margin) |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current per motor, ≤ 2 s |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ† | 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET at V_gs = 10 V |
| α | 0.0045 /K | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient (typical, verify) |
| t_r, t_f | 12 ns, 10 ns† | Effective switching times with the gate driver chosen |
| Q_g | 30 nC† | Total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 40 nC† | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge |
| C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF† | Energy-related output capacitance |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward drop |
| t_dt | 200 ns | Dead time per edge (2 edges per period) |
| V_drv | 10 V | Gate drive voltage |

**Feasibility warning.** At 80 A, a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 dissipates roughly 110 W during a 4-motor burst. That is only survivable for a few seconds and relies on board thermal mass. Section 2 quantifies this. A realistic continuous rating for this format is 35–45 A per motor, provided there is forced airflow from the props.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel, 6 MOSFETs)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) ≈ **1.66 mΩ**.

Iterate this with Section 2. The loss is self-heating and converges in 2–3 passes.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

**Commutation scheme.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM is used. At every instant, current flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:
- one high-side switch that is PWMing, or its complementary low-side switch, and
- one low-side return switch that is held on.

**Conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu} + I_{ph}^2 R_{shunt}$$

- FET term: 80² × 2 × 1.66 mΩ = **21.2 W**
- Copper term, with R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ (2 oz, 4 layers, phase pours): **1.9 W**
- Shunt term, if per-motor 0.25 mΩ sensing is used: **1.6 W**

**P_cond ≈ 24.7 W per motor.**

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. High-side and its synchronous low-side share the PWM interval as D and (1 − D):

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{6}\,(2 I^2 R_{ds}) \approx 3.5\ \text{W}$$

Instantaneous dissipation while a FET is conducting is I²R = **10.6 W**.

### 1.3 Switching loss (hard-switched leg, 48 kHz)

**Turn-on and turn-off overlap:**

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (synchronous FET body diode):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** Two FETs switch per PWM period. This power is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$

Dead-time loss exceeds V·I overlap loss here. Cutting t_dt to about 100 ns saves around 0.6 W per motor, but only if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it.

### 1.4 Totals

| Loss | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 21.2 W | 84.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| Switching + Q_rr + C_oss | 1.13 W | 4.5 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Gate drive | 0.03 W | 0.1 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.1 W** | **≈ 108 W** |

**Efficiency.** The I_ph-to-motor-power relation depends on duty cycle. At full throttle (D ≈ 1) the motor receives about 25.2 V × 80 A ≈ 2.0 kW per motor, so η ≈ 98.6 %. The problem is not efficiency; it is power density, about 27 W/cm².

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Steady-state network (per FET)

```
Tj ──Rth_jc(1.5 K/W†)── Tcase ──Rth_solder/via(1.0)── PCB Cu ──Rth_spread(board)── Tboard ──Rth_ba── Tamb
```

**Board-to-ambient resistance** for a 20×20 mm, 4-layer, 2 oz board with prop-wash airflow (h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K, both sides, A ≈ 8 cm²):

$$R_{th,ba} \approx \frac{1}{hA} \approx \frac{1}{80 \times 8\times10^{-4}} \approx 15.6\ \text{K/W}$$

Steady state at 108 W would give ΔT ≈ 1700 K. **80 A cannot be continuous.** The maximum continuous total for T_board ≤ 100 °C at 30 °C ambient is about 70 K / 15.6 ≈ **4.5 W**. Higher continuous ratings in real products come from heavier copper, heatsinks and airflow measured in situ; derive them from testing.

### 2.2 Burst (transient) model: lumped board capacity

$$C_{th} = \sum m_i c_{p,i} \approx (2.0\text{ g Cu} \times 0.385) + (1.5\text{ g FR4} \times 1.1) + (0.6\text{ g FETs/solder} \times 0.7) \approx 2.8\ \text{J/K}$$

$$T_b(t) = T_{b0} + P\,R_{ba}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right), \qquad \tau = R_{ba}C_{th} \approx 44\ \text{s}$$

For t ≪ τ the response is adiabatic: dT_b/dt ≈ P / C_th = 108 / 2.8 ≈ **38.6 K/s**.

### 2.3 Junction rise above board (per FET)

Use average FET power, because the electrical period is far shorter than τ_jc of a few ms:

$$\Delta T_{jb} = \bar P_{FET}\,(R_{jc}+R_{cb}) \approx (3.5 + 0.35_{sw/dt}) \times 2.5 \approx 9.6\ \text{K}$$

**Exception at low eRPM** (stall or startup at 80 A): the electrical period approaches milliseconds. Use the transient impedance Z_th,jc(t_on) with the 10.6 W instantaneous dissipation, which adds roughly 5–10 K.

### 2.4 Burst duration limit

**Limits:** T_j,max = 150 °C, with a 25 K derating, so the design limit is 125 °C. Board start temperature T_b0 = 50 °C (warm hover).

$$t_{burst} = \frac{(125 - 9.6 - 50)\,C_{th}}{P} = \frac{65.4 \times 2.8}{108} \approx \mathbf{1.7\ s}\ \text{(all 4 motors)}$$

If one motor bursts while the others are at hover (about 30 W total), the limit is roughly **5–6 s**.

**Firmware requirements:**
- Enforce an I²t limit in the ESC.
- Use the ESC NTC (place it centrally, between FET banks).
- In ArduPilot, set `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX` / `BATT_*` current limits and monitor the ESC telemetry temperature.
