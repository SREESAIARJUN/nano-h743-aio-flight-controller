# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #48): Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Feasibility Review

Before the numbers, three findings affect everything below:

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors itself.** ArduPilot has no on-FC 6-step or FOC motor driver. The ESC section needs **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, or STM32G071), each driving its own 3-phase bridge. The H743 sends them DShot600 commands over bi-directional single-wire lines.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a transient rating, not a thermal capability.** The model in §2–§3 shows roughly 31 W of dissipation per channel at 80 A. With all four channels at burst, the board heats at about 45 K/s. Treat 80 A as a ≤1 s, single-channel-dominant event. Plan on 25–35 A continuous per channel at best on a 20×20 board.
3. **The bulk capacitor ripple is the hidden killer**, not the MOSFETs (see §2.6).

All numeric parameters below are **stated assumptions**. Substitute datasheet values from the chosen parts before sign-off.

## 2. Loss Model at 80 A Burst, 48 kHz

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant the motor current flows through **exactly two FET channels**:

- The high-side FET of the PWM phase during time D·T, or its complementary low-side FET during (1−D)·T.
- The low-side FET of the sink phase, which stays on continuously for the 120° interval.

So the instantaneous conduction resistance is always 2·R_DS(on), **independent of duty cycle D**.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on),hot}\cdot k_{ripple}$$

The ripple factor is

$$k_{ripple} = 1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2 \approx 1.0$$

because the motor inductance keeps ΔI/I below 20%, which makes k_ripple ≈ 1.003.
