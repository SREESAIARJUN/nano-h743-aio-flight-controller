# Job #60: STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC

## Architecture Specification Rev A: Loss Model, Thermal Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results drive the rest of this document:

1. **An 80 A burst on 4 channels does not fit a 20×20 mm nano footprint.** At 80 A per channel, total board dissipation is about 95 to 100 W (derived in §2–3). A 20×20 board has roughly 1.5 J/K of thermal mass, so it heats at about 60 K/s. The usable burst is about 1 s, not the typical 5 to 10 s "burst" rating. Recommendation: a 30.5×30.5 mm stack, or derate the nano board to 45 A burst / 35 A continuous. §4 gives the math for both.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed). The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. That link is what the DMA plan in §5 covers.
3. **All device parameters below are representative values for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ PQFN MOSFET class.** The model is fully parametric. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values before freezing the BOM.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:

- **PWM ON:** HS(A) → motor → LS(B)
- **PWM OFF:** LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) → motor → LS(B)

So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution over one electrical revolution

Each FET is active in 2 of the 6 sectors. The sink LS FET conducts at 100% during its 120° interval, while the PWM leg's FETs share time in the ratio D : (1−D).

Peak instantaneous loss in the sink LS FET:

$$P_{LS,sink} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

The average per FET is about P_cond,ch / 6. However, the thermal design must use the **sink-FET peak**, because at low eRPM (startup, prop stall) the commutation period exceeds the FET's thermal time constant.

### 2.3 Numbers at T_j = 125 °C (R_ds = 1.6 mΩ)

| I_ph | P_cond per channel | Sink-FET instantaneous |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A | 2·6400·0.0016 = **20.5 W** | 10.2 W |
| 50 A | 8.0 W | 4.0 W |
| 35 A | 3.9 W | 2.0 W |

### 2.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, and significant)

The path runs bus → HS → phase pad → LS → GND return. Assume about 0.3 mΩ total for 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner planes in parallel on a nano board:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.0003 = 1.9\ \text{W per channel @ 80 A}$$

### 2.5 Current shunt

If you place a per-channel low-side shunt of 0.25 mΩ, it adds 1.6 W at 80 A. **Recommendation:** use a single bus-level shunt sensed by the FC, or MOSFET R_ds sensing in AM32 if the per-channel current isn't required.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The sink leg is static within each sector.

### 3.1 Gate drive currents and transition times

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}} = \frac{10-3.5}{5} = 1.3\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} = 0.7\ \text{A}$$

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 10\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{13\ \text{nC}}{0.7\ \text{A}} = 18.6\ \text{ns}$$

### 3.2 Loss components

| Component | Equation | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f = ½·25.2·80·28.6 ns·48 k | **1.38 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dead·f | **0.61 W** |
| C_oss charge/discharge | C_oss·V²·f | 0.046 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f | 0.048 W |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

**Design lever:** dead-time conduction is a third of switching loss. Cutting t_dead from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. That is safe given t_off ≈ 19 ns, with margin for driver propagation skew. Note that switching loss scales linearly with f. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of audible noise and ripple current.
