## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Assessment

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot frames. Each ESC channel is driven by its own ESC MCU (AM32 on STM32G071 or AT32F421 class), which runs the 48 kHz PWM, back-EMF commutation, and bidirectional DShot eRPM replies. All four channels sit on one PCB stack.

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, IMU, DShot600 TX, eRPM RX |
| ESC ×4 | STM32G071GBU6 or AT32F421K8U7 | 6-step / sinusoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) | Half-bridge drive, built-in dead time |
| Power stage ×4 | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN | Bridge |

**Feasibility warning.** As Section 4 shows, 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not thermally sustainable. It works only as a short burst (≤1–2 s) with a duty-cycle limit. I recommend specifying **"80 A per channel, 1 s burst, ≤10 % duty; 35 A continuous"**, or moving to a 30.5×30.5 mm format. All numbers below use stated, representative assumptions. Replace them with your chosen MOSFET's datasheet values before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths
With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, exactly two MOSFETs carry phase current at every instant:
- the high-side or low-side of the PWM'd phase, alternating with duty D, and
- the low-side of the "sink" phase, which stays fully on.

The instantaneous channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·1.00e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | **17.1 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |

### 2.3 Per-device distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg is "active" for 1/3 of the time, "sink" for 1/3, and floating for 1/3. Averaged:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3} \;\Rightarrow\; 4.3\text{ W (25 °C)} \rightarrow 6.2\text{ W (125 °C)}$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in one device is I²R ≈ 6.4–9.3 W. This matters for the Z_th transient analysis in Section 4.3, because at low eRPM a single FET can carry current for many milliseconds.

### 2.4 Electro-thermal convergence
Conduction loss and junction temperature depend on each other. The coupled solution is

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}P_0\alpha}$$

where P_0 = 2I²R_25.

**Thermal runaway criterion:** R_th·P_0·α < 1. For the board-level R_th of about 6 K/W derived in Section 4, P_0 = 51 W (4 channels): 6 × 51 × 0.0045 = **1.38 > 1**. There is no steady-state solution. This confirms mathematically that 80 A continuous on all four channels is impossible on this board.

### 2.5 PCB copper loss (often ignored, but not negligible)
Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) copper path, 8 mm wide, 15 mm long, from the battery pad to the bridge, with return:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho\frac{L}{wt} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.015}{0.008\cdot70\times10^{-6}} = 0.46\text{ mΩ (per layer)}$$

With 4 paralleled layers, the effective resistance is about 0.12 mΩ each way, roughly 0.25 mΩ for the loop. At 80 A per channel (if one channel is at burst): 6400 × 0.25e-3 ≈ **1.6 W**. At 4×80 A on the shared bus input (320 A), this becomes about 25 W, and the input pads and battery leads become the hottest point. **Use 12 AWG minimum and 3 oz inner planes.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per PWM edge, so one hard-switched commutation occurs per edge pair per channel.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 25\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery of the complementary body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.85\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (borne by the driver and regulator, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\text{ switching FETs} = 55\text{n}\cdot12\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = \mathbf{63\ mW}$$

For 4 channels, the total is 0.25 W. This load lands on the 12 V gate-drive rail.
