# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #59)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

Three architectural points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a production FC + 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230, either one per channel or one MCU driving two channels. The H743's HRTIM could drive gates directly, but ArduPilot has no commutation layer, so this design does not do that.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is limited by heat capacity, not steady-state cooling.** The model below shows that 80A per channel is only sustainable for about 1 s on 20×20 mm from a 40 °C start. It is roughly 3 s on 30.5×30.5 mm with 3 oz copper. Treat the burst rating as a time-bounded specification and state the duration.
3. **Component parameters are representative, not datasheet-verified.** Device values are typical of 30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN trench MOSFETs. The model is fully parametric: substitute the datasheet values for your selected part and re-run.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Operating mode

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

At any instant, exactly two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) switch conducts for D·T_sw, and the low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Static leg:** the opposite-phase LS switch is on for the full 60° sector.

Therefore there are **always exactly two MOSFET channels in series** with the phase current.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × (1 + 0.0065 × 85) = **1.86 mΩ**.

Including the ×1.55 temperature factor matters. Using the 25 °C datasheet value underestimates conduction loss by 35 %.

### 1.3 RMS current including ripple

Phase inductance L_ph is assumed at 15 µH, a typical value for a 2207-class motor line-to-line.

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is at D ≈ 0.5 with V_bemf ≈ V_bus·D:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \approx 6406\,\text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.1 % at this inductance. It becomes significant with low-inductance motors below 5 µH.

### 1.4 Conduction loss per channel

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6406 \times 1.86\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.8\ W}$$

**Per-MOSFET average:** each of the 6 FETs carries current for one-third of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is around 1–3 kHz, which is far faster than the junction thermal time constant, so cycle-averaging is valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx 23.8 / 6 = 3.97\ \text{W}$$

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace and pad, 2 oz, about 8 mm path | 0.35 mΩ | 2.24 W |
| Bus trace share | 0.15 mΩ | 0.96 W |
| Shunt (per channel, in the low-side return) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **4.8 W** |

The shunt sees the commutated return current, so I² × R is an upper bound.
