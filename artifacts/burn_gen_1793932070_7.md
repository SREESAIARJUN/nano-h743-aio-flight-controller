# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two premises in the brief need correcting before any math is useful.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware. It cannot run sensorless 6-step or FOC commutation for 12 half-bridges. The correct architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 x4──► 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                                     └──► 3x gate driver ──► 6x MOSFET per motor (24 total)
```

All DMA and timer analysis (§4) applies to the H743. All loss analysis (§2–3) applies to the ESC power stage.

**2. "80 A" on a "nano" form factor is a burst rating only.** Twenty-four power MOSFETs plus drivers do not fit on a 20×20 mm board. The realistic minimum is 30.5×30.5 mm with a stacked ESC, or 36×36 mm. §3.3 shows that 80 A continuous on a single board is thermally impossible. The model below quantifies how long a burst can last.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During any commutation sector, two things happen:
- **Phase A** is PWM'd: the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for 1−D.
- **Phase B** low-side is held fully on.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond} = I^2R_{DS}\,D + I^2R_{DS}(1-D) + I^2R_{DS} = 2\,I^2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is an important property of synchronous rectification.

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **2.01 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.01\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.7\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched high side)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Secondary Switching Terms

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Dead-time body diode | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | **0.036 W** |
| Coss charge | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | **0.048 W** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET die) |

### 2.4 Shunt and Copper Loss

- Shunt: I²R_sh = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**.
- Copper: with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective phase path, giving roughly 1.9 W.

### 2.5 Totals

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per motor, semiconductors | 25.7 + 1.94 + 0.61 + 0.08 ≈ **28.4 W** |
| Per motor, including shunt and copper | ≈ **33.5 W** |
| All 4 motors at 80 A | ≈ **134 W** |

Efficiency check: P_in ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.9 that is about 1.8 kW per motor, so stage efficiency is about 98%. The electrical efficiency is fine. The problem is that 134 W must leave a roughly 10 g board.

**Switching frequency sensitivity:**

$$\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx (1.94 + 0.65)/48\text{k} = 54\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs only about 1.3 W per motor. At 80 A, conduction loss dominates by about 10×.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of an electrical cycle, so the average per-FET loss is ≈ 28.4 / 6 ≈ **4.7 W**. The peak instantaneous loss is I²R ≈ 12.9 W while a FET is conducting.

### 3.2 Electrothermal Stability (thermal-runaway criterion)

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\,P(T_j), \qquad P(T_j)=P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_{th}P_0}$$

The loop is stable only if **α·R_th·P_0 < 1**.

With P_0 ≈ 3.6 W per FET at 25 °C, the limit is R_th,ja < 1 / (0.0045 × 3.6) ≈ **62 K/W**. Typical in-board R_th,ja is 25–40 K/W in still air and 10–15 K/W in prop wash, so the design is stable. Note the effective gain of 1/(1 − αR_thP_0) ≈ 1.3–2.5×. This gain is why "spec-sheet amps" are always optimistic.

### 3.3 Steady State: Why 80 A Continuous Fails

Assume a 36×36 mm, 8-layer board. Effective board-to-air resistance in prop wash is R_th,ba ≈ 2.5 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{board} = 134 \times 2.5 = 335\text{ K} \quad \Rightarrow \quad \text{not survivable}$$

Solving for a 75 K board rise (T_a = 35 °C, board at 110 °C, junction margin to 150 °C):

$$P_{max} = 75/2.5 = 30\text{ W total} \Rightarrow I_{cont} \approx 80\sqrt{30/134} \approx \mathbf{38\ A}\text{ per motor (all four loaded)}$$

### 3.4 Transient (Burst) Model: 2-Node Foster Network

```
P_FET ──[Zth_jc: 0.8 K/W, τ=5 ms]──●──[R_cb: 3 K/W/FET]──● Board (C_b) ──[R_ba 2.5 K/W]── Ambient
```

**Board thermal capacitance:**
- FR4 + copper ≈ 7 g, c_p ≈ 0.6–0.9 J/(g·K), giving about 5.5 J/K.
- 24 FETs plus drivers add about 1.0 J/K.
- C_b ≈ **6.5 J/K**, so τ_b = R_ba·C_b ≈ 16 s.

**Board rise during a burst:**

$$\Delta T_b(t) = P R_{ba}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_b}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_b}\quad (t\ll\tau_b)$$

At 134 W this is about **20.6 K/s**.
