# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #12): Thermal, Switching, DShot, and Netlist Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Every number below depends on these assumptions. They are stated so you can substitute real datasheet values.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S, 25.2 V max | 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, all four simultaneously (worst case) | |
| ESC PWM frequency | 48 kHz | Set in the ESC firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32), not ArduPilot |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-channel, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C | Single FET per switch, 24 total. α(125 °C) ≈ 1.5, so R_DS(on) ≈ 1.5 mΩ hot |
| Q_g / Q_rr / C_oss(eff) | 50 nC / 50 nC / 1.5 nF | |
| t_r + t_f | 40 ns total | 20 ns + 20 ns |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge | |
| Body-diode V_f | 0.8 V | |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz copper | |

**Topology.** The H743 is the flight controller MCU. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32). These drive three-phase bridges through gate drivers.

The H743 does not commutate the motors. Pushing six-step or FOC control for four motors onto the FC MCU would be a different design.

**Feasibility flags:**

1. **The 80 A rating is burst-only.** The analysis in §1.4 shows a 30.5 mm board sustains only about 30 A per motor continuously. An 80 A label is a burst rating of a few seconds.
2. **"Nano" form factor.** The H743 in LQFP100 is 14 × 14 mm. Fitting it alongside 24 power FETs on a 20 × 20 mm board is not realistic. Use 30.5 × 30.5 mm, or the TFBGA/UFBGA packages with HDI.

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC, per commutation state)

In six-step commutation, current flows through one high-side FET of phase A and one low-side FET of phase B. The PWM leg switches. With synchronous rectification, its complementary FET carries current during the off-time.

### 1.1 Conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the current path always contains two channel resistances, whatever the duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)]$$

The burst is assumed to be a flat 80 A, so I_rms ≈ 80 A:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Copper and trace path, assuming 0.3 mΩ including the motor pads:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.92\ W}$$

### 1.2 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per PWM period.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 kHz | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 50 nC·25.2·48 kHz | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f = 0.5·1.5 nF·635·48 kHz | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, dissipated in driver and R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f = 2·50 nC·10 V·48 kHz | **0.05 W** |

$$P_{sw} \approx 2.68\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Totals

| | Per ESC | All four ESCs |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all mechanisms) | 2.68 W | 10.7 W |
| PCB copper | 1.92 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.8 W** | **≈ 95 W** |

Electrical efficiency at burst is 1 − 95 / (4·25.2·80) ≈ 98.8 %. Conduction dominates at about 80 % of the loss, so choosing FETs by R_DS(on) is the main lever.

Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs about 1.3 W per ESC. That is acceptable in exchange for smoother current and lower acoustic noise.

### 1.4 Thermal model

**Junction rise (worst-case FET).** The worst FET is the switching high-side device, at roughly 9.6 W conduction plus 2.6 W switching, about 12 W.

- Z_th(j-c) at a 1 s pulse is about 0.8 K/W, giving ΔT_jc ≈ 10 K.
- Local spreading into the copper pour, Z_th(c-pcb) at 1 s, is about 1.5–2 K/W, giving ΔT ≈ 20 K.
- Allowable board temperature is therefore T_pcb,max = 150 − 10 (derating margin) − 10 − 20 ≈ **110 °C**.

**Board heat capacity (lumped).**

| Component | Calculation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | ≈ 4.5 g × 1.1 J/gK | 4.95 J/K |
| Copper (8 layers, 2 oz, about 60 % fill) | ≈ 3.5 g × 0.385 | 1.35 J/K |
| FETs and components | ≈ 2 g × 0.8 | 1.6 J/K |
| **C_th** | | **≈ 7.9 J/K** |

**Adiabatic burst duration.** Starting from a 40 °C ambient or soak:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{95}{7.9} \approx 12\ \text{K/s} \;\Rightarrow\; t_{burst} \approx \frac{110-40}{12} \approx \mathbf{5.8\ s}$$

**Continuous rating with prop-wash convection.** Assume h ≈ 100 W/m²K over A = 2 × 9.3×10⁻⁴ m², giving G ≈ 0.19 W/K. At ΔT = 70 K, the board can dissipate P_cont ≈ 13 W total, or 3.3 W per ESC:

$$2R I^2(1.5\text{m}\Omega) + 0.3\text{m}\Omega\, I^2 + 0.0335 I \approx 3.3 \;\Rightarrow\; I_{cont} \approx \mathbf{28\text{–}30\ A}$$

The switching term scales linearly as ≈ 2.68 W / 80 A × I.

**Burst time-constant model** (for firmware current limiting):

$$T(t) = T_a + P R_{th}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau}), \qquad R_{th} = 1/G = 5.3\ \text{K/W}, \qquad \tau = R_{th}C_{th} \approx 42\ \text{s}$$

**Recommendation:** implement an I²t limit in the ESC firmware, for example the AM32 current limit plus temperature cutback starting at 100 °C. Advertise the rating as **"30 A continuous / 80 A for 5 s"**.
