## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel uses its own small motor-control MCU. The design assumes an AT32F421 running AM32, at a 48 kHz PWM carrier.

ArduPilot cannot commutate motors itself. Running four sensorless motors from the H743 would also be a bad partitioning choice, so the stack is split into two boards.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.2 \times 1.40 = 1.68\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-Leg Loss with Synchronous Rectification

With complementary PWM, the leg current flows through the high-side FET for a fraction D of the period and through the low-side FET for (1−D):

$$P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}\,D + I^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D) = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle.

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, two legs carry the full phase current at any instant:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P per leg | P per motor | P for all 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 7.68 W | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.68 mΩ | 10.75 W | 21.5 W | **86.0 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Loss

Assume 2 oz copper on 6 layers. The phase path is about 8 mm long and paralleled across 4 layers, giving an effective R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path. Add a 0.5 mΩ shunt in the low-side return. Assume shunt current is roughly I²·(1−D), taking D = 0.5 at hover/burst for the average.

$$P_{Cu+shunt} \approx 2(80^2)(0.25\text{ m}\Omega) + 80^2(0.5\text{ m}\Omega)(0.5) = 3.2 + 1.6 = 4.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

This term is significant. **Shunt placement and copper pour are as important as the FET choice.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switching at a time in 6-step. The other active leg is static.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance (Hard-Switched Turn-On)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction (Two Edges per Period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{ ns})(48\text{ k}) = 1.229\ \text{W}$$

**This is the largest switching-related term.** Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. This is only safe if the driver propagation-delay mismatch plus FET t_d(off) leaves margin. Verify the margin on a scope at temperature.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Six FETs per motor, but only the PWM leg toggles at f_sw. Take a worst-case bound of 2 FETs switching:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(45\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

This power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 0.968 + 0.048 + 0.060 + 1.229 + 0.043 \approx \mathbf{2.35\ W}$$

### 2.7 Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Source | Per motor | All 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 21.5 W | 86.0 W |
| Copper + shunt | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.35 W | 9.4 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (see §2.8) | ~0.5 W | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **≈29.2 W** | **≈117 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 4 × 22.2 V × 80 A × ~0.9 (average duty) ≈ 6.4 kW. The 117 W loss is about 1.8 % of output, which is plausible. The problem is not efficiency but **power density on a 36 × 36 mm board**.

### 2.8 Bulk Capacitor Ripple

For a single PWM leg, the capacitor ripple current is:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5\,I = 40\ \text{A (worst case, D = 0.5)}$$

- **Unsynchronised channels.** The four ESC MCUs are not synchronised, so the combined ripple adds roughly in RMS: about 80 A RMS total across the shared bank in the worst case.
