## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Two corrections shape every number in this document.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and contains no BLDC commutation firmware. The only production-viable architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──4× bidirectional DShot600──► 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                                         └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6 MOSFETs per motor
```

The 48 kHz switching frequency is an AM32 setting (`PWM frequency = 48 kHz`). It does not belong to the H743.

**2. "80A burst" in a nano form factor is a time-limited rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model in §3 derives the allowed burst duration. Treat that result as a hard limit in marketing and in the ESC firmware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In six-step commutation, at any instant one phase leg is PWM'd and one leg is held low. The third phase floats for BEMF sensing. With synchronous rectification:

- **PWM leg, high side:** conducts I for fraction D.
- **PWM leg, low side:** conducts I for fraction (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg, low side:** conducts I for 100% of the time.

The per-motor conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result is independent of D. Synchronous rectification makes conduction loss duty-agnostic, apart from the dead-time intervals (§2).

### 1.2 Including board copper and the shunt

$$
P_{cond,total} = I^2\left[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{sh} + R_{Cu,path}\right]
$$

where R_Cu,path ≈ 0.6 mΩ. This covers the bus-to-FET-to-motor-pad loop on 2 oz copper plus via arrays (§5.4).

### 1.4 Electrothermal coupling

R_ds depends on T_j, so the junction temperature must be solved iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\cdot P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(n)})\right)
$$

The fixed point exists if dP/dT_j · Z_th < 1. For this design, at 80 A:

$$
\frac{dP_{FET}}{dT_j} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha = 6400 \times 1\text{m}\Omega \times 0.0055 = 35\ \text{mW/K}
$$

With Z_th(j-board) ≈ 1.5 K/W, the loop gain is about 0.05. The model is stable and converges in 3–4 iterations. Thermal runaway is not a concern at the FET level. It is at the board level, because the board has no steady-state heat path (§3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The switching transitions occur in the PWM leg only.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (hard-switched high side)

The gate driver's effective resistance sets the Miller-plateau time:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{cc} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,int} + R_{g,ext} + R_{drv}} = \frac{12 - 4.5}{1 + 4.7 + 5} \approx 0.70\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{0.70\ \text{A}} \approx 17\ \text{ns}\quad(\text{use } 20\ \text{ns with layout margin})
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$
