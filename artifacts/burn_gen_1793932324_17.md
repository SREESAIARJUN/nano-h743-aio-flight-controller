# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #40)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

I don't have the Job #40 requirements document, so I've stated every assumption below. Change any parameter and re-run the equations; they are written to be reused.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 8S would roughly double switching loss |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, DC-link side, about 5 s or less | |
| ESC topology | 4 independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421, AM32 firmware) | See note below |
| PWM | 48 kHz complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32 PWM-frequency setting |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 0.8 mΩ typ at 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 80 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, Q_oss ≈ 60 nC | 1 FET per switch position, 24 total |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q, about 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink, 100 ns dead time | |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 at 400 MHz (ArduPilot default clock), timer kernel clock 200 MHz | |

**Why separate ESC MCUs:** Bidirectional DShot only exists when a separate ESC processor reports eRPM. If the H743 drove the gate drivers directly, there would be no DShot link at all.

**Form-factor warning:** A true "nano" board (20×20 mm mounting) cannot host 24 power FETs rated for 80 A. Section 2 shows that even a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer board only sustains about 33 A per motor continuously. Treat "80 A" strictly as a burst rating. I recommend a minimum of 30.5×30.5 mm, or a stacked FC + ESC design.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor)

### 1.1 Current definitions (6-step trapezoidal commutation)

At duty D, the phase current relates to the DC-link current by:

I_ph = I_DC / D

At partial throttle the phase current is *higher* than the battery current. Size the FETs at worst case: high load at reduced duty, for example during punch-outs at low battery voltage.

In each 60° commutation sector, one high-side and one low-side path conduct. With complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two channels, either HS+LS or LS+LS during freewheeling. Therefore:

- **Per-FET RMS current:** I_FET,rms = I_ph · √(1/3)
- **At 80 A and D ≈ 1:** I_FET,rms = 80 × 0.577 = 46.2 A

### 1.2 Conduction loss

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

R_DS(on)(T_j) = R_25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005 /K

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R ≈ 1.2 mΩ. The conduction loss is then:

- **Per FET:** P_cond,FET = I_FET,rms² · R = 46.2² × 1.2 mΩ = **2.56 W**
- **Per motor:** P_cond,motor = 2 · I_ph² · R = 2 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = **15.36 W**

Check: 6 FETs × 2.56 W = 15.4 W ✓. This total is independent of D for a given I_ph.

### 1.3 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge is hard-switching at any instant.

**Transition time.** The Miller plateau is about 4.5 V. With R_g,total ≈ 5 Ω:

- I_g,on ≈ (12 − 4.5)/5 = 1.5 A, limited by the driver to about 1 A
- I_g,off ≈ 4.5/5 ≈ 0.9 A
- Q_gd ≈ 15 nC, so t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 17 ns
- **Total: t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns** (working value)

**Loss components:**

| Term | Equation | Value at 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k | **1.45 W** |
| Coss discharge | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 60 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.07 W |
| Body-diode reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 100 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2 × 80 nC × 12 × 48 k | 0.09 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.28 W** |

Switching loss is about 0.0285·I W/A per motor, so it scales linearly with current.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 1.1 W per motor at 80 A. That costs roughly 4.5 W board-wide in exchange for quieter, smoother motors. This is acceptable for a burst rating but costs about 2 A of continuous rating.
