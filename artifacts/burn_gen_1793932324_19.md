## 0. Feasibility Statement

Before going further, one constraint has to be stated plainly.

**The thermal math rules out "nano" (20×20 mm) for 80 A.** It shows that 80 A per channel is only viable as a timed burst of a few seconds. Even that requires the following:

- A **30.5×30.5 mm or larger** ESC board.
- **4–6 layers** with **≥2 oz copper** on the outer layers.
- A clamped heatsink or a high-airflow mount.

On a 20×20 board, 80 A per channel exceeds the available copper cross-section and heat capacity, regardless of the MOSFET chosen. The recommended architecture is therefore:

| Board | Format | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 | STM32H743VIT6 |
| ESC | 30.5×30.5 4-in-1 | Separate MCU per channel (AM32/BLHeli_32 class), 48 kHz PWM |

**Who generates the 48 kHz PWM.** The H743 does **not** generate it. Each ESC MCU does. The H743 sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

```
R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · (1 + α)^(T_j − 25)
R_ds(125 °C) = 0.90 mΩ · (1.0045)^100 = 0.90 · 1.566 = 1.41 mΩ
```

### 2.2 Six-step synchronous (complementary) PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, two phase legs are active:

- **Leg A** is PWM'd. Its high-side (HS) conducts for a fraction D of the period. Its low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B's LS** is held fully on.

Instantaneous conduction power per channel:

```
P_cond,ch = I_rms² · R_ds · [D + (1−D) + 1] = 2 · I_rms² · R_ds
```
