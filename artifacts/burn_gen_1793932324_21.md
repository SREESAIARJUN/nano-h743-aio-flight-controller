## 0. Critical Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

Two findings change the scope of this job. They are stated first because the rest of the document depends on them.

### 0.1 The H743 cannot also be the ESC controller

ArduPilot runs the flight control loop. It does not run sensorless BLDC commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection at 48 kHz. A production-viable architecture therefore needs two controller tiers:

| Function | Device | Firmware |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot |
| ESC ×4 | 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) | AM32 (bidirectional DShot, EDT) |

The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs over on-board traces. "48 kHz switching" is an AM32 PWM setting on the ESC MCUs, not an H743 function.

### 0.2 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a transient-only rating

Section 3 shows that sustained 80 A on all four channels is not thermally feasible on 20×20 mm. The rating must be specified as a time-limited burst. I recommend either:
- a 30.5×30.5 mm layout for anything beyond about a 1–2 s burst, or
- a firmware-enforced I²t limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32 default). At every instant, two phases conduct. During PWM-on, current flows through the high-side switch of phase A and the low-side switch of phase B. During PWM-off, the phase-A low-side synchronous FET carries the freewheel current.

So at any time, **two FET channels carry the full phase current I**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_J)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Motor phase inductance produces a ripple ΔI at f_sw:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5–7" motor, L_ph is about 15 µH. At D = 0.9 and V_bemf ≈ 21 V:

ΔI ≈ (4.2 × 0.9)/(15 µ × 48 k) ≈ 5.3 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 2.3 A².

This is negligible next to 6400 A². Therefore I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on) (self-consistent)

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_J-25)]$$

Junction temperature depends on the loss, and the loss depends on junction temperature. The fixed-point iteration is:

$$T_J = T_{board} + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}(T_J)$$

**At T_J = 125 °C:**
- R_DS = 1.1 × 1.55 = **1.705 mΩ**
- Per FET: P = 80² × 1.705 m = **10.9 W**
- Per ESC channel: **21.8 W**

**Copper path.** Add 0.3 mΩ for the battery-pad-to-FET, FET-to-motor-pad, and shunt trace. This assumes 0.25 mΩ shunt bypassed by the sense path, but 6 oz-equivalent internal copper. It adds 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W** per channel.

**Runaway check.**

$$\frac{dP}{dT} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha = 6400 \times 1.1\text{m} \times 0.0055 = 0.039\ \text{W/K per FET}$$

$$\frac{dP}{dT}\cdot R_{\theta JC} = 0.047 \ll 1$$

The junction-to-board loop is stable. Runaway is governed by the board-to-ambient path (see Section 3).
