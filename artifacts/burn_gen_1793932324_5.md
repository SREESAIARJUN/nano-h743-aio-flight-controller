# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Review

Three findings change the design before any math:

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/ARM running BLHeli). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. This document models them as separate domains.
2. **80 A burst per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows it is time-limited to a few seconds. A 4× simultaneous burst implies about 320 A at the battery input, which a nano board's XT30/XT60 pads and copper cannot carry. The design basis is therefore **80 A on one or two channels for ≤2 s, ~40 A continuous per channel.**
3. **All part numbers are representative candidates.** Verify every value against current datasheets before layout freeze.

### Design Basis

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V full, ~22 V under burst sag |
| Switching frequency f_sw | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM |
| Burst current I_pk | 80 A phase current, ≤2 s |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 40 V, 5×6 PQFN, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ max at 25 °C |
| R_DS(on) at T_j = 110 °C | ≈1.5× → 1.35 mΩ (use 1.45 mΩ with margin) |
| Q_g / Q_gd / Q_rr / Q_oss | 60 nC / 10 nC / 50 nC / 40 nC |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap (FD6288Q class), ~1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| Dead time t_dt | 200 ns |
| Body diode V_f | 0.8 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct. Take the case of PWM on the high side of leg A, with leg B low side held ON:

| Device | Conducts for fraction of sector | Current |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH (active HS) | D | I |
| Q_AL (sync rectifier) | 1 − D | I |
| Q_BL (return LS) | 1 | I |

Conduction energy per sector:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS,hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Ripple Correction

The phase current ripple is:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5″ motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-to-line), at D = 0.9 and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 3 V:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{3 \times 0.9}{15\times10^{-6}\times 48\,000} = 3.75\ \text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.17 \approx 6401\ \text{A}^2
$$

The correction is negligible (<0.02%).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 6401 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Per-FET distribution, time-averaged over an electrical revolution: each FET spends 1/3 of the time as "return" and 1/3 as "active leg".

$$
\bar P_{FET} = \frac{18.56}{6} \approx 3.1\ \text{W}
$$

The hottest FET at any instant is the return low side, at I²R = 9.28 W during its 120° window.

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses (Often Forgotten)

| Path | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz traces (×2 phases) | ~0.4 mΩ | 2.56 W |
| Current shunt (if present, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| DC bus share to this channel | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.92 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the active leg hard-switches; the return leg is static.

### 2.1 Transition Time

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,src}} \approx \frac{15\ \text{nC}}{1.0\ \text{A}} = 15\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
t_{f} \approx 10\ \text{ns}
$$

Use t_r + t_f = 30 ns to account for gate resistor and loop inductance.

### 2.2 V–I Overlap Loss

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw}
= \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\times10^{-9})(48\,000) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is a significant term. Cutting dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel, but only if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it.

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery, Output Capacitance, and Gate Charge

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}\ \text{(dissipated in driver/R}_G\text{)}
$$

### 2.5 Loss Budget Summary (One Channel, 80 A Burst)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 18.56 |
| Switching overlap | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + Q_g | 0.17 |
| **Semiconductor total** | **21.41** |
| Copper + shunt | 6.08 |
| **Channel total** | **≈27.5 W** |

Efficiency is about 1 − 27.5/(22 × 80 × D_eff). At D ≈ 0.9 that is ~98.3% electrically. The absolute heat is the problem, not the efficiency.

### 2.6 Frequency Sensitivity

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} = \left[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + 2V_f I t_{dt} + (Q_{rr}+Q_{oss})V + 2Q_gV_{GS}\right] \approx 61\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.47 W per channel at 80 A. Moving to 96 kHz costs 2.9 W.
