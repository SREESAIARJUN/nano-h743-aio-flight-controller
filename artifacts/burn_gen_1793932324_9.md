## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three problems in the brief must be resolved before any math matters.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an AIO build, the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

2. **80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not continuously achievable.** Section 3 shows roughly 85–90 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. That is only survivable as a sub-second to ~1 s transient. Treat "80 A burst" as a transient-thermal requirement, not a steady-state one.

3. **Packaging pressure.**
   - The H743 in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) plus 12 power FETs, drivers, and bulk capacitance will not fit on a single 20×20 mm board.
   - Realistic options:
     - a stacked FC + ESC pair at 20×20, with the H743 in TFBGA100;
     - a single-board AIO at 25.5×25.5 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, i.e., per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction loss, 6-step synchronous

In any commutation sector, two legs are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D; the complementary low side conducts for (1−D).
- **Other leg:** its low side conducts 100% of the sector.
- **Floating leg:** off.

The instantaneous current path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) in series, regardless of D (ignoring the dead-time intervals):

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = k_T\, R_{ds,25}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Per-FET distribution: each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The worst-case high-side FET RMS current is

$$I_{rms,HS} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}$$

Averaged over the electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs dissipates about P_cond/6 ≈ 3.2 W. During its active sector, however, a FET instantaneously carries 9.6 W (at D = 1) for 1/3 of the period. That sector-level peak is what the die-level transient thermal impedance Z_th sees at low eRPM.

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched PWM leg)

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\,\text{ns} = 40.3\ \mu\text{J}$$

$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} \cdot f_{sw} = 40.3\,\mu\text{J} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Only one leg switches at a time, so this applies once per channel.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.5 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 2 = 40\,\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.6 Gate drive

This loss lands in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\,\text{nC}\cdot 10 \cdot 48\,\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.7 Totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.76 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.44 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 W | 0.44 W |
| Gate | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 21.9 W** | **≈ 87.6 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 88% of the total. The levers, in order of impact:

1. Lower R_ds(on), or parallel FETs.
2. Heavier copper.
3. Lower f_sw. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.2 W per channel, so this matters least.
