## 0. Architectural Corrections and Feasibility Flags

Three points in the brief need correcting before any math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not perform the 48 kHz switching.** On every production AIO (BLHeli_32 / AM32 / Bluejay), each ESC channel runs on its own motor-control MCU (e.g., AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 for Bluejay). The H743 only sends DShot frames and receives eRPM telemetry. 48 kHz is an ESC firmware PWM setting. So the design splits into two domains:
   - **FC domain (H743):** ArduPilot, DShot600 bidirectional, DMA/timer allocation.
   - **ESC domain (4× AT32F421 + 3-phase gate driver + 24 MOSFETs):** commutation, 48 kHz PWM, all power losses.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" form factor is thermally marginal.** On a 20×20 mm board it is not physically credible. On 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 mm it is achievable only as a short burst, roughly 1–3 s, which §2.6 quantifies. This package assumes **30.5×30.5 mm, 6S, 8-layer 2 oz copper** as the minimum viable platform. "Nano" here means a compact AIO, not 20×20.

3. **The H743 itself needs no special thermal treatment.** At 480 MHz it dissipates about 0.4–0.6 W. The problem is the FET array next to it. Place the MCU and IMU on the opposite board edge from the power stage.

## 2. Power Stage Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive, exactly two phases conduct at any instant: one high-side (HS) leg and one low-side (LS) leg, in series with the motor winding. Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 on HS and 1/3 on LS.

With **complementary PWM**, during the PWM-off interval the opposite FET of the switching leg synchronously rectifies. So at every instant the current path contains **exactly two conducting FET channels** (dead-time intervals aside).

Assuming a flat-top phase current (good approximation at high load with low ripple):

**Instantaneous conduction power per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with the temperature-dependent resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

### 2.2 Burst Conduction Loss at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(T_j) | P_cond per motor (2·I²·R) | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| 75 °C | 1.84 mΩ | 23.5 W | 94.0 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 102.9 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 111.4 W |

Worked example at 100 °C: 2 × 80² × 2.006 mΩ = 2 × 6400 × 0.002006 = **25.7 W**.

The temperature dependence creates **positive thermal feedback**, so the operating point is the self-consistent solution of:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JC}$$

Rearranged, this gives a closed form for the single-FET equilibrium:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{board} + k\,(1 - 25\alpha)\,R_{\theta JC}}{1 - k\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}}, \qquad k = d_{FET}\, I^2 R_{25}$$

Here d_FET is the effective conduction duty of a single FET (see §2.5).

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching FET hard-switches. The synchronous-rectifier FET turns on after dead time, with its body diode already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS). Per motor, at any instant, there is one hard-switched device.

**(a) V-I overlap loss**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery** (hard turn-on into a recovering diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) C_oss charge/discharge** (both FETs of the active leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\, C_{oss,er}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 1\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2\,t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.306\ W}$$

> This is the hidden loss term at 48 kHz. Cutting dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.65 W per motor. AM32 exposes this setting, but shoot-through margin must be verified on the bench with the selected gate driver.

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{gs}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal (FETs, per motor at 80 A):**

$$P_{sw,tot} = 0.968 + 0.048 + 0.030 + 1.306 \approx \mathbf{2.35\ W}$
