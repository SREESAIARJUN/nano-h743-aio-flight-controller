# Design Package: STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53)

**Scope:** Loss model (conduction at 80 A burst, switching at 48 kHz), thermal model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA plan, and reference netlists.

> **Read this first.** No MOSFET part number, layer stack, or burst duration was given, so every number below comes from stated assumptions (§0). The equations are general. The numeric results are only as good as the parameters, so replace them with datasheet values before sign-off. My overall conclusion is in §5: **80 A burst on a nano footprint at 48 kHz is feasible only as a short (≈2–3 s) adiabatic burst. It cannot be a sustained rating.**

---

## 0. System Architecture and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

An STM32H743 does not drive MOSFET gates directly in any standard AIO design. Commutation runs on dedicated ESC MCUs. The chain is:

```
[STM32H743 / ArduPilot] --bidir DShot600 x4--> [ESC MCU(s), AM32 or BLHeli_32-class]
                                                   └─> 3-phase gate driver ─> 6 MOSFETs ─> motor (x4)
```

- The H743 handles flight control, DShot generation, and eRPM telemetry decode for the harmonic notch.
- The ESC side uses 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32, plus 4× FD6288Q-class 3-phase drivers and 24 MOSFETs.
