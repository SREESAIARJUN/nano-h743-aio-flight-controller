**Architectural flags.** Leadership needs to see these three before layout.

1. **Who switches at 48 kHz.** The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM. It sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), and those MCUs drive the bridges at 48 kHz. Section 3 covers the H7's DMA load. Sections 1 and 2 cover the ESC MCUs and bridges.
2. **80 A on a "nano" board is thermally capacity-limited, not steady-state.** On a 30×30 mm board, 4×80 A is a burst rating measured in seconds (Section 2.4). Marketing must say "80 A burst (≤ X s)" with X derived below.
3. **Battery-side current.** Simultaneous burst on all four motors at full throttle can reach 4×80 A = 320 A at the battery pads. That figure is the upper bound; real battery current is about D_avg × 320 A (PWM duty scaling). Pad, plane and connector design must still handle at least ~200 A peaks.

## 1. Loss Model (per ESC channel, one motor)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The bridge runs 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant two legs are active:

- the **PWM leg**: its HS and LS switch complementarily at f_sw;
- the **static leg**: its LS is held on.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two channels in series. Body diodes conduct only during dead time.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

Junction-temperature-dependent resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]$$

Instantaneous conduction loss of the motor path (independent of duty D, because sync rectification keeps two FETs in the path):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_b^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per-FET average over one electrical revolution. Each leg is the PWM leg for 1/3 of the period and the static leg for 1/3:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\,I_b^2 R_{DS},\qquad P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I_b^2 R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_b^2 R_{DS}$$

The low-side FET is the worst-case device. At high-thrust burst, D → 0.9, giving P_cond,LS ≈ 0.37·I²R.

**Numbers at I_b = 80 A:**

| T_j | R_DS (typ) | P_cond,motor | Worst LS FET avg | Instantaneous per conducting FET |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.4 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.41 mΩ | 18.0 W | 3.3 W | 9.0 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W | 3.7 W | 9.9 W |
| 125 °C (R_max 1.3 mΩ) | 2.02 mΩ | **25.8 W** | 4.8 W | 12.9 W |

**Electrothermal feedback.** The rising R_DS means losses grow as the board heats. Let θ be the effective thermal resistance to ambient for the loss mechanism. The steady-state junction temperature is

$$T_j = T_a + \theta\,P_0\,\frac{1-25\alpha}{1-\alpha\theta P_0}\;\;(\text{approx.}), \qquad P_0 = 2I_b^2R_{DS,25}$$

Runaway occurs when α·θ·P₀ → 1. This is one more reason 80 A must remain a burst rating.

### 1.3 Switching losses (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Its HS FET switches against the LS body diode or channel.

**Transition times** (Miller-plateau dominated, V_pl ≈ 4.5 V, V_drv = 10–12 V):

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_G}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} \;\to\; \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{10\text{ nC}}{1.2\text{ A}} \approx 8.3\text{ ns}$$

In practice R_G(ext) ≈ 4.7 Ω is chosen for EMI and ringing control. Including that, use t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 12 ns.

**V·I overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_b\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(27\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.31\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovers at HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with I and di/dt. At 80 A, derate the datasheet Q_rr by roughly 1.5–2×, giving ≈0.1 W.

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 1.8\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.055\ W}$$

The ½ factor is omitted because the HS charges the LS C_oss and dissipates its own.

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_b\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
