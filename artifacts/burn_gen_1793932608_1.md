# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6)
## Design Analysis: Thermal and Switching Losses at 80A Burst, Bidirectional DShot600, and Netlists

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and an Early Feasibility Warning

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges directly. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32. Each ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) runs its own 48 kHz commutation PWM.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nom | Use V_bus = 25.2 V for worst case |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor | 4 motors means 320 A total |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz | AM32 setting |
| MOSFET | 40 V, DFN 3.3×3.3 class, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | Representative part. **Replace with the datasheet values of your chosen part.** |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class), ~1 A source/sink | |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm (FC and ESC on one PCB), 8-layer, 2 oz | |
| Ambient | 40 °C | |

**Warning: 80 A per motor is a transient rating only on this form factor.** The math below shows it is sustainable for roughly 1 to 3 seconds, not continuously. A true "nano" (20 × 20 mm) board cuts the burst time by about 2.3×. Marketing a 20×20 board as "80A" would be a thermal-failure liability.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two of the three phases carry the full phase current I.

- **PWM ON (duty D):** current flows through the high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and the low-side FET of phase B.
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** current freewheels through the low-side FET of A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of B.

In both states, exactly **two channels conduct I**. So the bridge-level conduction loss does not depend on duty:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Per-FET RMS current is useful for hotspot analysis:

- **Switched leg, high side:** $I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{D/3}$
- **Switched leg, low side:** $I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{(1-D)/3}$
- **Return leg, low side:** $I_{rms} = I\sqrt{1/3}$, over a 120° conduction window

At full throttle (D → 1), the high-side FET carries almost all of the switched-leg loss.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.45) = 1.45\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

Add PCB copper and solder resistance per current path. For a 2 oz pour, 5 mm wide and 8 mm long, through two FETs: about 0.35 mΩ. This gives:

$$R_{path} = 2(1.45) + 0.35 = 3.25\ \mathrm{m\Omega}$$

### 1.3 Results

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 R_{path} = 80^2 \times 3.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.8\ W}$$

The hottest single FET (high side at D ≈ 1, conducting one third of the electrical period) dissipates:

$$P_{FET,max} = 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} \times \tfrac{1}{3} \approx \mathbf{3.1\ W}$$

This is averaged over commutation. Instantaneous dissipation is 9.3 W during conduction.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching transition (switched leg)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The transition time is set by the Miller and plateau charge:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}}$$

With Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 14 nC and I_gate ≈ 1 A (minus gate resistor drop, about 0.9 A effective), t ≈ 15.5 ns per edge.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 31\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.50\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle, with t_dt = 100 ns and V_F ≈ 0.8 V.

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_F\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (Q_rr ≈ 35 nC at 80 A)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\times10^{-9} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.042\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (Q_oss ≈ 40 nC)

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (Q_g ≈ 45 nC, V_drv = 10 V)

Two FETs switch at f_sw at any given time:

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\times10^{-9} \times 10 \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

Most of this is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### 2.6 Per-ESC loss budget at 80 A

| Loss term | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs + copper) | 20.80 |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.50 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.04 |
| Q_oss | 0.05 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if per-ESC sensing) | 1.60 |
| **Total per ESC** | **≈ 24.6 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | **≈ 98 W** |

At 80 A, conduction dominates by more than 10×. **Increasing f_sw to 48 kHz costs only about 2 W per ESC at this current.** Lowering it to 24 kHz saves about 1 W but raises motor ripple current. Keep 48 kHz.
