## 0. Architecture Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. This is the only partitioning ArduPilot and the ESC firmware ecosystem support.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max / 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current per motor | I_m | 80 A (phase current during conduction) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 (or 3.3×3.3) |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ~0.6 %/K, giving ×1.5 at T_j ≈ 110 °C |
| Rise + fall time (in circuit) | t_r + t_f | 20 ns + 20 ns |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 200 ns per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Board | — | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz inner/outer, 1.6 mm |
| Topology | — | 6 FETs per motor, 24 total, synchronous rectification, trapezoidal (6-step) |

**Reality check on the requirement.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 "nano" board is not physically credible. Even on 30.5×30.5 mm, it is a burst rating of seconds, as Section 3 shows. The stack is assumed to be FC 20×20 or 30.5 and ESC 30.5.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step, synchronous)

In every commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is fully on.

Phase current is I_m throughout, neglecting ripple; ripple is added in 1.3.

### 1.2 Equations

R_DS(on) as a function of junction temperature:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = I_{m}^2 R(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_m^2 R(T_j) = 2\,I_m^2\,R(T_j)$$

Add copper trace/plane resistance R_Cu (about 0.3 mΩ per path, estimated from the 2 oz pour geometry):

$$P_{cond,total} = 2 I_m^2 R(T_j) + I_m^2 R_{Cu,path}$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph-ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: L = 20 µH (phase-to-phase), V − E = 5 V, D = 0.9, f = 48 kHz.
- ΔI ≈ 4.7 A.
- ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.8 A², against I_m² = 6400 A². The correction is negligible at 80 A.

### 1.4 Numbers (T_j = 110 °C, R = 1.5 mΩ)

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 80² × 0.0015 | **19.2 W** |
| Copper path | 80² × 0.0003 | 1.9 W |
| **Per-motor conduction** | | **21.1 W** |

Per-FET loss is not uniform in time:
- A LS FET in its "return" role dissipates 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W instantaneous**.
- Each FET serves a given role about one-third of the electrical period.

At 14 poles and 30 krpm, f_elec = 3.5 kHz, so each sector is about 48 µs. The die thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms, so the average governs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\text{ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg HS FET hard-switches. Its LS partner commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Equations

| Loss component | Equation |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$ |
| Reverse recovery | $P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ |
| Output capacitance | $P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ (Q_oss approximation) |
| Dead-time body diode | $P_{dt} = V_f I_m \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$ |
| Gate drive | $P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ per switching FET (2 per motor), dissipated in the driver and R_g |

### 2.2 Numbers (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A)

| Term | Calculation | Watts |
|---|---|---|
| P_VI | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | 1.94 |
| P_rr | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 |
| P_oss | 0.5 × 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.02 |
| P_dt | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k | 1.23 |
| P_g | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.05 |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **3.30** |

### 2.3 Frequency sensitivity

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency:

$$\frac{dP_{sw}}{df_{sw}} \approx 68.7\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.65 W |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 3.3 W |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 6.6 W |

**Dead-time lever.** Reducing dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Do this only after measuring shoot-through margin with your gate driver and R_g.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total dissipation at 80 A burst (all four motors)

$$P_{tot} = 4\,(21.1 + 3.3) + P_{misc} \approx 97.6 + 2 \approx \mathbf{100\ W}$$

P_misc covers the shunts (0.5 mΩ: 3.2 W per motor if a per-phase/DC shunt carries the full current; the total assumes a shared DC-link shunt), plus the LDO and drivers.
