## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification in 6-step drive, phase current always flows through exactly one high-side and one low-side FET (or its complement), regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

With α ≈ 0.006 /K, the multiplier at 125 °C is about 1.6.

| I (A) | Rds at 25 °C | Rds at 125 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | 2.45 W | 3.92 W |
| 80 | 12.8 W | **20.5 W** |

**Copper path.** Two phase traces plus the shunt are assumed at 0.30 mΩ total:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.92 \text{ W}$$

The shunt alone (0.25 mΩ at 80 A) dissipates 1.6 W. I recommend phase-shunt-less, back-EMF-only sensing, or a single 0.5 mΩ DC-link shunt per ESC.

Per-FET distribution: each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Steady-state per-FET average is therefore ≈ P_cond/6 × 2 ≈ 6.8 W at 80 A. Instantaneous dissipation in the two active FETs is 10.25 W each.

### 1.2 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg switches per commutation sector.

**Hard-switching overlap:**
$$P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.45 \text{ W}}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.048 \text{ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\,\text{nF}\cdot 635\cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.023 \text{ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 100\,\text{ns}\cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.307 \text{ W}$$

**Gate drive (two FETs, from the driver supply):**
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 40\,\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\,\text{k} = 0.038 \text{ W}$$

**Total switching loss:** ≈ **1.87 W** at 80 A, and ≈ 0.80 W at 35 A.

### 1.4 Efficiency Implication of 48 kHz

Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. At 24 kHz, switching loss at 80 A falls to about 0.94 W, saving about 0.93 W per channel. The 48 kHz figure is justified by:
- lower current ripple on low-inductance motors (< 20 µH), and
- quieter operation.

**Ripple estimate.** For L_phase-to-phase = 15 µH and D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L f} = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{15\,\mu\text{H}\cdot48\,\text{k}} \approx 8.75 \text{ A}_{pp}$$

At 24 kHz this would be about 17.5 A pp, raising the RMS and copper losses.

**ESC timer setup.** Center-aligned PWM on a 120 MHz ESC MCU gives:

$$ARR = \frac{120\,\text{MHz}}{2\cdot48\,\text{kHz}} = 1250$$

This is equivalent to about 10.3 bits of duty resolution.
