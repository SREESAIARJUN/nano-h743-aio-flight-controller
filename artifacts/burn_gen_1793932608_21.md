## 0. Architectural Assumptions and an Early Warning

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production-grade 4-in-1 splits the work:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot. Generates 4× DShot600 and decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC control (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 (QFN) | Runs AM32 firmware. Handles commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM and dead time. |
| Gate drive (×4) | 3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q class, ~1.5 A/1.8 A) | High/low-side bootstrap drive. |
| Power stage (×4) | 6× 40 V N-MOSFET, 3.3×3.3 mm clip package | Three half-bridges per motor. |

**Bus voltage:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum. This sets a 40 V MOSFET class with 37% margin. That margin is the minimum acceptable given ringing; a TVS and bulk capacitance are mandatory.

### 0.2 Early warning on "80 A burst" in a nano form factor

Taken together, "80 A burst" and "nano" (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) are physically contradictory unless the burst is very short. Section 3 shows:

- Four channels at 80 A each dissipate about **76 W**.
- The board's heat capacity is only about **2.6 J/K**.
- The temperature therefore climbs at roughly **29 K/s**, which allows about **2 s** before the MOSFET junction limit.
- A battery current of 320 A is also unrealistic for any connector or pigtail that fits this format.

**Recommendation:** Rate the board as **80 A burst for ≤1.5 s per channel (single-channel peak), 30 A continuous per channel**. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm format. The derivations below let you defend whichever rating you publish.

> All MOSFET, driver, and PCB parameters below are **representative assumptions**. Before freezing the BOM, substitute values from the actual datasheets at your Vgs and Tj.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Parameters (assumed 40 V MOSFET, Vgs = 10 V)

| Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ (typ), 1.3 mΩ (max) | Use **max** for design. |
| α | 0.0045 /K | Rds(on) tempco (≈1.4× at 110 °C) |
| R_pcb,phase | 0.25 mΩ | Copper path per conducting leg (see 1.4) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Phase current during burst |
| D | 0–1 | PWM duty |

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C with the max value:

$$R_{ds}(110)=1.3\times(1+0.0045\times85)=1.3\times1.3825=\mathbf{1.80\ m\Omega}$$

### 1.3 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During any 60° sector there is one PWM'd phase (high-side at D, complementary low-side at 1−D) and one return phase (low-side fully on). Current flows continuously through exactly two device channels:

$$P_{cond,ch}=I^2R_{ds}\,[D+(1-D)]+I^2R_{ds}\cdot1=2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is **independent of duty** with synchronous rectification. That is a useful property: low-throttle, high-current conditions (e.g. prop stall) are no cooler.

$$P_{cond,ch}=2\times80^2\times1.80\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{23.0\ W}\ \text{(worst case, max Rds, 110 °C)}$$

Using the typical value (1.38 mΩ hot), the figure is **17.7 W**.

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries the high-side or low-side role for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$\bar P_{FET}=P_{cond,ch}/6\approx3.0\text{–}3.8\ W$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

$$R_{cu}=\frac{\rho_{cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t\,N_{layers}},\qquad \rho_{cu}(100^\circ C)=2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot m$$

Example: ℓ = 12 mm, w = 4 mm, 2 oz (70 µm), with 4 paralleled layers:

$$R_{cu}=\frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.012}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4}=0.236\ m\Omega$$

$$P_{cu,ch}\approx2\times80^2\times0.236\times10^{-3}=\mathbf{3.0\ W}$$

At 80 A, copper loss is ~15% of FET conduction loss and is not negligible. Use 2 oz on all inner power layers, and stitch with via arrays at ≥1 via per 1.5 A.

### 1.5 Ripple correction

For phase inductance L ≈ 10 µH (typical 2207 motor, 1700–1950 KV), 48 kHz PWM, and worst-case D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp}=\frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\times0.25}{10^{-5}\times48000}=13.1\ A$$

$$I_{rms}^2=I^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}=6400+14.3\Rightarrow +0.2\%$$

Ripple is negligible at this current.
