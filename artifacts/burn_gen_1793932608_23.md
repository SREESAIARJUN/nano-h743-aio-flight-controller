## 0. Scope and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled first because they change the math.

**A. Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs dedicated per-motor ESC MCUs. This design uses AM32-class firmware on AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to each ESC over bidirectional DShot600. "80A burst" and "48 kHz" are properties of the ESC power stage, so the loss model is written for that stage.

**B. Form factor.** 80A burst per motor (320A aggregate) is not thermally credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. The numbers in §3 show this. The baseline here is a **30.5×30.5 mm stacked AIO** (FC board over ESC board) with a heatsink option. The 20×20 numbers are included as a feasibility check.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two switches in series:

- **PWM-on interval (D):** high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- **PWM-off interval (1−D):** low-side of phase A (synchronous rectification), then low-side of phase B.

In both intervals two channels conduct. The total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Numbers

| Case | I | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst, hot (T_j = 125 °C) | 80 A | 1.35 mΩ | **17.28 W** | **69.1 W** |
| Burst, cold (T_j = 25 °C) | 80 A | 0.90 mΩ | 11.52 W | 46.1 W |
| Continuous, hot | 55 A | 1.35 mΩ | 8.17 W | 32.7 W |

### 1.3 Distribution across switches

Each switch position is active for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle. The high-side carries current for fraction D of that window, and its complementary low-side carries the remaining (1−D).

Worst-case single-device dissipation at high throttle (D → 1, high-side dominated):

$$P_{HS,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{hot}\,D + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{hot}\ (\text{when serving as the fixed low-side})$$

At 80 A this gives about **5.8 W average in the hottest FET**. The six devices share the 17.3 W unevenly.

At burst electrical frequencies (≥300 Hz), the per-cycle 120° pulsing is filtered by the die and package thermal capacitance (τ_jc ≈ 5–20 ms), so the average is valid for T_j.

### 1.4 Trace and copper losses (often forgotten)

- **Phase pad to motor wire:** 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner, 4 mm wide × 8 mm long ≈ 0.25 mΩ effective. At 80 A that is 1.6 W per phase-pair path, or about 1.6 W per motor.
- **Shunt (if used):** 0.25 mΩ at 80 A = 1.6 W, flowing on the DC link. Use a single bus shunt for the whole board, or omit it and use a hall sensor.
- **Copper total per motor:** about 2 W.
