# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 4-in-1 ESC Reference Design (Job #36)

**Scope:** Thermal and loss model for an 80 A burst per channel, 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Constraints

Two points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production AIO design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four independent ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM, commutation and gate drive all live on the ESC MCUs. This document models them that way. Running six-step commutation for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported and is not recommended.

2. **80 A burst on a "nano" footprint is thermally marginal.** Six 3.3×3.3 mm DFN FETs per channel, 24 in total, fit on a 30×30 mm board but not on a true 20×20 mm board. The model below shows that 80 A is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
With trapezoidal (six-step) commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, as in AM32 and BLHeli_32), the motor current I_ph always flows through exactly **two** conducting channels in series:
- During the PWM on-time: one high-side FET and one low-side FET.
- During the PWM off-time: two low-side FETs, freewheeling through the channel rather than the diode.

Total bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Distribution per FET
Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period (120° of 360°). The bridge loss spreads across 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6}$$

This holds as long as the electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to a few kHz) is well above the inverse of the die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms). The die then sees the average loss.

Low-side FETs carry somewhat more at low duty because they also handle freewheeling. The worst case at D → 0 is roughly 1.5× the average on the low side.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot (1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5) = 6400 \cdot 3.0\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 3.2\ \text{W (avg)},\quad P_{cond,FET,peak\ instantaneous} = 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 9.6\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated pair switches at f_sw. The commutation-step switching runs at the electrical frequency and is negligible by comparison.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (high-side, inductive load)
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 switching FETs)
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$
This loss lands in the gate driver and LDO, not the FET.

### 2.6 Duty-cycle note
At 80 A the throttle is usually near 100 %. As D → 1, the number of PWM edges falls, because most firmware skips pulses above about 98 %. P_sw then trends toward zero. The value above is the conservative mid-duty worst case.

### 2.7 Per-channel and total loss

| Loss term | Per ESC channel |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W |
| Q_rr | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | 0.02 W |
| **Total P_ch** | **≈ 20.6 W** |
| **4 channels at 80 A** | **≈ 82 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D ≈ 0.95, P_out ≈ 1.9 kW per channel, so η ≈ 98.9 %. That is plausible. The problem is heat density, not efficiency.

Two other loss sources must be added:
- **PCB copper:** a 2 oz, 4-layer phase/bus path of about 0.3 mΩ adds 80² × 0.3 m ≈ 1.9 W per channel.
- **Current shunt:** see §5.
