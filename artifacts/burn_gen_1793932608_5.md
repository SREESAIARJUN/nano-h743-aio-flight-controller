**Architecture.** The H743 does not drive gates directly. ArduPilot sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU drives its own 3-phase driver and six MOSFETs. This is the only architecture ArduPilot supports for this use case.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.5\,(1+0.6) = 2.4\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction

In 6-step drive, current always flows through two series devices:
- **On-time:** high side + low side.
- **Off-time:** low side + synchronous low side.

The 48 kHz duty cycle therefore does not change the conduction-path count.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot} = 2(80)^2(0.0024) = 30.7\ \text{W per ESC}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle (1/3 duty):

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0024}{3} = 5.12\ \text{W (average)}$$

> Note that the peak instantaneous FET dissipation is 15.4 W during its conduction window. At low eRPM the electrical period exceeds the junction thermal time constant (roughly 1–10 ms). In that case use the peak value, not the average. This matters for stall or low-speed punch-outs.

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction

Sheet resistance of 2 oz (70 µm) copper at 25 °C:

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\times10^{-6}} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

Assume a phase trace that is 3 mm wide × 8 mm long (≈2.7 squares), paralleled on 2 layers:

$$R \approx 0.33\ \text{m}\Omega \;\Rightarrow\; P = 80^2 \times 0.33\text{m} \approx 2.1\ \text{W per phase path}$$

Use roughly ×1.4 for hot copper (+0.39%/K). Budget about 4 W per ESC for copper, solder, and the motor pad. This loss is not negligible on a nano board.

### 1.4 System-level warning

Four motors at 80 A and ~100% duty draw **≈320 A from the battery**. That implies:
- **Input path:** an XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and 12 AWG leads are inadequate. Even XT90 is marginal for a burst.
- **Bus caps:** the bulk capacitor ripple current becomes the next limit.
- **Spec reading:** verify whether "80 A burst" means per motor or total.

The rest of this document assumes per motor, which is the worst case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS) after dead-time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched high side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f ≈ 35 ns, set by the gate driver and Miller plateau:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(35\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = 1.69\ \text{W}$$

Rise/fall time from gate charge:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drive}},\quad Q_{gd}\approx 12\text{ nC},\ I\approx 0.8\text{ A (effective, at plateau)} \Rightarrow \sim15\ \text{ns}$$
