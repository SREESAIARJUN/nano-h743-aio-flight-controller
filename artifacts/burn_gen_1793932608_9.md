**Job #0 · Rev A (pre-layout analytical baseline)**

## 0. Scope, Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Architecture.** In a 4-in-1 stack the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU does commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 never switches the MOSFETs directly. The loss model below applies to the ESC power stage, and the DMA model applies to the H743.

**Feasibility flag.** Four simultaneous 80 A bursts on a nano (20×20 or 30×30 mm) board are thermally limited to seconds, not tens of seconds. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat "80 A" as a per-channel, short-duration rating.

No specific MOSFETs or gate drivers were specified, so representative parameters are used. **Replace every value in this table with datasheet values before sign-off.**

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × 1.57 = **1.57 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Conducting devices per commutation state

With synchronous rectification, exactly two MOSFETs conduct at all times in each 60° sector:

- **PWM-on:** HS(A) + LS(B)
- **PWM-off:** LS(A) + LS(B)

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.57\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper and solder path

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-device distribution

Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,ESC}/6 \approx 3.35\ \text{W}$$

### 1.5 Electro-thermal fixed point

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges quickly when the stability condition holds:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,Z_{th} < 1, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha$$

At 80 A, ∂P/∂T = 6400 × 2 × 1 mΩ × 0.0067 = **0.086 W/K**.

- With Z_th of about 7 K/W (board to air), the loop gain is about 0.6. The solution is stable, but the temperature-dependent term adds substantial heating.
- Above about 105 A the loop gain exceeds 1 and **thermal runaway** results.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side device hard-switches. The low side commutates softly via its body diode.

### 2.1 Gate-current-limited transition times

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Source current | I_src = (V_drv − V_pl)/R_g = 6.5/3 | 2.17 A |
| Sink current | I_snk = V_pl/R_g = 3.5/3 | 1.17 A |
| Rise time | t_r = Q_sw/I_src | 5.5 ns |
| Fall time | t_f = Q_sw/I_snk | 10.3 ns |

### 2.2 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 15.8\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.76\ \text{W}$$

Apply a parasitic-inductance and ringing factor k_par ≈ 2, which is typical for a nano layout:

$$P_{VI} \approx \mathbf{1.5\ W}$$
