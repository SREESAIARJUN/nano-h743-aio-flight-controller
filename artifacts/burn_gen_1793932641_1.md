# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

A practical clarification first. A 4-in-1 ESC does not commutate motors from the H743. Each motor channel is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to those ESC MCUs. The model below follows that partitioning.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths within one commutation sector (60° electrical)

Two phases conduct in each sector, for example A+ and B−. Three devices carry current:

- **Q_AH** (high side, PWM): conducts for duty D.
- **Q_AL** (low side, synchronous rectifier): conducts for 1 − D, excluding dead time.
- **Q_BL** (low side, static on): conducts for 100 % of the sector.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

With phase current I and ripple ΔI_pp, the RMS current is:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

The temperature-corrected resistance is:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{ds,25}
$$

Summing the three conducting devices:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

**Key result.** With synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of duty cycle. The worst case is therefore set by current, not throttle.
