## 0. Architecture Decision (Read First)

Two points shape everything downstream.

1. **The H743 should not drive the gates.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and does not commutate motors. The standard and correct partition is:
   - **H743**: ArduPilot, with bidirectional DShot600 output on 4 timer channels.
   - **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071), running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These handle commutation, 48 kHz PWM, and eRPM telemetry.
   - **Gate drivers**: 4× 3-phase drivers (e.g., FD6288Q class).
   - **Power stage**: 24 N-MOSFETs (4 × 6).
2. **80 A on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating.** As the math below shows, this is thermally achievable only for seconds and only with airflow. On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per motor is not credible. Treat 80 A burst as a 30×30 mm-class claim at minimum.

**Interpretation used here:** 80 A per motor channel, 6S LiPo (V_bus = 25.2 V max), f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) trapezoidal commutation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

In trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary PWM at duty D, each 120° sector has:
- one leg's high side (HS) PWM'd, with its low side (LS) as the synchronous complement;
- another leg's LS held fully on.

Per leg, over one electrical period, the conduction fractions are:

| FET | Conduction fraction of period |
|---|---|
| HS | δ_HS = D/3 |
| LS | δ_LS = 1/3 + (1−D)/3 = (2−D)/3 |

### 1.2 Per-FET and Per-Channel Loss

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\,\frac{D}{3},\qquad P_{cond,LS} = I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\,\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Summing over 3 legs gives the per-channel loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3\left[I^2R\frac{D}{3} + I^2R\frac{2-D}{3}\right] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, because two FETs are always in the current path under synchronous rectification.

- **Worst single FET:** the LS at low duty, where δ_LS → 2/3.
- **At full throttle (D ≈ 1):** losses are balanced at 1/3 each.

### 1.3 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.4 Copper and Shunt Losses

These are often ignored, but they matter at 80 A:

$$P_{Cu} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{sheet}\,\frac{L}{W}\cdot n_{paths},\qquad R_{sheet}(2\text{oz}) \approx 0.245\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

Example: 4 squares of 2 oz copper at 80 A gives 6400 × 0.98 mΩ ≈ **6.3 W**. You need parallel inner layers (e.g., 6-layer, 2 oz outer plus 2 oz inner pours) to bring this under about 1.5 W per channel.

Current shunt: with 0.25 mΩ (single shared shunt per ESC), 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**. Many AIOs omit per-channel shunts; consider a single bus shunt.

### 1.5 Numerical Example

Assumed FET: 40 V, R_25 = 1.2 mΩ, α = 0.0045/K.

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per channel (80 A) | Hottest FET (LS, D→0) | FET at D = 1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 5.1 W | 2.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 20.6 W | 6.9 W | 3.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 7.4 W | 3.7 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd HS (and its complementary LS) switch. One leg is active per sector, so per channel the switching events equal one leg's worth at f_sw.

### 2.1 Loss Components

| Term | Expression | Assumed values | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 25.2 V, 80 A, t_r+t_f = 40 ns | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | V_f = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | Q_rr = 50 nC | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | C_oss = 1.5 nF | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | Q_g = 60 nC, 10 V | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching-related** | | | **≈ 2.7 W per channel** |

The overlap times come from the gate drive:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

Design levers:
- **Overlap loss:** set by R_g. Lower R_g reduces overlap loss but increases dv/dt, ringing, and EMI.
- **Dead time:** at 48 kHz, 100 ns of dead time costs about 0.6 W. Use the minimum safe dead time; AM32 lets you tune it per hardware target.
- **48 kHz vs 24 kHz:** switching loss scales linearly, so 48 kHz roughly doubles it. You gain smoother low-throttle control and quieter motors.

## 3. Total Burst Power Budget

| Item | Per channel | ×4 (all motors at 80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j ≈ 110 °C) | 21.2 W | 84.8 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Copper (good 6-layer) | 1.5 W | 6.0 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27 W** | **≈ 108 W** |

**Input capacitor ripple sanity check.** For a single channel:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

At D = 0.5 this is **40 A rms per channel**. Use a low-ESR bulk electrolytic of at least 470 µF/35 V at the pads, plus 4–8 × 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel placed at the half-bridges.
