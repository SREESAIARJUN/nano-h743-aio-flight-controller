# Job #60: STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC

**Engineering Model: 80A Burst Thermal, 48kHz Switching Losses, and ArduPilot BDShot600 DMA Allocation**

---

## 0. Executive Summary

**The 80A burst requirement fails on a nano form factor.**

| Requirement | Physics-limited result (20×20mm, 6S, 1.0mΩ FETs) |
|---|---|
| 80A per channel, sustained | **Not achievable.** About 84W of dissipation on about 3 J/K of thermal mass. |
| 80A per channel, burst | **≈1.0–1.5s** before T_j reaches 150°C, starting from 50°C board temperature. |
| Continuous rating (60K rise, prop-wash cooling) | **≈22–25A per channel.** This matches real-world 20×20 ESC ratings. |
| 80A, 10s burst | Needs **≥30×30mm, 3oz copper, and a clamped heatsink**, or a 4×4 split ESC. |

The models below show this quantitatively. Section 6 gives a redesign envelope that closes.

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. The ESC section uses four dedicated low-cost MCUs (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32), one per motor. The H743 drives them via bidirectional DShot600. This is the only practical topology: AM32 supports 48kHz PWM and BDShot eRPM telemetry natively.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:

- **During PWM on-time:** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **During off-time:** LS(phase A) synchronously rectifying → motor → LS(phase B).

So instantaneous conduction loss per channel is independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical period. Averaged across the six FETs of one channel:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

Hot-spot FETs (the low-side devices at high duty) peak higher instantaneously. Use the channel total for board-level thermal analysis.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25°C | 1.0mΩ | 2·6400·0.001 = **12.8W** | 51.2W |
| 125°C | 1.5mΩ | **19.2W** | **76.8W** |

### 2.4 Additional I²R paths (non-negligible at 80A)

| Path | Resistance | Loss per channel @ 80A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (if used) | 0.25mΩ | 1.6W |
| PCB copper, 2oz, ~10mm × 3mm × 2 layers | ≈0.4mΩ | 2.6W |
| Solder pads and motor wire joints | ≈0.2mΩ | 1.3W |

**Recommendation:** omit per-channel shunts. Use a single DC-link shunt or a Hall-effect sensor on the battery lead, and sense phase current through Rds(on).

**Total conduction loss per channel (hot, no shunt): ≈23W.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48kHz

### 3.1 Switching transition time from gate drive

$$
I_{g} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{10 + 1} = 0.59\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_{r} \approx t_{f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{8\,\text{nC}}{0.59\,\text{A}} \approx 13.5\ \text{ns}
$$

Turn-off is typically slower: the plateau-to-0V drive is weaker. Use t_f ≈ 18ns, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 31ns.

### 3.2 Loss components

Only one half-bridge per channel is PWM-switched at any time in 6-step commutation.

**(a) Hard-switching V–I overlap loss**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 31\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.50\ \text{W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance loss**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Body diode reverse recovery**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$
