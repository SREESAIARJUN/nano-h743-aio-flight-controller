## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

With synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly one FET in each of the two active legs. In the PWM leg, the high-side and low-side FETs alternate, split by duty D. The total channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

P_cond,ch = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)

At I_ph = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.74×10⁻³ = **22.3 W per motor channel**

### 1.3 Dead-time diode conduction

During each dead-time window, current flows through the body diode instead of the channel. This happens twice per PWM period on the switching leg.

P_dt = V_f · I · 2 · t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 2 × 300 ns × 48 kHz = **1.84 W**

Effect of dead time on the loss terms:

- Each 100 ns of dead time removed saves about 0.6 W.
- Going below about 150 ns risks shoot-through with this gate drive.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal feedback (runaway criterion)

Conduction loss grows with temperature, and temperature grows with loss. Define:

- R_eff = total junction-to-ambient thermal resistance seen by the conduction loss
- P₀ = conduction loss at the 25 °C reference

The steady-state junction rise then converges to:

ΔT = (P₀ · R_eff) / (1 − 2I² · R_DS,25 · α · R_eff)

The system runs away when the denominator reaches zero, i.e. when 2I² · R_DS,25 · α · R_eff ≥ 1.

At 80 A:

2 × 6400 × 1.2×10⁻³ × 0.0045 = 0.069 W/K

**Thermal runaway occurs if R_eff ≥ 14.5 K/W per channel.** The board in §3 is below this, but only with airflow, so propwash cooling is a design requirement.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The three components are:

1. **Hard-switching V·I overlap:**
   P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 35 ns × 48 kHz = **1.69 W**
2. **Reverse recovery:**
   P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.060 W**
3. **Output capacitance:**
   P_oss = ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw = **0.036 W**

**Gate drive loss** is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = **0.058 W per channel**

**Switching loss: P_sw,total ≈ 1.79 W per channel.**

### Frequency trade-off

P_sw scales linearly with f_sw (about 37 mW/kHz here). Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz would save about 0.9 W per channel, at the cost of:

- higher current ripple, and
- audible noise and motor iron loss.

### Loss budget at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.3 W | 89.2 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 W | 7.4 W |
| Switching (V·I, Q_rr, Q_oss) | 1.79 W | 7.2 W |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, I²R) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |

**Hottest single FET:** the low-side FET of the PWM leg carries (1 − D) · I² · R_DS plus diode loss. At low throttle it carries nearly the full I² · R_DS:

- Loss: about 11 W
- Junction rise above the board: T_j − T_pcb ≈ 11 W × 1.0 K/W = 11 K

## 3. Transient Thermal Model (Burst Capability)

### 3.1 Model structure

The model has two nodes:

- **Junction node:** junction-to-PCB rise follows the transient impedance Z_th,jc(t), with time constant τ ≈ 50 ms.
- **PCB node:** a lumped heat capacity that absorbs the full loss and drains to ambient.

Board equation:

C_b · dT_b/dt = P_tot − (T_b − T_a) / R_ba

Solution:

T_b(t) = T_a + P_tot · R_ba · (1 − e^(−t/τ_b)), with τ_b = R_ba · C_b
