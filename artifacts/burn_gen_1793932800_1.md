**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 does not drive the gates. It runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), and those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. Keep the H743 on a separate stacked board, or thermally isolated on an AIO. Section 2 shows the ESC copper reaching more than 100 °C during bursts.

**Form-factor flag.** 80 A × 4 on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is not thermally credible. The model below uses 30×30 mm and shows it is already burst-limited to a few seconds.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, two switch positions always carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for fraction D, and its complementary low side for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low side is fully on.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

The result is **independent of duty cycle**, which is a useful simplification.

The temperature dependence of R_DS(on) is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}\ ⚠$$

At an initial guess of T_j ≈ 90 °C, R_DS = 0.8 × 1.39 ≈ **1.12 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.12\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.3\ W}$$

### 1.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is a major lever. Cutting dead time to 100 ns halves it, but must be verified against shoot-through using the driver's propagation-delay mismatch.

### 1.3 Hard-switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the PWM'd high side hard-switches. The low side switches at near-zero voltage after diode commutation.

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{15\text{ nC}}{0.75\text{ A}} = 20\text{ ns}\ ⚠$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
