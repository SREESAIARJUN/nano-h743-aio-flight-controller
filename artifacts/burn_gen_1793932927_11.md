# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Loss, Thermal, and DShot Model

I don't have Job #40's spec sheet, so every assumption below is explicit and parameterized. Replace the bracketed values with your selected MOSFET and stackup data and the equations still hold. Three of the results invalidate a naive "80A × 4 on a nano board" spec, so they come first.

---

## 0. Architectural Findings (Read First)

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot on the H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The netlist below reflects this split.
2. **80A is only valid as a short, mostly per-channel burst.** Four channels at 80A simultaneously dissipate about 90 W in roughly 4 cm² of board. The thermal budget for that case is about 2–3 s from a 25 °C start (§3).
3. **The battery path is the real limiter.** 4 × 80A = 320A, and an XT60 is rated about 60A continuous. In practice, simultaneous 80A on all motors does not occur. Specify the rating as **"80A burst per channel, ≤ 2 s, non-simultaneous; 45–55A sustained-burst total-dependent."**

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

Because two FETs are always in the current path:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j ≈ 125 °C: R_DS = 0.90 × 1.5 = 1.35 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

**Copper path.** Assume about 1.2 squares effective per leg, 4 layers paralleled, plus the phase pad:

$$R_{Cu} \approx 0.30\ \text{m}\Omega \Rightarrow P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. Solve self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

This loop is stable only if R_θ · 2I² · R₂₅ · α < 1. With α ≈ 0.005/K the loop gain is G = 0.0576 · α · R_θ, which gives the steady-state limit R_θ < 3.5 K/W per channel. A nano board is around 12 K/W (§3), so **steady-state 80A diverges**. 80A is mathematically burst-only.

### 2.2 Switching losses (only the PWM leg switches)

**V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (sync FET body diode during dead time):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**C_oss charge/discharge** (two FETs per leg):
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss,er}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Dead-time body diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \,(2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Switching subtotal ≈ 2.7 W.**

**Design lever.** Dead time is about 23% of switching loss. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation skew first.
