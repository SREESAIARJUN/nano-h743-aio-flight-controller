# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Format): 80 A Burst Electrothermal Model, 48 kHz Switching, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

I don't have Job #39's BOM, stackup, or layout files. Everything below is a parametric model with representative values, each flagged with ⚠. Substitute your datasheet numbers before sign-off.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight-controller MCU. It does not commutate motors. Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. Any model that has the H743 driving gates directly is wrong for this class of product.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

R(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ**

**Six-step BLDC with synchronous rectification.** At any instant, two phase legs carry I:

- One leg is PWM'd. Its high-side FET conducts for duty D and its low-side FET for (1 − D). Because of synchronous rectification, the current always flows through one FET of that leg.
- The other leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

**Total conduction loss per motor (independent of D):**

P_cond = 2 · I²_rms · R(T_j), where I²_rms = I² + ΔI²_pp / 12

The ripple term is ΔI_pp = (V_bus · D(1 − D)) / (L_ph · f_sw). For typical 2–10 µH phase inductance at 48 kHz this adds less than 2%, so it is neglected here.

P_cond = 2 · 80² · 1.71×10⁻³ = **21.9 W per motor**

**Copper loss.** Add the bus and phase-trace copper, P_Cu = I² · R_trace. With roughly 0.15 mΩ effective path resistance ⚠, P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.15×10⁻³ ≈ **1.0 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Per edge, the gate is in the Miller plateau for t ≈ Q_gd / I_g = 10 nC / 0.7 A ≈ 14 ns.

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 28 ns · 48 kHz = **1.36 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f_sw | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 2.1 W per motor** |

**Tradeoff note.** At 24 kHz (AM32 default), switching loss halves to about 1.05 W. 48 kHz buys quieter, smoother low-throttle operation at a cost of about 1 W per motor at burst. Dead time is the second-largest term. Trimming it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, but only if your driver and FETs tolerate it without shoot-through.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped board capacitance (20×20 mm)

- **Copper:** 4×10⁻⁴ m² × 70 µm × 6 layers × 70% fill ≈ 1.18×10⁻⁷ m³. That is 1.06 g, giving C ≈ 0.41 J/K.
- **FR4:** 4×10⁻⁴ m² × 1.6 mm ≈ 1.18 g, giving C ≈ 1.3 J/K.
- **FETs, caps, solder:** C ≈ 0.3 J/K ⚠
- **Total:** C_board ≈ **2.0 J/K**

### 4.2 Board-to-air resistance

With prop-wash forced convection at h ≈ 50 W/m²K over about 8×10⁻⁴ m² of exposed area:

R_θ,ba ≈ 1 / (50 × 8×10⁻⁴) ≈ **25 K/W**, giving τ = R·C ≈ **50 s**
