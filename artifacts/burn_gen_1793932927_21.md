### ⚠ Feasibility flag (read before committing to layout)

A true "nano" (20 × 20 mm) board cannot carry 4 × 80 A. Section 4 shows the dissipation is about **105 W at a 4-motor burst**, which a 20 × 20 board can absorb for less than about 1 s. The recommended architecture is:

- **FC:** STM32H743, 20 × 20 mm (nano)
- **ESC:** 30 × 30 mm 4-in-1, stacked

Alternatively, re-rate a 20 × 20 ESC to about 35 A continuous / 50 A burst.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

(α is fitted to the typical normalized R_DS(on) curve, which reads about 1.55× at 125 °C.)

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant the motor current $I_m$ flows through **two** device channels:

1. The PWM'd phase leg: either the HS FET (during *D·T*) or the LS sync-rectifier (during *(1−D)·T*).
2. The returning phase: the LS FET, held on for the full 60° sector.

Dead time is handled separately in §3. Instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore duty-independent:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Per-FET average loss, with each FET active for 1/3 of the electrical period (120°):

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I_m^2 R \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,\big[(1-D) + 1\big]\,I_m^2 R \;\;\text{(worst case, return + SR roles)}$$

Average over the six FETs: $\bar P_{FET} = P_{cond,ch}/6$.

Note that the LS FETs run hotter at low duty (high current at low throttle, e.g. punch-out from hover). The worst-case LS FET is evaluated below.

### 2.3 Self-consistent junction temperature (iteration)

Use $I_m = 80$ A. The worst-case LS FET carries, at D ≈ 0.5, $P_{LS} = 0.5\,I^2R$.
