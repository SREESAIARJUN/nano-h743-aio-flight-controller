## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Red Flags

### 0.1 Architecture

In every production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does **not** drive the MOSFETs. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, and a 3-phase gate driver such as the FD6288Q drives the bridge. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over one bidirectional DShot600 wire per motor. This document models that architecture.

### 0.3 Red Flags

These conclusions follow from the math in Sections 1–3.

1. **80 A on a 20×20 mm board is not physically sustainable.** Section 3 shows that with all four channels at 80 A, the board hits its thermal limit in about 1.5 s. Commercial 20×20 boards are typically rated 20–45 A continuous. Either redefine "burst" as ≤1 s and single-channel, move to 30×30 mm, or parallel the FETs (Section 1.4).
2. **4 × 80 A = 320 A battery current.** The XT60/XT90 connector, pack C-rating, battery leads and input copper must all be sized for this. That alone is incompatible with a nano stack.
3. **All silicon values are placeholders.** The model is parametric, so re-run it with the actual datasheet values.
