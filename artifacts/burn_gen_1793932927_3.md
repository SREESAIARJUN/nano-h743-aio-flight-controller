# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #1)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Two premises in the brief would cause design errors if left unaddressed.

1. **The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to dedicated ESC MCUs. ArduPilot has no 6-step or FOC commutation path for direct gate drive. The correct topology is:
   - H743 (ArduPilot) sends DShot600 bidirectional signals to 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32).
   - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver, which drives 6 MOSFETs.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano/20×20 board is thermally marginal.** The model below shows that 80 A on all four channels at once is survivable only for a few seconds. Treat 80 A as a per-channel, short-transient rating, not a continuous one.

All device parameters below are representative values for the 40 V, sub-1 mΩ, PQFN 3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. **Substitute values from your chosen datasheet before sign-off.**

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In trapezoidal (6-step) synchronous commutation, current flows through one high-side and one low-side path at a time.

- The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS FET (duty D) and its LS FET acting as a synchronous rectifier (1 − D).
- The return leg's LS FET conducts continuously.

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\cdot k_T = 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.28\,\text{m}\Omega$$

**Channel conduction loss** (independent of D when sync rectification is used, neglecting dead time):

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2I^2R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.28\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Hottest single FET:** the return-leg LS FET, which conducts 100% of the time.

$$P_{FET,max} = I^2R_{hot} = \mathbf{8.2\ W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | 1.94 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charging | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·60 nC·10·48 k | 0.06 W (dissipated in the driver/R_g) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.2 W, which saves roughly 1.2 W per channel. However, the lower frequency increases current ripple in low-inductance motors:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. At full throttle, the loss fraction is about 1.1% per channel. Efficiency is good, but absolute heat flux is the problem.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Junction rise above case (steady, per FET)

$$\Delta T_{jc} = P_{FET,max}\cdot R_{\theta JC} = 8.2 \times 1.0 = 8.2\ \text{K}$$

### 5.2 Board-level transient (lumped RC)

Model the board as a single lumped mass:

- Board plus components, about 8 g, effective c_p ≈ 0.9 J/(g·K), so **C_th ≈ 7.2 J/K**.
- Convective R_θ,board-ambient ≈ 6 K/W under prop wash, giving **τ = RC ≈ 43 s**.

$$T(t) = T_a + P R\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The steady-state asymptote is P·R = 88 × 6 = **528 K**, which is physically impossible. **Continuous 4 × 80 A is not viable.**

### 5.3 Allowable burst duration

Conditions: T_a = 40 °C, board limit 100 °C (leaving margin for T_j ≈ 110 °C after the ΔT_jc and spreading rises).

$$t_{burst} = -\tau\ln\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{PR}\right) = -43\ln\left(1 - \frac{60}{528}\right) \approx \mathbf{5.2\ s}$$

The initial heating slope is P/C ≈ **12 K/s**.

| Scenario | Board P | Max duration (40 → 100 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A | 88 W | ~5 s |
| 4 × 60 A | ~50 W | ~10 s |
| 1 × 80 A, 3 × 20 A | ~25 W | ~20 s |
| Continuous limit (ΔT = 60 K) | 10 W | Continuous → ≈ 4 × 33 A |

### 5.4 Layout requirements implied by the model

- Use 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper. Stitch FET drain pads with at least 20 vias of 0.3 mm diameter per FET to the inner planes.
- Hold the R_cu budget of 0.5 mΩ. With 2 oz copper (sheet resistance ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□), this means at most 2 squares per path across parallel layers.
- Bulk capacitance: estimate ripple current as I_rms ≈ 0.5 × I_phase ≈ 40 A per channel. Use low-ESR polymer capacitors (at least 4 × 10 µF ceramic per leg) plus an external 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR capacitor at the pads.
- Place an NTC on the FET copper, read by ESC firmware with derating starting at 90 °C. Read board temperature on the H743 via ADC and report it to ArduPilot.

## 6. ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 Timing and DMA

### 6.1 Timing math

The H743 runs at 480 MHz. APB2 timers (TIM1, TIM8) are clocked at 240 MHz.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| DShot600 bit period | 1/600 kHz = 1.667 µs = 400 ticks |
| Logic "1" high time | 75% = 300 ticks |
| Logic "0" high time | 37.5% = 150 ticks |
| Command frame (16 bits) | 26.7 µs |
| ESC turnaround | ~30 µs |
| eRPM telemetry (21-bit GCR at 750 kbit/s) | 28 µs |
| **Total transaction** | **≈ 85 µs** |

The total fits inside a 125 µs (8 kHz) budget. ArduPilot's practical rate is the main loop rate, typically 400 Hz to 1 kHz, so there is ample margin.

### 6.2 Timer and pin allocation

Put all four motors on one advanced timer so a single TIM1_UP burst DMA updates all CCRs atomically.

| Motor | Pin | Timer/channel | DMA request (via DMAMUX) |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | PE9 | TIM1_CH1 | TIM1_CH1 (input capture) |
| M2 | PE11 | TIM1_CH2 | TIM1_CH2 |
| M3 | PE13 | TIM1_CH3 | TIM1_CH3 |
| M4 | PE14 | TIM1_CH4 | TIM1_CH4 |
| Output burst | — | — | TIM1_UP |

This needs 5 DMA streams. On the H743, DMAMUX1 maps any request to any of the 16 streams on DMA1/DMA2, so the remaining 11 cover the other peripherals. BDMA handles the D3 domain (SPI6, LPUART1).
