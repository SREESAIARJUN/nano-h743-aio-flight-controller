## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

I don't have the Job #48 requirements document, so this package rests on the assumptions below. Please correct any that are wrong, because the numbers depend on them.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V full / 22.2 V nominal | Drives the 40 V FET class |
| Burst current | 80 A **phase RMS per motor**, 5 s | "Burst" duration is not specified |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous), trapezoidal 6-step | This is AM32 or BLHeli_32 style |
| Architecture | H743 FC drives **four separate ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32) over DShot | See flag 1 |
| Form factor | 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack | See flag 2 |
| Ambient | 40 °C, prop-wash cooled | |
| Board T limit | 110 °C (FR4 / solder / connector derating) | T<sub>j</sub> limit is 150 °C |

> ⚠️ **Flag 1: Architecture.** The H743 should **not** commutate the FETs directly. ArduPilot does not contain ESC commutation firmware. Bi-directional DShot also implies a separate ESC MCU that returns eRPM. This document models the H743 as the flight controller and a 4-in-1 board with one MCU per motor.

> ⚠️ **Flag 2: "Nano" plus 80 A.** A 20 × 20 mm board cannot thermally sustain 4 × 80 A. Section 3 shows that even 30.5 × 30.5 mm is **duration-limited** to a few seconds at 80 A. Treat 80 A as a transient rating, not a continuous one.

> ⚠️ **Flag 3: Battery current.** Phase current is not battery current. At duty D, I<sub>batt</sub> ≈ D·I<sub>phase</sub>. Four motors at 80 A and D = 0.9 draw about **288 A from the pack**. That load affects the XT60/XT90 connector, the bulk capacitors, and the battery C-rating. Confirm that this is really the requirement.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Reference MOSFET (placeholder: substitute your datasheet values)

The design uses a representative 40 V device in a 5 × 6 mm PQFN package (BSC010N04LS / TPHR8504PL class):

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R<sub>DS(on),25</sub> | 0.90 mΩ @ V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V | On-resistance at 25 °C |
| α | ≈ 0.0045 /K | R<sub>DS(on)</sub> tempco (×1.6 at 150 °C) |
| Q<sub>g</sub> | 50 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q<sub>gs2</sub> + Q<sub>gd</sub> | 15 nC | Charge during the switching interval |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> | 40 nC | Reverse recovery charge |
| C<sub>oss(er)</sub> | 1.5 nF | Energy-related output capacitance |
| V<sub>SD</sub> | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |
| R<sub>θJC</sub> | 1.0 K/W | Junction to case |

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At the design point T<sub>j</sub> = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.90 \times 1.34 \approx 1.20\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Current paths in 6-step synchronous drive

In each commutation sector, one phase is high-side (HS) driven, one is low-side (LS) driven, and one floats. The PWM leg alternates between two states:

- **ON state (fraction D):** current flows through HS<sub>A</sub>, the motor, and LS<sub>B</sub>.
- **Freewheel state (fraction 1−D, synchronous rectification):** current flows through LS<sub>A</sub>, the motor, and LS<sub>B</sub>.

In both states, exactly **two channels** carry the phase current. The total conduction loss is therefore independent of D, apart from dead time (covered in §2):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Applying this to the burst condition:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.20\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

The loss averaged over the six FETs is 15.4 / 6 ≈ **2.56 W per FET**. Within a sector, however, the loss is not shared evenly. The worst-case device is the static LS switch, which dissipates I²R ≈ 7.7 W during its 120° window.

**Conservative assumption.** The thermal time constant of the junction-to-case path is short (milliseconds), but at high eRPM (>1 kHz electrical) the board smooths the loss. The model therefore uses the cycle average for board heating and the peak sector loss for the T<sub>j</sub> check.

### 1.4 Copper loss

Assume the phase and bus copper path is about 15 mm of 4 layers × 2 oz copper, 6 mm wide.

- Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□. Four layers in parallel give about 0.0625 mΩ/□.
- 15 mm / 6 mm = 2.5 squares, so R ≈ 0.16 mΩ per path.
- With two paths per motor, R<sub>cu</sub> ≈ 0.32 mΩ, rising to about 0.40 mΩ at 80 °C.

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.40\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.6\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Shunt loss is extra if you use per-motor shunts: 0.25 mΩ × 80² = 1.6 W.
