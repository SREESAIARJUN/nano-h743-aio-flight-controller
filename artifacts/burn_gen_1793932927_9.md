## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Derivation

Two premises in the request need correcting, because they change the math.

**1. The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly.**
- Production designs use the H743 as the flight controller running ArduPilot.
- It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or the EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_S.
- ArduPilot has no in-tree sensorless BLDC commutation, and four motors' commutation ISRs would conflict with the scheduler's timing.
- The H743 therefore owns **4 timer output channels**, not 12 gate-drive PWMs plus comparators.

**2. 80 A per motor on a "nano" board is a burst rating only. It is not continuous.**
- Section 3 shows that a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot reject the ~80–90 W generated at 4×80 A for more than about 1–3 seconds.
- The deliverable below rates **80 A burst (≤1 s), with ~25–35 A continuous per channel** under prop-wash cooling.
- Marketing "80A" figures for this form factor are generally burst numbers measured at ambient with no thermal steady state.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM:
- At any instant, current flows through **one high-side path and one low-side path**.
- The PWM leg alternates HS/LS at duty D. Synchronous rectification keeps the current in a MOSFET channel rather than the body diode, except during dead time.
- At full throttle (D → 1), the conduction path is effectively two MOSFETs in series for the whole period.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

For the active pair, assuming flat-top trapezoidal current (I_rms ≈ I_dc for the conducting phases):

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \left(2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{trace}\right)$$

Temperature dependence of R_DS(on):

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad k \approx 0.006\,/\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R ≈ 1.6 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot (2 \times 1.6 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C (start of burst): 6400 × 2.3 mΩ = **14.7 W**.

This creates **positive electrothermal feedback**: loss rises with temperature. The coupled equation is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\cdot I^2\left(2R_{25}[1+k(T_j-25)] + R_{tr}\right)$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}I^2\left(2R_{25}(1-25k) + R_{tr}\right)}{1 - 2R_{th}I^2R_{25}k}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when the denominator reaches zero:

$$R_{th}\,I^2 \geq \frac{1}{2R_{25}k} = \frac{1}{2(10^{-3})(0.006)} \approx 83{,}300\ \mathrm{A^2\cdot K/W}$$

At 80 A this gives R_th,crit ≈ 13 K/W per channel. A nano board in still air is close to this limit. This is the formal reason the 80 A rating must be burst-limited.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched PWM Leg)

At high D, only one device per channel switches per PWM cycle:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Loss

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Reverse Recovery Loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\times10^{-9}\times 25.2\times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The diode recovery also adds current to the turn-on edge. A first-order correction to the turn-on loss is:

$$\Delta P_{on} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{rr} t_{rr} f_{sw}$$

This term is typically below 0.3 W. It is included in the margin below.

### 2.6 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times30\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### 2.7 Gate Drive Loss

Both devices in the switching leg are driven each cycle. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the MOSFETs:

$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2\times40\times10^{-9}\times10\times48\times10^3 = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.8 Loss Totals

| Term | 1 channel, T_j = 25 °C | 1 channel, T_j = 125 °C | 4 channels, T_j = 125 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 14.7 W | 22.4 W | 89.6 W |
| Switching | 1.45 W | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + I_rr correction | ~0.3 W | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| Coss + gate | 0.06 W | 0.06 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **~17.1 W** | **~24.8 W** | **~99 W** |

**Design insight:** at 80 A, conduction is about 90% of total loss. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 2 W per channel.

The highest-leverage improvements are:
- Lower R_DS(on), including paralleling two FETs per switch, which halves P_cond.
- Copper thickness.

Faster edges help much less, and they worsen EMI and ringing.
