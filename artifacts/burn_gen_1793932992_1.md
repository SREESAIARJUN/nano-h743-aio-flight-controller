# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC) Design Package: Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation, Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Any Math

Three assumptions in the brief need correcting first. Each one changes the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It outputs DShot and does not run 6-step or FOC commutation. On every production AIO, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU produces the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The netlist below follows this partition.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board is a transient rating, not a steady one.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board reaches about 120 W total dissipation at 80 A on all four channels. It can survive that for only about 1–2 s. The honest continuous rating is about 30 A per channel.
3. **The parameters below are assumptions.** No FET, gate driver, or copper stack-up was specified. Every number traces back to the parameter table in section 1. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel, 6-Step Synchronous)

At any instant in trapezoidal commutation, two legs conduct:
- one leg is PWM'd: high-side (HS) at duty D, with the complementary low-side (LS) at 1−D;
- the opposite leg's LS is held fully ON.

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_J) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_J-25)]$$

Per-device conduction losses:

$$P_{cond,HS} = D\,I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{cond,LS,sync} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{cond,LS,on} = I^2 R_{ds}$$

These sum to a total that does not depend on duty:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_J)$$

At T_J = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Conduction is about 92% of the per-channel loss.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (Hard-Switched Leg Only)

**Turn-on and turn-off crossover:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.968\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V\,f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**Output charge (HS and LS):**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V\,f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_f\,I\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive (six FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver):**

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g\,V_{gs}\,f_{sw} = 6 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.144\ \text{W}$$

These sum to:

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.83\ W}$$

Design levers:
- Dead time is the largest switching term. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- Raising f_sw to 96 kHz doubles the switching terms to about 3.7 W per channel.

### 2.3 Copper and Shunt Loss

Assumptions: 2 oz copper on 6 layers, an effective path of about 0.4 mΩ per channel, and a 0.25 mΩ current shunt.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.65\,\text{mΩ} \approx \mathbf{4.2\ W}$$

### 2.4 Totals

| Term | Per channel | All 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 1.8 W | 7.3 W |
| Copper + shunt | 4.2 W | 16.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.2 W** | **≈101 W** |

Add about 2 W for the H743, regulators, and ESC MCUs. Design to **≈103 W** at full burst.

### 2.5 Input Capacitor Ripple

Ripple current per channel is largest at D = 0.5:

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}$$

If all four channels switch in phase with no interleave, the board-level cap bank sees up to about 160 A RMS. On a nano board this is impossible.

Mitigations:
- Rely on the external low-ESR bulk cap (≥1000 µF, 35 V) mounted at the battery pads.
- Use about 4 × 10 µF X7R 0805 parts per channel, placed within 3 mm of each half-bridge.
- Stagger the PWM phase between ESC MCUs if the firmware supports it.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above Case (Per FET)

The worst-case device is the always-on low-side FET:

$$P = I^2 R_{ds} = 9.6\ \text{W}, \qquad \Delta T_{JC} = 9.6 \times 1.5 = 14.4\ \text{K}$$

The die thermal time constant is on the millisecond scale, so this offset is effectively instant. The board must therefore stay below 125 − 14 ≈ **111 °C**.

### 3.2 Board Lumped Capacity (Adiabatic Burst)

Assumptions: a 25.5 × 25.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board of about 2.8 g, plus 24 FETs, components, and conformal coat.

$$C_{th} \approx 2.8\,\text{g}\times 0.9\ \text{J/gK} + 1.5\ \text{J/K} \approx 4\ \text{J/K}$$

Board temperature over time:

$$T(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{θ,BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/(R_{θ,BA}C_{th})}\right)$$

### 3.3 Board-to-Air
