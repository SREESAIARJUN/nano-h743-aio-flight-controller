## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Findings

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC requires one commutation MCU per motor (or a dual-motor MCU). This design uses:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) running ArduPilot.
- **ESC:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32, each with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs.
- **Link:** Bi-directional DShot600 from H743 timer pins to each ESC MCU signal input.

### 0.3 Critical finding: "80 A burst" must be defined

| Interpretation | Result |
|---|---|
| **80 A per channel** | Approximately 120 W total dissipation on a 4 cm² board. The board rises about 65 K/s. This is **not achievable** on a 20×20 nano form factor, even for sub-second bursts, once junction-to-board gradients are included. |
| **80 A aggregate (20 A per channel)** | Approximately 10 W total. This is feasible as a **burst rating** with prop-wash cooling. It is thermally limited as a continuous rating. |

The math for both cases is below. **Recommendation:** label the product "20 A continuous-burst per channel, 80 A aggregate." Advertising 80 A per channel on this form factor would fail validation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase's high side is PWM-modulated (duty D). Its low side is driven complementarily (1−D). A second phase's low side is held on.

- During **on-time**, current flows through Q_AH and Q_BL.
- During **off-time**, current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous) and Q_BL.

At every instant, **exactly two MOSFETs carry the phase current I**, outside of dead time. Conduction loss per bridge is therefore independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big]
$$
