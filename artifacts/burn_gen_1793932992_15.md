# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #75): Electrical, Thermal and Timer/DMA Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Architecture Corrections

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors and also run ArduPilot. The standard and only sane architecture is:

- **FC board:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bi-directional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **4-in-1 ESC board:** four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These perform commutation and 48 kHz PWM, and return eRPM telemetry over the same DShot wire.

**Unspecified parameters.** No MOSFET, board size, battery or burst duration was specified. All numbers below use the parameters in this table. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values and recompute using the formulas, which are the actual deliverable.

**Feasibility warning.** A true "nano" (20×20 mm, 3.3×3.3 FETs) cannot do 80 A per channel, even as a burst. The derivation in §3 shows why. This document is therefore sized on a 30.5×30.5 mm frame with 5×6 FETs. If you must stay at 20×20, cap the burst rating at about 35–45 A per channel.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 0.70 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.03 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction (one commutation sector, duty D)

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd high (with a complementary low-side), and one phase's low-side is held on:

| FET role (per 120° conduction window) | Conduction fraction | Power while active |
|---|---|---|
| PWM high-side | D | I²R |
| Complementary low-side (sync rect) | 1 − D | I²R |
| Static low-side (return path) | 1 | I²R |

Summing over the bridge (each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle):

$$P_{cond,bridge}=I_{ph}^2R\,[D+(1-D)+1]=2\,I_{ph,rms}^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This total is independent of D. The average per FET is $P_{FET}=I^2R/3$.

Including PWM ripple, use $I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$. At 48 kHz with a typical 5–20 µH motor, ΔI ≈ 2–6 A, which adds less than 0.5 %. It is negligible.

### 1.3 Numbers at an 80 A burst

- Per channel: P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.03 mΩ = **13.2 W**
- Per FET (average): 13.2 / 6 = **2.2 W** (instantaneous 6.6 W while conducting)
- Four channels: **52.7 W**

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (often forgotten)

Assume a 2 oz (70 µm) phase or bus path, 6 mm wide, 15 mm long, on 2 parallel layers:

R_cu = ρL / (wt) = (1.72e-8 × 0.015) / (0.006 × 70e-6 × 2) = 0.31 mΩ, rising to ×1.33 at 110 °C, giving about 0.41 mΩ.

At 80 A in two conducting paths, this adds about **5 W per channel**. Use more layers in parallel, or solder-bar reinforcement, on the battery and phase pours.

### 1.5 Current-shunt loss

With a 0.25 mΩ common shunt: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W per channel. Use 0.2 mΩ (2512, 3 W), or sense via R_ds.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses are per channel, for the single PWM'd half-bridge.

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = 0.05 W |
| Output capacitance | ½ · Q_oss · V · f (×2 FETs) | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode | 2 · V_SD · I · t_dt · f | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48 k = 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.7 W/channel** |

**Key behaviour:** P_sw scales with I × V × f and vanishes as D → 1, because there is no PWM switching at 100 % duty and only commutation edges remain.

The worst combined case is therefore not full throttle but around D ≈ 0.7–0.9 with high current. Use:

$$P_{total}(D)=2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)+\begin{cases}P_{sw}&D<0.98\\ \approx 0.05\,P_{sw}&D\to1\end{cases}+P_{cu}+P_{shunt}$$
