## 0. Architecture Review: Three Issues Before Any Math

### Flag 1: The H743 does not drive the bridges

In every production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and emits DShot. A separate ESC MCU per motor (or one per pair) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and does the commutation, typically an STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230. The H743 should not run 4× FOC/six-step commutation alongside the ArduPilot scheduler. This document assumes that two-board architecture:

```
[H743 FC, ArduPilot] --DShot600 bidir x4--> [4x ESC MCU] --> [4x 3-ph gate driver] --> [24 MOSFETs]
```

### Flag 2: "80A burst" on a nano footprint is thermally marginal

A nano board is 20×20 mm (M2 holes) or at most 25.5×25.5 mm. As Section 2 shows, 4× 80A simultaneous dissipates about 65 W. A 20×20 board has a thermal mass of roughly 6–10 J/K and no meaningful steady-state sink. The 80A rating is therefore **only a time-limited burst** (seconds), and the firmware/ESC current limit must enforce it. The burst duration is derived below so the datasheet rating is defensible.

### Flag 3: "80A" must be defined

This model uses **80A per motor phase current (peak burst), 6S (25.2 V max)**. If 80A is meant as total battery current, every loss figure drops by about 16× (I² scaling from 20A/motor).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\bigl[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\bigr],\quad k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ\text{C}) = 0.7 \times 1.5 = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This coupling is a positive feedback loop: losses raise T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises losses. It must be solved iteratively (Section 3.4).

### 1.2 Six-step current path

In any 60° sector, current flows through one high-side FET of phase A, the motor, and one low-side FET of phase B. With synchronous PWM, the "off" interval of the high side transfers current to the complementary low side, which has the same R_ds. So **two channel resistances are always in series with I_ph**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle (duty 1/3):

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = 6400 \times 1.05\times10^{-3}/3 = \mathbf{2.24\ W\ (average)}$$

During its conduction window, the FET's instantaneous dissipation is 6.72 W. At typical electrical frequencies (1–3 kHz for a 14-pole motor at 20–25k RPM), the window is 100–300 µs. That is shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms), so the junction mostly sees the average. Add about 3–5 K of ripple at the peak.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, often dominant on nano boards)

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\,\ell}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}(100^\circ\text{C}) \approx 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}$$

**Example:** a 12 mm path, 4 mm wide, 3 oz (105 µm), doubled on 2 layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.012}{0.004\times 2\times105\times10^{-6}} = 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.31\times10^{-3}\times 2\ (\text{in + out path}) \approx \mathbf{4.0\ W/motor}$$

**Mitigation:** use 6-layer 2 oz inner planes, stitched with via arrays (≥ 20 vias per FET pad of 0.3 mm diameter), and solder-fill the battery pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. One high-side and one low-side complementary pair commutate per PWM cycle.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48{,}000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

More precise: replace t_r and t_f with the Miller plateau time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 15 nC, R_g,total = 10 Ω (5 Ω external + 2 Ω driver + 3 Ω internal), V_drv = 12 V and V_plat = 4.5 V:

$$t_{sw} = \frac{15\times10^{-9}\times10}{7.5} = 20\ \text{ns} \checkmark$$

### 2.2 Reverse-recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 60\times10^{-9}\times25.2\times48{,}000 = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced current spike also adds turn-on loss in the opposite FET. A conservative estimate adds roughly 0.1 W. **Budget: 0.17 W.**

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\times10^{-9}\times48{,}000 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
