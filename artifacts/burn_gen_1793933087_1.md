# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Several points in the brief need resolving before the math is meaningful.

| Issue | Position taken |
|---|---|
| **The H743 is not the ESC controller.** ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation. Bi-directional DShot only makes sense when separate ESC MCUs exist. | Four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware) drive the bridges. The H743 is the FC and talks BDShot600 to them. |
| **"80A burst" is ambiguous.** | Interpreted as **80 A phase current per channel**, with 4 channels simultaneously, for a bounded burst. Battery current is lower (I_batt ≈ D·I_phase) but still roughly 200–320 A total. |
| **Nano form factor (≤ 30.5 mm, typically 20×20 mm)** | **80 A × 4 continuous is physically unachievable** on this board. Section 3 shows a burst limit of roughly 2–4 s. Marketing ratings of "80A burst" on nano AIOs usually mean milliseconds to about 10 s with prop-wash airflow. |
| Component values | MOSFET and driver parameters below are **representative placeholders**. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected parts before release. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Synchronous Commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, exactly two phase legs are active. One leg is PWM-switched (high-side on for D, low-side on for 1−D under synchronous rectification). The other leg holds its low-side (or high-side) FET fully on.

At every instant, therefore, the phase current flows through **exactly two conducting MOSFET channels**: one in the PWM leg and one in the static leg. Dead-time body-diode conduction is treated separately in §2.

Per channel (one motor):

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

Phase-current ripple adds a small RMS correction:

$$
I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

**Example ripple calculation** for a low-inductance motor at mid-throttle (L_ph ≈ 10 µH, D = 0.5, V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V):

$$
\Delta I = \frac{12 \times 0.5}{10\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 12.5\ \text{A}
$$

The correction term is ΔI²/12 ≈ 13 A², which is about 0.2 % of 6400 A². It is negligible at 80 A, but **not** negligible at low current.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Electro-Thermal Coupling)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

Combining with the conduction equation gives a positive-feedback loop:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\cdot P(T_j)
$$

Solving for T_j yields:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P_{0}\,(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - Z_{th}\,P_0\,\alpha}, \qquad P_0 = 2 I^2 R_{DS,25}
$$

**Thermal runaway occurs when Z_th · P₀ · α ≥ 1.** This is the hard stability limit.

### 1.3 Numerical Results at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 68.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** | **74.2 W** |

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (as PWM switch or static switch). The average per FET at 125 °C is therefore:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx 18.6/6 \approx 3.1\ \text{W}
$$

At low eRPM (start-up, stall, hard punch), a single FET can carry its share for many milliseconds. Use ½ · P_cond ≈ 9.3 W per FET as the peak case.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Per channel, only **one leg hard-switches per PWM period** (the PWM leg). All terms below therefore apply once per channel.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (Hard-Switching) Loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 0.968\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance Loss

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

The Q·V form is used rather than ½CV², because C_oss is nonlinear.

### 2.3 Body-Diode Dead-Time Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 20\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}
$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature. A 2–3× derate is advisable.

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in the Driver, Not the FET)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 45\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.043\ \text{W per channel}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Summary per Channel

| Term | Power |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.968 W |
| C_oss | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.307 W |
| Q_rr | 0.024 W |
