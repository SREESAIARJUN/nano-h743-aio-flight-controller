# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Power-Stage Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Model, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Constraints

**1. The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8/AT32 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and drive the gate drivers. The two domains are therefore modeled separately:

- **FC domain:** timers, DMA, DShot.
- **ESC domain:** 48 kHz power stage, losses, thermal.

**2. 80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating, not a steady-state one.** The math below shows that 4×80 A dissipates about 80–95 W. A 20×20 board cannot reject that continuously. The model is built to compute the burst duration limit, which is the number that matters.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, two half-bridges conduct the phase current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET carries current for duty D. The low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for 1−D.
- **Fixed leg:** its LS FET is on 100% of the time.

Because synchronous rectification gives one conducting FET per leg at every instant, the per-channel conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

The temperature dependence of on-resistance is:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

**Cold (T_j = 25 °C):**

$$P = 2\cdot 6400\cdot 0.9\text{ mΩ} = 11.5\text{ W}$$

**Hot (T_j = 115 °C, R_ds = 1.35 mΩ):**

$$P = 2\cdot 6400\cdot 1.35\text{ mΩ} = 17.3\text{ W}$$

**Copper path:** take R_Cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per phase path (2 oz outer + 2 oz inner, short pours, plus pad and solder). Two phases conduct, so:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} \approx 3.2\text{ W}$$

**Per-FET average** (each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, shared between HS and LS):

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = 2.9\text{ W (hot)}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is on is I²R = 8.6 W. This matters for Z_th(t) at low eRPM.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

The inductor ripple adds a correction term:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″ motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH (line-to-line ≈ 30 µH):

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{12.6\cdot 0.5}{30\,\mu\text{H}\cdot 48\,\text{kHz}} \approx 4.4\text{ A}$$

The correction is about 0.02%, so it is negligible at 80 A. It matters only for low-inductance pancake motors.

### 1.4 FOC / sinusoidal variant (if the ESC firmware uses it)

With peak current Î, all three legs conduct:

$$P_{cond} = 3\left(\frac{\hat I}{\sqrt2}\right)^2 R_{ds}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM leg's HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET turns on after its body diode conducts, so it switches near zero voltage and contributes dead-time and Q_rr losses instead.

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

Plateau voltage V_pl ≈ 4.5 V at 80 A, with R_g,int ≈ 1 Ω and R_g,ext = 2.2 Ω.

**Turn-on:**

$$I_{g,on} \approx \frac{10-4.5}{3.2\,Ω} \approx 1.7\text{ A}$$

The driver limits this to about 1.0 A, so:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{13\text{ nC}}{1.0\text{ A}} = 13\text{ ns}$$

**Turn-off:**

$$I_{g,off} \approx \frac{4.5}{3.2\,Ω} \approx 1.4\text{ A}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{13\text{ nC}}{1.4\text{ A}} \approx 9.5\text{ ns}$$

Use 15 ns and 15 ns to cover the layout inductance and Miller-plateau extension.

### 2.2 Loss terms per channel (V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode (LS) | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f (+ extra I·t_rr overlap ≈ ×2) | 50 nC·25.2·48k ≈ 0.06 W → **0.12 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f | ½·1.5 nF·635·48k = **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly in the driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | 2·45 nC·10·48k = **0.04 W** |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.25 W / channel** |

Sensitivity to switching frequency: switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 1.1 W, and at 96 kHz about 4.5 W. At 80 A, conduction dominates by roughly 8:1, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice. It gives a quieter motor, a smaller ΔI, and still a minor switching penalty.

### 2.3 Total per channel and board

| | Per channel | ×4 board |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, hot) | 17.3 W | 69.1 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.25 W | 9.0 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, 80 A, if a per-phase shunt is present) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.3 W** | **≈ 97 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × ~20 V × D_eff. Losses are about 5–7% of that, which is normal. The problem is the absolute wattage on a small board.
