# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

I don't have prior project documents for Job #42. Everything below is derived from stated assumptions, so swap in your actual MOSFET datasheet values, board stack-up, and pinout. Every equation is parametric so the numbers re-derive cleanly.

The math leads to a significant conclusion, summarized here and justified in §1.6. **80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board is only achievable as a time-limited burst of roughly 1–2 s**, not a continuous rating. Steady-state, the stage dissipates about 85 W into a board that can reject only a few watts. The firmware must enforce a burst limiter, and the "80 A" marketing figure must be qualified accordingly.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (Per Motor, Then ×4)

### 1.1 Conduction Losses

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly **two** channel resistances: one in the PWM'd leg (HS during on-time, LS during off-time) and the static LS.

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

This is **independent of duty cycle** to first order, which is why throttle-down at high current doesn't relieve heating much.

Each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. The average per-FET share is:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}}{3}
$$

The equivalent RMS current per FET is I_ph/√3 ≈ 46 A.

**Numbers at T_j = 125 °C (R_DS = 1.45 mΩ):**

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = 18.6\ \text{W}
$$

This gives 3.1 W average per FET. Instantaneous dissipation in the static-LS FET is 9.3 W during its window. Electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz at high RPM, while the die thermal time constant is on the order of milliseconds, so the average figure is valid.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_DS rises with T_j, which raises P, which raises T_j. Solve iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P(T_j)\, R_{th,j\text{-}b}
$$

The loop is stable as long as $2 I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha\, R_{th} < 1$.

> **FOC note:** With sinusoidal FOC and peak current I_pk, P_cond = (3/2)·I_pk²·R_DS. This is similar magnitude for an equal-torque comparison.

### 1.2 Switching (V–I Overlap) Losses

Only one leg switches per sector, and it has one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.3 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Losses

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

This is a direct lever: cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Only do this if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it.

### 1.4 Reverse Recovery, Coss, and Gate Drive

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 0.038\ \text{W} \quad (\text{dissipated mostly in driver and } R_g)
$$

### 1.5 Copper / Shunt Losses

For a 4-in-1 board with 2 oz copper inner planes:

- Phase path + shunt: about 0.5 mΩ, giving P = 80² × 0.5 m ≈ **3.2 W per motor**. If you sense current, a 0.25 mΩ shunt alone accounts for 1.6 W.
- Battery input: I_bat ≈ ΣP_out / V_bus, which can be 150–250 A peak on 6S. Size pads for 10 AWG equivalent and a Low-ESR bulk capacitor (≥ 1000 µF external plus ≥ 4×10 µF ceramic per phase on-board).

The required ripple current rating for the bulk capacitor is approximately:

$$
I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}
$$

This reaches a worst case of 40 A at D = 0.5 per motor, **which a nano board cannot absorb without the external capacitor**.
