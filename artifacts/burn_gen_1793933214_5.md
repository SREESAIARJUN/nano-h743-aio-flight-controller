# Job #40: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (80 A Burst) System Architecture Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility

**Architectural boundary.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM and the switching losses therefore belong to the ESC MCUs and power stage. The H743 owns the bidirectional DShot600 link and its DMA.

**Feasibility.** A 20×20 mm "nano" 4-in-1 rated 80 A burst per channel (320 A aggregate) only works as a time-limited burst rating, on the order of seconds. The model below shows this quantitatively in §1.5.

**Baseline parameters.** These are representative values for a 40 V, 5×6 mm or 3.3×3.3 mm class MOSFET. Every value marked † must be replaced with the selected part's datasheet value.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full charge) | DC link |
| I_ph | 80 A (burst) | Phase current, trapezoidal |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM |
| R_ds(on),25 † | 0.90 mΩ | at V_gs = 10 V |
| α † | 0.0055 K⁻¹ | R_ds temperature coefficient |
| t_r + t_f † | 10 ns + 10 ns | Effective V–I overlap at I = 80 A |
| Q_g † | 50 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_oss † | 30 nC | Output charge |
| Q_rr † | 40 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| V_SD † | 0.80 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 50 ns | Dead time per edge |
| R_θJC † | 1.2 K/W | Junction to case (bottom pad) |

**Topology.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM on the active leg, the opposite low side held on). This is how AM32 and BLHeli_32 drive it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.90 × 1.55 = **1.395 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path per commutation sector

In each 60° sector, phase current flows through exactly two switch positions at any instant:

- **PWM-on (duty D):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **PWM-off (1−D):** LS_A (synchronous) → motor → LS_B

Current is always flowing through two conducting MOSFETs (excluding dead time), so for one ESC:

$$P_{cond,ESC}=I_{ph}^2\cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)+I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}+I_{ph}^2R_{shunt}$$

### 1.3 Per-device split (for junction temperature)

Over one electrical revolution, each switch position carries current for 1/3 of the period. For the high side, conduction is weighted by D. For the synchronous low side, it is weighted by (1−D) plus its "held-on" sector. The worst case is the low side at low D:

$$P_{LS,max}\approx I_{ph}^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}\qquad P_{HS}\approx I_{ph}^2R_{ds}\cdot\tfrac{D}{3}$$

### 1.4 Numerical results at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Expression | Per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction | 80² × 2 × 1.395 mΩ | **17.86 W** |
| PCB copper (phase + bus, ~0.25 mΩ) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Shunt (if 0.2 mΩ per ESC) | 80² × 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |
| **Conduction subtotal** | | **≈ 20.7 W** |
| Worst single LS FET | 80² × 1.395 m × 2/3 | 5.95 W |

> **Design rule:** Do not use a single common battery shunt on a nano board. At 320 A through 0.1 mΩ it dissipates 10.2 W. Use per-ESC low-side shunts or a Hall sensor such as ACS772 or MCx1101.

### 1.5 Electrothermal self-consistency (runaway check)

Conduction loss rises with temperature, so the operating point must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta JA}\,P(T_j),\qquad P(T_j)=P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Closed form:

$$T_j=\frac{T_a+R_{\theta JA}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_{\theta JA}P_0}$$

The stability condition is:

$$\alpha\,R_{\theta JA}\,P_0<1$$

With P_0 = 4 × 11.5 W (four ESCs at 25 °C), α = 0.0055, and a nano-board R_θJA ≈ 6 K/W in prop wash, αR_θP_0 = 1.52 > 1.

**Result: there is no steady-state solution, so 80 A continuous on all four channels is thermal runaway.** The rating must be defined as a transient burst (§3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step drive, only one leg per ESC is PWM-switching at a time. Per ESC:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz})=\mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time and then recovers when the HS turns on:

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k}=0.048\text{ W}$$

Recovery current also adds to HS turn-on loss. That is roughly covered by the margin in t_r.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\times80\times100\text{n}\times48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors. HS and LS both switch.

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}=2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}=0.048\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching summary per ESC

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| P_VI | 0.968 |
| P_oss | 0.036 |
| P_rr | 0.048 |
| P_dt | 0.307 |
| P_g | 0.048 |
| **Total switching** | **1.41 W** |
