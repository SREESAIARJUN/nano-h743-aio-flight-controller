## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) conducts for duty D; the low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the LS is on for 100% of the sector.

At any instant, the motor current I passes through exactly two FET channels, ignoring dead time. The per-channel bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}\,[\,D + (1-D)\,] + I^2 R_{DS(on),hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}
$$

This is independent of D, which is why synchronous rectification matters.

### 1.2 Temperature coefficient

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(1+\alpha\right)^{(T_j-25)},\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.0045¹⁰⁰ ≈ **1.57 mΩ**.

### 1.3 PCB copper path

Per channel: battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad.

- Assume 4 oz outer / 2 oz inner copper.
- Assume an effective path of R_Cu ≈ 0.35 mΩ, including vias.
- Copper's α_Cu = 0.0039/K gives ≈ 0.42 mΩ at 70 °C.

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.42\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 2.69\ \mathrm{W}
$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.57\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{20.1\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Per FET (time-averaged over one electrical cycle, 6 FETs):

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx 20.1/6 = 3.35\ \mathrm{W}
$$

> **Ripple correction.** The phase-current ripple ΔI adds ΔI²/12 to the RMS². With motor L = 15 µH, 25 V and 48 kHz, ΔI ≈ 8.7 A p-p. That adds 6.3 A² against 6400 A², about **0.1%**, which is negligible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The other leg's LS is static. Per channel, per switching period:

### 2.1 V–I overlap (HS hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}
$$

Approximating rise and fall times by the gate-plateau charge Q_gd = 10 nC, plateau voltage V_pl = 3.2 V, V_drv = 12 V and R_g,total = 4 Ω:

- Turn-on: I_g,on = (12 − 3.2)/4 = 2.2 A, limited by the driver to 1.2 A, so t_r ≈ 10 nC / 1.2 A = 8.3 ns.
- Turn-off: I_g,off = 3.2/4 = 0.8 A, so t_f ≈ 10 nC / 0.8 A = 12.5 ns.

$$
P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20.8\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.01\ W}
$$
