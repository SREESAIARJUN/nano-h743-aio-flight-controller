## 0. Architectural Assumptions

A 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot carry **80 A per channel continuously**. Everything below treats 80 A as a **transient burst rating** with a calculated time limit. I also fix the architecture because one point is often misunderstood:

- **The STM32H743 is the flight controller.** It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot.
- **Each ESC channel has its own small MCU** (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These MCUs handle the 48 kHz PWM and commutation.
- The H743 never drives gates directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In each commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side switch conducts for fraction D, and the low-side switch conducts (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- **Phase B (static leg):** the low-side switch conducts for 100 % of the sector.

In any interval, exactly **two** MOSFET channels carry the full phase current I:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, apart from the dead-time interval handled in §2.4.

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Electrothermal Feedback)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

The junction temperature depends on its own loss:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta JA}$$

Solving this loop gives a closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + P_0 R_{\theta JA}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - \alpha P_0 R_{\theta JA}}, \quad P_0 = 2I^2R_{ds,25}$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** α·P₀·R_θJA ≥ 1. This defines the absolute continuous ceiling (see §5.3).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A (Hot, T_j = 125 °C)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-Device Peak

The worst-stressed single FET is the static low-side switch. It conducts 100 % for 1/3 of the electrical cycle and is idle otherwise:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2R_{ds} = 9.6\ \mathrm{W}, \qquad P_{FET,avg} \approx 9.6/3 + \text{sync share} \approx 4.8\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Conduction

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad and trace (2 oz, ~6 mm, multilayer parallel) | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Battery-side bus, shared (I_bus ≈ D·ΣI, take 160 A peak at 4×80 A, D = 0.5) | ~0.15 mΩ | 3.8 W (total) |
| Current shunt, 0.2 mΩ, on the shared bus | 0.2 mΩ | 5.1 W (total, burst) |

> **Note:** At 160 A, the shunt alone dissipates 5 W. A 2512 shunt is rated around 3 W. Either use 2× 0.4 mΩ in parallel across two 2512 packages, or accept a burst-only rating with documented derating.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The static leg switches at commutation frequency, which is negligible at f_comm ≪ f_sw.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard Switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

A more accurate version uses the Miller plateau. Effective t_r ≈ Q_gd·R_g,tot / (V_drv − V_plateau). Use this if you have Q_gd.

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac12 \approx 0.5 \times 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.024\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\,\mathrm{nC}\times25.2\times48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.060\ \mathrm{W}$$

Reverse recovery also injects about 50 nC into the opposite FET at turn-on. This is partially included in 2.1, so the total is slightly conservative.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

This loss is often neglected, but it is significant at 80 A:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\,\mathrm{ns}\times48\,\mathrm{kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$
