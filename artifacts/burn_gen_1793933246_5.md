## 0. Architectural Clarifications

These decisions shape the rest of the model.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own local MCU running AM32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM and handles back-EMF commutation. This document uses an AT32F421 or STM32G071-class part for that role. The 48 kHz switching model applies to the ESC stage, and the DShot model applies to the H743.
2. **"Nano" plus 80A burst is a physical contradiction at 20×20 mm.** No 20×20 board can dissipate the roughly 100 W of total burst loss derived below. This document uses a **30.5×30.5 mm footprint (~36×36 mm board)**. Even there, 80A is only achievable as a **seconds-scale burst**, which Section 3 quantifies.
3. **Component parameters are representative, not vendor-verified.** Values are typical for 40 V, 5×6 mm PDFN power MOSFETs. Before layout freeze, replace every parameter in Table 1 with the datasheet value of the selected part. The equations hold regardless.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. AM32 uses complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- **Active leg:** high side conducts for D·T_sw. The synchronous low side of the *same* leg conducts for (1−D)·T_sw. Dead time is handled separately in §2.3.
- **Return leg:** low side is fully on.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two channels' worth of R_DS(on)**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \frac{2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Phase current is not pure DC. With motor phase-pair inductance L_m:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_m f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5. For a typical 5" motor with L_m ≈ 20 µH:

ΔI_pp = 25.2·0.25 / (20e-6 · 48e3) = **6.6 A**

The correction term is 6.6²/12 = 3.6 A², which is under 0.1 % of 6400 A². **It is negligible at 48 kHz.** This is one of the benefits of the high switching frequency.
