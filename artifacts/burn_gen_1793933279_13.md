# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #67)

## Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning of the H743 and the ESC MCUs

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each ESC channel is commutated by its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This is how every production AIO board is partitioned.

- **48 kHz** is the ESC-side PWM frequency. It is configurable in AM32 and sits at the upper end of typical ranges.
- **DShot600** is the H743 → ESC command link.

Putting six-step FOC for four motors on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is not supported by ArduPilot and is not recommended.

> **Engineering flag.** An "80 A" rating on a 25.5 mm board is a **burst** figure. The analysis below shows it is thermally sustainable only for roughly 1–3 s, depending on airflow. Four channels at 80 A also implies **320 A of battery current**. That exceeds XT60/XT90 connector ratings and practical pad and wire limits. Specify the product as *"80 A burst per channel (≤1 s), 4× combined current limited by the battery interface."*

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct.

- **Static leg:** one low-side FET is held ON for the entire sector and carries I_ph continuously.
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1 − D, synchronous rectification).

At any instant, exactly **two FET channels** are in the current path. The duty cycle therefore cancels out of the conduction total:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

(Ripple correction: multiply I² by (1 + r²/12), where r = ΔI/I_ph. At 48 kHz with typical 5–20 µH motor phase inductance, r < 0.1, so the correction is below 0.1% and is ignored.)

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel @ 80 A | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.30 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0013 = **16.6 W** | 66.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.86 mΩ | **23.8 W** | 95.2 W |
| 125 °C | 2.05 mΩ | **26.2 W** | 104.8 W |

**Worst-case single-FET dissipation:** the static-leg low-side FET carries I²·R for the full sector, which is 1/3 of the electrical period. Its instantaneous loss is 80² × 2.05 mΩ = **13.1 W** during that sector, averaging about 4.4 W per FET over rotation.

Electrical frequencies are in the hundreds of Hz to kHz range, while the die thermal time constant is about 1–10 ms. The die therefore sees partially smoothed pulses, so design to the **peak 13 W** for the die-level ΔT.

### 1.3 Additional Copper and Interconnect Losses

Copper sheet resistance:

$$
R_\square = \rho / t = 1.72\times10^{-8} / 70\times10^{-6} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square \quad \text{(2 oz)}
$$

For a phase path of 8 mm length × 4 mm width = 2 squares → 0.49 mΩ per layer. Paralleling two layers gives about 0.25 mΩ, so **≈1.6 W per channel at 80 A**.

Shunt (if used, 0.25 mΩ): 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**.

> **Recommendation:** use low-side R_ds sensing or a single DC-bus shunt rather than per-phase shunts.
