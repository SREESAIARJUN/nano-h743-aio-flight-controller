# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Design Model, Loss Analysis, DShot/DMA Allocation and Netlist

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Findings

Four problems with the brief affect every number below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO, each ESC channel or ESC pair has its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those MCUs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to them. The architecture below follows that split.
2. **80 A per channel is not survivable on a nano board.** Section 2 shows about 25 W of loss per channel at 80 A, or about 100 W for four channels, on a 20×20 or 25×25 mm board with roughly 4–8 J/K of heat capacity. That supports a burst of a few seconds at best. It also implies about 320 A from the pack, which no nano-class XT30/XT60 lead or 2 oz trace can carry.
   - **Recommendation:** rate the board at 80 A *total* burst (20 A per channel), or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board rated 45–55 A per channel for 5 s bursts. I model the full 80 A per channel case as the bounding case and also give the 20 A per channel case.
3. **Component values are representative.** MOSFET and driver figures are typical of 40 V, 3×3 mm DFN parts and FD6288-class drivers. Replace them with your BOM's datasheet values before sign-off. The equations remain valid.
4. **The netlist is a schematic-level connectivity reference, not fab-ready.** Verify pin and ball numbers against the exact H743 package (LQFP100, TFBGA100 or UFBGA176), and run ERC/DRC.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel: 6 MOSFETs)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step complementary PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two conducting MOSFETs: either HS_A + LS_B, or LS_A + LS_B during freewheeling. The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle D:

```
P_cond,ch = 2 · I_rms² · R_DS(on)(T_j)
```

Inductor ripple adds a small correction:

```
I_rms² = I_ph² + ΔI_pp²/12
ΔI_pp  = V_bus·D(1−D) / (L_ph · f_sw)
```

For a typical 1404–2207 motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5:

- ΔI_pp = 25.2 · 0.25 / (15e-6 · 48e3) = **8.75 A**
- ΔI_pp²/12 = 6.4 A²
- This is negligible against 6400 A².
