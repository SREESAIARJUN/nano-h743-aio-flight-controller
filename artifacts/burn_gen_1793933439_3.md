# 3-Axis Gimbal FOC Control & Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion: Design Specification

I don't have the original specification for Job #7, so this design rests on the assumptions in §0. Every gain is derived from motor and inertia parameters, so you can substitute your real values directly.

---

## 0. Baseline Assumptions

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class, FPU, ≥170 MHz |
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC (e.g. GBM2804), 12N14P, so pole pairs `p = 7` |
| Encoder | 14-bit absolute magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701), one per axis |
| Current sense | 2-shunt or in-line, 20 kHz synchronous sampling |
| PWM | 20 kHz center-aligned, SVPWM |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, separate SPI buses, ODR 8 kHz, FIFO off, DRDY interrupt |

**Loop rates**

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q PI) | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s |
| Velocity (PI, inertial-rate feedback) | 4 kHz | ω_v ≈ 2π·120 rad/s |
| Angle (P + feedforward) | 1 kHz | ω_p ≈ 2π·25 rad/s |
| IMU fusion + attitude | 4 kHz (decimated from 8 kHz) | n/a |

**Voltage-mode motors:** if your motors are high-resistance voltage-mode types (R ≈ 5–15 Ω) without current sensing, replace the current loop with `v_q = R·i_q* + ω_e·ψ_m` (see §1.5).

## 1. FOC Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with `i_a + i_b + i_c = 0`):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2i_b)
$$

Park transform, with θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of this matrix.

### 1.2 PMSM model in the dq frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$
$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \psi_m
$$
$$
\tau = \tfrac32 p\,\psi_m\, i_q = K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.3 PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

Pole-zero cancellation gives a first-order closed loop with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c \quad(\text{continuous}),\qquad K_i^{d}=R\,\omega_c T_s
$$

The voltage commands are:

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$
$$
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e\psi_m
$$

**Voltage limiting:** the magnitude is clamped to `V_max = V_bus/√3` (linear SVPWM region). The d-axis is given priority, and the q-axis receives the remaining headroom:

$$
v_q^{\max}=\sqrt{V_{\max}^2 - v_d^2}
$$

**Anti-windup:** back-calculation with K_aw = 1/K_p.

### 1.4 SVPWM (min-max zero-sequence injection)

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha,v_\beta)
$$
$$
v_{0} = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc})+\min(v_{abc})\big)
$$
$$
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

This is mathematically identical to sector-based SVPWM and is branch-light.

### 1.5 Voltage-mode fallback (no current sensing)

$$
v_q^* = R\,i_q^* + \omega_e\psi_m,\qquad v_d^*=0
$$

### 1.6 Encoder angle/velocity: PLL observer

The phase error uses the sine, which handles wrap-around automatically:

$$
e=\sin(\theta_{enc}-\hat\theta)
$$
$$
\hat\omega \mathrel{+}= K_i e T_s,\qquad
\hat\theta \mathrel{+}= (\hat\omega + K_p e)T_s
$$
$$
K_p = 2\zeta\omega_n,\qquad K_i=\omega_n^2
$$

Suggested tuning: ω_n ≈ 2π·300 rad/s, ζ = 0.9. The PLL velocity estimate is used for back-EMF compensation and for the anti-cogging phase reference.

## 2. Cascaded Angle → Velocity → Current Control

### 2.1 Plant

$$
\frac{\omega(s)}{i_q(s)}=\frac{K_t}{Js+B}
$$

J is the axis inertia
