# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Flags

I have no prior context for "Job #42", so this design is built from a stated set of assumptions. Every number below derives from parameters you can replace with real datasheet values. Treat the outputs as a design model, not verified silicon data.

### 0.1 Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. The H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, which run the 48 kHz commutation PWM. The ESC MCUs would typically be AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware.

This split matters because:

- The H743's timers and DMA handle only the DShot protocol. They never touch the 48 kHz PWM.
- The thermal and switching model applies to the ESC power stage.

### 0.2 Feasibility Flags

**80 A burst on a "nano" form factor is not credible.**
- A 4-in-1 needs 24 MOSFETs. On a 20×20 mm board, the copper area, connector ampacity (solder pads and AWG14 to 16 leads), and thermal mass cannot support 4 × 80 A.
- Section 2.4 shows that full-throttle 80 A on all four motors takes only about 2 to 6 seconds to overheat a nano board.
- **Recommendation:** use a 30.5×30.5 mm (or larger) ESC board, or re-spec the rating to "80 A per motor, single-motor transient ≤ 1 s."

**The 80 A rating must say which current it means.** I assume 80 A phase (motor) current. Battery current is I_batt ≈ D·I_phase, so it is lower at partial duty.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM):

- At any instant, two phase legs carry I_ph: one sourcing, one sinking.
- The third leg floats for BEMF zero-cross sensing.
- Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical period: 1/3 as high-side, 1/3 as low-side.

During a high-side active interval with duty D:

- The high-side FET conducts for fraction D.
- The complementary low-side FET conducts for fraction (1−D), after dead time.
- Current flows through exactly one R_ds(on) in the switched leg and one in the static leg at all times.

### 1.2 Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{hot}=k_T\cdot R_{ds(on),25}=1.5\times1.0\,\text{m}\Omega=1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per motor (independent of D, because sync-rect keeps the path always through two channels):

$$P_{cond,motor}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{hot}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Worst-case per-FET average (D → 1, each FET conducting 1/3 of the period):

$$P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I_{ph}^2R_{hot}}{3}=\frac{6400\times0.0015}{3}=\mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The reconciliation between the two figures is that 6 FETs × 3.2 W = 19.2 W. ✓

### 1.3 Self-Consistent Temperature Iteration

R_ds(on) rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. Model the dependence as:

$$R(T_j)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha\approx0.005\,/\text{K}$$

Iterate the loop:

$$T_j = T_{board}+P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta JC}$$

With T_board = 100 °C, it converges at T_j ≈ 105 °C. The real limit is the board temperature, not the junction-to-case drop (see §2).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive, only one leg switches at a time. That leg's pair (HS and LS) incurs the switching losses.

### 2.1 Loss Components

**V-I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{ov}=\tfrac12 V_{bus}I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle):**

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}=Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Gate drive (two switching FETs).** This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.2 Totals at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per motor | × 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.08 W | 0.3 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 20.9 W** | **≈ 83.7 W** |

**Takeaway:** at 80 A, conduction is about 92% of the loss. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs roughly 0.8 W per motor. The far bigger lever is R_ds(on): two FETs in parallel per position halves conduction loss.

### 2.3 DC-Link Capacitor Ripple

Each ESC behaves as a buck stage. The capacitor RMS current is:

$$I_{C,rms}=I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)}\le 0.5\,I_{ph}=40\ \text{A (at }D=0.5)$$

- The four ESC MCUs run unsynchronized PWM, so ripple partially cancels statistically. Do not rely on that.
- Recommended bulk capacitance:
  - 1 × 1000 µF / 35 V low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads.
  - Per motor: ≥ 4 × 10 µF / 50 V X7R 1206, plus 0.1 µF placed at each half-bridge.
- Ceramic loss: with ESR_eff ≈ 2 mΩ, P = 40² × 0.002 = 3.2 W per bank at worst-case D. This is why the electrolytic is mandatory: it absorbs low-frequency ripple and damps ringing.

### 2.4 Thermal Model

**Steady-state (lumped):**

$$T_{board}=T_{amb}+P_{tot}\cdot R_{\theta,BA}$$

For a 30.5 mm board in prop-wash airflow, R_θ,BA ≈ 3 to 5 K/W. At 83.7 W, the steady-state rise would be ΔT ≈ 250 to 420 K, which is impossible. **80 A is therefore strictly a burst rating.**

**Transient (adiabatic, short burst):** use the board thermal capacitance, C_th = m·c_p. Copper is 0.385 J/g·K and FR4 is about 1.1 J/g·K.
