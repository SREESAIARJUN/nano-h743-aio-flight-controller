## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning: the H743 does not commutate the motors

ArduPilot has no native FOC/6-step motor-control path. The supported topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4 × ESC MCU (AM32 firmware)
                                                  └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6 × N-FET
```

| Block | Selection (baseline) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6, LQFP100, 480 MHz | ArduPilot H7 target. DMAMUX gives flexible DMA routing. |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32), ×4 | AM32-supported, bidir DShot, cheap |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap), ×4 | Common AM32 reference, VCC up to 20 V |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN class, ×24 | Only package that fits a nano footprint |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Target class |

### 0.2 Footprint warning (read before the math)

"Nano" (20×20 mm) and "80 A per motor" conflict physically. The math below shows that 80 A is a **1 to 3 second burst rating** on any board this size. It is not a sustained rating. I recommend:

- a 25.5×25.5 or 30.5×30.5 board for an 80 A burst / 45–55 A continuous class, or
- if the 20×20 form factor is mandatory, rating the board at 80 A for **≤1 s** and about 35 A continuous.
