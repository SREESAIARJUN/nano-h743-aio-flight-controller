# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC + Nano FC), Job #32: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Architecture

---

## 0. Scope and Load Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture clarification

On an AIO board the STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC section uses four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), which take DShot600 from the H743. The model below treats the H743 as the DShot master and the ESC MCUs as the gate-drive controllers.

### 0.2 Sanity check on "80A burst"

If 80 A is **per channel**, the board must carry up to 320 A at 6S (about 8 kW) through a 25–30 mm nano PCB. That is beyond what a nano footprint can sustain for more than a few seconds, and the battery pads and bus copper become the limiter before the FETs do.

- I model the worst case: 80 A per channel, all four motors simultaneously.
- Section 3.6 gives the burst-time limit that falls out of it.
- If marketing means 80 A total, divide the conduction losses by 16 (I² scaling at 20 A per channel).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

- At any instant the phase current flows through **exactly two conducting channels**: one high-side-path element and one low-side element.
- On the PWM-switched leg, current alternates between the HS FET (during D) and the LS FET (during 1−D). Both are conducting MOSFET channels, so the resistance in the path is always ≈ 2·R_DS(on).
- Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, which is 1/3 of the period.

### 1.2 Hot R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C using the max value: 1.3 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.89 mΩ**.
Using the typical value: 1.0 × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms,ripple}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}$$

The ripple term is negligible at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (ΔI ≈ a few A pk-pk), so it is dropped.

| Case | Calculation | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Typical, T_j = 125 °C | 2 · 6400 · 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** |
| Worst, T_j = 125 °C | 2 · 6400 · 1.89 mΩ | **24.2 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET average

Each of the six FETs conducts 1/3 of the time, so P_FET,avg = P_cond,ch / 6 ≈ **3.1–4.0 W**.

The **instantaneous** dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 6400 · 1.89 mΩ ≈ **12.1 W**. This matters for Z_θJC at low eRPM (stall or startup), where one FET pair holds current for many milliseconds.

### 1.5 Copper / interconnect loss (frequently forgotten)

Bus plus phase copper on a nano board is roughly 0.3–0.6 mΩ per channel path. At 0.5 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W per channel}$$

The battery pad, lead, and ground-return copper carry 320 A. At 0.2 mΩ that is 320² × 0.2 mΩ = **20.5 W**, which lands right at the XT60/pad area.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched leg per channel at any time. The other active leg is static-on for that 60° sector.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (hard switching, inductive load)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is significant. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Only do so if the gate-driver propagation-delay mismatch allows it.
