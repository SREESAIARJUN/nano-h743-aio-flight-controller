## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant in one 60° sector, exactly two legs carry the full phase current I:

- **Leg A** (PWM leg): the high side Q_AH conducts for duty D. The low side Q_AL conducts for (1 − D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B** (return leg): the low side Q_BL is on continuously.

So the current always flows through exactly two channel resistances in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch}(t) = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \;+\; I^2\,R_{Cu} \;+\; I^2 R_{shunt}
$$

This is independent of D, because synchronous rectification replaces diode conduction except during dead time.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (positive feedback term)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution each of the 6 FETs carries current for 120° (1/3 of the period). The total channel loss 2I²R is therefore shared:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \frac{I^2 R}{3}
$$

**Caveat.** At low eRPM (sector time > ~5 ms) the die sees the full I²R instantaneously, so the **instantaneous** I²R must also be checked against Z_th (§3.4).

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond, ch (2I²R) | Per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 11.52 W | 3.84 W |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 5.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 16.8 W | 5.6 W |

**Copper and shunt contributions:**

- Copper path, about 0.25 mΩ (phase pad → FET → bus, 2 oz): 6400 × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**.
- Low-side shunt, if used (0.25 mΩ): **1.6 W**.
- Many AM32 4-in-1 designs use one shared bus shunt instead, which is mathematically identical per channel during conduction.

**Conduction total per channel @ T_j = 100 °C ≈ 18.6 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off of the HS FET at current I. The LS FET switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS via dead time), so its loss is dominated by body-diode conduction and Q_rr.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
$$

$$
= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30\text{ ns} × 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**Refinement for the Miller plateau.** The effective t_r/t_f are set by the gate charge:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}}
$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3.5 V and R_total = 4.5 Ω, I_g ≈ 1.44 A. With Q_gd + Q_gs2 ≈ 20 nC, t_r ≈ 14 ns, which is consistent with A6.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode recovers when the HS turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}×25.2×48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}
$$

Add the induced HS turn-on loss, roughly Q_rr·V·f again: **≈ 0.10 W total**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} × 635 × 48\text{k} = 0.046\text{ W}
$$

This accounts for the energy discharged in the HS plus the charge of the LS.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 × 80 × 200\text{ ns} × 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (supplied by the driver LDO, not dissipated in the FETs)

$$
P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} × N_{switching} = 50\text{n} × 10 × 48\text{k} × 2 = 0.048\text{ W/ch}
$$

### 2.6 Total switching-related loss per channel

$$
P_{sw,tot} ≈ 1.45 + 0.10 + 0.05 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.2\ W}
$$

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 1.1 W. At 96 kHz it would be about 4.4 W, but motor current ripple would fall:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-2E)\,D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5″ motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at 48 kHz this is about 8 A_pp. The ripple RMS adds ΔI²/12 to the I² terms (~0.4 % here, which is negligible).
