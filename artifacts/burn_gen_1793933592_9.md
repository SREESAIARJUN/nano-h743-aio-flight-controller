# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & Bi-Dir DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Corrections

I don't have a prior spec for "Job #26", so the baseline below is explicit. **Every number marked ⚠ must be replaced with your selected parts' datasheet values before release.**

| Parameter | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full), design for 30 V transients |
| Burst current | 80 A **per channel**, all four channels simultaneously (worst case) |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step trapezoidal |
| MOSFET ⚠ | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS=10 V, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive | 10 V, ~1 A source/sink effective |
| Dead time | 100 ns |
| Ambient | 50 °C (inside frame, early flight) |

Two corrections affect the whole design.

**1. The H743 cannot directly run four BLDC motors.** Four motors need 24 complementary gate signals at 48 kHz with per-channel dead-time and BEMF zero-cross sensing. The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1, TIM8), which provide 6 complementary pairs, enough for two motors.

The architecture is therefore:

- **H743** runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **Four ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) handle commutation and send eRPM telemetry back.

That split is the reason bi-directional DShot exists in this design.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst" are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot physically carry 24 PDFN3.3 FETs plus an H743. Realistic options:

- (a) A stacked 20×20 FC on a 30.5×30.5 ESC.
- (b) A single 30.5×30.5 AIO with 80 A rated for **≤ ~2 s bursts**, as derived in §3.

The math below shows why.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ ⚠$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**. This is the design value.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, exactly two legs carry the phase current I:

- one high-side path (HS during t_on, LS sync-rectifying during t_off of the PWM leg), and
- one low-side FET in the "sinking" leg.

So the instantaneous conducting resistance is always 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET carries current for 120° of 360° electrical (1/3 of the period):

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS(on)} = \tfrac{1}{3}\times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 3.2 W = 19.2 W ✓.

This assumes ideal flat-top current. Real trapezoidal ripple adds roughly:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$$

which is negligible at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance (ΔI_pp ≈ 5–10 A gives < 0.2 % extra).

### 1.3 PCB copper and shunt

Assume ~0.30 mΩ total path resistance (pads, planes, vias, battery pad) per channel ⚠:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\text{m} \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W/ch}$$

**The R_DS(on)–T_j coupling must be solved iteratively.** Start at T_j = T_board, compute losses, compute T_j, update R, and repeat. It converges in 3–4 iterations. If it does not converge, you are in **thermal runaway**, and that is your hard current limit.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg's HS/LS pair switches. The sinking leg's LS is static for the 60° sector.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, HS FET)

Switching times from Miller charge and gate current:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} + t_{ri} \approx 10\,\text{ns} + 5\,\text{ns} = 15\,\text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5\times 25.2\times 80\times 30\text{ns}\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS FET)

Two dead-time intervals occur per switching period:

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2\,t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → dissipated in HS)

$$P_{Qrr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

$$P_{Coss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{nF}\times635\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$
