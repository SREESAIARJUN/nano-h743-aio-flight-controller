# STM32H743 Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #77): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC uses four dedicated ESC MCUs, assumed here to be AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to each ESC over bidirectional DShot600. This document covers:

- the power stage (losses and thermal), in §1–§2,
- the FC↔ESC timer/DMA plan, in §3,
- reference netlists, in §4.

**Treat this as a design model, not verified data.** Component parameters below are representative placeholders. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values and validate on hardware.

**Key finding up front.** Under the assumed parameters, 4 × 80 A on a 20×20 mm board is not thermally sustainable. It survives only a burst of about 1 s. Read §2.4 before committing to the rating.

## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel, 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two conducting MOSFETs in series. During the on-time this is HS(x) + LS(y). During the off-time it is LS(x) freewheeling + LS(y).

So, to first order, conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.6 × 1.5 = 2.4 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Copper trace and shunt losses add to this. The phase plus bus path is roughly 0.3 mΩ, giving 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W**.

**Thermal runaway check.** Because P_cond depends on R(T_j), you must iterate:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta}$$

The solution is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta = I^2\,2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1$$

### 1.2 Switching Losses (only the PWM-ed leg switches)

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,2t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs per PWM cycle, dissipated mostly in the driver):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 0.03\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal: P_sw ≈ 2.65 W.** This scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz would save about 1.3 W per channel.

### 1.3 Totals

| Conduction (FETs) | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |

**Efficiency check.** Assume roughly 75 % effective duty at burst, so P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.75 × 80 ≈ 1.5 kW per motor. That gives η ≈ 97.7 %. The efficiency is fine; the problem is the heat density.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Per-FET Load

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the six FETs in one channel:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 35.3/6 \approx 5.9\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous load on a conducting FET is about 15.4 W.

At 30k eRPM, the electrical period is 2 ms. This is comparable to the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms), so add ripple:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx P_{pk}\,Z_{\theta JC}(t_{on}) \approx 15.4 \times 0.5 \approx 8\ \text{K}$$

Junction rise above case:

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 5.9 \times 1.5 + 8 \approx \mathbf{17\ K}$$

### 2.2 Steady State (Board to Air)

Assume propwash convection h ≈ 80 W/m²K over two board faces, A = 8×10⁻⁴ m²:

$$R_{\theta CA} = \frac{1}{hA} = 15.6\ \text{K/W}$$

With 141 W, the steady-state rise would be about 2200 K. **No steady-state operating point exists at 80 A.**

The maximum continuous total loss for an 80 °C board rise is:

$$P_{max} = 80/15.6 \approx 5.1\ \text{W}$$

Solving for current:

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{5.1/4 - P_{sw}'}{2R}}$$

This gives roughly **12–15 A continuous per motor** for an unheatsunk 20×20 board. That figure is consistent with real nano-class ESC ratings.
