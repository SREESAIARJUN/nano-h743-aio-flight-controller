**Architectural correction.** ArduPilot on the H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 stage requires dedicated ESC MCUs that run the 48 kHz PWM and send bidirectional DShot telemetry back:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──► 4× FD6288Q gate driver ──► 24× N-MOSFET
```

**Bottom line.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is a **seconds-scale burst rating**. It is thermally capacitance-limited, not steady-state capable (see §3).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
With complementary PWM at duty D during one 60° commutation step, the active current path is:
- High-side FET of the driven leg: conducts for D.
- Low-side FET of the driven leg: conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- Low-side FET of the return leg: conducts 100%.

So at any instant, current flows through exactly **two** channels in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D)}$$

Each FET conducts during 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Electrical frequency is about 3 kHz at 25k RPM with 7 pole pairs, which is far faster than the die thermal time constant (ms). The time-averaged loss is therefore valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ESC}}{6}\ \text{(avg)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence and ripple

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

Ripple at the worst case (D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ \text{A}$$

The ripple term is ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is 0.06% of 6400 A². It is negligible, which is one benefit of 48 kHz.
