# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Constraints and Design Flags

Three issues should be settled before layout.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or the STM32G071. The two halves of this document therefore cover two separate processor domains:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control | STM32H743 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot, DShot600 TX/RX via TIM+DMA |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 + 3-phase gate driver + 6 FETs | 48 kHz PWM, commutation, telemetry |

**2. "Nano" plus 4×80 A burst is thermally marginal.** On a 20×20 mm board, the model in §2 gives roughly 100 W of total dissipation with all four channels at 80 A. That is a few seconds of adiabatic headroom, not a sustained rating. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC board stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC. Alternatively, define the 80 A figure as a single-channel 10-second burst.

**3. The input supply is extreme.** At full throttle, I_bat ≈ D·I_phase per channel. That reaches about 320 A at 4×80 A, D≈1, which far exceeds XT60 and XT90 connectors and typical 6S packs. This is a real constraint on the burst specification, not just a connector-selection detail.

**All device parameters below are stated assumptions** representative of 40 V, 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm) trench MOSFETs. Replace them with the datasheet values of the selected part. Every equation is written so you can substitute directly.

## 2. Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The model assumes 120° trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At every instant, exactly two FETs carry the full phase current:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side FET for (1−D)·T_sw. Either way, one FET carries I.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the 120° interval.

This gives the per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Each leg is active 2/3 of the electrical period. The thermally averaged per-leg loss (valid when f_elec ≫ 1/τ_th,die, i.e. above about 200 Hz electrical) is therefore:

$$P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{1}{3}P_{cond,ch}$$

Copper and shunt loss adds:

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$
