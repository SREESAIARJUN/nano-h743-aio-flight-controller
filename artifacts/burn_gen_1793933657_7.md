# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model (Job #52)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

Two architectural points shape the rest of this model.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 4-in-1 ESC has four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). These run the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 only generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. The power-stage math (sections 1–3) belongs to the ESC side. The DMA/timer math (section 4) belongs to the FC side.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm board) is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows it is thermally limited to about 1–2 s. Any datasheet that implies otherwise is marketing.

**Representative MOSFET** (substitute your actual datasheet values; the equations are general):

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005 /°C.

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ** (worst case).

### 1.2 Six-step synchronous commutation

At any instant, two phase legs carry motor current I. With complementary (synchronous) rectification, exactly one FET per active leg conducts, either the high-side during t_on or the low-side during t_off. Duty cycle therefore cancels out of conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 \left(R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\right)$$

R_Cu is the copper/solder path per leg. Assume 0.3 mΩ for a nano board with 2 oz inner layers.

| Term | Value |
|---|---|
| FET: 2 × 80² × 1.8 mΩ | 23.04 W |
| Copper: 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 3.84 W |
| **P_cond per channel** | **26.9 W** |
