# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis & Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

**Scope and assumptions.** I don't have your Job #48 BOM or layout files. Every component value below is a representative assumption, flagged as such, chosen to match typical 30×30 mm-class AIO stacks. The equations are general: substitute your datasheet values and the conclusions will update.

**Architecture.** This assumes the standard architecture:

- The **STM32H743 runs ArduPilot** and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- A **separate ESC MCU** (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or EFM8/ARM BLHeli) does commutation and generates the 48 kHz PWM.
- The H743 never drives gate drivers directly. If your design deviates from this, the timer section changes substantially.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.2 × 1.51 = **1.81 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° electrical sector, current flows through two legs. Consider a high-side FET of phase A paired with the low-side FET of phase B:

- **PWM on (fraction D):** current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- **PWM off (fraction 1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (sync-rect) and LS_B.

In both states, two channels are in the path. Per motor, independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This assumes flat-top current, which is reasonable at high load with adequate motor inductance. For ripple current, replace I² with I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12.

### 2.3 Distribution per FET

Each FET conducts during 1/3 to 2/3 of the electrical cycle depending on its role. Averaged over a revolution, the loss spreads approximately evenly over the 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6}$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is I²·R_ds. Because commutation is fast (at 30k eRPM a 60° sector lasts ~0.33 ms), die thermal ripple is small. Use the average value for junction estimates and the peak value for SOA checks.
