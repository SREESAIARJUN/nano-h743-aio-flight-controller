# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #31): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The ArduPilot-class STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The 48 kHz switching is done by four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to them.

| Parameter | Value / Assumption |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V max (worst case for switching) |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor channel, 4 channels simultaneously (worst case) |
| PWM | 48 kHz, synchronous rectification, 6-step (trapezoidal) |
| MOSFET (placeholder) | 40 V, R_DS(on),25 = 0.9 mΩ @ V_GS=10 V, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, E_oss = 0.3 µJ, V_SD = 0.8 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 |
| FET count | 6 per ESC, 24 total |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (e.g. FD6288Q class), ~1.5 A source / 2 A sink |
| ESC MCU | STM32G071 / AT32F421 class (AM32-compatible) |
| PCB | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz inner/outer power planes |

**Substitute real datasheet values before release.** The numbers below are a parametric model and must be re-run with the selected FET.

**Form-factor warning:** As shown in §3, 4×80 A on a 20×20 "nano" footprint is not thermally credible beyond a sub-second burst. I have modelled the 30.5×30.5 board, which is itself burst-limited.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This corresponds to a normalized R_DS(on) of about 1.6× at 125 °C.

At a design point of T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 0.9 \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.36\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two legs are active:
- **Active leg:** HS on for duty D, complementary LS on for (1−D). One FET always carries I.
- **Return leg:** LS fully on. One FET carries I.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2\left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS}\right] + I^2 R_{pcb} \approx 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{pcb}$$

With I = 80 A:

$$P_{FET,cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.36\times10^{-3} = 17.4\ \text{W per ESC}$$

**PCB copper path** (pad, via, plane, motor pad), assuming ≈ 0.15 mΩ effective:

$$P_{pcb} = 6400 \times 0.15\times10^{-3} \approx 0.96\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° (1/3 of the electrical period):

$$P_{LS,return} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R \approx 2.9\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R + P_{sw}/3$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The effective t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns comes from the Miller plateau: Q_gd ≈ 12 nC at ~1.5 A gate drive gives ~8 ns, plus di/dt limiting from loop inductance.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, dead-time, and gate drive

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.048 W |
| Coss | E_oss · f = 0.3 µJ × 48 k | 0.014 W |
| Dead-time diode (t_dt = 50 ns, 2 edges) | V_SD · I · 2 t_dt · f = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive (in driver IC, not FET) | Q_g V_GS f × 2 active = 60 nC × 10 × 48 k × 2 | 0.058 W |

**Dead-time insight:** Dead-time conduction scales linearly with t_dt. Minimize it in AM32 (typically 20–40 ns). Always verify there is no shoot-through on a scope at temperature.

### 2.3 Loss summary at 80 A burst

| Loss | Per ESC | ×4 Board |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.4 W | 69.6 W |
| PCB copper | 0.96 W | 3.8 W |
| Switching (V·I) | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Q_rr + Coss | 0.06 W | 0.25 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 W | 1.2 W |
| Gate driver | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 20.2 W** | **≈ 81 W** |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so η_stage ≈ 99%. The losses are small relative to throughput, but large relative to the board's ability to dissipate them.

**Design lever:** Conduction is 86% of the loss. Halving R_DS(on) (parallel FETs or 5×6 parts) beats any switching-frequency optimization. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.9 W per ESC.
