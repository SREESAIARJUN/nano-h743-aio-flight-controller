# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Mathematical Model, DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points affect the whole design, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In any practical 4-in-1 design, each phase bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are AT32F421, STM32G071, or one MCU per motor. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz switching model below applies to the ESC MCU/driver/FET domain.
2. **"80 A on a nano board" is a burst rating, not continuous.** The model below shows that at 80 A per channel the board dissipates about 80 W. A 20×20 or 30×30 board cannot reject that steadily. The model therefore produces a **burst duration limit**, which is what the rating should be specified against.
3. **Component values are representative placeholders.** They are chosen for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ PDFN5×6 MOSFET class. Substitute the exact datasheet values before sign-off. Every equation is parametric so you can re-run it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] = 0.90\,(1+0.005\cdot 85) = 1.28\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Current path during one commutation sector

In trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) rectification:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET carries the freewheel current for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for 100 % of the sector.

So there are always **two FET channels in series** with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\cdot 80^2\cdot 1.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

The per-FET split matters for T_j:

| FET | Loss | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Return-leg low side (worst FET) | I²R | 8.2 W |
| PWM-leg high side | D·I²R | — |
| PWM-leg low side | (1−D)·I²R | — |

### 2.3 Copper and interconnect loss

$$R_{cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t},\quad \rho = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

A 15 mm × 10 mm pour in 2 oz (70 µm) copper gives R = 0.37 mΩ per layer. With four paralleled layers this is about 0.09 mΩ. Add solder, via, and pad resistance and budget **R_cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path**:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400\cdot 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W/ch}$$

### 2.4 Shunt loss (if per-channel sensing is fitted)

With a 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying full current, P = 1.6 W. Most 4-in-1 designs use **one shared bus shunt** instead. That shunt carries the sum of all four channel currents, not 4×80 A simultaneously in practice. Budget it separately.
