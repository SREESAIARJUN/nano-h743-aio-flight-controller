## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

These points must be settled before the math is meaningful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and drives the gate driver. The H743 only emits DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM/EDT replies. The switching-loss model applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst on a nano board" is a thermal claim to be proven, not assumed.** The math below shows it is credible only as a bounded-duration burst on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm ESC with a heat spreader. On a 20 × 20 mm board, the FET footprint area alone does not support it. I baseline the 30.5 mm ESC stacked under the H743 FC and flag 20 × 20 as non-compliant (§2.7).
3. **No MOSFET was specified.** I use a representative 40 V-class part and keep every equation parametric, so a datasheet can be substituted directly.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step synchronous drive, at any instant exactly two phase legs carry current I:

- One leg PWMs: its high-side FET conducts during D and its low-side FET (synchronous rectification) during 1−D.
- The opposite leg's low-side FET is held on.

The current therefore always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**, independent of D (ignoring dead time).

### 1.2 Conduction loss

R_DS(on) is temperature dependent:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \mathrm{K^{-1}} \Rightarrow k_T(125^\circ\mathrm{C}) = 1.6$$

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.6\ \mathrm{m\Omega}) = \mathbf{20.48\ W}$$

Copper and shunt losses add to this:

$$P_{cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(0.25\,\mathrm{m\Omega}) = 3.20\ \mathrm{W}$$

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400(0.2\,\mathrm{m\Omega}) = 1.28\ \mathrm{W}$$
