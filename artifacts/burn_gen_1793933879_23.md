### Feasibility warning (read before layout)

At 80 A on four channels simultaneously, the board carries **up to 320 A of phase current**. The model below shows about **90 W of total dissipation**. This has three consequences:

1. **A 20×20 mm "nano" stack cannot do this.** It lacks the copper cross-section for 80 A DC entry (~3 mm² per 80 A path minimum) and the space for 24 power MOSFETs in 5×6 packages. Realistic options:
   - a 30.5/36 mm 4-in-1 ESC stacked under a 20×20 or 30.5 FC; or
   - re-rate the target to about 80 A *total* burst (20 A per motor) on a 20×20 board.
2. **80 A is a seconds-long burst rating**, not a continuous one. The thermal analysis in §3 bounds the duration at roughly 5–6 s.
3. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each ESC channel needs its own controller (e.g., AT32F421/STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those controllers. This document covers both sides.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct. Take the case where phase A is PWM-driven high and phase B is held low:

- During t_on (fraction D): current flows through Q_AH, then the motor, then Q_BL.
- During t_off (fraction 1−D): current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous), then the motor, then Q_BL.

So at any instant, current I flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels in series, regardless of D. Ignoring dead time, conduction loss per ESC channel is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2 I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\cdot k_T = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.2 Including PCB copper

Each path (bus+ entry, high-side, phase pad, low-side, bus− return) adds resistance. Use an effective value of R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per series path at 100 °C (0.39 %/K copper tempco). Validate this with a DC IR-drop solver (e.g., Altium PDN / SIwave).

$$
P_{cond,total} = I^2\,(2R_{ds,hot} + R_{cu})
$$
