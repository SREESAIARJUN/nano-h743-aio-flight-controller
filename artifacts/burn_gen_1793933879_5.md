## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

Several decisions and assumptions shape every number in this package.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 running AM32, or an equivalent BLHeli_32-class part). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs and decodes eRPM telemetry. The STM32H743 has two advanced timers, which is not enough for 4×6 complementary FOC/6-step PWM plus flight control. That approach is not recommended.
2. **Form factor.** I assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm 4-in-1 ESC stacked under a 20 × 20 or 30.5 mm FC. The thermal results in §3 show that a true 20 × 20 board cannot sustain 4 × 80 A for more than about 2 s.
3. **Bus voltage.** 6S LiPo: 25.2 V maximum and 22.2 V nominal. All worst-case calculations use 25.2 V.
4. **MOSFETs.** I used generic parameters for a 40 V, 5×6 DFN-class device, listed below. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; every equation is parameterized.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| On-resistance @25 °C (max) | R₂₅ | 1.0 mΩ |
| Rds(on) temp. coefficient | α | 0.0065 /K (→1.65× at 125 °C) |
| Total gate charge @10 V | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Gate-source (post-threshold) | Q_gs2 | 5 nC |
| Gate-drain (Miller) | Q_gd | 10 nC |
| Miller plateau | V_pl | 3.5 V |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output energy @25 V | E_oss | 0.47 µJ |
| Body diode V_SD @80 A | V_SD | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case | R_θJC | 1.2 K/W |
| Internal gate R | R_g,int | 1.5 Ω |

**Gate driver:** 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288Q class), with V_drv = 10 V, external gate resistor R_g,ext = 10 Ω on turn-on, and an effective 5 Ω on turn-off (diode-bypassed).

**PWM:** f_sw = 48 kHz, with complementary synchronous rectification and dead time t_dt = 100 ns.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active. Current flows through:
- the PWM-ing leg, either the HS FET (duty D) or the LS FET (1−D, synchronous), and
- the static return leg, whose LS FET is on for the whole 60° sector.

So exactly **two FET channels carry I_ph at all times**, apart from dead time:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) \cdot (1 - 2 t_{dt} f_{sw})
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent Rds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.7 W | 50.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ | 18.9 W | 75.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ | 20.9 W | 83.6 W |

The dead-time factor (1 − 2·100 ns·48 kHz) = 0.9904 is applied.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (averaged over an electrical cycle)

Each FET is in the static-return role for 1/3 of the cycle and in the PWM role for 1/3 of the cycle:

$$
P_{HS} = I^2R\left(\tfrac{1}{3}D + \tfrac{1}{3}\right),\quad
P_{LS} = I^2R\left(\tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3}\right)
$$

The worst case is D → 1 (full throttle burst). At 125 °C this gives:
- HS ≈ (6400 × 1.65 mΩ) × 2/3 = **7.04 W**
- LS ≈ 3.52 W

**The HS FET is the thermal limiter.**

### 1.5 PCB copper losses

These are often forgotten and are significant at 320 A total.

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,L}{w\,t},\quad \rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]
$$

The battery pad to bus is a 6-layer, 2 oz copper pour, with 3 layers paralleled for VBAT and 3 for GND (t_eff = 210 µm), w = 20 mm, L = 15 mm:

- At 100 °C: R ≈ 0.074 mΩ per rail, 0.148 mΩ for the loop.
- At 320 A: P ≈ **15.2 W**.

Phase traces (L = 8 mm, w = 4 mm, t_eff = 210 µm) are about 0.20 mΩ each. With 2 phases conducting at 80 A, that is about 2.6 W per channel, or **10.4 W total**.

> **Takeaway:** Copper adds about 25 W at 4 × 80 A. Use 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, place the battery pads centrally, and add a bus capacitor bank on each channel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Transition times (gate-charge method)

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,src}},\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,snk}}
$$

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}}
$$

Assuming driver output impedances of R_src ≈ R_snk ≈ 0:
- I_g,on = 6.5 V / 11.5 Ω = 0.565 A, so **t_on = 26.5 ns**
- I_g,off = 3.5 V / 6.5 Ω = 0.538 A, so **t_off = 27.9 ns**
