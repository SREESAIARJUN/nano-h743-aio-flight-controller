## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Several points in the request need clarifying, because they change the model.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 design, each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51. The H743 runs ArduPilot and only emits DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz figure is an ESC firmware setting (`PWM_FREQUENCY = 48`).
2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** I model **80 A per motor, 6S (25.2 V full charge), for a burst of ≤ 5 s**. The results show this is not thermally credible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. It is marginal on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. Section 4 quantifies both cases.
3. **Assumed MOSFET [A]:** 40 V N-channel, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN8.
   - R_DS(on) = 1.3 mΩ at 25 °C, temperature coefficient ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C
   - Q_g = 45 nC, Q_gd = 10 nC, Q_rr = 60 nC at 80 A
   - Q_oss = 40 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V
   - R_θJC = 1.2 K/W
   - Six FETs per motor (one per switch position), 24 FETs in total.
4. **Assumed gate driver [A]:** 3-phase driver (e.g. FD6288Q class), 12 V gate drive, about 1 A source / 1.5 A sink. Dead time t_dt = 100 ns.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.3 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.95 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant the current flows through two phase legs:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D. With complementary (synchronous) switching, the low side conducts for 1 − D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side conducts continuously during that 60° sector.

Total conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$

This result is independent of duty, a useful property of synchronous six-step drive.

Each FET is active for one third of the electrical period, so the average loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

Within a single electrical cycle (milliseconds, far shorter than the thermal time constant), the peak instantaneous loss in one FET is I²R_DS.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I² R_DS (one device) | 80² × 1.95 mΩ = **12.5 W** (instantaneous) |
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 12.5 = **25.0 W** |
| P_cond per FET (average) | **4.16 W** |
| P_cond for 4 motors | **100 W** |

### 1.4 Copper and shunt conduction (often underestimated)

- **Current shunt [A]:** 0.25 mΩ single shunt per ESC. 80² × 0.25 m = 1.6 W per motor.
- **Phase pads and traces:** assume 0.3 mΩ effective per active path. 80² × 0.3 m × 2 legs = 3.8 W per motor.
- **Battery bus:** carries 4 × 80 = 320 A total. With 0.1 mΩ of pad plus plane, loss is 320² × 0.1 m = **10.2 W**.

These losses argue for 2 oz or 3 oz copper on at least four layers. The battery bus needs heavy direct-solder pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg is hard-switched each PWM cycle: the high-side switch of the PWM leg, with its complementary low side acting as the synchronous rectifier.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard turn-on and turn-off)

Gate plateau voltage V_pl ≈ 4.5 V [A]. With a 12 V drive and R_g,total ≈ 6 Ω:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{dr}-V_{pl}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}\cdot 6}{7.5} = 8\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}R_g}{V_{pl}} = 13\ \text{ns}$$

Add Q_gs2 and layout inductance. Use t_r + t_f ≈ **30 ns** in practice.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the synchronous FET's body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\times40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor. Only do this if shoot-through margin is verified on the scope.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = 2\times45\text{n}\times12\times48\text{k} = 0.052\ \text{W}$$
