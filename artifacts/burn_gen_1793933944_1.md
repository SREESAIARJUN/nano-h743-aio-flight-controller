# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Model

**Job #59: Architecture and Analysis Package, Rev A**

> **Scope note: the H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every practical H743 AIO design, the 48 kHz PWM and 6-step commutation run on four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600.
>
> Asking ArduPilot to directly generate 24 complementary gate signals with dead-time and back-EMF sensing is not supported and would be a poor architecture. This package assumes the standard split.

> **About the numbers:** MOSFET and board parameters below are representative assumptions for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN-class device. Substitute your actual datasheet values. The equations are general; the conclusions depend on the inputs.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### Current path
In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- **During the on-time (D):** HS(phase A) → winding → LS(phase B)
- **During the off-time (1−D):** LS(phase A) freewheels → winding → LS(phase B)

### Per-FET conduction loss

$$P_{cond,HS} = I_m^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,D \qquad P_{cond,LS,A} = I_m^2 R_{DS}(T_j)(1-D) \qquad P_{cond,LS,B} = I_m^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ /\mathrm{K}$$

### Per-channel total (any D)

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_m^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### Hottest FET (HS, PWM phase)

$$80^2 \cdot 1.5\text{m} \cdot 0.9 = \mathbf{8.64\ W}$$

The continuously-on LS(B) carries 9.6 W during its 120° conduction window. Averaged over an electrical cycle, it runs about 3.2 W because commutation rotates the roles among the six switches.

### Copper loss (do not omit)
Phase and bus traces at about 0.3 mΩ per channel add another 1.9 W:

$$I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_g} = \frac{7}{7.2} = 0.97\,\text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_g} = \frac{3}{4.7} = 0.64\,\text{A}$$

Transition times:

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 12.4\,\text{ns} \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 18.8\,\text{ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_m (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(31.2\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.51\ W}$$

### 3.2 Remaining switching terms

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | 2 · V_f · I_m · t_dt · f_sw | 0.61 W (in LS) |
| Output capacitance | C_oss · V_bus² · f_sw | 0.046 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 2.24 W** |

### 3.3 Sensitivity
P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel, but costs:
- more acoustic noise,
- larger current ripple,
- worse low-throttle resolution.
