## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design the bridges are commutated by dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071, or one MCU per pair of channels. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. Accordingly:

- The **48 kHz switching-loss model** belongs to the ESC MCU's PWM.
- The **DMA/timer allocation** belongs to the H743.

**80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25×25 mm) board is not a continuous rating.** The math below shows it is a ~2–4 second thermal-capacity event if all four channels are loaded together. I state this explicitly so the spec sheet doesn't promise something the copper can't deliver.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation, at any instant current flows through exactly two legs: the PWM'd leg and the low-side return leg.

- During PWM **on** time (duty D): high-side Q_H(x) and low-side Q_L(y) conduct.
- During **off** time (with synchronous rectification): low-side Q_L(x) and Q_L(y) conduct.

Either way, **two R_ds(on) are always in series with the phase current.**

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}
$$

With temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

The coefficient α ≈ 0.0053 K⁻¹ fits the ×1.45 factor at 110 °C. This makes the model **positive-feedback**: hotter junctions mean higher resistance, which means more heat. You must iterate it rather than evaluate it once.

**At 80 A, T_j = 110 °C:**

$$
P_{FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W}
$$

**Copper path** (bus plane → bridge → phase pad, plus return): estimated R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ on 2 oz with short paths.

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.6\ \text{W}
$$

**Distribution across the six FETs.** Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, so averaged over a revolution:

$$
\bar P_{FET,i} \approx \frac{20.5}{6} \approx 3.4\ \text{W}
$$

This averaging holds above roughly 50 erpm-Hz, where the electrical period is far shorter than the package thermal time constant (~10–50 ms). It does **not** hold at stall or startup. There, one FET pair carries the full current, so desync and stall protection limits are thermally critical.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. Its complementary low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like). Its loss shows up instead as dead-time and Q_rr terms.

### 2.1 Gate-current-limited transition times (Miller plateau)

$$
t_{rise} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{out}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int})}{V_{drv}-V_{pl}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}\times 16\,\Omega}{9\,\text{V}} \approx 18\ \text{ns}
$$

$$
t_{fall} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{out}+R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int})}{V_{pl}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}\times 16\,\Omega}{3\,\text{V}} \approx 53\ \text{ns}
$$

Turn-off is the dominant edge.

### 2.2 Loss components (per channel, 80 A, 25.2 V)

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·71 ns·48 k | **3.44 W** |
| Dead-time diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2·0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs active) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.07 W (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 4.8 W** |
