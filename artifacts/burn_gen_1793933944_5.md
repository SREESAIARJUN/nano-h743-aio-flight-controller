## 0. Architectural Ground Truths and Red Flags

These points constrain everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no native FOC or 6-step ESC firmware. The H743 sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A is the dominant risk.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per motor is not physically supportable, either thermally or in copper cross-section. This package assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power** stack. Even there, 80 A is a **≤1–2 s burst rating**, not continuous. The math below shows why.
3. **4 × 80 A = 320 A simultaneous** exceeds any XT60/XT90 lead and most 6S packs. Treat "80 A" as a **per-channel, non-simultaneous** burst figure.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path topology (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous/complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector, two phases conduct:
- **Leg A** (PWM leg): the high-side conducts for duty D, and the low-side of the same leg (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D.
- **Leg B:** the low-side is held on for the entire sector.

At every instant, **exactly two MOSFET channels are in series with the motor current**, independent of D (neglecting dead time). Therefore:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

Each MOSFET carries current for 120° of 360° electrical, so the average loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

### 1.2 Numerical evaluation at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond / motor | P_cond / FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.56 W |
| 100 °C | 1.65 mΩ | 21.1 W | 3.52 W |
| 125 °C | 1.80 mΩ | **23.0 W** | **3.84 W** |

| Element | Model | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Current shunt | I²·0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Phase copper (≈0.15 mΩ per path, 2 paths) | 2·I²·0.15 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| Bus copper share | ≈0.1 mΩ effective | 0.64 W |
| **Subtotal (non-FET)** | | **≈4.2 W** |

### 1.4 Electrothermal runaway criterion

Let P₀ be the conduction loss at 25 °C and R_th the junction-to-ambient thermal resistance. The steady state is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P_0\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j - 25 = \frac{(T_a-25) + R_{th}P_0}{1-\alpha R_{th} P_0}$$

**Stability requires α·R_th·P₀ < 1.**

Per FET: P₀ = 2.56 W, α = 0.005. Runaway occurs when R_th ≥ 78 K/W. A DFN3.3 on 2 oz copper with via arrays reaches roughly 25–35 K/W junction-to-ambient. That is stable, but a 2–3× gain multiplier applies:

$$\frac{1}{1-\alpha R_{th}P_0} = \frac{1}{1-0.005\cdot30\cdot2.56} = 1.62$$

The temperature rise is therefore **62% higher than a naive constant-R_DS estimate**.
