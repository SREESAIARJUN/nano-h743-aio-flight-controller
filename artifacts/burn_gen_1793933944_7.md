## 0. Architecture Clarification (Important)

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware (e.g., STM32G071 or AT32F421). That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

```
 6S LiPo ──┬── Bulk caps ── Shunt ── 4× [3-phase bridge (6 FET)] ── Motors 1-4
           │                              ▲
           │                    4× ESC MCU + gate driver
           │                              ▲  bidir DShot600 (1-wire)
           └── BEC 5V/3V3 ── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

**"80A burst" interpretation:** this model uses **80 A total battery current, i.e., 20 A per channel**. As Section 2.6 shows, 80 A *per channel* is physically incompatible with a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm nano footprint.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction Losses (6-step, complementary/synchronous PWM)

Each 60° electrical sector has three conduction roles:
- One high-side FET is PWM'd at duty D.
- Its complementary low-side conducts as a synchronous rectifier for (1−D).
- One low-side on another leg is continuously ON.

Each leg is active for 1/3 of the electrical period in each role. Averaging over a full electrical cycle for one leg:

$$
P_{leg} = I_m^2 R_{DS,hot}\left[\tfrac{D}{3} + \tfrac{1-D}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}\right] = \tfrac{2}{3} I_m^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

Summing three legs gives the key result: **two FETs are always in the current path**, independent of D.

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_m^2\, R_{DS,hot}}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2(25)^2(0.00232) = \mathbf{2.90\ W}, \qquad P_{cond,4ch} = \mathbf{11.6\ W}
$$

The **per-FET average is ≈ 0.48 W**. The always-ON low-side carries the most in its sector, so peaks are higher.

**Ripple correction (optional):** with phase inductance L and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI, replace I_m² with:

$$
I_m^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\, D}{L\, f_{sw}}
$$

For low-L racing motors at 48 kHz this adds about 1–5 %.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; one hard-switched transition pair per period.

| Mechanism | Equation | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I_m (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½·25.2·25·22 ns·48k = **0.333 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| Output capacitance | E_oss f_sw | 0.30 µJ·48k = **0.014 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD I_m (2 t_dead) f_sw | 0.8·25·200 ns·48k = **0.192 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g V_GS f_sw ×2 | 30 nC·10·48k·2 = **0.029 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 0.60 W/ch → 2.4 W (4 ch)** |

Design levers:
- **Dead-time:** diode loss scales linearly with dead-time. Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.1 W per channel. Verify this against shoot-through margin at minimum V_GS.
- **Switching frequency:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.3 W per channel, but audible noise and current ripple increase.
