```
       │   └──────────────┘          └────────┘          └───────┘    └───────┘  │   └───────┘
       │      2–8 kHz                   1 kHz              2 kHz        4 kHz    │     20 kHz
                                                                  i_q,cog ───────┘ (anti-cogging FF)
```

Two rules keep the cascade stable:

- **Bandwidth separation.** Each outer loop should be at least 4–5× slower than the loop inside it: ω_c : ω_v : ω_p ≈ 2π·(1500 : 120 : 25) rad/s.
- **Sample rate.** Each loop rate f_s should be at least 10× its crossover frequency, so ZOH and computation delay stay under about 20° of phase.

## 2. Field-Oriented Control (Inner Current Loop, T_c = 50 µs)

### 2.1 Electrical angle with delay compensation

Raw angle from the encoder count:

$$\theta_m = \frac{2\pi}{2^{14}}\,\text{cnt} - \theta_{off,m},\qquad \theta_e = p\,\theta_m \bmod 2\pi$$

The angle used for the inverse Park transform must lead by the expected rotation during the delay. The delay is the ADC-to-PWM-update time (1 T_c) plus half a PWM period (0.5 T_c):

$$\theta_e^{\,inv} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_c$$

### 2.2 Clarke and Park transforms

Amplitude-invariant Clarke transform, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0:

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

### 2.3 Plant model and decoupling

The motor in the rotating frame (surface PMSM, L_d = L_q = L):

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$
