## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the request need correcting before any numbers are meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware; it has no BLDC commutation, BEMF zero-cross detection, or gate-drive code. In every production "H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC" design, the H743 sends **bidirectional DShot600** to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 targets. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not the H743. The architecture below follows that split.

2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** In this document it means **80 A RMS phase current per channel**, for ≤ 3 s, near full duty (D ≈ 0.95).
   - At partial throttle, phase current exceeds battery current: I_batt ≈ D · I_phase.
   - All four channels at 80 A simultaneously would mean about 300 A from the pack. That is a pack and wiring problem, not just an ESC one.

3. **"Nano" form factor (20×20 or 30×30) cannot sustain 80 A/channel.** The math in §3 shows this is strictly a **transient, heat-capacity-limited rating**. Continuous capability is roughly 25–35 A/channel depending on airflow.

**All MOSFET and driver parameters below are representative placeholders for a 40 V, DFN5×6, single-FET-per-switch design. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology state

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any 60° sector:
- **Leg A** (PWM leg): high side on for D, synchronous low side on for (1 − D).
- **Leg B** (return leg): low side held on continuously.
- **Leg C**: floating; used for BEMF sensing.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 0.90 × 1.45 = **1.305 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Instantaneous per-switch conduction losses

$$P_{HS,A} = D\,I^2 R_{ds} = 0.95 \times 6400 \times 1.305\text{m} = 7.93\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{LS,A} = (1-D)\,I^2 R_{ds} = 0.42\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{LS,B} = I^2 R_{ds} = 8.35\ \text{W}$$

Total semiconductor conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2 I^2 R_{ds} = 16.70\ \text{W}\quad(\text{independent of } D)$$

### 1.4 Copper loss

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.30\text{m} = 1.92\ \text{W}$$

The R_cu figure assumes a 2 oz copper phase path, paralleled over four layers, about 5 squares long. Copper has its own temperature coefficient of about 0.0039 /K; include it in the iteration of §3.4.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check

Conduction loss rises with junction temperature through R_ds, which raises temperature further. The loop is stable only if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta,\text{total}} < 1$$

- ∂P/∂T ≈ 2I²R_ds,25·α = 2 × 6400 × 0.9m × 0.006 = **0.069 W/K per channel**.
- With board-level R_θ ≈ 6 K/W shared across four channels, the loop gain is 4 × 0.069 × 6 ≈ **1.66, which is greater than 1.**

**Conclusion: at 80 A on all channels, steady state has no stable solution.** The design is thermally runaway-limited, and firmware current limiting (AM32 current limit plus ArduPilot `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX`) is a safety requirement, not an option.
