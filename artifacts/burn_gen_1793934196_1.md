### Architecture Clarification (Important)

ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the **flight controller**. The ESC commutation itself should run on **four dedicated ESC MCUs**, typically STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to them. Running four sensorless BLDC control loops at 48 kHz on the H743 *alongside* ArduPilot is not a supported configuration. This package assumes the FC + 4×ESC-MCU partition.

### Red-Flag Feasibility Notice

"Nano" (20×20 mm) combined with 80 A per motor is at or beyond physical limits. Two issues dominate:

- **Peak battery current:** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at full duty. At this level, battery leads, pads, and connectors become the dominant failure mode.
- **Thermal mass:** As Section 3 shows, the board heats at roughly 50 K/s at full burst. Treat 80 A as a **sub-second to ~1.5 s transient rating**, not a sustained one. Section 3.5 lists what to change if you need more margin.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS ≈ 0.80 × 1.6 = **1.28 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current Path in 6-Step Commutation

At any instant, two phase legs are active:

- One leg is PWM'd (HS on for D, LS on for 1−D under synchronous rectification).
- The other leg has its LS held on.
- The third leg floats (BEMF sensing).

The motor current therefore always flows through **exactly two conducting FETs** (one per active leg, ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

Here R_Cu is the PCB copper path from the bus to the phase pad and back. For a nano board, budget about **0.3 mΩ round-trip**.

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each leg is driven high for 120° and low for 120° per electrical cycle, and floats for 120°. Near full throttle (D → 1):

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per FET (hot) | 80² × 1.28 mΩ / 3 | **2.73 W** |
| Per motor (6 FETs) | 80² × 2 × 1.28 mΩ | **16.38 W** |
| Copper, per motor | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | **1.92 W** |
| **Conduction total, 4 motors** | | **73.2 W** |

Note that copper loss is about 10% of FET loss. On a nano board, pour copper (4–6 layers, 2 oz outer) and use via-stitched bus planes. Copper is not a negligible term.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the **PWM'd leg** hard-switches. One HS/LS pair per motor sees transitions at any time.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (dominant term)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (LS body diode, after dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not in the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Summary

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 W | 5.80 W |
| Coss | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| Qrr | 0.04 W | 0.14 W |
| Dead time | 0.31 W | 1.23 W |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| **Switching total** | **1.89 W** | **7.55 W** |

**Design insight:** At 48 kHz, switching loss is only about 10% of conduction loss. Lowering R_DS(on) helps far more than faster edges. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 7.5 W total, but it would worsen EMI and gate-driver heating.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total Burst Dissipation

$$P_{tot} = 4(16.38 + 1.92 + 1.89) \approx \mathbf{80.8\ W}$$

Add about 1.5 W for the H743, regulators, and ESC MCUs, giving **≈ 82 W**.

### 3.2 Lumped Board Heat Capacity (20×20 mm, 1.6 mm, 6-layer)

| Element | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 (0.64 cm³) | 1.18 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 1.30 J/K |
| Copper (6 × 70 µm × 4 cm²) | 0.15 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 0.06 J/K |
| 24 FETs + passives | ~1.2 g | ~0.8 J/g·K | ~1.0 J/K |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 2.4 J/K** |

### 3.3 Adiabatic Burst Rise

This is a conservative model that ignores convection during the burst:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P_{tot}}{C_{th}} = \frac{82}{2.4} \approx \mathbf{34\ K/s}$$

### 3.4 Full Thermal RC with Prop-Wash Cooling

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{th,ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right), \quad \tau = R_{th,ba} C_{th}$$

Assume prop-wash forced convection, h ≈ 80 W/m²·K over both faces (8 cm²):

$$R_{th,ba} \approx \frac{1}{80 \times 8 \times 10^{-4}} = 15.6\ \text{K/W}, \quad \tau \approx 37\ \text{s}$$

The steady-state rise would be about 1,280 K, which is physically impossible. **The design cannot sustain 80 A.** It is purely energy-storage limited.

The junction sits above the board. Use Z_th(j-pcb) ≈ 1.5 K/W per FET as a placeholder, which reaches near-steady in about 10 ms:

$$T_j(t) = T_{amb} + \Delta T_{board}(t) + P_{FET} \cdot Z_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$
