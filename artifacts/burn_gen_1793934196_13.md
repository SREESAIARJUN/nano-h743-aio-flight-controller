# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Analysis (Job #49)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture clarification.** ArduPilot on the STM32H743 does not commutate motors. A production AIO uses:
- the **H743** as the flight controller, which generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry, and
- **4 dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), each driving 3 half-bridges.

This document models that topology.

**Assumed operating point.** No part list was supplied, so values are representative. Substitute your datasheet numbers into the equations.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility warning.** As shown below, 80 A per channel on a nano/20×20 form factor dissipates **about 100 W across the board**. This is a burst rating of roughly 1–3 s at best and is not sustainable. Treat "80 A" as a marketing burst figure unless the design uses 5×6 FETs, a heatsink, and airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification, two phase legs carry current at any instant:
- one **PWM leg**, where HS conducts for D and LS conducts for (1−D), and
- one **static leg**, where the LS is held ON.

The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **2 R_ds(on)** in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\,/K
$$

$$
R_{ds(on)}(100^\circ C) \approx 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 1.885\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

**FET conduction loss:**

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.885\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.77\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.1\ W/channel}
$$

**Copper loss.** Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for the phase pad, the bus plane, and the motor-pad path in 2 oz copper:

$$
P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W/channel}
$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

There are 6 FETs per channel. Over one electrical cycle, each device carries:

- **Static LS state:** 1/3 of the cycle.
- **PWM state:** 1/3 of the cycle, split D / (1−D).

At D → 1 (full-throttle burst), the time-averaged loss per FET is:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{24.1}{6} \approx 4.0\ \text{W}
$$

The **instantaneous** loss during conduction is 80² × 1.885 mΩ = **12.1 W**.

Whether the junction sees the average or the peak depends on the electrical frequency:
- **High eRPM** (f_e > 1 kHz): the period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~0.5–2 ms), so the junction sees the average.
- **Low eRPM or stall at high current:** the junction tracks the 12 W peak. This is the worst case, and the firmware must enforce stall/desync protection.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each cycle contains one hard turn-on, one hard turn-off, two dead-time intervals, and one reverse-recovery event.
