## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Design Warning

### 0.1 System partition

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. The 4-in-1 ESC carries its **own** MCU per channel, or one MCU per board pair (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That ESC MCU runs commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 does not drive gates.

> ⚠️ **Architect's note:** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 board is **not thermally sustainable**. Section 3 shows it is a sub-second transient capability at best. Also, four motors at 80 A and high duty imply 250–320 A of battery current, which exceeds any connector or wiring that fits a nano build. If "80 A burst" refers to the **total** ESC rating (20 A per channel), every per-channel loss below scales by (20/80)² for conduction and 20/80 for switching. I flag this explicitly so the spec can be clarified at design review.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.425) = 1.71\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Trapezoidal six-step with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd at duty D and phase B is held low.

| Device | Conduction fraction | Current |
|---|---|---|
| HS_A | D | I_ph |
| LS_A (sync rect, freewheel) | 1 − D (minus dead time) | I_ph |
| LS_B | 1 | I_ph |

$$P_{cond,step} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$

The result is **independent of duty**, which is a key property of sync-rect six-step.

### 1.3 Copper path

Each phase sees one leg plus one return, so the bus-plus-phase trace resistance is roughly 2 × R_trace. Take R_trace ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path for 2 oz plus a via array on a short 20×20 trace:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 (R_{DS} + R_{trace}) = 2 \times 6400 \times (1.71 + 0.25)\,\text{m}\Omega = 25.1\ \text{W}$$

Breakdown:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| MOSFET portion | 21.9 W per channel |
| Per FET (time-averaged, 6 FETs share over a revolution) | 21.9 / 6 ≈ **3.65 W** |
| Peak per FET (LS_B during its 120° conduction window) | I² R = 10.9 W |
| **4-channel total conduction** | **≈ 100 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The worst case is V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

The gate driver sources and sinks about 1 A into the Miller plateau, so:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g} = 11\ \text{ns} \;\;(\text{use 12 ns with layout parasitics})$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.16\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

Recovery also adds overlap current to the HS turn-on. Add about 10% to P_VI, which gives +0.12 W.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

Two dead-time edges per cycle, t_dt = 50 ns:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$
