## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Assumptions

The brief combines two subsystems that must be kept distinct.

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller.** It does not commutate the motors. It generates bi-directional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry.
2. **Each of the four ESC channels has its own small MCU** running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware) with a 48 kHz PWM carrier. Typical parts are the STM32G071 or AT32F421. This is how every production 4-in-1 ESC is built. An H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops while also running ArduPilot.

> **Feasibility flag:** A 20×20 mm (or 25.5×25.5 mm) "nano" footprint cannot fit 12 × 5×6 mm FETs. On 3×3 FETs, 80 A per motor is a **sub-second burst rating only**. Continuous ratings for this form factor are realistically 35–45 A. The model below quantifies exactly how long the burst can last.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_J - 25)]$$

At T_J = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.40 = 2.24\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Add the gate-drive derating (V_GS ≈ 7.5–10 V from a bootstrap driver, roughly +5 %) and use:

$$R_{hot} \approx 2.3\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction (6-step, synchronous rectification)

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs are active in each 60° sector. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two channels in series: either HS(A) and LS(B), or LS(A) and LS(B) during the PWM off-time. Duty cycle therefore does not change the total conduction loss to first order.

Each phase leg carries current for 240° of the electrical cycle. That splits into 120° on the high side and 120° on the low side, but time-shared with PWM. The worst-case FET is the low-side FET at low duty, where it conducts nearly its full 120° plus freewheel. Bounding conservatively, each FET carries I_pk for one-third of the period:

$$I_{FET,rms} = I_{pk}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 80 \times 0.577 = 46.2\ \text{A}$$
