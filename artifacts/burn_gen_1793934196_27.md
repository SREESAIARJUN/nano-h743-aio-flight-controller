# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #13)

## 0. Architecture Decisions Before the Math

Two framing decisions change the analysis.

**1. The H743 should not commutate the motors.**
- ArduPilot has no in-flight-controller motor commutation path. Its output is DShot to ESC firmware.
- The architecture is therefore an H743 flight controller plus four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 (e.g., AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071), all on one PCB stack.
- The 48 kHz PWM lives on the ESC MCUs. Bi-directional DShot600 lives on the H743.
- If you want a single-MCU design where the H743 drives the gates, that is custom firmware and outside ArduPilot. Treat that as a separate program.

**2. "80 A burst" must be specified per channel or aggregate.**
- 80 A on all four channels at once means about 290 A of battery current at high duty. A nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board cannot dissipate this beyond about 2 s, as shown below.
- I model both cases and derive a burst-time limit.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step synchronous conduction

In any commutation sector, current flows through two devices:
- During PWM-on: the high-side FET of the PWM'd phase plus the low-side FET of the sink phase.
- During PWM-off with synchronous rectification: the low-side FET of the PWM'd phase replaces the high-side FET. The sink-phase low-side FET stays on.

So two R_ds,on are always in the current path, independent of duty D (dead time is handled separately):

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.41 ≈ 1.7 mΩ. Using 1.8 mΩ for margin:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is ≈ 3.84 W. Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 11.5 W. That matters for short-term junction ripple at low eRPM (see §3.3).
