# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Power-Stage and DShot Design Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Design Review Flags

The analysis rests on the following assumptions and design-review findings.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller. Each motor channel runs its own ESC MCU with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor is not a continuous rating.** The math below shows it is a **transient, thermal-capacitance-limited** rating of roughly 1–2 s. It is not a steady-state capability. Marketing ratings in this class routinely ignore this.
3. **Not all four channels hit 80 A at once.** Four channels at 80 A would mean about 320 A from the battery. The battery pad, copper, and connector cannot sustain that. The pack-level case is modeled separately.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology

AM32 and BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In any commutation sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** Current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** One FET is held on for the whole sector.

Instantaneous conduction is therefore always through **exactly two FETs**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds | Per FET (80 A) | Per channel (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 6.4 W | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 8.8 W | 17.6 W |
| 125 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 9.6 W | **19.2 W** |

### 1.3 PCB Copper Loss (Usually Ignored, Shouldn't Be)

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), sheet resistance is:

$$R_\square = \rho/t = 1.72\times10^{-8}/70\times10^{-6} \approx 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

At 100 °C this rises ×1.3, to about 0.32 mΩ/□.

A nano phase path is about 4 squares (battery pad → FET → phase pad, plus the return), giving about 1.3 mΩ. That is comparable to the FET itself:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 1.3\times10^{-3} \approx 8.3\ \text{W}$$

**Mitigation:** Use 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, paralleled planes, and via stitching (≥ 20 vias of 0.3 mm per high-current node). These measures can cut this loss by about 60%.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. One hard-switched transition pair occurs per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 (25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

The low-side body diode conducts in dead time, then the high side turns on.

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.07\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n}\times 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Total per Channel at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 19.20 |
| PCB copper | 8.3 (≈3.3 with mitigation) |
| V–I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.31 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 |
| **Total** | **≈ 29.9 W (≈ 24.9 W mitigated)** |

**Key insight:** At 48 kHz, conduction dominates about 10:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz for smoother low-throttle response costs only about 2 W per channel. Lower R_ds,on and copper matter far more than gate-drive tuning.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady State: Infeasible at 80 A

$$A = 8\times10^{-4}\ \text{m}^2$$
