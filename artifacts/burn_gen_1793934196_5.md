# STM32H743 FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC (Job #38): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, Netlist, Thermal

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Any Math

Three assumptions in the brief need correcting first. They change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor channel on the 4-in-1 board has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay firmware. Typical MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The 48 kHz switching frequency is a parameter of those ESC MCUs, not of the H743. The design is therefore two partitions: an **FC partition** (H743, IMU, baro, DShot) and an **ESC partition** (4× MCU, gate drivers, 24× MOSFETs).
2. **"80A burst" is taken as 80A per motor channel, for ≤ 5 s.** All four channels at burst means **320A from the battery**. No nano-class board (20×20 or 30×30 mm) can sustain this thermally. The connector, XT60, and battery leads cannot carry it either. Section 5 quantifies the real burst window.
3. **48 kHz is unusually high** for an 80A stage. The math below shows its cost, and Section 6 recommends 24 kHz.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Each motor channel is a 3-phase bridge (6 FETs) running six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant the motor current I_ph flows through exactly **two** conducting channels:
- the high-side (HS) FET of the source phase, and
- the low-side (LS) FET of the sink phase.

During the PWM-off interval the chopping leg's complementary FET carries the current instead of the body diode, so the current path still contains two R_DS(on) elements.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{125} = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

At 80A, T_j = 125 °C, and a phase-path copper resistance R_Cu = 0.5 mΩ (pad → plane → phase pad, 2 oz inner layers):

$$P_{FET} = 80^2 \times 3.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET loss

Each FET conducts for a 120° block, i.e. 1/3 duty of the electrical cycle. The two FETs sharing the current split the loss equally.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\tfrac{1}{3} = 6400 \times 1.6\text{m} / 3 = 3.41\ \text{W}$$

The **instantaneous** loss in a conducting FET is 10.2 W. This value drives the junction transient (§5.3), because the electrical period at high eRPM is far shorter than the die thermal time constant. The FET therefore sees the average, but with ripple.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg chops per commutation step, so switching losses are counted per channel for one leg.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover)

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{DC} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f)$$
$$E_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} = 40.3\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = 40.3\,\mu\text{J} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns}\times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{DC} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr rises steeply with di/dt and temperature. Budget ×2 for derating: **0.12 W**.

### 2.4 C_oss charge/discharge

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{DC}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g)

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{n}\times 7.5\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.043\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel switching total

| Loss term | W @ 48 kHz | W @ 24 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I crossover | 1.94 | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 0.31 |
| Q_rr (derated) | 0.12 | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.02 | 0.01 |
| Gate | 0.04 | 0.02 |
| **Σ switching** | **2.73** | **1.37** |

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Item | Per channel (W) | 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction @ 125 °C | 20.5 | 82.0 |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 | 12.8 |
| Switching @ 48 kHz | 2.73 | 10.9 |
| Gate drivers, LDOs, ESC MCU | ~0.15 | 0.6 |
| **Total** | **26.6** | **106 W** |

**Shared bus losses (all four channels at 80A, I_bat = 320A):**
- Battery pad, XT60, and leads (~1.5 mΩ): 320² × 1.5m = **154 W**.
- This exceeds the ESC itself. 320A is not a realistic simultaneous operating point.

**Efficiency at burst:** P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V × ~0.9 duty ≈ 7.3 kW → η_ESC ≈ 98.6%. The percentage looks excellent, but the 106 W is concentrated in roughly 9 cm² of PCB. Section 5 shows why that is the real constraint.

**Bus capacitor ripple (per channel, worst case at D = 0.5):**

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 80 \times 0.5 = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}$$
