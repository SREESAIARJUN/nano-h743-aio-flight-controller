# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Design Package (Job #2)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

Several parameters are not yet frozen for Job #2, so I've fixed a baseline. Every equation is parametric, so you can substitute real datasheet values once the BOM is locked.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot. It is **not** the commutation MCU. Each of the four ESC channels has its own AT32F421 running AM32, which provides 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot natively. Driving 12 FETs directly from the H743 is possible in theory, but ArduPilot doesn't support it and it would destroy loop-timing determinism.

| Parameter | Baseline value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 5×6 PDFN (example class: R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V, Q_g = 50 nC, C_oss,eff ≈ 1.5 nF, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at drive strength used, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W) |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap), 10 V gate rail |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz all layers, FR4 1.6 mm |
| Burst | 80 A per motor phase current, all four channels simultaneously (worst case) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, dead time 100 ns |

> **Reality flag (up front):** The model in Section 3 shows that 4 × 80 A simultaneously on a 30.5 mm board is a **~2–4 second** capability, not a sustained rating. Marketing should label it "80 A burst (≤2 s)". Sustained current is in the 30–35 A range with airflow.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- **On-state (duty D):** high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- **Off-state (1−D):** the A low-side (synchronous freewheel) plus the B low-side.

Two FETs therefore carry I during the entire PWM period, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence (Self-Consistent)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 75) = **1.41 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2(80)^2(1.41\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{18.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 PCB Copper Loss

The phase loop includes the bus plane, the FET pads and the motor pad. With R_cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per channel (2 oz, ~15 mm path, paralleled across 3 layers) and α_Cu = 0.0039 K⁻¹:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu}(T) = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3}\times1.29 \approx \mathbf{3.3\ W}$$

### 1.4 Shunt Loss

Each channel has a 0.2 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{shunt} = 6400 \times 0.2\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.28\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one FET pair hard-switches per PWM edge: the switching high-side and its complementary low-side.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = ½ (25.2)(80)(40 ns)(48 k) | **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F I (2 t_dt) f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (charge/discharge) | ½ C_oss V² f_sw × 2 = 1.5 nF × 635 × 48 k | **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6 × Q_g V_GS f_sw = 6 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | **0.14 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.80 W** |

**Design note on 48 kHz versus 24 kHz.** Switching loss scales linearly with frequency:

$$P_{sw} \propto f_{sw}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1.3 W per ESC, which is about 5% of the total at 80 A. The benefit of 48 kHz is lower current ripple:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a 20 µH motor at D = 0.5, ripple falls from ~13 A to ~6.6 A. 48 kHz is justified for low-inductance racing motors
