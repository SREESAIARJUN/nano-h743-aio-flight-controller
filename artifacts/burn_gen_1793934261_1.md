## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Feasibility Flags

Two points shape the rest of this document, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., AT32F421, STM32G071, GD32E230). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC-side PWM frequency, not an H743 output. I model it that way below.

2. **80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a burst rating that lasts seconds, not a continuous rating.** The math in §3 shows that 4×80 A drives roughly 100 W into a board with about 4 J/K of thermal mass. The design must be specified as *burst duration at a given current*. A bare "80 A" claim should not be accepted.

All component parameters below are representative assumptions for a 40 V PQFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET class. Before sign-off, replace them with values from the selected part's datasheet. Every equation is written parametrically so you can substitute directly.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous complementary PWM. At any instant, two phases carry current:

- **On-time:** current flows through the high side (HS) of phase X, the motor, then the low side (LS) of phase Y.
- **Off-time:** current flows through the LS of phase X (synchronous rectification), the motor, then the LS of phase Y.

So exactly two channels conduct at all times, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

With α ≈ 0.0053 /K (so k_T ≈ 1.4 at 100 °C), R_ds = 1.68 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.68\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.5\ W}$$

The loss splits between the devices as follows:
- The HS of the PWM leg carries I²R·D.
- The LS of the same leg carries I²R·(1−D).
- The static LS on the return phase carries I²R the whole time (100 %).

**The return-path LS FET is the hottest device: 10.75 W at 80 A.**

Because R_ds rises with temperature and loss rises with R_ds, there is an electrothermal feedback loop. Solve it iteratively:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\cdot I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_c + R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}\alpha}$$

Thermal runaway occurs when the denominator reaches zero, i.e., when $R_{\theta JC} I^2 R_{25}\alpha \ge 1$. Here that product is 1.5 × 6400 × 1.2e-3 × 0.0053 = 0.061, so the device is stable at the junction level. The limiting factor is the board, not the junction.

### 2.2 Switching Losses

Only one leg switches at a time: the HS/LS pair of the PWM phase.

**Hard-switching V·I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery loss** (body diode of the complementary FET, snapped off at HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Output capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} = E_{oss} f_{sw} = 0.6\,\mu\text{J}\times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.029\ W}$$
