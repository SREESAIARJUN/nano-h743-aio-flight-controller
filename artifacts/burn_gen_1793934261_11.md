## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs. The ESC board has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.

The H743 is responsible for:
- DShot600 command generation
- Bi-directional eRPM telemetry capture
- Current and voltage ADC sensing

This document models both sides, with the interface between them treated as a contract.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current paths

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry current I:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** HS_A on for D·T_sw. LS_A conducts synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (static leg):** LS_B is held on for the entire sector.

At every instant, exactly two R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current. Neglecting dead time:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \qquad \text{(independent of D)}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution over one electrical cycle

Each leg spends 1/3 of the cycle as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static low leg, and 1/3 floating.

$$P_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}\,I^2R_{DS}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{(1-D)+1}{3}\,I^2R_{DS} = \frac{2-D}{3}\,I^2R_{DS}$$

**The low-side FETs are always the hottest devices.**

### 1.3 Numerical evaluation at 80 A burst

Electrothermal iteration, assuming T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1 + 0.0065(85)] = 1.86\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$I^2R = 6400 \times 1.86\times10^{-3} = 11.9\ \text{W}$$
