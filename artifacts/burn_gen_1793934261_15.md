# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Timing Design

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

Three points need resolving before the math, because they change the results.

**1. Partitioning.** The H743 should run ArduPilot on the FC board. Each ESC channel is driven by its own small ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or an EFM8/BLHeli_S class part). The H743 does not commutate motors. It generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. All DMA math below follows this split.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano board is not thermally viable.** The model in §2–§4 shows roughly 100 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. That is too much for a 20×20 mm board even for one second, and 24 FETs at 3.3×3.3 mm do not fit with the needed copper. My recommendation:

| Form factor | Realistic rating | FETs/switch |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 "nano" | 35 A cont / 45 A burst (≤5 s) | 1× DFN3.3 |
| 30.5×30.5 | 55 A cont / 80 A burst (≤1–2 s) | 2× DFN3.3 or 1× DFN5×6 |

The equations are parameterized so you can rerun them for either.

## 1. Commutation Model (6-step, synchronous rectification, AM32-style)

Per electrical cycle, each phase spends ⅓ as the PWM leg, ⅓ as the sink leg (low side fully on), and ⅓ floating.

Conduction duty per switch position, with D = PWM duty:

- High side: δ_HS = D/3
- Low side: δ_LS = (1−D)/3 + 1/3 = (2−D)/3

At any instant exactly two switch positions carry I. Summed over all six positions:

Σδ = 3·(D/3) + 3·((2−D)/3) = 2

**Result: with sync rectification, conduction loss is independent of throttle duty.**

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Hot resistance per switch position:**

R_sw(T_j) = R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)] / N_p

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

P_cond = 2 · I² · R_sw(T_j)

**Per-FET instantaneous loss while conducting:**

P_FET = (I/N_p)² · R₂₅ · [1 + α(T_j − 25)]

**Numbers at T_j = 125 °C** (factor 1.6 → 3.68 mΩ per FET, R_sw = 1.84 mΩ):

| Quantity | N_p = 2 | N_p = 1 |
|---|---|---|
| P_cond per motor | 2·6400·1.84 m = **23.6 W** | **47.1 W** |
| P_cond total (4 motors) | **94.2 W** | 188 W |
| Instantaneous P_FET | 40²·3.68 m = **5.9 W** | 23.6 W |

**Electrothermal stability.** Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, there is a runaway condition. It is stable only if:

α · P_FET · R_θ(j–a, eff) < 1

With R_θ ≈ 3 K/W junction-to-board: 0.006 · 5.9 · 3 = 0.11, so the loop is stable. The steady-state multiplier is 1/(1−0.11) ≈ 1.12, which is already folded into choosing T_j = 125 °C.
