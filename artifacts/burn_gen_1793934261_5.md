## 0. Architecture and Scope

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Four local ESC MCUs (for example, AT32F421 running AM32) each drive a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

```
          ┌──────────── STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ────────────┐
 6S LiPo  │ TIM1_CH1..CH4 (PE9/PE11/PE13/PE14) ── BDShot600│
   │      └──────┬─────────┬─────────┬─────────┬──────────┘
   │           ESC1      ESC2      ESC3      ESC4   (AT32F421 + FD6288Q + 6× N-FET)
   └── VBAT bus ─┴─────────┴─────────┴─────────┴── Motors M1–M4
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Topology.** The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs:

- the high-side FET (or, during the off-portion of PWM, its complementary low-side FET), and
- the low-side FET of the return phase.

Because the PWM pair shares the current over the cycle according to D and (1−D), conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

**Temperature dependence:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Copper loss** in the phase path:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### Electrothermal feedback

Losses raise T_j, which raises R_DS, which raises losses. Solving for a steady state:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,P(T_j), \quad P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$\Rightarrow\ T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,2I^2R_{25}\alpha}$$

**Thermal runaway** occurs when $R_{th} \geq \dfrac{1}{2I^2R_{25}\alpha}$.

At 80 A:

$$\frac{1}{2 \times 6400 \times 0.001 \times 0.005} = \mathbf{15.6\ K/W}$$

The board-level R_th (Section 4) is close to this limit. **Do not allow sustained 80 A operation.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per ESC, only the PWM pair commutates, giving one hard-switched transition pair per cycle.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_d · f | 0.8 × 80 × 600 ns × 48 k = **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss charge | ½ · Q_oss · V · f | **0.02 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.95 W** |

**Design note.** Dead-time loss exceeds overlap loss. Reducing t_d to 150 ns (FD6288Q internal dead time plus AM32 setting) saves about 0.9 W per ESC. You must verify shoot-through margin on the scope first.

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| | Per ESC | All 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.95 W | 11.8 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.4 W** | **≈ 101 W** |

**Efficiency at burst.** Output power ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 0.9 this is ≈ 1.8 kW per ESC, giving a stage efficiency of about 98.6%. The efficiency is fine. The problem is power **density**: 101 W on 4 cm².

### Input capacitor ripple (often the actual failure point)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}\ \Rightarrow\ 40\text{ A}_{rms}\text{ worst case per ESC at } D = 0.5$$

Interleaving between ESCs is uncontrolled, so budget for this.
