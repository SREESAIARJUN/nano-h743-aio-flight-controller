## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step trapezoidal operation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry current I. During the PWM on-time, the current path is one high-side (HS) FET plus one low-side (LS) FET. During the off-time with synchronous rectification, the path is two LS FETs, or the complementary FET of the chopped leg. In both states the current flows through exactly two R_ds(on).

Per-ESC conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Here α ≈ 0.006 /K, giving k_T ≈ 1.5 at 110 °C.

Including inductor current ripple ΔI (peak-to-peak):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph ≈ 15 µH (typical 5–7" motor), D = 0.9, and E_bemf ≈ 0.85·V_bus:

- ΔI ≈ 3.4·0.9 / (2·15e-6·48e3) ≈ 2.1 A
- The ripple term is negligible: 0.37 A² against 6400 A².

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---|
| R_ds(on) at 110 °C | 2.25 mΩ |
| P_cond per ESC | 6400 · 2 · 2.25e-3 = **28.8 W** |
| Instantaneous loss in one conducting FET | 80² · 2.25e-3 = **14.4 W** |
| Average per FET (each FET's share is about 1/3 of the electrical cycle) | ≈ 28.8/6 ≈ **4.8 W** |
| All four ESCs (full-throttle punch) | **115 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step operation, one leg per ESC is hard-switched at a time.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2 V_{SD} I t_{dead} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{GS} f}_{\text{gate (driver)}}$$

| Term | Calculation | W per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·40e-9·48e3 | 1.94 |
| Q_rr | 50e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.06 |
| Q_oss (≈30 nC) | 0.5·30e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.02 |
| Dead time | 2·0.8·80·50e-9·48e3 | 0.31 |
| Gate (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2·50e-9·10·48e3 | 0.05 |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz roughly doubles switching loss, from about 1.2 W to 2.4 W. Even so, conduction dominates at 80 A, 28.8 W against 2.4 W, so 48 kHz is affordable. Its benefits are lower ripple and quieter motors.

**Combined loss:**

- Per ESC: ≈ **31.2 W**
- Board (4 ESCs): ≈ **125 W**
- Add the bus-cap ESR loss from Section 4.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Lumped board-level thermal capacitance

Example: 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on every layer.

| Element | Calculation | C_th |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 9e-4 m² · 8 · 70 µm · 8960 kg/m³ ≈ 4.5 g × 385 J/kg·K | 1.74 J/K |
| FR-4 | 1.44 cm³ · 1.85 g/cm³ ≈ 2.7 g × 1100 J/kg·K | 2.9 J/K |
| FETs, caps, connectors, solder | | ~1.0 J/K |
| **Total** | | **≈ 5.6 J/K** |

A 20×20 mm board scales by area to roughly **2.5 J/K**.

### 3.2 Adiabatic burst time

Convection under prop-wash at about 10 m/s gives R_th,board-air ≈ 8 K/W, so h·A ≈ 0.12 W/K. That is negligible on a 1–3 s timescale.

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\,\Delta T_{allow}}{P_{tot}}$$

With ΔT_allow = 110 − 45 = 65 K:

| Board | Burst time at 125 W |
|---|---|
| 30×30 mm | 5.6·65/125 ≈ **2.9 s** |
| 20×20 mm | ≈ **1.3 s** |

### 3.3 Junction rise above the local board

Per FET, using Z_th(j-c) from the datasheet transient curve. Typical DFN5x6 values are about 0.3 K/W at 10 ms and about 1.0 K/W steady state. Pad-to-plane spreading under the FET adds about 4 K/W on 2 oz copper with 12+ vias.

- ΔT_j-board ≈ P_FET · (R_jc + R_spread) = 4.8 · (1.0 + 4.0) ≈ **24 K**.
- Peak during a conduction interval: 14.4 W × Z_th(≈0.3 ms) ≈ 14.4 · 0.1 ≈ **1.5 K** of ripple on top. This is negligible.

**Junction limit:** T_j ≈ T_board + 24 K. To keep T_j ≤ 150 °C (the T_j,max of most 40 V FETs), keep T_board ≤ 125 °C. The 110 °C board limit used above leaves 16 K of margin.
