# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture Model

**Job #40, Rev A (engineering model; requires validation before fab)**

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design-Risk Flags

This model rests on four architectural assumptions:

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** ArduPilot runs on the H743, and four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32) commutate the motors. The H743 does not commutate. Bidirectional DShot600 runs between the H743 timers and those ESC MCUs.
2. **"80A burst" means per motor channel.** This model treats it as a short-duration rating, not a continuous one.
3. **Battery is 6S Li-ion/LiPo:** V_bus,max = 25.2 V and V_bus,nom = 22.2 V.
4. **Form factor is "nano" (25.5×25.5 mm or 20×20 mm stack).**

All device parameters are symbolic first, then evaluated with representative values. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected MOSFETs before sign-off.

> ⚠️ **Critical risk:** The math below shows that 4 × 80 A on a nano footprint is thermally viable only for burst durations of a few seconds. Continuous ratings will be about 25–35 A per channel at best. It also implies a 320 A battery draw, which exceeds XT30/XT60 connector ratings and most nano-class pack C-ratings. Treat 80 A as a marketing-burst figure with firmware current limiting, or move to a 30.5 mm platform.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha\approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step current path

In any commutation sector, current flows through one PWM-switched phase leg and one static leg:

- **PWM phase leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T. The synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D)·T. The leg therefore always contributes one R_ds in series.
- **Static phase leg:** the low-side is on for the whole sector, contributing one R_ds.

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle (to first order):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.3 Ripple correction

With phase inductance L_ph and peak-to-peak ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For low-inductance racing motors (L_ph ≈ 10 µH, D = 0.5):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} \approx 13\ \text{A}$$

The ripple term is ΔI²/12 ≈ 14 A². That is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80.1 A.

### 2.4 Evaluated conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80.1^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 2.5 PCB copper loss (often the larger hidden term)

Sheet resistance is ρ_□ = 0.5 mΩ/□ for 1 oz copper and 0.25 mΩ/□ for 2 oz.

A nano phase/bus path is about 5 squares:

| Copper stack | R_path | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Single 2 oz layer | 1.25 mΩ | 8.0 W |
| 4 × 2 oz layers paralleled (stitched) | ≈ 0.31 mΩ | **2.0 W** |

**Requirement:** use a 6–8 layer, 2 oz (min) stackup with power planes paralleled on at least 4 layers and dense via stitching (≥ 0.3 mm vias, about 1 A/via budget at burst).

## 3. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Per channel, hard switching occurs on one leg per sector: one turn-on and one turn-off per PWM period.

### 3.1 V–I overlap loss
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Output-capacitance loss (two FETs per leg)
$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
