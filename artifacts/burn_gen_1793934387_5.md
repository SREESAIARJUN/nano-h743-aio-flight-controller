## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Several requirements interact, and three findings shape everything below.

1. **The H743 cannot also be the ESC controller.** ArduPilot runs the flight stack and does not commutate motors. The ESC therefore needs **4× dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 is the reference choice). The H743 talks to them over on-board bidirectional DShot600. This is the only reason BDShot DMA allocation matters.
2. **"80 A burst" must be defined.** I model it as **80 A RMS phase current per motor, all four motors simultaneously, 6S (25.2 V), for a time-limited burst**. If the requirement is 80 A *battery* current total, the losses below drop by roughly 16× and the design is easy.
3. **On a nano (20×20 mm) board, 4×80 A is not thermally sustainable.** The model shows a burst budget of about 2–3 s from a warm start. It also shows copper, not just the MOSFETs, is a co-limiting loss. This needs to be fed back to the requirements owner.

**Assumed MOSFET.** The model uses a generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part, 24 FETs total. **Replace these values with your chosen part's datasheet values.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation geometry (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous complementary PWM)

AM32 uses this scheme. In any 60° sector:
- Phase A conducts high, PWM with duty D. Its complement is the A-low FET, active during (1−D).
- Phase B is held low (low-side FET fully on).
- Phase C floats (BEMF sensing).

As a result:
- During the A-leg on-time, current flows through the A-high FET and the B-low FET.
- During the A-leg off-time, current flows through the A-low FET (synchronous rectification) and the B-low FET.

So exactly **two FET channels carry I_ph at every instant**, regardless of D.

Two loss expressions follow:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}$$

The per-FET average is valid because the electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to kHz) is far above the junction thermal time constant (~ms) only for the *slow* package/board network. Strictly, junction ripple is small but non-zero; see 1.4.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I_ph = 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.80 mΩ | 23.0 W | 3.84 W |
| 100 °C | 2.54 mΩ | 32.5 W | 5.42 W |
| 125 °C | 2.79 mΩ | 35.7 W | 5.95 W |

### 1.4 Junction ripple at low eRPM

Each FET conducts in a 120° window. At low electrical frequency f_e, the junction sees a pulse of 3× its average loss for 1/(3f_e).

For f_e = 50 Hz, the pulse is 6.7 ms. The junction Z_th at ~7 ms is about 0.6 K/W, so:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx 3 \cdot 5.95\,\text{W} \cdot 0.6 \approx 11\ \text{K}$$

This matters at **stall or startup at high current**, which is exactly the burst-on-punch-out case. Keep a 15 K margin for it.

### 1.5 Copper conduction loss (often ignored, cannot be here)

Assume per-phase high-current path ≈ 15 mm, width 3 mm, 4 layers of 2 oz (70 µm) in parallel. Copper resistivity is ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m at 25 °C, with tempco +0.393 %/K.

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.003\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The current path has two phase traces plus a share of the bus planes, about 0.8 mΩ total at 25 °C and about 1.1 mΩ hot.

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \cdot 1.1\times10^{-3} \approx 7\ \text{W per channel}$$

Additional copper losses:
- Pads, vias, and solder: about 0.3 mΩ per path. Use ≥ 12 × 0.3 mm vias per FET drain pad.
- Battery pads and lead: these are outside the model, but the battery current at 80 A phase is D·I_ph, so it is also significant.

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per channel there is **one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per PWM period**. The complementary FET switches softly after dead time.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

Transition times from gate charge and drive current:

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,src}}$$

$$t_{off} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}},\quad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,snk}}$$

Assumed gate-path values: V_drv = 10 V, R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω (on) and 2.2 Ω (off, via a diode bypass), R_g,int = 1 Ω, driver source/sink ≈ 3 Ω / 1.5 Ω.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|

| I_g,off | 3.2 V / 4.7 Ω ≈ 0.68 A, giving t_off ≈ 13.2 ns |
