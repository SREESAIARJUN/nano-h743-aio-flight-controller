# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #29)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Key Findings

I have no prior project record for Job #29, so the baseline assumptions are listed below. Change any of them and the equations still hold; only the numbers move.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mount, ~36×36 mm PCB, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers |
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 4 motors simultaneously (worst case) |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM, center-aligned |
| ESC MCU | 4× AT32F421 / STM32G071-class running AM32 (one per motor) |
| FC MCU | STM32H743 @ 480 MHz (ArduPilot runs it at 400 MHz by default), timer clock 200 MHz |
| MOSFET | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ typ / 1.3 mΩ max @ 10 V, 25 °C |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, ~1.5 A peak, V_GS = 10 V |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal (AM32 default) |

**Key findings:**

- **The thermal result dominates the design.** At 80 A on all four motors, the board dissipates about 85 W. A nano AIO cannot sustain this. The burst is thermally limited to roughly **3–5 seconds** starting from a warm board. Specify it as a burst rating, not a continuous one.
- **Treat outputs as estimates.** Validate them with a thermal camera, junction-temperature estimation via R_DS(on), and logged ESC telemetry.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases are active and one floats. During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B). During the off-time, it flows through LS(A) (synchronous freewheel) → motor → LS(B).

So **exactly two MOSFETs conduct the full phase current at every instant**, apart from dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS (typ) | P_cond per motor at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |
| 150 °C | 1.56 mΩ | 20.0 W |

### 1.3 Loss distribution between the two conducting FETs

With duty D on the PWM'd phase:

- HS(A): I²R·D
- LS(A): I²R·(1−D)
- LS(B): I²R (the static leg carries 100%)

The static low-side FET is therefore the hot spot. At 125 °C it dissipates **9.3 W alone**.

Commutation rotates this role across all six FETs every electrical cycle. At typical eRPM (above 1 kHz electrical) the rotation is faster than the FET thermal time constant of about 10 ms. The *time-averaged* per-FET loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor} + P_{sw}}{6} \approx 3.4\ \text{W}$$

**Exception:** at low RPM or stall, the rotation is slower than the FET time constant. Design the transient for **9.3 W on a single FET**.

### 1.4 Parasitic conduction (per motor)

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (single, low-side) | 0.5 mΩ | I²R·duty-weighted ≈ 3.2 W |
| Phase copper (pad→FET, ~0.3 mΩ) | 2 phases | 2 × 1.9 W = 3.8 W |
| Battery pad → bus planes (shared) | ~0.15 mΩ, 320 A total | 15.4 W (board total) |

**The shared bus copper loss is not negligible.** At 320 A total, every 0.1 mΩ of bus copper costs about 10 W. Use pour-only bus planes on at least 4 layers and solder the XT60 leads directly to the pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the other active leg is static.

### 2.1 Gate-charge-based transition times

The driver delivers I_G ≈ (V_drv − V_plateau)/(R_G,ext + R_G,int):

$$I_G \approx \frac{10 - 3.0}{4.7 + 1.0} \approx 1.2\ \text{A}$$

With Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 12 nC:

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.2\ \text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

### 2.2 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

With Q_rr ≈ 30 nC at high di/dt:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

The current-dependent t_rr overlap adds about 0.1 W. Use **0.14 W**.

With Q_oss ≈ 40 nC:

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
