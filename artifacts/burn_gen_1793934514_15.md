## 1. Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction losses (6-step, synchronous complementary PWM)

At any instant, current flows through two legs:
- The PWM leg conducts through the high-side FET (duty D) or its synchronous low-side FET (duty 1−D).
- The fixed leg conducts through its low-side FET.

So exactly two R_ds(on) are always in the current path, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C the multiplier is 1 + 0.006·85 = 1.51.

| Case | R_ds(110 °C) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Typical | 1.51 mΩ | 2·6400·1.51e-3 = **19.3 W** |
| Worst (max R_ds) | 1.96 mΩ | **25.1 W** |

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Averaged over the cycle, each FET carries about P_ch/6 ≈ 3.2–4.2 W. At low eRPM, the instantaneous per-FET loss approaches the full I²R ≈ 9.7–12.5 W for up to one commutation interval.

Add copper and shunt losses separately: P_Cu = I²·R_trace. A 0.3 mΩ path adds **1.9 W per channel**.
