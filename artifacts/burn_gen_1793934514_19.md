## 0. Feasibility Issues to Resolve First

Three problems in the brief need addressing before any numbers mean anything.

| # | Issue | Consequence | Resolution adopted below |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The H743 cannot be the ESC controller under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. It emits DShot. Sensorless 6-step or FOC at 48 kHz needs a dedicated MCU per motor (or a multi-motor ESC firmware, which ArduPilot does not run on the FC core). | The architecture must be split. | H743 = flight controller. **4 × AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32** = ESC. 48 kHz is the AM32 PWM frequency. |
| 2 | **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically credible per motor.** 4 × 80 A means about 290 A of battery current at 90 % duty, which is roughly 7 kW through XT30-class pads into 4 cm² of copper. | The board would reach destructive temperature in under 1 s (shown in §3). | Every equation is derived generally, then evaluated for two cases: **Case A = 80 A per motor** (the brief as literally stated) and **Case B = 80 A total burst (20 A per motor)**, which is what a 20×20 product can actually carry. |
| 3 | **No component part numbers were specified.** | MOSFET parameters drive every result. | A representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN N-FET is assumed (table below). Substitute datasheet values; the equations are parameterised for that. |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step, complementary/synchronous PWM)

During the **ON** interval (duty D), current flows: HS of phase X → motor → LS of phase Y.

During the **OFF** interval (1−D), it freewheels: LS of phase X (synchronous) → motor → LS of phase Y.

So at every instant exactly **two FETs** carry the phase current I_ph. That gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple and temperature corrections

The ripple-corrected RMS current is:

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-line), ΔI is about 10–15 A pk-pk at 48 kHz. That adds roughly 0.3 % to I² at 80 A, so it is negligible but kept in the model.

The temperature dependence makes R_ds a closed-loop variable:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Because R_ds rises with T_j, and T_j rises with loss, the two must be solved **iteratively** (§3.4).
