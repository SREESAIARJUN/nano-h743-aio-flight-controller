# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7): Power-Stage Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Note

The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors in this design. It runs ArduPilot as the flight controller and emits **bidirectional DShot600** to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Each motor channel then runs its own commutation firmware.

| Block | Part (assumed) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot, DShot/BDShot |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 (QFN32) | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | 6 gate outputs per motor |
| MOSFET ×24 | 40 V N-ch, DFN3.3×3.3 (assumed parameters below) | 3 half-bridges per motor |
| Current sense | 1 shared 0.2 mΩ shunt + INA186A3 | Total battery current → FC ADC |
| Input | 3–6S (V_bus,max = 25.2 V) | |

**Important constraint:** an 80 A burst on all four channels of a ~25.5 × 25.5 mm board is not thermally sustainable. The model in §3 shows it is valid for roughly 1–2 seconds. Treat 80 A as a per-motor, short-duration rating, not as a 4 × 80 A design point.

## 2. Loss Model: One Motor Channel, 6-Step Commutation, Synchronous Rectification

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step operation, two phases are active at any instant. With complementary (synchronous) PWM, the phase current always flows through exactly **two channel resistances in series**:

- during PWM-on: the high-side FET of leg A and the low-side FET of leg B;
- during PWM-off: the low-side FET of leg A (synchronous freewheel) and the low-side FET of leg B.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.40 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 1.40 m = **17.9 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.03 mΩ | **26.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.24 mΩ | **28.7 W** |

**Per-device share.** Each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical period, so the loss spreads over 6 FETs. The worst-case device is the low-side FET, which carries freewheel current plus commutated current:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}P_{cond} \approx 9.6\ \text{W at }125\,°C$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 20 ns · 48 k | **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 30 n · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw = ½ · 1 n · 635 · 48 k | 0.015 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2·t_dead · f_sw = 0.8 · 80 · 400 ns · 48 k | **1.229 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2 · Q_g · V_g · f_sw = 2 · 30 n · 10 · 48 k | 0.029 W (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |
| **P_sw,total** | | **≈ 2.28 W** |

Two observations follow:

- **Dead time dominates over V–I overlap.** Reducing t_dead from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. This is only safe if the driver's propagation-delay skew and the gate loop allow it.
- **Frequency sensitivity:** ∂P_sw/∂f ≈ 47.5 µW/Hz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.14 W per motor, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.3 Total Power-Stage Loss

$$P_{motor}(T_j{=}125\,°C) = 28.7 + 2.28 \approx 31.0\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{ESC,4\times80A} \approx 124\ \text{W} \;(+\;\text{shunt: } (320\,\text{A})^2 \cdot 0.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 20.5\ \text{W})$$

**The shunt is a real problem.** 320 A total requires splitting into 4 × 0.8 mΩ shunts (one per motor feed) or using a magnetic (Hall) sensor. My recommendation is a Hall-effect sensor, e.g. TMCS1100-class, or per-motor sensing in the ESC MCUs.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Lumped Network

```
P_FET → [R_θJC 1.5 K/W] → case → [R_θCB ≈ 2 K/W per FET via thermal vias] → board node (C_B) → [R_θBA] → ambient
```

### 3.2 Board Thermal Capacitance

Assume a 6-layer, 2 oz copper board of about 6.5 cm².

- **Copper:** 6.5 cm² × 6 × 70 µm = 0.273 cm³ → 2.45 g → C = 2.45 × 0.385 = **0.94 J/K**
- **FR-4:** 6.5 × 0.16 = 1.04 cm³ → 1.92 g → C ≈ 2.1 J/K. It is weakly coupled, so take an effective value of about 1.5 J/K.
- **FETs and solder:** about 0.3 J/K.
- **Total C_B ≈ 2.7 J/K**

### 3.3 Convection

Prop-wash forced convection: h ≈ 80–120 W/m²K over about 13 cm² (both sides).

$$R_{θBA} \approx \frac{1}{100 \cdot 0.0013} \approx 7.7\ \text{K/W}$$

The steady-state capacity at a 75 K rise is therefore only about **9.8 W total**. That is roughly 4 × 25 A continuous, which matches real 20×20 AIO ratings.

### 3.4 Burst Duration (Adiabatic Approximation, τ = R·C ≈ 21 s ≫ t_burst)

$$t_{burst} = \frac{C_B\,\Delta T}{P - \Delta T/R_{θBA}}$$

| Scenario | P | ΔT budget (25 → 100 °C board) | t_burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A | 124 W (+ shunt) | 75 K | 2.7 × 75 / (124 − 9.8) ≈ **1.8 s** |
| 1 × 80 A, 3 × 30 A | 31 + 3 × 5.7 ≈ 48 W | 75 K | ≈ **5.3 s** |
| 4 × 50 A | ≈ 4 × 12.5 = 50 W | 75 K | ≈ **5.0 s** |

### 3.5 Junction Check During Burst

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(R_{θJC}+R_{θCB}) = 100 + 9.6 \cdot 3.5 = \mathbf{133.6\,°C}$$

This is below the T_j,max of 150 °C, with about 16 K of margin. This drives the following **firmware requirements**:
