# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #18): Power-Stage & Timing Architecture

## 0. Architectural Clarifications

Three points shape the rest of this document:

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In an ArduPilot stack, the H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, or to one MCU per motor on a 4-in-1. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. Section 3 covers the H743 side and Sections 1–2 cover the ESC power stage.
2. **80 A on a nano form factor is a burst rating only.** The analysis below shows a 30×30 mm board sustains roughly 20–25 A continuous per motor. 80 A is achievable for about 2–4 s, and only with good FETs and heavy copper.
3. **All device parameters are stated assumptions.** Substitute your actual BOM datasheet values into the equations. The structure of the model holds; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes trapezoidal six-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. During each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for duty D and the low-side (synchronous rectifier) for 1−D.
- **Commutated return leg:** the low-side conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of D. Synchronous rectification makes the PWM leg look like one FET that is always on.

### 1.2 Ripple contribution

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\,f_{sw}} \;\xrightarrow{D=0.5}\; \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple term is negligible (<0.1 %) at 48 kHz. This is one benefit of the high switching frequency.
