## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Bridge current path (6-step, complementary/synchronous PWM)

At every instant two legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for D, LS conducts synchronously for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** LS on for the full sector.

The motor current therefore always flows through exactly **two** channels in series. Ignoring dead time, this gives:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Each leg rotates through PWM-leg, static-leg and floating roles every 120° electrical. Averaged over a rotation, the loss is shared across the 6 devices:

$$P_{cond,dev} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3}$$

**Caveat (instantaneous hotspot):** within a sector, the static LS carries the full I²R. At 80 A and 125 °C that is 14.4 W for one sector, about 1/(6·f_e) long. With f_e ≈ 1–2 kHz, that is ≈100 µs, shorter than the device die τ (~1 ms). So the average model is valid. It is **not** valid at stall or low-speed high-torque, where one device carries 14.4 W continuously.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond,ESC | P_cond,dev |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | 26.4 W | 4.4 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** | **4.8 W** |

### 1.4 Shunt and copper losses

- Shunt: P = I²R = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per ESC**. Consider 0.25 mΩ, or drop to 0.2 mΩ with a higher-gain amplifier.
- Copper: a 2 oz plane has ≈0.25 mΩ/□. The effective path is ≈0.3 mΩ including vias, giving ≈ **1.9 W per ESC**.

### 1.5 Thermal runaway criterion

Conduction loss rises with temperature:

$$P(T)=P_0[1+\alpha(T-25)],\qquad T = T_a + R_\theta P$$

Solving:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

The solution is stable only if:

$$\boxed{\alpha\,R_\theta\,P_0 < 1}$$

With P₀ = 3.2 W per device and α = 0.005, R_θ(j-a) must be below **62 K/W per device**. Steady state is not the binding constraint. The binding constraint is the absolute T_j that results, shown in §4.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. There is one turn-on and one turn-off per period, and the LS operates in synchronous rectification.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The LS body diode conducts during dead time, then the HS turns on into it:

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\text{nC}\times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$
