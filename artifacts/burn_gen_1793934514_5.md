# Job #17: STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Power and Timing Model

This model is built on assumed component parameters. Treat every number as a design estimate to be validated on the bench, not as a qualified result. Section 4 concludes that **80 A burst on a nano-format board is limited to roughly 1 second by thermal mass**, and the spec should be worded to reflect that.

## 0. Architecture Baseline and Assumptions

The H743 cannot commutate motors itself. It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32.

| Block | Selection (assumed) |
|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6, SYSCLK 480 MHz, APB1/APB2 timer clock 240 MHz |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, one per motor |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) |
| MOSFETs (×24) | 40 V N-ch, PDFN3.3×3.3 class |
| Bus | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| PCB | 8-layer, 2 oz on all layers, 20×20 mm mount (≈ 26×26 mm outline) |

|---|---|
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.5 mΩ |

40 V parts on 6S leave only about 37 % margin above 25.2 V. A layout with poor commutation-loop inductance will ring past 40 V at 80 A. **I recommend evaluating 60 V parts.** They carry about 1.8× the R_DS(on), and the trade-off is quantified in §1.4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With six-step trapezoidal drive and complementary PWM, two legs are active in each 60° sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts during D·T_sw. The low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies during (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET is held on for the whole sector.

So, ignoring dead time, **phase current always flows through exactly two FET channels in series**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 80² × 3.0 mΩ = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| 125 °C | 2.4 mΩ | 80² × 4.8 mΩ = **30.7 W** | 122.9 W |

### 1.3 Distribution per FET

Each phase is active for 240° of the 360° electrical period:

- 120° as the PWM leg (HS for D, LS for 1−D)
- 120° as the return leg (LS continuously)

Per-FET average loss at 125 °C, assuming D = 0.8:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2R = \tfrac{1}{3}(0.8)(6400)(2.4\text{m}) = 4.10\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = 1.02 + 5.12 = 6.14\ \text{W}$$

Check: 3 × (4.10 + 6.14) = 30.7 W ✓

**The low-side FETs are the thermal bottleneck.** Give them the best via farm and the largest copper pour.

### 1.4 The 60 V alternative

With R_DS(on),25 ≈ 2.7 mΩ:

- P_cond(125 °C) ≈ 80² × 2 × 4.3 mΩ = **55 W per motor**
- This is about 1.8× worse, and roughly halves the burst duration calculated in §4.

### 1.5 Copper losses (often forgotten)

**Phase pad to motor wire:**
- 1 mm wide, 4 layers × 70 µm, ~8 mm long
- R ≈ ρL/A = 1.72e-8 × 0.008 / (1e-3 × 2.8e-4) = **0.49 mΩ**
- Loss: 80² × 0.49 m ≈ **3.1 W per phase-pair path**, about 6 W per motor counting two active phases

**Battery bus:**
- I_bat = Σ I_ph · D ≈ 4 × 80 × 0.8 = **256 A**. This is an extreme figure. Realistic "all four motors at 80 A" occurs only momentarily.
- With ~0.3 mΩ of bus copper: 256² × 0.3 m ≈ **20 W**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, only the PWM leg hard-switches: one turn-on and one turn-off per T_sw. The LS FET switches at zero-voltage (soft), because its body diode is already conducting.

### 2.1 Gate drive currents

Assumptions: FD6288 output at V_drv ≈ 10 V (VCC 12 V), R_g,ext = 10 Ω for turn-on, and 4.7 Ω ∥ diode for turn-off.

$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{10-3.5}{11}=0.59\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}+R_{g,int}}=\frac{3.5}{5.7}=0.61\ \text{A}$$
