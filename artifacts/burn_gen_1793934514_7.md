# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Gate

Three points must be settled before any numbers are trusted.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO/4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (BLHeli_32 is discontinued). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 timer and DMA plan covers DShot only.

2. **An "80A burst × 4" rating on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.** Section 3 shows it holds for about 1–2.5 s. Any spec sheet claiming more is not credible.

3. **All component parameters below are assumed values.** They are representative of 40 V PDFN 5×6 MOSFETs and must be replaced with your actual BOM datasheet values. The model is the deliverable. The numbers show its magnitude.

### Assumed Parameters

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, fully charged |
| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current per motor |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | ESC PWM frequency |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ | At V_gs = 10 V |
| k_T | 1 + 0.0045·(T_j − 25) | R_ds(on) temperature coefficient |
| Q_g / Q_gd / Q_gs2 | 60 / 10 / 5 nC | Gate charges |
| Q_oss / Q_rr | 40 / 50 nC | Output and reverse-recovery charge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** high-side (HS) conducts for D·T, synchronous low-side (LS) for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** LS fully on.

### Per Channel

The current always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### Per FET (averaged over an electrical cycle)

Each FET is in the switching position for 120° and in the return position for 120°. For PWM on the high side:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,[(1-D) + 1]\, I^2 R_{ds}$$

At burst (D → 1), both are ≈ ⅓·I²R, so the load spreads evenly across the 6 FETs.

### Numerical Result

Take T_j = 125 °C, so k_T = 1.45 and R_ds = 1.74 mΩ:

- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.74×10⁻³ = **22.3 W per channel**
- That is **≈ 3.7 W per FET**.

### Copper Loss (often ignored, significant here)

Assume a 2 oz copper, 4 mm wide, 10 mm phase path with R ≈ 0.6 mΩ per path segment. Two segments add 2 × 6400 × 0.6 mΩ ≈ **7.7 W per channel**.

Mitigation: stitch the phase pours on all layers. This roughly halves the copper loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### Transition Times

$$t_r \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

### Loss Components (per channel; one leg switches at a time)

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.97 W** |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | **0.024 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | **0.061 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dead·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (HS + LS) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | **0.058 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 1.72 W per channel** |

### Design Notes

- **Dead-time loss is about 35 % of switching loss.** Trim t_dead to about 50 ns once you measure the driver's propagation skew. That saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- **Switching frequency trade-off:** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is ≈ 0.86 W, but current ripple rises:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V(1-D)D}{L_{ph}\, f}$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### Total Burst Dissipation

| Contribution | Per channel | × 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.3 W | 89.2 W |
| FET switching | 1.7 W | 6.9 W |
| Copper | 7.7 W | 30.8 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 127 W** (≈ 108 W with stitched copper) |

### Steady State: Infeasible

A 20×20 mm, 4-layer board under prop wash has R_th,board→amb of about 8–12 K/W:

ΔT = 108 W × 10 K/W ≈ **1080 K**

Continuous 80A on all four channels is physically impossible on this board.

### Transient (lumped capacitance)

Board mass is about 2.2 g:

- FR4: 1.4 g × 1.1 J/(g·K)
- Copper: 0.6 g × 0.385 J/(g·K)
- Plus FETs and connectors

This gives C_th ≈ 2.5 J/K and a time constant τ = R_th·C_th ≈ 25 s ≫ burst duration, so the board heating is close to linear:

$$\frac{dT_{board}}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{108}{2.5} \approx 43\ \text{K/s}$$

### Junction Rise Above Board

Use Z_th(1 s) of about 1.0 K/W:

ΔT_jc ≈ 3.7 W × 1.0 ≈ 4 K

### Allowable Burst Duration

With T_amb = 40 °C, T_j,max = 150 °C, and 10 K derating margin:

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{150-10-40-4}{43} \approx 2.2\ \text{s}$$

### Iterate R_ds(T_j)

1. Start from the 125 °C value above.
2. Recompute k_T at the predicted T_j and recompute P.
3. The model converges in about 3 passes to roughly **2.0 s for all four channels at 80A**.
4. A single motor at 80A gives about **6–8 s**.

**Specify the rating as "80A / 2 s burst, 4 channels simultaneous."**

### Bulk Capacitor Ripple

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

This peaks at 40 A per channel at D = 0.5. Because the channels' PWM is not phase-aligned, the aggregate is about √4 × 40 = 80 A rms worst case.

You need:

- A low-ESR external electrolytic (1000 µF, 35 V) at the battery pads.
- At least 4 × 10 µF X7R 50 V ceramics per channel, placed at the half-bridges.
