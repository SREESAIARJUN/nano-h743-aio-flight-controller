# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture Derivation (Job #1)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Design Warning

The power-stage numbers below depend on the MOSFET you choose. I have used representative parameters for a 40 V, 5×6 mm trench MOSFET. Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations in §1–§2 before releasing the design. The structure of the math stays the same.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_ds25 | 0.65 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature multiplier @ T_j ≈ 125 °C | k_T | 1.55 |
| Gate-drain charge | Q_gd | 9 nC |
| Gate-source charge (post-threshold) | Q_gs2 | 4 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage @ 80 A | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Effective gate drive current (plateau) | I_g | ~0.6 A |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θjc | 1.2 K/W |
| Board form factor | — | 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack, 8-layer, 2 oz inner power planes |

**Design warning.** 80 A is physically only a *burst* rating on a nano form factor. §3 shows a continuous rating of roughly 25–30 A per motor, and a burst window of a few seconds. Label the product accordingly; "80A" marketing without a time qualifier will cause field failures.

**Architecture note.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the H743 does not commutate the motors. Each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g., an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743's job is to generate DShot frames and capture the bidirectional eRPM telemetry. §4 covers the H743 side; §1–§3 cover the ESC power stage.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Phase vs. battery current

Rate the switches by phase current, not battery current:

$$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{ph}$$

At a duty of D = 0.6, an 80 A phase current is only about 48 A from the battery. Logging battery current alone will understate MOSFET stress at partial throttle.

### 1.2 Synchronous six-step (complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, current flows through two conducting legs. These are the PWM leg (high-side for D, synchronous low-side for 1−D) and the static leg (one FET fully on). With synchronous rectification, there is always one FET in each leg of the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx k_T R_{ds25}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.65 \times 1.55 \approx 1.0\ \text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.0\times10^{-3} = 12.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

(This would be 8.3 W cold. The positive temperature coefficient creates a thermal feedback loop; see §3.4.)

### 1.3 Copper / interconnect loss

Assume about 0.3 mΩ of path resistance per phase, covering planes, vias, and solder joints. Two phases conduct at once:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} \approx 3.8\ \text{W per motor}$$

Do not neglect this term. On nano boards it is often 25–30 % of the conduction loss.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs per motor carries current for 120° electrical (1/3 of the time). The average loss per FET is:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{12.8}{6} \approx 2.1\ \text{W}$$

The PWM-switching high-side FETs additionally carry the switching loss from §2, which raises them to about 2.5–3 W.
