## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

### 0.1 Who does what

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. This is a two-tier system:

| Tier | Device | Role | Relevant clocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), ArduPilot | Attitude control, DShot600 command and eRPM capture | 480 MHz core, 240 MHz timer clock |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), AM32 firmware | 6-step / sinusoidal commutation, **48 kHz PWM**, eRPM reply | 120 MHz (AT32) |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q, or similar 3-phase half-bridge driver | Bootstrap high-side drive, roughly 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink | n/a |

The 48 kHz switching losses belong to the ESC MCUs and power stage. The H743 owns only the DShot timing and DMA problem.

### 0.2 Findings to settle before layout

1. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is thermally a 1–2 second rating, not continuous.** The model in §4 predicts:
   - Tj ≈ 118 °C after 1 s.
   - Tj ≈ 148 °C after 2 s, at 40 °C ambient with all four channels at 80 A.
   - A defensible continuous rating is **≈25 A per channel**.
2. **Total battery current at full burst is 320 A.** Board bus resistance and bulk-capacitor ripple become first-order problems, not footnotes (§3.6, §3.7).
