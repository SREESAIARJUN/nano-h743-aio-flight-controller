## 0. Architectural Findings

The math below supports three conclusions that shape everything else:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The 48 kHz PWM, dead time, and BEMF/zero-cross control run on four ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks DShot600 to those MCUs. Generating 48 kHz complementary PWM for 12 half-bridges from the H743 while also running the flight stack is possible in principle, but it gives up firmware ecosystem support and isolation of failures.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano-format board is thermally limited to seconds, not tens of seconds.** With all four channels at 80 A, total dissipation is about 85–90 W. A 25–30 mm board has roughly 3–5 J/K of heat capacity and roughly 10 K/W to air. The rating must be specified as **80 A for ≤ 2 s (single channel ≤ 5 s), 45–55 A continuous with prop-wash cooling**. Section 2.6 derives this.
3. **Input capacitor ripple current, not the MOSFETs, is the next failure point.** At 80 A and D = 0.5 it reaches 40 A RMS per channel. An external low-ESR bulk capacitor is mandatory.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction path in 6-step synchronous mode

In any commutation sector, current flows through exactly two device positions:

- **Phase X (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Phase Y (return phase):** the low-side FET is held on for 100 % of the sector.

So at every instant two R_DS(on) are in series with the motor current, independent of D (ripple neglected because L_motor is large relative to 1/f_sw).

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

**Per-device distribution.** Each phase is active in 4 of 6 sectors: 2 sectors as the PWM phase and 2 as the return phase. The worst-case device is the low-side FET:

$$P_{LS,avg} = I^2 R_{DS}\left[\tfrac{2}{6}(1) + \tfrac{2}{6}(1-D)\right]$$

At D → 0 this gives about 4.9 W. At D = 1 the high-side carries the equivalent. Design each device for **≈5 W conduction average**, with an instantaneous peak of 7.7 W while conducting.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase switches in each sector: one high-side/low-side complementary pair.

**Gate current during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{dr}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{7}{6.2} = 1.13\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 0.48\ \text{A}$$

**Voltage transition times:**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 7.1\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,off}} = 16.5\ \text{ns}$$

Current-commutation time is added via the Q_gs2 term. Use a total overlap of about **24 ns**.
