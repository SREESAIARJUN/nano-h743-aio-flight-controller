# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Notes

**System partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU. This design uses 4× AT32F421 running AM32, which provides bidirectional DShot and adjustable PWM frequency. The H743 talks to them over four DShot600 lines.

**Feasibility of 80 A on a nano board.** Treat 80 A per motor (phase current) on a nano footprint (≈30×30 mm board, 25.5 mm mounting) as **burst only, for about 2 s or less**. The math below shows why. Continuous rating will land around 35–45 A depending on airflow.

**Parameter sources.** Every device parameter below is a representative value from the stated part class. Replace each one with the values from your selected datasheet before release. The equations stay the same.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The drive is 120° trapezoidal (six-step) with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, one phase high-side (HS) and one phase low-side (LS) carry current.
- During PWM off-time, the active-leg LS FET (synchronous rectification) replaces the HS FET.
- The current path therefore always contains **two R_ds(on)**, independent of duty cycle D.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence of on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS Current

Each of the 6 FETs per motor conducts within one 120° electrical window. Inside that window:

- **Active-PWM FET:** I_rms² = I² · D/3
- **Sync-rectifying partner:** I_rms² = I² · (1−D)/3
- **Static-on FET** (the LS of the returning phase): I_rms² = I²/3

Summing over the six FETs:

$$\sum I_{rms}^2 = I^2 \cdot \tfrac{2}{3}\cdot 3 \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 3 = 2I^2$$

This confirms the 2·R_ds result.

The **worst single FET** is the static-on LS FET, plus its own PWM window. Upper bound:

$$I_{rms,FET,max} = I\sqrt{2/3} = 65.3\text{ A}$$

### 1.3 Self-Consistent Iteration (T_j ↔ R_ds)

| Iteration | T_j assumed | R_ds | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 80²·2·1.0 m = **12.8 W** |
| 1 | 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | **17.6 W** |
| 2 (converged, see §3) | 95 °C | 1.35 mΩ | **17.3 W** |

### 1.4 PCB Copper Loss

Phase and bus copper adds loss. Assumptions: 4 power layers of 2 oz copper, effective path ≈ 0.25 mΩ per phase loop, hot.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6\text{ W per motor}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **active-PWM HS FET hard-switches**. The sync LS FET turns on after its body diode conducts, so it switches at near-zero voltage (ZVS-like).

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

Recovery of the LS body diode is dumped into the HS FET at turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}\cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

The factor of 2 accounts for the C_oss of the HS and LS FETs in the leg.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I_{ph}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is the dominant secondary term. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per motor, but requires a verified shoot-through margin.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}\cdot 2\ \text{FETs switching} = 60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.058\text{ W per motor}$$

For all 4 motors this is 0.23 W, which the 10 V gate-supply regulator must provide.
