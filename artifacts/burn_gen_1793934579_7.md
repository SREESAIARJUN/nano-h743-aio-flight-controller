# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #67): Power-Stage, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architecture
This document assumes the conventional split architecture:

- **STM32H743 (ArduPilot)** generates four bi-directional DShot600 streams.
- **Four ESC MCUs** run AM32. A representative choice is the AT32F421K8U7, which needs one per motor. Each ESC MCU generates its own 48 kHz complementary PWM and runs commutation.

The H743 does not drive gates directly. A single H743 cannot run four sensorless 6-step commutation loops at 48 kHz alongside ArduPilot. If you intended a direct-drive design, the DShot section becomes irrelevant and the power section still applies.

### Physical assumptions
"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is aggressive. Section 4 shows that the limiting factor is board heat capacity, not the MOSFETs. I model a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** and give the 20×20 numbers alongside.

## 1. Operating Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### Commutation
AM32 uses 120° commutation. At any instant, one phase is high, one is low, and one is floating. Each FET therefore conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

Within a sector, the PWM'd high-side is complementary-switched against its own low-side (synchronous rectification). The opposite phase's low-side stays fully on.

### Current relationships
Neglecting ripple:

$$
I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph} \qquad\qquad I_{FET,rms}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 \;\;(\text{averaged over electrical cycle, per FET})
$$

### Current ripple
Phase current ripple at 48 kHz depends on motor phase-phase inductance L_pp:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{pp}\, f_{sw}} = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{pp}f_{sw}}
$$

For a 2207-class motor with L_pp ≈ 20 µH at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} \approx 6.6\ \text{A p-p}
$$

The RMS correction is ΔI²/12, which is negligible next to 80 A. This is why 48 kHz is chosen: low ripple and quiet operation, paid for in switching loss.

## 2. Conduction Losses at 80 A Burst

### MOSFET channel
Two FETs carry I_ph at every instant:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot} = 2\cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

The per-FET average is 1/3 of the per-conducting loss:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac13\, I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is 9.6 W. That peak matters for the junction transient (§4.1).

### Temperature dependence (closed loop)
Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, conduction loss and junction temperature must be solved together:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + R_{\theta JC} \cdot P_{FET}(T_j)
$$

The loop is stable (no thermal runaway) as long as:

$$
\alpha\, R_{\theta JC}\, P_{FET} \ll 1
$$

Here 0.0059 × 1 × 9.6 ≈ 0.057, so the loop is stable.

### Copper path (often forgotten)
With the battery pad to FET to phase pad path at 3 × 2 oz layers in parallel and ~15 mm length, R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per path. Two paths carry current at once:

$$
P_{Cu,motor} \approx 2\cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ \text{W}
$$

Copper loss is about 20% of FET conduction loss. Do not omit it.

### Shunt (if per-motor current sense)
A 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt carrying the motor's DC current at D ≈ 1:

$$
P = 80^2 \cdot 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}
