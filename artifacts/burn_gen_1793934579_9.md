## 0. Architecture Decisions and Their Consequences

Some architectural points need settling before the math, because the numbers depend on them.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and *sends* bidirectional DShot600. Motor commutation at 48 kHz runs on per-channel ESC MCUs. This design uses **4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32**. A single-MCU option (one STM32G4 driving all four bridges) exists, but AM32/BLHeli_32 tooling and ArduPilot's BDShot/ESC-telemetry path assume one MCU per motor.
2. **"80 A burst" is per channel, for a bounded time.** The thermal analysis below shows that a nano footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm mounting) **cannot sustain 4×80 A**. 80 A is a *transient* rating that the thermal capacitance must absorb. I give the burst-duration limit explicitly.
3. **Battery: 6S.** This gives 25.2 V max and 22.2 V nominal, so 40 V MOSFETs are mandatory (30 V parts lack margin for regenerative and inductive spikes on 6S).
4. **Commutation: 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM.** This is AM32's default. The loss model below is specific to this scheme.

**Reference MOSFET parameter set.** The model is symbolic; the numbers assume a representative 40 V, 5×6 mm (or 3.3×3.3 mm dual) class part. **Substitute the actual datasheet values for your chosen FET.**

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any 60° commutation sector, two phases carry the full phase current I:
- **Phase A (PWM'd leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET conducts for (1−D)−2t_dt·f_sw as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B (grounded leg):** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

The current therefore always flows through **exactly two channels**, regardless of D (dead time excluded):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \left[\,2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\,\right]
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.90 × 1.50 = **1.35 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

Phase current RMS includes PWM ripple:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$
