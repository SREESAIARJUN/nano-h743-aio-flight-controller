# FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion for a 3-Axis Gimbal

I don't have the specifications for "Job #44", so this design rests on the assumptions in §0. Every gain is derived symbolically, so you can substitute your real motor and mechanical parameters.

---

## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal PMSM, e.g. 12N14P: N_s = 12 slots, p = 7 pole pairs |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701), used for commutation |
| Inverter | 3-phase, 2- or 3-shunt current sensing, V_bus ≈ 12–25 V |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 (STM32G4/H7) with single-precision FPU |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, SPI at 24 MHz, ODR 8 kHz, FIFO or DRDY interrupt |

**Loop rates:**

| Loop | Rate | Trigger | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | 20 kHz | PWM centre (ADC trigger) | ω_c ≈ 2π·1.5 kHz |
| Velocity | 4 kHz | IMU DRDY (decimated 8k→4k) | ω_v ≈ 2π·80 Hz |
| Angle | 1 kHz | — | ω_p ≈ 2π·15 Hz |
| Attitude estimator | 4 kHz | — | — |

**Bandwidth separation rule:** each loop should be at least 5× faster than the loop around it (ω_c ≥ 5ω_v ≥ 25ω_p), and each sampling rate at least 10× its loop bandwidth.

**Sensor roles:**
- The encoder is used for commutation (θ_e) and for the cogging lookup (θ_m).
- The camera-mounted IMUs provide the velocity feedback (gyro) and angle feedback (attitude). Stabilisation is inertial, not joint-space.

## 1. FOC Current Loop (Inner, 20 kHz)

### 1.1 Motor model in the rotor (dq) frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2}\,p\left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \;\xrightarrow{\text{SPM},\ L_d = L_q}\; \tau_e = K_t\, i_q, \qquad K_t = \tfrac{3}{2} p \lambda_m
$$

Here ω_e = p·ω_m and θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_offset. The offset is found at calibration by applying v_d with θ_e = 0 and reading the encoder.

### 1.2 Transforms (amplitude-invariant)

**Clarke:**

$$
i_\alpha = i_a, \qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt{3}} \qquad (\text{using } i_a + i_b + i_c = 0)
$$

**Park:**

$$
i_d = i_\alpha \cos\theta_e + i_\beta \sin\theta_e
$$

$$
i_q = -i_\alpha \sin\theta_e + i_\beta \cos\theta_e
$$

**Inverse Park:**

$$
v_\alpha = v_d \cos\theta_e - v_q \sin\theta_e
$$

$$
v_\beta = v_d \sin\theta_e + v_q \cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.3 Current PI tuning (pole–zero cancellation)

With decoupling feedforward, the plant reduces to G_i(s) = 1 / (L s + R). Place the PI zero on the electrical pole:

$$
C_i(s) = K_p + \frac{K_i}{s}, \qquad K_p = L\,\omega_c, \qquad K_i = R\,\omega_c
$$

The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
\frac{i}{i^*} = \frac{\omega_c}{s + \omega_c}
$$

Constraint: ω_c · T_s ≤ 0.2, because the PWM transport delay is about 1.5 T_s. At 20 kHz this gives ω_c ≤ 2π·640 Hz for a strict design; up to about 2π·1.5 kHz is acceptable if you compensate the delay with θ_e + 1.5·ω_e·T_s.

### 1.4 Decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^* - i_d) - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^* - i_q) + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

The reference i_d* = 0 (SPM motor, no field weakening needed for a gimbal).

### 1.5 Voltage limiting

Limit the voltage vector to the linear SVPWM hexagon's inscribed circle:

$$
|v| \le V_{max} = \frac{V_{bus}}{\sqrt{3}} \cdot m, \qquad m \approx 0.95
$$

Use d-axis priority:

$$
v_d \leftarrow \text{sat}(v_d, \pm V_{max}), \qquad v_q \leftarrow \text{sat}\!\left(v_q, \pm\sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}\right)
$$

### 1.6 SVPWM by min–max zero-sequence injection

This is mathematically equivalent to 7-segment SVPWM.

Phase voltages:

$$
v_a = v_\alpha, \qquad v_b = -\tfrac{1}{2} v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} v_\beta, \qquad v_c = -\tfrac{1}{2} v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} v_\beta
$$

Zero-sequence offset:

$$
v_0 = -\frac{\max(v_a, v_b, v_c) + \min(v_a, v_b, v_c)}{2}
$$

Duty cycles:

$$
d_x = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$
