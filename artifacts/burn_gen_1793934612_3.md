## 0. Assumptions and Timing Architecture

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC, 12N14P (pole pairs $p = 7$), $R \approx 5\,\Omega$, $L \approx 2\,\text{mH}$ |
| Commutation encoder | 14-bit magnetic (AS5048A / MT6701 class) |
| MCU | STM32G4 / H7 class with FPU, center-aligned PWM |
| IMUs | 2× ICM-42688-P on the camera stage, CLKIN-synchronized, ODR 8 kHz |

The control rates are separated so that each outer loop is much slower than the loop inside it:

| Loop | Rate | Target bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| Current (FOC) | $f_c = 20$ kHz | ~1.5 kHz |
| Velocity | $f_v = 4$ kHz | ~150 Hz |
| Angle / attitude | $f_a = 1$ kHz | ~20–30 Hz |

```
IMU1 ─┐  AAF+notch (on-chip)   ┌─────────────┐  ω̂,q̂  ┌─────────┐ ω_ref ┌──────────┐ iq_ref ┌─────────┐  PWM
IMU2 ─┴─► align/lever-arm/fuse ─► dyn. notch  ├──────►│ Angle P ├──────►│ Vel PI   ├──┬────►│ FOC PI  ├──────► motor
                                └─────────────┘       └─────────┘       └──────────┘  │     └─────────┘
                                                                       cogging FF ────┘ (+ accel FF)
encoder θ_m ─► θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_off (commutation + cogging lookup)
```

Two design rules follow from this structure:

- **Stabilization feedback comes from the IMU, not the encoder.** The camera's inertial rate is the quantity being controlled.
- **The encoder is used only for commutation and cogging compensation.**

## 1. Field-Oriented Current Loop

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with $i_a + i_b + i_c = 0$):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a, \qquad i_\beta = \frac{i_a + 2 i_b}{\sqrt{3}}
$$

Park transform, using the electrical angle $\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{\text{off}}$:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

Inverse Park transform:

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e, \qquad
v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 1.2 Plant Model and PI Design

The dq-frame plant model is:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q, \qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

**Decoupling feedforward.** Adding these terms removes the cross-coupling and back-EMF:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q, \qquad
v_q^{ff} = \omega_e (L i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

With decoupling applied, each axis reduces to $G(s) = 1/(Ls + R)$.

**Pole-zero cancellation tuning.** Choosing

$$
K_p = L\,\omega_{cc}, \qquad K_i = R\,\omega_{cc}
$$

gives a closed loop of $\omega_{cc}/(s + \omega_{cc})$.

**Bandwidth limit.** The PWM update adds a delay of about $1.5\,T_s$, so keep $\omega_{cc} \le 2\pi f_c / 10 \cdot 0.75$, which is roughly 1.5 kHz at 20 kHz. Gimbal motors have high $R$. If you skip current sensing, voltage-mode control ($v_q = R\,i_q^* + \omega_e\lambda_m$) is a valid fallback.

### 1.3 Voltage Limiting and SVPWM

Limit the voltage vector to the inscribed circle, $|v| \le V_{dc}/\sqrt{3}$, with priority given to $v_d$:

$$
v_q^{\max} = \sqrt{(V_{dc}/\sqrt3)^2 - v_d^2}
$$

Space-vector modulation uses min-max zero-sequence injection:

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta), \qquad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big), \qquad
D_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$
