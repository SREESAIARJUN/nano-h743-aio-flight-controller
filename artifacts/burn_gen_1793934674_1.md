# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): Electro-Thermal Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller in this design.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Four dedicated ESC MCUs run AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generate the 48 kHz gate PWM. I use AT32F421 for these, the standard AM32 target.

**Form-factor warning.** Twenty-four 5×6 mm FETs plus drivers cannot fit a 20×20 nano board. The 80 A burst figure requires 30.5×30.5 mm at minimum. Section 3 shows that, even at that size, 80 A is only a short-duration rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, current always flows through two conducting switches:
- the PWM leg's high-side FET, or its low-side FET during the off-time (synchronous rectification), and
- the low-side FET of the return leg, which is on continuously.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction
$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\,\mu H \cdot 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is negligible (<0.1%) at 80 A. It matters more at light load.
