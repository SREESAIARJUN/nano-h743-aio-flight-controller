# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production "AIO/stack" design that runs ArduPilot, the system splits into two parts:

| Subsystem | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller (FC) | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, emits bidirectional DShot600 |
| 4-in-1 ESC | 4× AT32F421 (or STM32G071 / EFM8BB21) | AM32 or BLHeli_32/S | 6-step commutation, gate drive, eRPM telemetry |

Running four sensorless FOC/6-step loops plus ArduPilot on one H743 is technically possible. However, ArduPilot does not support it, and it is not done in practice. The model below treats the H743 as the FC and the 4-in-1 as the power stage.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant two phases conduct and one floats:

- **PWM on-time (duty D):** current flows high-side A → motor → low-side B.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** current flows low-side A (synchronous rectification) → motor → low-side B.

Either way, exactly **two channel resistances are in series** with the phase current. The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle, apart from dead-time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

Ripple correction: I_rms² = I_dc²(1 + r²/12), where r is the peak-peak ripple ratio. With a low-inductance racing motor at 48 kHz, r ≈ 0.2, so the correction is +0.3%. It is neglected below.

### 1.2 Electro-thermal fixed point

Because R_ds rises with T_j and T_j rises with loss, the loss must be solved iteratively:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) = 2I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_{pcb} + \tfrac{1}{2}P_{ch}\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CS})$$

At I = 80 A:

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond per channel (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 100 | 1.41 | 18.1 |
| 125 | 1.55 | 19.8 |

**Use 19.8 W per channel at a 125 °C design point. Across all four channels this is ≈ 79 W.**

Phase mapping: the two conducting FETs share this loss. Commutation rotates it across six FETs per electrical period. At eRPM greater than a few hundred Hz, the thermal mass of the die averages it out, so the hot-spot FET sees roughly P/2 ≈ 9.9 W while conducting.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side switch hard-switches. Its complementary low-side switch turns on with zero voltage after the body diode has already conducted during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched high-side)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

This loss mainly lands in the high-side FET.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Design note:** this term is 63% as large as the overlap loss. Trimming dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns (if the FD6288 plus FET Q_gd allows it) saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs/leg} = 50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| Q_oss | 0.04 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Gate (driver side) | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 1.73** |

Scaling law: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 0.85 W per channel but raises current ripple and acoustic noise.
