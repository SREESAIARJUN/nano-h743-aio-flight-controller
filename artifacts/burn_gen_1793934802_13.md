# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600

**Architecture assumption:** The H743 does not commutate motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to a 4-in-1 ESC. The ESC has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The model below is split along that boundary.

**Feasibility flag:** "80A burst" at nano scale (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mounting) is aggressive. I assume **80 A per channel, 6S, burst ≤ 2–3 s**. The thermal model shows this is the binding constraint. If the 80 A figure is actually the total battery current, every loss below drops by about 16× and the design becomes easy.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Bridge conduction (6-step with synchronous rectification)

At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs in series. These are the high-side (HS) of the driven phase and the low-side (LS) of the return phase. During PWM-off time, the LS of the PWM leg (synchronous rectifier) replaces the HS. This gives a duty-independent total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Per-FET losses, with each leg conducting for 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$P_{HS}=\tfrac13 I^2 R\,D,\qquad P_{LS,pwm}=\tfrac13 I^2R(1-D),\qquad P_{LS,return}=\tfrac13 I^2 R$$

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | P_cond per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2·6400·1.0 m = 12.8 W | 51 W |
| 125 °C | 2·6400·1.6 m = **20.5 W** | **82 W** |

**Average per FET:** 20.5 / 6 ≈ 3.4 W.

**Instantaneous peak per FET** (while conducting): I²R ≈ 10.2 W.

**Electrical frequency check:** a 14-pole motor at 30 krpm gives f_e = (30000/60)·7 = 3.5 kHz, a period of about 0.29 ms. This is much shorter than the die-plus-solder thermal time constant (several ms), so the per-FET average is valid. Ripple adds roughly +2 K.

### 2.4 Phase-current RMS correction (FOC/sinusoidal mode)

If the ESC runs sinusoidal commutation with I_pk = 80 A, then I_rms = 56.6 A per phase. The conduction loss becomes:

$$P_{cond} = 3 \cdot I_{rms}^2 R = 3 \cdot 3200 \cdot 1.6\text{ m} = 15.4\text{ W/channel}$$

This is about 25% lower than the trapezoidal case.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return-leg LS is static.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (HS, hard turn-on and turn-off)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, dissipated in the HS)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.4 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 3.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

Dead-time loss is the second-largest switching term. Cutting t_dt to 30 ns saves 0.12 W per channel, but only if the gate driver's propagation-delay matching allows it.

### 3.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 0.97+0.05+0.06+0.31 \approx \mathbf{1.4\ W/channel}$$

Switching loss is about 6% of conduction loss. **At 80 A this design is conduction-dominated**, so f_sw = 48 kHz costs little. Going to 24 kHz would save only about 0.65 W per channel, while increasing current ripple and audible noise.
