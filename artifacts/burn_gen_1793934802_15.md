## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Flag

### 0.1 Partitioning
ArduPilot runs only on the H743. It does not commutate motors. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore has four dedicated ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.

```
 6S LiPo ─┬─ Bulk caps ─┬─ 4× 3-phase bridges (24 FETs) ─ Motors
          │             └─ 4× ESC MCU (AM32, 48 kHz) ◄── BDShot600 ── STM32H743 (ArduPilot)
          └─ Buck 5V/3A ─ LDO 3.3V ─ H743, IMU, Baro, OSD, RX
```

### 0.2 Feasibility flag
On a "nano" (20×20 mm mount, ~25×25 mm board), 80 A **per motor** is a burst rating of a few seconds at most. Section 3 shows that four motors at 80 A dissipate about 85 W on a board with only about 4 J/K of heat capacity. Treat 80 A as a ≤1–2 s per-motor burst. Plan on about 25–35 A continuous, or move to a 30×30 mm board.

## 2. Loss Model per Motor

### 2.1 Conduction loss in six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification
At every instant two legs carry the phase current I:
- **PWM leg:** current flows through the high-side FET for D·T_sw and the low-side FET (synchronous) for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Fixed leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two channel resistances:

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,\big[\,2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\,\big],\qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,k(T_j)$$

Substituting the values (80 A, 1.5 mΩ hot):

$$P_{cond}=6400\,(2\cdot1.5\,\text{m}\Omega + 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega)=6400 \times 3.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

- The FET-only share is 19.2 W.
- Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries 1/6 of it, about 3.2 W.
- The instantaneous peak per conducting FET is 9.6 W for one commutation sector (60°), which matters for transient T_j (Section 3.3).

**Electro-thermal coupling:** R_DS(on) rises with temperature, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,jc}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

With R_θjc ≈ 1.5 K/W this converges within 2–3 iterations. Section 3 shows it diverges (thermal runaway) only for continuous operation.

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz
Only the PWM-leg high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous FET switches with near-zero voltage after dead time.

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**
$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the sync FET, recovering at HS turn-on):
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V f_{sw}=30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss}=\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V f_{sw}= \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead times per period):
$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs per PWM leg, dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}=2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}= \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$

### 2.3 Totals

| Term | Per motor @ 80 A | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 1.92 W | 7.7 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + Q_g | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.8 W** | **≈ 91 W** |

**Interpretation:**
- At 80 A, the loss is **about 85% conduction**. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.6 W per motor, so 48 kHz is justified for lower acoustic noise and current ripple.
- Every 0.1 mΩ of R_DS(on) is worth about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A.
- Dead-time loss rivals switching loss. Tune dead time down to the minimum safe value; 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 2.4 Efficiency check
At 80 A and a typical burst duty D ≈ 0.9, output power is about V·D·I ≈ 25.2·0.9·80 ≈ 1.81 kW per motor. Efficiency is about 1 − 22.8/1814 ≈ **98.7%**. The problem is not efficiency but power density.
