# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #43): Electrical, Thermal and Timing Design Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Design Warnings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and that MCU produces the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model is split along that boundary.

**Baseline assumptions.** Change these and re-run the equations.

| Symbol | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V (6S full) | Worst case for switching loss |
| I_burst | 80 A per motor | Phase/DC current during burst |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | AM32 `PWM_FREQ = 48` |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_ds(on),25 = 0.90 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V, Q_g ≈ 90 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, C_oss,eff ≈ 1.5 nF, R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W (5×6 or 3.3×3.3 clip package) | Representative class; substitute your datasheet |
| Gate driver | 3-phase, ~1.5 A source/sink, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns at the switch node | e.g. FD6288Q-class |
| Dead time | t_dt = 300 ns per edge | Typical AM32 setting |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz inner and outer | A 20 × 20 "nano" cannot reach the burst durations below |

> **Design warning.** "80 A burst" on a nano-format 4-in-1 is a marketing-class number. The model below shows it is a **2–4 second** capability under real cooling. Continuous capability is roughly 25–35 A per motor. Specify both numbers on the datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.90 \times 1.45 = 1.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path (trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

Consider a single 60° commutation sector. Phase A is PWM'd and phase B low-side is held on.

- **ON interval (fraction D):** current flows through Q_AH and then Q_BL.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** current freewheels through Q_AL (synchronous) and then Q_BL.

Exactly **two** FETs carry I at every instant, independent of D. So:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.31\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.8\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over a full electrical cycle the six FETs share the loss equally in the average:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} = \mathbf{2.8\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak in the always-on low-side FET is higher:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{ds} = 8.4\ \text{W}$$

This peak lasts for a 120° window. At low eRPM, with a slow commutation period, the junction follows it. Use 8.4 W for the transient junction check at startup or stall.

### 1.4 Other copper paths (often forgotten)

| Path | Model | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + pour (~0.4 mΩ effective) | I²·R | 2.6 W per motor |
| Current shunt | I_tot²·R_sh | See note below |

**Shunt note.** If a single total-current shunt is used, the burst current is I_tot = 4 × 80 = 320 A.

- A 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates 320² × 0.25 m = **25.6 W**. This is not acceptable.
- Use **0.1 mΩ**: 10.2 W burst, 4–5 mV full-scale per 50 A, with a gain-of-50 amplifier.
- Alternatively, estimate current in ESC firmware and omit the shunt.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET switches at near-zero V_ds after its body diode conducts.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Refined form, with the driver-limited edge time set by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r}\approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext})}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

Example: Q_gd = 15 nC, total R = 6 Ω, V_drv − V_pl = 10 − 4.5 V gives t_r ≈ 16 ns. This is consistent with the 20 ns assumption.

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{dt} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 600\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

> Dead time is as large a loss as switching itself. Dropping t_dt from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per motor at 80 A. Only do this after measuring shoot-through margin on the real layout.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

The recovery current also adds turn-on overlap in the high-side FET. Fold this in by taking about +10 % on P_V
