## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, two legs of a motor bridge are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

Ignoring dead time, the per-bridge conduction loss is:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,\big] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This is **independent of duty cycle**, because synchronous rectification removes the diode path. Dead-time diode loss is handled separately in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.81\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss (often ignored, and it shouldn't be)

Each motor channel carries current through three paths:

- the phase pad,
- the FET drain/source copper,
- the shared bus.

Assume about 0.25 mΩ effective per motor current path (2 oz copper, short pours, solder joints). Then:

$$
P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = 1.6\ \text{W per motor}
$$

### 1.4 Electro-thermal fixed point and runaway criterion

Let P₂₅ be the loss evaluated at 25 °C and R_θ the effective thermal resistance from junction to ambient:

$$
T_j = T_a + R_\theta\,P_{25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
\boxed{\,T_j^{*} = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_{25}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_{25}\,\alpha}\,}
$$
