# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): Architecture & Analysis Package

**Rev A: Preliminary design-basis document**

> **Three design facts govern everything below.**
> 1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** The 48 kHz PWM, gate drive and commutation run on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to them. Every number below assumes this split.
> 2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80A per motor on 6S (25.2 V maximum).** At that rating, a 30.5 mm-class board is thermally limited to roughly 1–2 seconds of simultaneous four-motor burst. The math in Section 3 shows why. The rating must be published with a duration and ambient, or it will not survive design review.
> 3. **All component parameters are datasheet-class placeholders.** Replace them with the selected part's values and re-run the model.

## 3. Mathematical Model

### 3.1 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

During each 60° electrical sector, current flows through two phase legs: the PWM leg and the fixed low-side leg. On the PWM leg, current alternates between the high-side FET (duty D) and the low-side FET (duty 1−D, synchronous rectification). Because both paths share the same R_ds, the instantaneous conducting resistance is always two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At an assumed T_j of 100 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.3375 = **1.34 mΩ**

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.34\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.1\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

Copper path loss:

$$P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\ \mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current for 120° of the 360° electrical cycle, so the six FETs share the load roughly equally:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx 17.1/6 = 2.85\ \mathrm{W}$$

The PWM-leg high-side FET is worst case at high duty. Its peak instantaneous dissipation is 80² × 1.34 mΩ = 8.6 W.

**Electro-thermal fixed point.** Because R_ds depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. It diverges (thermal runaway) if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot Z_{th} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha \cdot Z_{th} \ge 1$$

### 3.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard: one high-side turn-on/turn-off per period, with the low side acting as the synchronous rectifier.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{12 - 4}{5.7} = 1.40\ \mathrm{A}$$

Switching transition time per edge:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\ \mathrm{nC}}{1.40\ \mathrm{A}} = 8.6\ \mathrm{ns}$$

Turn-off is slower because the FD6288 sinks current through a lower plateau-to-ground differential, so use t_off ≈ 2·t_on, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 26 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 26\mathrm{n} \times 48\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{1.26\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\mathrm{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

The partner FET's diode also dissipates part of this energy. Treat it as negligible at this Q_rr.

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\mathrm{n} \times 635 \times 48\mathrm{k} = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

This term covers both FETs in the leg.
