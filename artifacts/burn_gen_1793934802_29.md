# Job #38: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC System Model

## 0. Architecture Note and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate the FETs. Each of the four ESC channels has its own MCU (AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed here; BLHeli_32-class parts behave the same). Those ESC MCUs generate the **48 kHz PWM**. The H743 sends **bidirectional DShot600** and receives eRPM telemetry. The math below treats the two domains separately.

**Assumptions.** Replace the placeholder values with your actual BOM datasheet values. Every equation is parameterized, so you can re-plug them.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |

| I_ph | 80 A | Burst phase current per motor |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ | 40 V, 5×6 DFN, V_GS = 10 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Commutation scheme.** AM32/BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

At every instant, current flows through **exactly two FETs in series**:
- During PWM ON, the current path is the high-side FET of leg A, then the motor, then the low-side FET of leg B.
- During PWM OFF, the current path is the low-side FET of leg A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then the low-side FET of leg B.

This gives:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.007/\text{K}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.7) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.04\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{26.1\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg conducts 240° of 360° and shares the load symmetrically. The average per FET is therefore

$$
P_{FET} \approx \frac{26.1}{6} = 4.35\ \text{W}
$$

The instantaneous peak on the always-on low-side FET is 13 W.

**Copper loss.**

$$
P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Battery-side relation.**

$$
I_{dc} = D\cdot I_{ph}
$$

At burst, D ≈ 0.95, so I_dc ≈ 76 A per channel and **≈ 304 A total** from the pack.

**Temperature coupling.** R_ds depends on T_j, so the loss and temperature are coupled. Solve iteratively (Section 3).
