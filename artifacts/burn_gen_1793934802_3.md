# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #26): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Model, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Two premises in the request need correcting before any of the math is useful.

**1. The STM32H743 does not drive the bridges.**
- ArduPilot does not commutate MOSFETs. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
- Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and runs commutation.
- So the 48 kHz switching-loss model applies to the ESC MCU's bridge. The DShot model applies to the H743 ↔ ESC link. These are two separate timing domains.

**2. "80A burst" on a nano board is a transient-only rating.**
- A nano board is 20×20 mm (or a 25.5 mm whoop pattern). Below I show it cannot sustain 80 A per motor in steady state.
- The deliverable is therefore a burst-duration limit (seconds), not a continuous rating.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** In 6-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification:
- During PWM on-time: high-side FET of phase A plus low-side FET of phase B.
- During PWM off-time: low-side FET of phase A (freewheeling, synchronous) plus low-side FET of phase B.

So exactly two FET channels carry I_ph at all times, apart from dead time (covered in §3).

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so it carries the full load for about 1/3 of the time. At high throttle (D → 1) the high-side FETs carry most of it:

$$P_{cond,HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R, \qquad P_{cond,LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(1 + (1-D))\, I^2 R$$

**Numbers at T_j = 25 °C:**
- P_cond = 2 · 6400 · 1.6 mΩ = **20.5 W per motor**

**Numbers at T_j = 110 °C** (R = 1.6 mΩ · 1.4675 = 2.35 mΩ):
- P_cond = **30.1 W per motor**

**Electrothermal stability.** The positive temperature coefficient creates feedback. The system is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta,JA} = 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{\theta,JA} < 1$$

At 80 A this is 0.113 · R_θJA. Runaway therefore occurs if the effective per-motor R_θJA exceeds about 8.9 K/W. A nano board is near or above this in still air. This is the first hard argument for a time-limited burst rating.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg PWMs at a time in 6-step operation, so these losses apply per motor (not per phase).

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Hard-switching V·I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 40 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f_sw (approx.) | 50 nC · 25.2 · 48k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 600 ns · 48k = **1.84 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | 2 · 45 nC · 10 · 48k = **0.04 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 3.44 W per motor** |

Key insights:
- **Dead time dominates switching loss at 48 kHz.** Cutting t_dt from 300 ns to 150 ns saves about 0.9 W per motor, more than any FET swap would.
- **Frequency scaling.** Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves this term, saving about 1.7 W per motor, at the cost of audible noise and current ripple.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Quantity | T_j = 25 °C | T_j = 110 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 20.5 + 3.4 = 23.9 W | 30.1 + 3.6 = 33.7 W |
| 4 motors | **95.6 W** | **134.8 W** |
| Hottest FET (HS, D ≈ 1) | ≈ 3.4 W + 1.2 W (switching share) ≈ 4.6 W | ≈ 6.7 W |

Copper and connection losses must be added on top:
- **Battery copper path.** At 4 × 80 A = 320 A, even 0.1 mΩ of pour/pad resistance adds about 10 W. Realistically, nano XT30/XT60 pads cannot source 320 A.
- **Battery-limited reality.** Simultaneous 4 × 80 A will not happen. Battery and connector limits cap typical bursts near about 4 × 40–50 A. Use the 80 A figure as the per-channel worst case.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Network

```
Pj(FET) ─[RθJC 1.8]─ case ─[Rθ,spread ≈ 12 K/W per FET to board]─ PCB node
PCB node ─[Cpcb]─ GND
PCB node ─[Rθ,board-amb]─ Tamb
```

Board-to-ambient resistance, Rθ,board-amb:
- About 8 K/W in still air.
- About 3 K/W at prop-wash airflow of roughly 5–8 m/s.

### 5.2 Board Heat Capacity

Estimate for a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board including FETs:
- Copper: about 4 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.5 J/K
- FR4 plus components: about 3 g × 1.1 ≈ 3.3 J/K
- **C_pcb ≈ 5 J/K**
