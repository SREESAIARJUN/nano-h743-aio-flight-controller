# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model & Design Package (Job #15)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 acts as the flight controller (FC). It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four separate ESC MCUs, which handle commutation. This document uses **4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32** as the ESC MCUs. If you intend a single-MCU design with the H743 driving 24 gates, that is a separate (and inadvisable) architecture. Tell me and I'll re-derive it.

All numbers below are derived from these stated assumptions. Swap in your datasheet values and the equations still hold.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag up front:** "80 A burst per motor" on a nano-class board is a **phase-current, seconds-scale burst rating only**. Section 3 shows continuous capability is roughly 25–30 A/motor without dedicated airflow. True 20×20 mm boards cannot carry this. Their copper cross-section and connector (XT30/XT60 pads) fail first.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, phase current always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**:

- **PWM ON (fraction D):** high-side of leg A → motor → low-side of leg B.
- **PWM OFF (fraction 1−D):** low-side of leg A (synchronous freewheel) → motor → low-side of leg B.

So, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{on}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{on}(T_j) = R_{on,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0067\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

This gives 1.5 mΩ at 100 °C and 1.75 mΩ at 137 °C. **This is a positive-feedback loop.** Solve it iteratively, or design to the hot value.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_on | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 51.2 W |
| **100 °C** | **1.50 mΩ** | **19.2 W** | **76.8 W** |
| 137 °C | 1.75 mΩ | 22.4 W | 89.6 W |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each switch position conducts 120°, so on average 2 of 6 FETs are in the path at any instant.

- Per-FET average loss: 19.2 W / 6 = **3.2 W**.
- Instantaneous peak loss on a conducting FET: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**.
- At typical burst eRPM (>50 k eRPM, i.e. commutation period <200 µs), the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) averages this out. Use 3.2 W for junction-to-board. The exception is **stall/low-RPM bursts**, where the 9.6 W peak sits on one FET pair. **This is the real failure mode** (desync, prop strike).

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, and significant here)

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace + pad, per motor (2 oz, 8 layers paralleled, ~12 mm) | ≈ 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current shunt, per motor (0.25 mΩ, battery-side) | 0.25 mΩ × I_bat² | I_bat ≈ D·I_ph: ~1.6 W at D = 1 |
| Shared battery bus, 4 × 80 A = 320 A, 0.1 mΩ | 0.1 mΩ | **10.2 W** |

> ⚠️ **320 A battery current is not achievable** through XT60 (≈60 A continuous / ~180 A burst) on a nano board. Realistic simultaneous burst is bounded by the pack and connector. Treat the 4×80 A case as the thermal worst-case envelope, not an operating point.
