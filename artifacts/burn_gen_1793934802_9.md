## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125°C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

Add a parasitic copper/via term per conduction path. On a 20×20 board, R_pcb ≈ 0.3–0.6 mΩ is realistic, so:

$$R_{path} = 2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb}$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° electrical sector, current flows through:

- **Active leg.** The high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side FET (sync rect) for (1−D)·T_sw. Either way, one R_DS is in the path 100% of the time (ignoring dead time).
- **Return leg.** The low-side FET is on continuously.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2\,(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{pcb})$$

At I = 80 A, T_j = 125°C, R_pcb = 0.4 mΩ:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times (4.8 + 0.4)\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{33.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution (worst FET)

Each FET carries current for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Averaged over a revolution, the 6 FETs share the 2R_DS term equally:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2 I^2 R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{6400 \times 4.8\text{m}}{3} = 10.2\ \text{W}$$

The peak instantaneous value (return-leg LS, full sector) is I²·R_DS = **15.4 W**. Use this peak for the junction-to-case transient, because the electrical sector period at low eRPM can exceed the FET's die thermal time constant (~ms).
