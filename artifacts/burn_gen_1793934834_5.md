# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

**Job #9: Design Basis Document, Rev A**

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Reality Check

### 0.1 Architecture
The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own commutation MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 firmware, which handles the 48 kHz PWM. This matches all production H7 AIO boards. Trying to commutate 4 motors from the H743 while also running ArduPilot is not viable.

### 0.3 Reality check (read first)
- **Form factor.** A 20×20 "nano" footprint cannot carry 4 × 80 A. The FET area alone (24 × 3.3×3.3 mm DFN) and the bus copper exceed the board. I have scaled the design to 30.5×30.5 mm. On a true 20×20 board, the honest burst rating is about 35–45 A.
- **Durations.** "80 A burst" must be specified with a duration. The model below returns ≈2–3 s from a 40 °C start.
- **Component values.** All MOSFET parameters are a representative 40 V / 3.3×3.3 DFN class. Before layout freeze, re-run §1–§3 with the selected part's datasheet values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.004\text{–}0.005\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

Representative FET: R_DS,25 = 1.2 mΩ (V_GS = 10 V).

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.2 \times 1.38 = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

I round this to **1.7 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step with synchronous rectification
In each commutation sector:
- The PWM phase's high side conducts for duty D.
- Its low side conducts for (1 − D − 2t_d·f_sw), with the body diode covering the dead time.
- The opposite phase's low side conducts 100%.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\Big[D + (1-D-2t_d f_{sw})\Big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$
