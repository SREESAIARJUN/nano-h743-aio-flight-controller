## 0. Architecture Constraints

Two points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC stack, each motor channel (or pair) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and runs commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This document therefore covers two timer domains:
   - **ESC MCU:** 48 kHz center-aligned complementary PWM. Switching losses live here.
   - **H743:** DShot600 output plus input capture for eRPM telemetry. DMA allocation lives here.

2. **"Nano" and "80A" conflict thermally.** Section 3 shows that 80A burst on all four channels produces about 100 W of dissipation. On a 20×20 mm board this is survivable only for seconds. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm (or at minimum 25.5×25.5) ESC board stacked under a 20×20 FC. The numbers assume a 36×36 mm ESC PCB envelope.
