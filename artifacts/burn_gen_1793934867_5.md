## 0. Scope, Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Three architectural points need correcting before the math, because they change where each calculation applies.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. The H743 sends DShot600 frames and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz figure is an ESC-firmware setting (AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY`).
   - Section 2 (switching losses) applies to the ESC power stage.
   - Section 4 (DMA and timers) applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a transient rating only.** The model below shows that 80 A per channel produces about 160 W of board dissipation. That is survivable for well under a second. Treat the 80 A figure as a ≤1 s burst spec with a firmware current limit, not a continuous rating.
3. **No MOSFET was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN part (parameters below). Substitute datasheet values before sign-off. Every equation is parametric.

**Design point:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge), I_phase = 80 A burst, f_sw = 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous) PWM, trapezoidal 6-step commutation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any commutation step, one phase is high, one is low and one floats. With complementary PWM, the PWM'd leg alternates between its high-side FET (duty D) and its low-side FET (1 − D). Current therefore always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}
$$

where R_Cu is the PCB copper path from bus to motor pad and back.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on): fixed-point iteration

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta JB}
$$
