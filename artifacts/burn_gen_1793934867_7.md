## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in Trapezoidal 6-Step Commutation

At any instant, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high side is on during duty D. The complementary low side conducts the freewheel current during (1 − D) because of synchronous rectification.
- **Return leg:** the low side is on continuously for that 60° sector.

As a result, phase current always flows through exactly **two** R_ds(on) in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

with

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current (low ripple at 48 kHz), I_rms ≈ I_dc ≈ I_ph. In this model, the 80 A burst is treated as the phase current.

### 2.2 Electrothermal Fixed-Point Solution

Because R_ds rises with temperature, losses and junction temperature must be solved together:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,eff}
$$

$$
P_{FET}(T_j) = \tfrac{1}{3}\, I^2\, R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\cdot k_{share}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the electrical cycle when HS and LS duty are combined. The factor k_share ≈ 1 for the average case; use the worst-case LS (higher duty at low throttle) for margining.

**Closed form.** Let A = I²·R₂₅·k/3 and B = R_th,eff. Then:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + A B\,(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - A B \alpha}
$$

**Thermal runaway condition:** A·B·α ≥ 1. With A = 6400 × 0.0016 / 3 = 3.41 W:

$$
B_{crit} = \frac{1}{3.41 \times 0.0045} = 65\ \text{K/W}
$$

The FET is not at intrinsic runaway risk. The limit is the board heat budget (§3).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period has one hard turn-on and one turn-off of the HS. The LS switches with near-zero voltage (ZVS) after dead time, but its body diode must recover.

### 3.1 V-I Overlap (Hard-Switched HS)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

The Miller-plateau estimate is a useful cross-check for t_r/t_f:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}} = \frac{9\text{ nC} \times 6\ \Omega}{10 - 3.5} \approx 8.3\ \text{ns per edge (Miller only)}
$$

The current-rise interval comes on top of this. Adding package and loop inductance (≈1–2 nH) brings the practical total to about 30 ns.

### 3.2 Reverse Recovery of the LS Body Diode

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 30\text{ nC} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so derate ×2 for margin: **0.07 W**.

### 3.3 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 \approx 0.03\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\, t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

This is the second-largest switching-related term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but only if the driver's propagation skew allows it.
