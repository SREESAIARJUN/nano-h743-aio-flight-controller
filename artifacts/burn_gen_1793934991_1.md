# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model and DShot Timer/DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Flagged Risks

I have no prior context for "Job #24," so this document is self-contained. **Every component value below is a stated assumption.** Replace each one with your selected part's datasheet value before release.

### 0.1 Architectural clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In a standard ArduPilot AIO design:

- The **H743** runs ArduPilot and emits 4× bidirectional DShot600 signals.
- **4× ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or similar, running AM32 or BLHeli_32) each drive 3 half-bridges through a gate driver.
- The **48 kHz PWM** is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

### 0.2 Red flag: 80 A on a "nano" board

On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 80 A per motor is physically unrealistic except for sub-second bursts. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a **transient rating with a defined duration**, never as continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With trapezoidal or sinusoidal six-step drive and complementary (synchronous) PWM, two phases carry current at any instant: one high side and one low side.

- **During the ON portion (duty D):** HS of phase A and LS of phase B conduct I.
- **During the OFF portion (1−D):** the LS of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and the LS of phase B conduct I.

In both states, exactly **two channels** carry the full phase current.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α_R(T_j − 25)]

### 1.3 Per-ESC and board conduction loss

P_cond,ESC = 2 · I² · R_ds(T_j) + I² · R_cu

R_cu is the copper path resistance (bus, phase pads, vias). Assume 0.3 mΩ per motor path.

At T_j = 100 °C, R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.34 mΩ**.

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET | 2 × 80² × 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| **P_cond per ESC** | | **≈ 19.0 W** |
| **Board (4 motors at burst)** | | **≈ 76 W** |

### 1.4 Electrothermal fixed point

Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve:

T_j = T_case + R_θJC · P_FET(T_j)

P_FET(T) = I² R_ds,25 [1 + α(T − 25)]

Solving for T_j:

T_j = [T_c + R_θJC I² R_25 (1 − 25α)] / [1 − R_θJC I² R_25 α]

**Stability condition (thermal runaway):** R_θJC · I² · R_25 · α < 1.

At 80 A: 1.0 × 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0045 = 0.029. This is stable at the junction level. The real runaway risk sits at the **board** level (Section 3), where the effective R_θ is 10–20 K/W.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Per ESC, that is one half-bridge with 2 edges per cycle.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

P_dt = V_SD · I · 2 · t_dead · f_sw

= 0.85 × 80 × 2 × 150 ns × 48 kHz = **0.98 W**

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.06 W**

The diode-recovery energy also adds to turn-on loss in the opposite FET. It is folded into the 15 ns t_r margin.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

P_oss = C_oss · V² · f_sw (two FETs, ½CV² each) = 1.2 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.04 W**

### 2.5 Gate drive (from driver/LDO, not the FETs)

P_g = 6 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 6 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = **0.17 W per ESC**

Only the PWM leg toggles at f_sw, so this is an upper bound. Size the 10–12 V gate-drive rail for ~0.7 W total plus driver quiescent current.

### 2.6 Total switching loss

| Scope | Loss |
|---|---|
| Per ESC (FET-side) | ≈ 2.5 W |
| Board | ≈ 10 W |

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

- ∂P/∂f ≈ 52 µW/Hz per ESC.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.25 W per ESC, but raises current ripple. That is why AM32 commonly uses variable PWM frequency (e.g., 24→48 kHz with throttle).

**Ripple check** (L_phase ≈ 15 µH, motor-dependent):

ΔI_pp ≈ V_bus · D(1−D) / (L · f_sw)

At D = 0.5: 25.2 × 0.25 / (2 × 15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 4.4 A pp. The phase-phase inductance is 2L, which accounts for the factor of 2.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Burst power budget

| Source | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (4 ESCs, T_j = 100 °C) | 76 |
| Switching (4 ESCs) | 10 |
| Gate drive, ESC MCUs, BEC, H743 | ~3 |
| **P_total at 4 × 80 A** | **≈ 89 W** |

### 3.2 Lumped-capacitance (adiabatic) burst model

Over sub-second to few-second timescales, convection is negligible versus storage.

**Board thermal mass (30.5×30.5×1.6 mm, 6-layer):**

| Element | Calculation | J/K |
|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 1.49 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ × 1.1 J/gK | 3.0 |
| Copper (~25% effective fill across layers) | ~3.0 g × 0.385 | 1.2 |
| 24 MOSFETs + passives + solder | | ~1.0 |
| **C_th** | | **≈ 5.2 J/K** |

The single-node model:

C_th · dT/dt = P(T) − (T − T_amb) / R_θBA

The solution, with P treated as constant at its 100 °C value:

T(t) = T_amb + P · R_θBA · (1 − e^(−t/τ)), where τ = R_θBA · C_th

Assume R_θBA ≈ 12 K/W in still air, falling to ~4 K/W with prop wash at 10 m/s.

| Condition | τ | Steady-state ΔT at 89 W |
|---|---|---|
| Still air | 62 s | 1068 K (impossible: board destroyed) |
| Prop wash | 21 s | 356 K (impossible) |

**Time to reach board T = 110 °C from T_amb = 40 °C (ΔT = 70 K):**

t = −τ · ln(1 − ΔT / (P · R_θBA))

| Condition | Time to 110 °C |
|---|---|
| Still air | 62 × −ln(1 − 0.0655) ≈ **4.2 s** |
| Prop wash | 21 × −ln(1 − 0.197) ≈ **4.6 s** |

**Junction rise above board during burst:** per conducting FET, 80² × 1.34 mΩ = 8.6 W. Using Z_θJC(10 ms) ≈ 0.4 K/W gives ΔT_jc ≈ 3.4 K at 10 ms, approaching 8.6 K at steady state. The limiting element is the **board**, not the die.
