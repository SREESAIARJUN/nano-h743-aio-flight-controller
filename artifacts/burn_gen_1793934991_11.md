## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility Check

### 0.1 System partitioning

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. The standard, flight-proven architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 bidir (x4)--> 4x ESC MCU (AM32 firmware) --> 4x 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs)
```

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by each ESC MCU.
  - Candidates are AT32F421 or STM32G071 **[VERIFY]**. AM32 supports 24, 48, and 96 kHz switching.
- The H743 only needs:
  - DShot output,
  - eRPM telemetry capture,
  - current and voltage ADC.

### 0.2 Form-factor feasibility (80 A burst)

| Form factor | Realistic burst/phase | Verdict for 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 20×20 mm "nano" | 25–40 A | **Not viable.** There is insufficient copper cross-section and thermal mass. |
| 25.5×25.5 mm whoop | 20–35 A | Not viable |
| 30.5×30.5 mm | 55–80 A | **Marginal–viable** with 3 oz copper, 8 layers, and a burst of 10 s or less |
| 36×36 mm or larger | 80 A+ | Viable |

**Recommendation:** Build on 30.5×30.5 mm mounting, 8-layer stackup, 3 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper.

I keep the "nano" label for the stacked-height profile only. The rest of this document assumes the 30.5 mm board. Section 2.4 shows why 20×20 fails.

### 1.1 Assumptions

| Symbol | Value | Note |

| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V | |
| Q_oss | 40 nC @ 25 V | |
| Q_rr | 30 nC | |
| Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V | |
| R_copper/phase path | 0.6 mΩ total | pad + trace + solder, 3 oz |

### 1.2 Conduction loss

With 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through **two** conducting devices in series. These are the high side of phase A and the low side of phase B, or their synchronous equivalents. This holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^{2}\,\big(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big)
$$

Using R_ds at the junction temperature of the hottest device:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond (80 A) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 6400·(1.80+0.6) m = **15.4 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 6400·(2.40+0.6) m = **19.2 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 6400·(2.62+0.6) m = **20.6 W** |

R_ds rises with temperature, so conduction loss rises with temperature. That creates positive electro-thermal feedback. This coupling is solved in §2.3.

### 1.3 Switching loss (hard-switched leg only)

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Using a linear-transition approximation:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

### 1.4 Dead-time, reverse-recovery, and output-capacitance losses

The body diode conducts during the dead time on both edges each switching period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot300\text{ ns}\cdot48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.92\ W}
$$

Reverse recovery and output-capacitance charge loss:

$$
P_{rr} + P_{oss} = (Q_{rr}+Q_{oss})\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 70\text{ nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.085\ W}
$$

### 1.5 Gate drive loss (per bridge, 2 devices switching)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{ nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{ k} = 0.048\ W
$$

Most of this dissipates in the driver and R_g, not in the MOSFET.

### 1.6 Per-motor total at 80 A burst (T_j = 100 °C)

| Component | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (MOSFET + copper) | 19.2 |
| Switching | 1.45 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.92 |
| Q_rr / Q_oss | 0.09 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈21.7 W** |
| **Board total, 4 motors simultaneous** | **≈87 W** |

Efficiency at the operating point: the motor input is about 25.2 V · 80 A · D_eff. At full throttle the bridge dissipates 21.7 W of roughly 2 kW, so η ≈ 98.9%.

### 1.7 Frequency trade-off (why 48 kHz)

Switching-related loss scales linearly with frequency. Ripple current falls roughly as 1/f.

$$
P_{fdep}(f) = (1.45 + 0.92 + 0.09 + 0.05)\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}} = 2.51\cdot\frac{f}{48\text{k}}\ \text{W}
$$

| f_sw | Frequency-dependent loss | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.26 W | audible whine, larger ripple on low-L motors |
| **48 kHz** | **2.51 W** | good balance for low-inductance 6S motors |
| 96 kHz | 5.02 W | only for very-low-L motors |

At 80 A, conduction loss dominates: about 88% of the total. Choosing 48 kHz costs about 1.25 W per motor over 24 kHz. Going to 48 kHz is justified only if motor phase inductance is below roughly 20 µH.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Thermal network (per motor bridge, lumped)

```
P_hot (one FET pair carries ~ 2/3 of loss during a commutation sector, conservative)
  │
 R_jc (0.6 K/W per FET, bottom pad)          [VERIFY]
  │
 R_solder+pad (0.3 K/W)
  │
 R_spread (PCB lateral, 3 oz, 8 layers, vias under pad: ~2.5 K/W per bridge)
  │
 ───────── C_board (shared copper/FR4 thermal mass) ────────
  │
 R_conv (board → air): still air ≈ 18 K/W; prop-wash (8 m/s) ≈ 5 K/W  [whole board]
  │
 T_ambient
```

### 2.2 Board thermal capacitance (30.5 mm, 8L)

- Copper: 8 layers, area 36×36 mm, average 2.25 oz (78 µm), about 70% fill. Volume = 1296 mm² · 0.078 mm · 8 · 0.7 ≈ 566 mm³, giving ≈ 5.0 g. C = 5.0 g · 0.385 = **1.94 J/K**.
- FR4: 1.6 mm board gives ≈ 3.9 g. C = 3.9 · 1.1 = **4.3 J/K**.
- MOSFETs, capacitors, and other components: about 3 g, roughly 2.0 J/K.
- **C_board ≈ 8.2 J/K**
