# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, DShot Timing, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

These points affect the rest of the design, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every practical AIO (all-in-one) or 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot600 commands to dedicated ESC MCUs, either four AT32F421/STM32G071 chips running AM32, or one per channel running BLHeli_32. Those ESC MCUs run commutation and 48 kHz PWM. Asking the H743 to run commutation for four motors while also running ArduPilot is not viable: there are not enough advanced-control timers, the ISR latency is too high, and ArduPilot has no support for it.
2. **"Nano" form factor with 80 A per motor is thermally impossible except as a seconds-scale burst.** Section 3 shows this numerically. Treat 80 A as a transient rating with a time limit, and size the continuous rating separately.
3. **All component values below are design-intent placeholders.** They are representative 40 V / ~1 mΩ MOSFETs and FD6288-class drivers. Rerun the model with datasheet values for your BOM before release.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification)

### 1.1 Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds(on),25 | Per FET, at V_gs = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds temperature coefficient at T_j = 125 °C | 1.5 |
| t_r + t_f | Effective V–I overlap time | 20 ns |
| Q_oss | MOSFET output charge | 40 nC |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 50 nC |
| V_f | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| Q_g | Gate charge per FET | 40 nC |
| V_drv | Gate drive voltage | 10 V |

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In any 60° commutation sector, one high-side/low-side pair carries I_ph. With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg's complementary FET carries the current during the off-time. The current path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) elements.

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot k_T R_{ds(on),25} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is 19.2 / 6 = **3.2 W**. The worst instantaneous per-FET loss during its active sector is 9.6 W.

### 1.3 Switching Losses (one hard-switched leg per PWM period)

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.968 W |
| Coss charge | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.038 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 1.73 W** |

**Design lever:** dead-time loss is about 35% of the switching total. In AM32, dropping dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Before doing so, verify there is no shoot-through using a scope on V_gs(HS) and V_gs(LS).

### 1.4 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A Burst

$$P_{ch} = 19.2 + 1.73 \approx \mathbf{20.9\ W}, \qquad P_{4ch} \approx \mathbf{83.7\ W}$$

Add PCB copper loss. For 2 oz copper (≈0.25 mΩ/□), each phase path is roughly 4 squares, giving 1 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \cdot 1\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 4 \approx 25.6\ W$$

This is often the forgotten term. Mitigate it with 6-layer 2 oz construction, paralleled phase pours on multiple layers, and via arrays (≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias per phase transition).

**Board burst total: about 110 W.**

### 1.5 Frequency Sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{ch}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 1.73\ W / 48\ kHz = 36\ \mu W/Hz$$

Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.87 W per channel. That is a small gain next to conduction loss, so the 48 kHz choice is defensible for acoustic and current-ripple reasons.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Junction Rise (Steady per-FET, Short Timescale)

Using R_th(j-c) ≈ 1.2 K/W for a 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 package with an exposed pad:

$$\Delta T_{j-c} = 3.2\ W \times 1.2 = 3.8\ K\ (\text{average}), \quad 9.6\ W \times Z_{th}(10\,ms) \approx 9.6 \times 0.5 = 4.8\ K\ (\text{peak sector})$$

Junction-to-case is not the bottleneck. The board is.

### 2.2 Board Heat Capacity (Lumped, Adiabatic)

Assume a 25.5 × 25.5 mm board, about 8 g including FETs and copper, with blended c_p ≈ 0.7 J/(g·K):

$$C_{th} \approx 5.6\ J/K, \qquad \frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{110\ W}{5.6\ J/K} \approx \mathbf{20\ K/s}$$

### 2.3 Convection to Prop-Wash Air

Assume h ≈ 100 W/(m²K) under prop wash, and both sides exposed (A ≈ 1.3×10⁻³ m²):

$$G_{conv} = hA \approx 0.13\ W/K, \qquad \tau = C/G \approx 43\ s$$

The steady-state rise at 110 W would be 110 / 0.13 ≈ 850 K. **Continuous operation at 80 A is physically impossible.**
