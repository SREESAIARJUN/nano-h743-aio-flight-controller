## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology

Each channel is a 3-phase bridge with 6 FETs. With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation and synchronous rectification, at any instant current flows through two FETs in series:

- the high-side FET of one phase, and
- the low-side FET of another phase.

During the PWM off-time, the active leg's low-side FET (synchronous) carries the freewheel current instead of the high-side FET. So the conducting device count stays at 2 for nearly the whole period.

### 1.2 Hot R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25} = 1.5 \times 1.0 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot} = 80^2 \times 3.0\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Copper and shunt path:**

$$P_{cu} = I_{pk}^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Ripple correction.** With PWM ripple ΔI, use I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12. At 48 kHz with a typical motor inductance of ~20 µH, ΔI is a few amps, so the correction is under 0.5%. It is neglected here.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} = \frac{I^2 R_{DS,hot}}{3} = \frac{9.6}{3} = 3.2\ \text{W (average)}$$

Each FET still sees **9.6 W instantaneous** while it is conducting. This matters for the transient junction temperature (§3.3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge switches at f_sw. The other conducting FET is static for that 60° sector.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are 2 dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F I_{pk} \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

This covers both FETs in the switching leg:

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_{g} = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper / shunt | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.76 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.92 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.16 W | 0.66 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

### 2.7 Efficiency check

Assume 80 A phase current at roughly 70% duty. Battery current is then about 56 A, and input power is about 56 A × 25.2 V ≈ 1.41 kW per channel.

$$\eta \approx 1 - 25.7/1411 \approx 98.2\%$$

This is plausible for this class of hardware.

### 2.8 Design levers

- **Conduction loss dominates (about 87%).** R_DS(on) and copper are the main levers.
- **Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz** adds only about 1.6 W per channel.
- **Dead time is the largest switching-related term.** Tightening t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Only do this if driver propagation matching allows it, because shoot-through is fatal.
