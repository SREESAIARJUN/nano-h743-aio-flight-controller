## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 runs the **flight controller** (ArduPilot). It does not commutate motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own dedicated ESC MCU. This partitioning drives the whole design, so it is stated first.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |

> **Engineering flag:** "80 A burst per motor" on a 25.5 mm board is at the edge of physical feasibility. The model below quantifies it, and the answer is that 80 A is sustainable for roughly 1–2 s per motor (all four loaded) or about 5–10 s (single motor). If marketing means 80 A *total*, the design has large margin. Resolve this definition before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_J - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{representative})$$

At T_J = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.2\ \text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{mΩ}$$

Add package and PCB parasitic resistance per conduction path (copper pour plus vias, ≈ 0.3 mΩ per FET path):

$$R_{path} \approx 2.1\ \text{mΩ per FET}$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant two FETs carry the phase current. In the active leg, the high side conducts for duty fraction D and the complementary low side conducts for (1 − D).

Per-motor conduction loss is therefore independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{path}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.1\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° commutation). Averaged over the cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{path}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 2.1\times10^{-3}}{3} = \mathbf{4.48\ W}$$

At 1 Hz-class mechanical loading the thermal time constant of the die (~ms) is short compared with the commutation period at low eRPM. At high eRPM (>10 k eRPM) the averaging is valid. For low-speed stall bursts, use the peak instead:

$$P_{peak,FET} = I^2 R_{path} = 13.4\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM-switched FET in the active leg incurs hard-switching loss. The complementary FET switches at near-zero V_DS (ZVS after dead time).

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t_r = t_f = 20 ns (representative, FD6288 with 10 Ω gate resistor), V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A:

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss (complementary FET body diode)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 60\ \text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,000 = 0.07\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals per PWM period, t_dt = 50 ns, V_F = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2 t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET. Two FETs switch per leg:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\ \text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,000 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss versus duty cycle

Switching loss is maximal near mid-throttle. At 100 % duty (true full-throttle burst), AM32 stops chopping, and P_sw drops toward zero. The conservative case is a 90 % duty burst, which keeps the full value.

$$P_{sw,motor} = P_{VI} + P_{oss} + P_{rr} + P_{dt} \approx \mathbf{2.38\ W}$$
