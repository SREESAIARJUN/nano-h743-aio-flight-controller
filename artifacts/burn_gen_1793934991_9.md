# STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC (Job #8): Electro-Thermal and DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Feasibility

Several points in the brief need correcting before any numbers are useful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the FETs are driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32. The 48 kHz PWM frequency is therefore an **ESC firmware setting**. ArduPilot on the H743 only produces DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
2. **80 A burst per channel is not achievable on a 20×20 nano footprint.** Section 3 shows why: the burst consumes the board's entire thermal capacity in under 1 s. I give the full model, then derive the realistic rating.
3. **Component parameters are assumptions.** All FET, PCB and thermal values below are parameterised. You must substitute datasheet values (R_DS(on) vs. T_j, Q_g, Q_rr, C_oss, Z_th(j-c)) for the FET you actually select. A specific part number does not validate these numbers.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel, 6 FETs, 3 half-bridges)

### 1.1 Operating Assumptions

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current (burst) | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_DS25 | R_DS(on) at 25 °C (40 V, 5×6 DFN class) | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_DS temperature coefficient | ≈0.006 /K |
| t_r, t_f | Switching edges | 20 ns each |
| t_d | Dead time (per edge) | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| Q_rr | Reverse recovery charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss(eff) | Output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g, V_GS | Gate charge, drive voltage | 60 nC, 10 V |
| R_cu | Copper/via path per conduction loop | 0.3 mΩ |

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant two legs carry I_ph:
- **PWM leg:** high side for D, low-side synchronous rectifier for (1−D).
- **Return leg:** low side on for 100% of the commutation step.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

Temperature-dependent resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

Conduction in the two legs:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\big[D\,R_{DS} + (1-D)\,R_{DS}\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS} + I_{ph}^2 R_{cu} = I_{ph}^2\,(2R_{DS}+R_{cu})$$

This is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.6 mΩ:

$$P_{cond}=6400\times(3.2+0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.4\ W}$$

### 1.3 Switching Loss (only the PWM leg switches)

**Hard-switching overlap:**
$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance (2 FETs per leg):**
$$P_{oss}=2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}=1.5\text{n}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 dead times per period):**
$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}I_{ph}\cdot 2t_d\cdot f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated in the driver/gate resistors, not the FET die):**
$$P_g=Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}\times2=\mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 1.4 Totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | W / channel | W / board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 20.5 | 82.0 |
| Copper | 1.9 | 7.7 |
| Switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead time | 0.61 | 2.5 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.16 | 0.7 |
| **Total** | **≈25.1** | **≈100 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 90% of the total. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz therefore saves only about 1.3 W per channel. The real levers are R_DS(on) and copper.

### 1.5 Self-Consistent Junction Temperature (Thermal Runaway Check)

For a single FET with steady-state thermal resistance θ:

$$T_j = T_a + \theta\, I^2 R_{DS25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j=\frac{T_a+\theta I^2R_{DS25}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\theta I^2R_{DS25}\alpha}$$
