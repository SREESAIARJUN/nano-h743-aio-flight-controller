## 0. Scope, Assumptions and One Design Concern

**System assumptions.** Every value below is stated so it can be replaced with your datasheet values.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | Sets the 40 V FET class |
| Burst current | 80 A RMS phase current per motor | "Burst" means 10 s or less |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification) | AM32/BLHeli_32 style |
| ESC topology | 4 independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class running AM32) | The H743 does **not** commutate motors |
| FC to ESC link | Bidirectional DShot600 from the H743 | ArduPilot |
| Form factor | 20×20 mm mounting, about 26×26 mm PCB, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner | "Nano" |

**Design concern: 80 A sustained is not achievable on this form factor.** The math in Section 3 shows that a nano-class board can sustain roughly 25–35 A per motor in prop-wash cooling. 80 A is a transient rating limited to about 2 s with all four motors loaded, or about 6–8 s with one motor loaded. The rating sheet should say "80 A burst (≤2 s, 4 motors)", not "80 A".

## 1. Power-Stage Electrical Model

### 1.1 Reference MOSFET parameter set (40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3)

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V | On-resistance at 25 °C |
| α | 0.0045 /K | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient |
| Q_g | 40 nC | Total gate charge |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 40 nC | Output charge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| t_r, t_f | 20 ns, 15 ns | Effective rise and fall at a 1.5 Ω gate resistance |
| R_th,j-pcb | 3.0 K/W | Junction to board copper (thermal pad) |

The temperature-corrected on-resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives **R_DS = 1.5 × 1.45 = 2.18 mΩ**. Use 2.2 mΩ.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

With complementary 120° six-step drive (or FOC, which gives a similar result), two FETs carry the phase current at every instant:

- the PWM'd high side (duty D) or its complementary low side (1 − D), and
- the fully-on low side of the return phase.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)+I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

For the FET term at 80 A:

$$P_{cond,FET}=2(80)^2(2.2\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{28.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The electrical period at about 100k eRPM is roughly 0.6 ms, far shorter than the die thermal time constant (several ms). The loss is therefore spread over the 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = 28.2/6 = \mathbf{4.7\ W\ per\ FET}$$

**Copper loss.** The phase pad, trace and via path is about 0.25 mΩ per phase, with two phases conducting:

$$P_{Cu}=2(80)^2(0.25\times10^{-3}) = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

### 1.3 Switching loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge hard-switches per PWM period.

| Term | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(35 ns)(48k) | 1.69 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction (t_dt = 200 ns, 2 edges) | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48k | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not FET) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching (FET-side)** | | **≈ 3.0 W** |

**Sensitivity:**

- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it saves about 1.5 W per motor.
- Dead-time loss is the largest controllable term. Cutting dead-time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor, provided the FD6288-class driver propagation-delay matching allows it.

### 1.4 Total burst budget

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 28.2 W | 112.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 3.0 W | 12.0 W |
| Bus capacitor ESR | 0.5 W | 2.0 W |
| **Total** | **34.9 W** | **≈140 W** |

The capacitor ESR figure is derived from I_cap,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)), which peaks at 40 A at D = 0.5, through about 0.3 mΩ ESR on the ceramic bank.

**Efficiency check.** At D ≈ 0.9 the motor input power is about 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 × 4 ≈ 7.3 kW, so η ≈ 98%. The electrical efficiency is fine; heat flux density is the real problem.
