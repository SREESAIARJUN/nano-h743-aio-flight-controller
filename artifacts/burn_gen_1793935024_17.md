# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #6)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

I've fixed some assumptions to make the math concrete. Change any of them and the equations still hold.

| Item | Assumption | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | H743 flight controller plus four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class, running AM32 firmware) | The H743 cannot do sensorless 6-step commutation for four motors *and* run ArduPilot. ArduPilot bidirectional DShot runs FC to ESC MCU. |
| Bus | 6S LiPo, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | Worst-case switching voltage |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 48 kHz | AM32 standard |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on),25 = 1.2 mΩ max at V_GS = 10 V, PQFN 5×6 | **Representative parameters. Replace with your datasheet values.** |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q triple half-bridge (bootstrap) | Common in 4-in-1 designs |
| Burst | 80 A per channel, all four motors simultaneously, ≤ 10 s | Worst case |

> ⚠️ **Form factor reality check:** 4 × 80 A burst dissipates about 105 W (derived below). That is physically impossible on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. The minimum credible footprint is 30.5×30.5 mm, with 36×36 mm preferred, using 8-layer, 2–3 oz copper. Treat "nano" as describing the FC section only, or stack the boards. Co-locating the IMU with a 100 W heat source also requires an IMU heater and thermal isolation slots (§6).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0048\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This value of α corresponds to the typical 1.6× normalisation at 150 °C.

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.2 × 1.36 = **1.63 mΩ**

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

At any instant, motor current I_ph flows through exactly two conducting channels in series:
- **PWM leg:** the high side (HS) for time D, then the synchronous low side (LS) for time (1−D). One channel is always conducting except during dead time.
- **Static leg:** the LS held on for the whole 60° sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}\right]$$

Here I_ph is related to battery current by $I_{ph} \approx I_{bat}/D$. At burst (D → 1), I_ph ≈ I_bat = 80 A.

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$I_{ph,rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$
