## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step mode with complementary PWM, the current always flows through exactly **two** conducting channels in series at any instant:

- **During the on-time D:** high-side (HS) of the PWM'd leg plus the low-side (LS) of the static leg.
- **During the off-time (1–D):** LS of the PWM'd leg (synchronous rectifier) plus the LS of the static leg.

Therefore, independent of duty cycle (outside the dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 2.2 Ripple-corrected RMS current

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 15 µH (phase-to-phase), V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 3 V at heavy load, D ≈ 0.9:

- ΔI = 3.75 A
- ΔI²/12 = 1.2 A², against I² = 6400 A², so the correction is negligible at burst.
- It does matter at light load or with low-inductance motors.

### 2.3 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on) (electro-thermal feedback)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.65 = **2.48 mΩ**.
