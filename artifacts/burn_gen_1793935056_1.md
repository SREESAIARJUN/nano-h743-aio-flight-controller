# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #58): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and the Key Finding

Three points need to be established before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFET bridges.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and has no field-oriented or 6-step commutation firmware. On any integrated "FC + 4-in-1" board, the H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Those MCUs run the bridges at 48 kHz. This document models both domains on that basis.
2. **"80A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per motor.** That is how the industry labels ESCs. Section 2.6 also shows the 80 A total case.
3. **Key finding: 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally viable.** The model below gives roughly 100 W of dissipation into about 3 J/K of thermal mass, which allows about 2 s before limits. Section 3 recommends either a 30.5×30.5 mm form factor or a derated rating.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With synchronous rectification, the current path always passes through **two** conducting FETs: one in the PWM leg (high side during t_on, low side during t_off) and one in the static return leg. Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle to first order:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx R_{25}\cdot k_T$$

**Ripple correction.** Phase current ripple adds to the RMS value:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" to 7" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH), at D = 0.5 and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 8.3 A
- The ripple term is about 5.8 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

**Numbers at 80 A:**

| Junction temperature | R_DS(on) | P_cond (= 6400 × 2 × R) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.2 mΩ | 15.4 W |
| 125 °C (hot) | 1.92 mΩ | **24.6 W** |

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature and loss rises with R_DS(on), there is positive feedback. Solve iteratively with T_j = T_case + P_FET · R_θJC. Convergence is reached around T_j ≈ 115 °C to 125 °C at burst.

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz = **0.97 W**

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery, Output Capacitance, Dead Time, Gate Charge

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| Coss (2 devices) | 2 × ½ Q_oss V_bus f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F · I_ph · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | **0.038 W** |

### 2.4 Total per Motor at 80 A, Hot

$$P_{motor} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_{rr} + P_{oss} + P_{dt} + P_{gate}$$

24.6 + 0.97 + 0.04 + 0.05 + 0.61 + 0.04 ≈ **26.3 W**

**Observation.** At 48 kHz, switching-related losses total about 1.7 W, roughly 6% of the total. Conduction dominates by more than 10×. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per motor at this current. Note that at D = 100% there is no switching, so the worst *combined* point is around D ≈ 0.9.

### 2.5 Additional Board-Level Losses at 80 A per Motor

| Source | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ per motor) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W each, **6.4 W** total |
| Copper (bus + phase, ~0.3 mΩ effective per channel at 2 oz) | ~1.9 W each | **~7.7 W** |
| Bulk-capacitor ESR | — | **~0.5 W** |

**Board total:** 4 × 26.3 + 6.4 + 7.7 + 0.5 ≈ **120 W**

### 2.6 If "80 A" Means Total (20 A per Motor)

- Conduction: 400 × 2 × 1.5 mΩ ≈ 1.2 W per motor
- Switching and related: ≈ 0.43 W per motor
- Board total: **about 7 W**, which a nano board handles comfortably.
