## System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot/DMA Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Three facts shape the rest of this design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot runs flight control and emits DShot. It cannot run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops at 48 kHz PWM. The board therefore contains **one H743 (flight controller) plus four ESC MCUs** (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) on a shared PCB, linked by internal DShot traces. The 48 kHz figure is the AM32 `PWM_FREQUENCY` setting, not an ArduPilot parameter.
2. **80 A on a nano (≈30.5×30.5 mm) board is burst-only.** The analysis in §3 shows about 75–85 W of dissipation at 4×80 A. That gives an adiabatic window of roughly 3–5 s before the 110 °C board limit. Continuous rating will be about 35–45 A per channel with prop wash.
3. **Separate the FC from the power stage, or at least decouple it thermally.** I recommend a two-board stack (FC 20×20 above ESC 30.5×30.5) with a board-to-board connector. If a single PCB is mandatory, place the IMU on a slotted island with its own LDO.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology state

The ESC runs trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In any 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low.

- The switched leg conducts I through the high-side FET for D·T and through the low-side FET for (1−D)·T.
- The fixed leg conducts I through its low-side FET for the full period.

The conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Take L_ph = 15 µH (a typical 2207 motor, phase-to-phase), D = 0.9, and V_bemf ≈ 0.85·V_bus:

ΔI ≈ (3.78 × 0.9)/(15e-6 × 48e3) ≈ 4.7 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 1.8 A².

This is negligible against 6400 A². At 48 kHz, ripple loss stays below 0.1 % of conduction loss.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_ds,on (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB})$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 0.80 × 1.4875 = **1.19 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,motor}(80\,A) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.19\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.2\ W}$$

### 1.4 Thermal runaway criterion

The loop is stable when the loop gain is below 1:

$$G = \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,JB} < 1$$

For the worst FET, take 6400 × 0.8e-3 × 0.0065 × 2.5 ≈ 0.083. That is well below 1, so the loop is stable. The board temperature, not the junction loop, is the limiting factor.
