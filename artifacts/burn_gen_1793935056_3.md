# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Scope and Ground Rules

There is no frozen BOM for this job, so every result depends on the assumptions below. Each formula is parameterized so you can substitute datasheet values once the MOSFETs and gate drivers are selected.

### Architectural clarifications

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an AIO with a 4-in-1 ESC:
   - The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600.
   - Each motor has its own ESC MCU, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware.
   - That ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
   - The loss model (Sections 2 to 4) applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model (Section 5) applies to the H743.

2. **80 A on a "nano" board is a burst-only rating.**
   - On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per motor cannot be sustained. You physically cannot fit enough copper, FET area or connector cross-section.
   - I have modeled a **30.5×30.5 mm board** (the largest form factor "nano" builders realistically use). Even there, the numbers show 80 A is limited to roughly **1–3 seconds**.
   - Treat this as a design finding, not a rounding issue.

3. **80 A is taken as motor phase current at near-full duty.** Battery current is I_batt ≈ D·I_phase. At part throttle, phase current exceeds battery current, which makes conduction losses worse than battery-side telemetry suggests.

## 1. Commutation Model

In each 60° electrical sector of 6-step commutation, two phases are active:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):**
  - The high-side FET conducts for duty fraction D.
  - The complementary low-side FET conducts for the remaining (1−D), minus dead time.
  - During dead time, the low-side body diode carries the current.
- **Phase B (sink phase):** the low-side FET is held on for the full 120° conduction interval.
- **Phase C:** floating, used for BEMF zero-cross sensing.

Two consequences:

- At any instant, **exactly two FET channels carry I_ph**: one in the PWM leg and one in the sink leg.
- Over a full electrical cycle, **each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time**. The high/low split within that time depends on D.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 General form (per motor, instantaneous)

```
P_cond,motor = I_ph² · R_hot · [ D + (1−D)·(1 − 2·t_dt·f_sw) ]  +  I_ph² · R_hot
               \____________ PWM leg (HS + sync LS) ___________/     \_ sink leg _/
```

Dead-time body-diode conduction is treated separately in Section 3 as a switching-related loss.

Since 2·t_dt·f_sw = 2 × 100 ns × 48 kHz = 0.0096, which is negligible here:

```
P_cond,motor ≈ 2 · I_ph² · R_hot
```

### 2.2 R_ds(on) temperature dependence

Use a first-order temperature model and solve it iteratively with the thermal model:

```
R(Tj) = R_25 · [1 + α·(Tj − 25)],   α ≈ 0.0059 /K  (gives ×1.5 at 110 °C)
```

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| I² | 80² | 6400 A² |
| Per conducting channel | 6400 × 2.25 mΩ | **14.4 W** |
| Per motor (2 channels) | 2 × 14.4 W | **28.8 W** |
| Per FET (time-averaged, 1/3 duty) | 14.4 / 3 | **4.8 W** |
| **All 4 motors** | 4 × 28.8 W | **115 W** |

Two further conduction-path losses also apply:

- **PCB copper and connectors:** each 0.2 mΩ of phase-trace plus pad resistance adds 6400 × 0.2 mΩ = 1.28 W per conducting path. At ~0.3 mΩ round trip, that is about **+2.6 W per motor**.
- **Shunt resistor (if used):** a 0.25 mΩ shunt in the DC return adds 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W per motor** at full duty.

**Conduction subtotal ≈ 33 W per motor, ≈ 132 W for the board at 4 × 80 A.**

### 2.4 Is the averaging valid?

Example: a 14-pole motor at 20 000 rpm has an electrical frequency of 7 × 333 Hz ≈ 2.33 kHz, so each sector lasts ~72 µs.

The die thermal time constant is roughly 1–5 ms, so each FET sees its **average** 4.8 W rather than the 14.4 W instantaneous peak.

The exception is **stall or very low rpm** (e.g., desync, prop strike). There, one sector can dwell for milliseconds, and the FET sees the full 14.4 W. Mitigate this with ESC stall protection and current limiting, not with hardware margin.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches; the sink leg is static. The switching loss components per motor are:

| Component | Formula | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 kHz | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.5 × 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·(2·t_dt)·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in the driver, not the FET) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw·2 | 40 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz × 2 | **0.04 W** |
| **Total per motor** | | | **≈ 1.70 W** |
| **Total, 4 motors** | | | **≈ 6.8 W** |

### Observations

- **Switching loss is only ~5% of total loss.** At 80 A, conduction dominates by ~17×. Dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves ~0.85 W per motor, which is meaningless here.
  - Choose 48 kHz for acoustic and current-ripple reasons.
  - For R_ds(on), buy the lowest you can fit, even at the cost of higher Q_g.
- **Dead time is the second-largest switching term.** Trimming t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns (if the driver propagation-delay mismatch allows) saves 0.3 W per motor.
- **The overlap term scales linearly with V_bus.** On 8S (33.6 V), it rises to ~1.3 W per motor, and the 40 V FETs lose avalanche margin. Specify 60 V FETs for 8S.
