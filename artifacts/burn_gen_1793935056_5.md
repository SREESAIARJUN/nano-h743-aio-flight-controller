**System partition.**

- The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot. It emits four bidirectional DShot600 lines and receives eRPM telemetry on the same wires.
- Each ESC channel runs its own **ESC MCU** (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM.
- The H743 never generates the 48 kHz bridge PWM. This split matters for the timer allocation in §4.

> **Engineering flag.** 4 × 80 A at 25 V is about 8 kW peak through a "nano" (20×20 or 30×30 mm) board. The math below shows this is only survivable as a **seconds-scale burst**. A continuous rating on this form factor will realistically be 35–45 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

For any 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A (PWM leg).** The high-side FET (HS) conducts for fraction D. The low-side FET (LS) conducts for 1−D as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B (sink leg).** The LS is held on for the whole sector.
- **Phase C** is floating, used for BEMF zero-cross detection.

Each phase spends 120° as the PWM leg, 120° as the sink leg and 120° floating. Averaged over one electrical revolution:

$$
P_{cond,HS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\frac{D}{3}
$$

$$
P_{cond,LS} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\left[\frac{1-D}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right] = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\frac{2-D}{3}
$$

Summed over one leg, this gives (2/3)·I²R. Summed over three legs, it gives the expected result that exactly two FETs are always in series with the motor current:

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (requires iteration)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

The conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, so the result must be iterated (§3.4). At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.55 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.55\times10^{-3} = 19.84\ \text{W per motor channel}
$$

**Per-device split at D = 0.9** (typical at full-throttle burst):

| Device | Formula | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| HS FET | 6400 · 1.55 m · 0.9/3 | 2.98 W |
| LS FET | 6400 · 1.55 m · 1.1/3 | 3.64 W |

The LS works harder in conduction. The HS takes the switching loss instead (§2).

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W (at D-weighted RMS ≈ I_ph) |
| Phase pad + trace | 2 oz outer + 2 × 1 oz inner, ~0.3 mΩ per path, two paths active | 2 × 6400 × 0.3 m = 3.84 W |
| Bus copper share | ~0.2 mΩ | 6400 × 0.2 m = 1.28 W |

$$
P_{Cu+sh} \approx 6.7\ \text{W per channel}
$$

This is not negligible. On nano boards copper loss is often 25–35 % of FET conduction loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Assuming positive (motoring) current, the HS hard-switches and the LS commutates softly through its body diode.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (HS)

$$
P_{sw,VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 1.935\ \text{W}
$$

A more precise form uses current rise and voltage fall times split by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{sw,on} = Q_{gd}\,\frac{R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}
$$

The lumped value is adequate for sizing.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

This loss is deposited mostly in the **HS**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}
$
