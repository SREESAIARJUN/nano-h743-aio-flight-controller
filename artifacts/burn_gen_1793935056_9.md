# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and ArduPilot Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Several assumptions shape everything below:

1. **The H743 is not the ESC MCU.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and that MCU runs the 48 kHz commutation PWM. The switching-loss model therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs, and the DShot/DMA model belongs to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano form factor is a short-duration rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows the board is thermally capacitance-limited. The honest continuous rating is roughly 30 to 35 A per motor with all four loaded.
3. **The power-stage parameters are representative values.** They assume a 40 V, approximately 0.9 mΩ class MOSFET in a 3.3×3.3 mm package. Substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations.
4. **The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready design.** Pin numbers vary by package and vendor, so nets are given by pin function. You still need ERC/DRC review, datasheet pin verification, and a layout review before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Trapezoidal Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, two phase legs carry current:
- One high-side switch is PWM'd.
- One low-side switch is held on.

During PWM off-time, the complementary low-side switch of the PWM'd leg conducts. The current therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels in series.

**Temperature-adjusted resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9 \times 1.34 \approx 1.2\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Current ripple correction.** For a triangular ripple of peak-to-peak ΔI = r·I:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

Assuming r = 0.3, the correction factor is 1.0075. This is negligible and is dropped below.

**Total conduction loss per ESC channel:**

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} = 15.4\ \text{W}$$

**Per FET.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120° of 360°). Averaged over rotation:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{15.4}{6} \approx 2.6\ \text{W}$$

**Paralleled FETs.** With 2 FETs per switch position (12 per channel), R_DS halves. Conduction loss drops to 7.7 W per channel, or 1.3 W per FET.

### 1.2 Copper and Shunt Losses

| Path | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad and trace (2 oz copper, about 4 layers in parallel) | ≈ 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| Bus copper share | ≈ 0.15 mΩ | ~1.0 W |
| Per-channel copper subtotal | | **≈ 3.6 W** |
| Shared battery shunt (0.25 mΩ) at 320 A total | | 25.6 W |

The shared-shunt figure explains why 4-in-1 boards use 0.1 to 0.2 mΩ shunts or Hall/sense-FET current sensing. This model assumes a 0.1 mΩ shunt, which dissipates **10.2 W** at 320 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per PWM period, there is one turn-on and one turn-off of the active switch.

### 2.1 V·I Overlap Loss
