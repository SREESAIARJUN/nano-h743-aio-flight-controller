# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #65): Thermal, Switching and BDShot Timer Model

## 0. Critical findings

1. **An 80 A per-motor burst is not thermally achievable on a nano-format (20×20 to 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 board with 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs.** The FET junction temperature rise alone exceeds the limit in under one second (§2.4). The model below shows why. It also shows the two interpretations that do close:
   - **(a)** 80 A is total battery burst current, about 20 A per motor.
   - **(b)** The board grows to 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs.
2. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** On every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller. It sends DShot to one or more ESC MCUs (AT32F421/STM32G071/EFM8 running AM32 or BLHeli). Those MCUs drive three-phase gate drivers. The netlist is partitioned this way.
3. **Conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 20:1 at 48 kHz and 6S.** The switching frequency is not the thermal problem; Rds(on) and copper are.

All component values below are **reference-design assumptions**. Rds(on), Qgd, Qrr and Rθ must be replaced with datasheet values for the FET you select before release.

## 2. Conduction loss model

### 2.1 Per-motor bridge

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two FETs always carry the phase current I_ph:
- one low-side FET that is continuously on, and
- one high-side FET, or its complementary low-side FET during PWM-off.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Phase current relates to battery current by I_ph ≈ I_bat / D. At full throttle, D → 1 and I_ph ≈ I_bat.

### 2.2 Case A: 80 A per motor (literal spec)

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 2.0 \times 1.5 = 3.0\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 3.0\times10^{-3} = 38.4\ \text{W}$$
