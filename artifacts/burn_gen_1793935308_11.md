# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Engineering Design Package (Job #20)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Corrections

**Architecture correction.** An STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four sensorless BLDC motors and also receive DShot from itself. DShot only makes sense across an MCU boundary. This design therefore uses:

- **FC:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels.
- **ESC:** one 4-in-1 board with 4 independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-compatible firmware). Each MCU runs 6-step or sinusoidal commutation at 48 kHz PWM.

**Working parameters.** These are explicit so you can re-run every equation with your real values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

With synchronous rectification in 6-step operation, the phase current always flows through exactly one FET in each of the two active half-bridges. The high-side vs low-side split depends on duty cycle D, but the total does not:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The per-FET loss, worst case, assumes one FET carries the full current (D → 1, or low-side at D → 0):

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

For sinusoidal (FOC-style) drive with peak phase current $\hat I$, average over the cycle to get $P_{cond,ch} = \tfrac{3}{2}\hat I^2 R_{ds}$.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature (electro-thermal feedback)

Start from the steady-state thermal relation:

$$T_j = T_b + R_\theta\, I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

where $R_\theta = R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,spread} = 4.5$ K/W. Define $k = R_\theta I^2 R_{ds,25}$ and solve for T_j:

$$\boxed{T_j = \frac{T_b + k(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha}}$$

The stability (thermal-runaway) criterion is $k\alpha < 1$.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

- $k = 4.5 \times 6400 \times 1.2\times10^{-3} = 34.56$ K
- $k\alpha = 0.173$, so the design is stable with margin.
- For a board temperature $T_b = 70$ °C:
  - $T_j = (70 + 34.56 \times 0.875)/0.827 = 121.2$ °C
  - $R_{ds}(121\,°C) = 1.2 \times 1.481 = 1.78$ mΩ
  - $P_{cond,FET} = 6400 \times 1.78\text{ mΩ} = 11.4$ W
  - $P_{cond,ch} = 22.8$ W
- At the 40 A continuous rating, $P_{cond,ch} \approx 4.8$ W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses apply to the PWM-switching half-bridge. The synchronous FET mainly sees diode and dead-time losses.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5·25.2·80·27 ns·48 k = **1.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V f_{sw}$ | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | $\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw}$ | **0.030 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw}$ | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs) | $6\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}$ | 6·40 nC·10·48 k = **0.115 W** (dissipated in driver and gate resistors) |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

The **worst-case single FET** is the switching high-side at D → 1. It sees 11.4 W conduction + 1.39 W switching ≈ **12.8 W**.

Conclusion: at 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 11:1. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per channel. Lowering R_ds(on), for example by paralleling FETs or using a larger package, is the only meaningful lever.

## 3. System-Level Thermal Budget

### 3.1 Total dissipation

$$P_{ESC} = 4\,(P_{cond,ch} + P_{sw,ch}) + P_{shunt} + P_{caps} + P_{traces}$$

| Term | 80 A ×4 burst | 40 A ×4 continuous |
