## 0. Architecture Corrections and Scope

Three points affect the whole model.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each of the four ESC channels needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Candidates are the AT32F421K8U7 and STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally marginal.** The math below shows roughly 85–90 W dissipated board-wide at full burst. That can only be sustained for seconds, and only with heavy copper and airflow. Define "burst" contractually. This document assumes **80A phase RMS for ≤ 2 s, 6S bus**.
3. **"80A" must specify battery or phase current.** Phase RMS ≈ I_batt / √D at partial throttle. I model 80A **phase** current, which is the worst case for the FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.45) = **1.45 mΩ**. Use 1.5 mΩ as the design value.

### 1.2 Current Path in Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly two conducting devices:

- the switching leg's high-side FET (on-time) or its low-side FET (off-time, synchronous rectification), and
- the static low-side FET of the return phase.

Therefore, per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each of the six FETs conducts for 120° electrical, which is 1/3 of the cycle. Averaged over one electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{19.2}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The instantaneous peak on a single FET is I²R = 9.6 W during its conduction window. At low eRPM, the electrical period becomes comparable to the die thermal time constant, so design to the instantaneous peak. This matters at stall and at hard punch-outs from low RPM.

### 1.4 PCB Copper Loss (Do Not Ignore)

Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper:

$$R_\square = \rho/t = 1.72\times10^{-8} / 70\times10^{-6} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square$$

A 5-square path from the bus to the FET and on to the motor pad gives 1.23 mΩ per layer. With 3 paralleled layers, that is about 0.41 mΩ per path. Two paths conduct, so:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 2 \times 0.41\text{m}\Omega \cdot (1+0.0039\cdot\Delta T) \approx \mathbf{5.2\text{–}6\ W/ESC}$$

**Design rule:** copper loss is about 30% of FET conduction loss on a nano board. Pour motor and bus copper on every available layer and stitch with via arrays of at least 20 vias of 0.3 mm per pad.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching losses occur only on the PWM leg.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching Overlap Loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Totals

| Loss | W per ESC |
|---|---|
| Overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.11 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total** | **≈ 2.7 W** |

**Trade-off:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz you save about 1.3 W per ESC. However, at low inductance (high-KV motors), current ripple ΔI = V·D(1−D)/(L·f) doubles. 48 kHz is justified for motors with L_phase < 10 µH.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 (all motors at 80A) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| FET switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Copper | 5.5 W | 22 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (§3.1) | ~0.8 W | 3.2 W |
| **Total** | **~28 W** | **~113 W** |

**Efficiency check:** output power at 80A with D ≈ 0.9 is about 1.8 kW per ESC. The loss of about 28 W gives roughly 98.5% efficiency. Efficiency is therefore acceptable; the problem is purely **heat density**, about 113 W on roughly 6 cm².
