**Architectural clarification.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and *sends* bidirectional DShot600. The commutation is done by four separate ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 running AM32). **Key finding up front.** On a 30 × 30 mm board, 80 A per motor is a **burst rating of roughly 2–4 seconds**. The continuous rating is about **25 A per motor** with prop-wash cooling. The derivation is in Section 3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-motor topology

The motor uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, the phase current I flows through two conducting paths: one high-side or its synchronous low-side complement in the PWM leg, and one low-side in the return leg. Over a PWM period, the resistive path is always 2 × R_ds(on). Duty cycle does not change the total conduction loss when complementary PWM is used.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

With α ≈ 0.006 /K, k_T(125 °C) ≈ 1.6.

### 1.2 At 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\,\text{mΩ} = 6400 \times 3.2\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Copper loss in the motor phase and power traces adds to this. With an 8-layer, 2 oz stack and about 0.15 mΩ effective per motor current path:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.15\,\text{mΩ} \approx 0.96\ \text{W per motor}$$

The current-sense shunt is shared on the battery return. Assuming 4 × 80 A = 320 A total and 0.1 mΩ:

$$P_{shunt}=320^2\times 0.1\,\text{mΩ}=10.2\ \text{W}$$

That shunt loss is not acceptable. Use either per-ESC shunts (0.25 mΩ each, 1.6 W at 80 A) or R_ds-based/Hall sensing. The rest of this design uses **per-ESC 0.25 mΩ shunts**.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each leg conducts for 240° electrical out of 360°, split between its high-side and low-side FETs.

- Average conduction per FET ≈ P_cond,motor / 6 ≈ 3.4 W.
- Peak instantaneous per FET is up to 80² × 1.6 mΩ = 10.2 W, during its active interval.

At 2 kHz electrical, the active interval is about 167 µs. The die thermal impedance Z_th(jc) at about 100 µs is roughly 0.2 K/W, so per-interval ripple is about 2 K. Using the average is valid.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The model counts one hard-switched transition pair per period per motor.

### 2.1 V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss}= Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

The synchronous FET's body diode conducts during dead time.

$$P_{rr}= Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I \,(2 t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times 80\times 200\text{ ns}\times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors.

$$P_g = 6\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 6\times 30\text{ nC}\times 10\times 48\text{k} = 0.086\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A

| Loss | W per motor |
|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 20.5 |
| Copper traces | 0.96 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | 1.60 |
| V-I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr + gate | 0.17 |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 99 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 83%. At 48 kHz, switching loss is a secondary concern. **Lowering R_ds(on) or paralleling FETs is the only lever that matters.** Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.8 W per motor. Paralleling two FETs per switch saves about 10 W per motor.

**Efficiency check.** Total input power is 4 × 80 × 25.2 × D. At D ≈ 0.9, that is about 7.3 kW, so 99 W is roughly 1.4% loss. The efficiency is fine; the heat density is the problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to board

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,(R_{th,jc} + R_{th,cs})$$

With R_th,jc = 1.2 K/W (bottom pad) and R_th,cs ≈ 2.5 K/W (solder plus via array of about 12 × 0.3 mm filled vias):

$$\Delta T_{j-pcb} = 3.4\text{ W}\times 3.7 \approx 13\ \text{K}$$
