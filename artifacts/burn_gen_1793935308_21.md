# STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package for Job #44

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

**Assumed architecture**

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600 for four motors.
- **ESC:** four independent ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32. Each drives one 3-phase bridge at **48 kHz complementary PWM** with 6-step commutation.
  - The H743 does **not** perform commutation. 48 kHz is the ESC-MCU PWM frequency, and DShot is the command link.
- **Battery:** 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum (22.2 V nominal).
- **80 A rating:** a **per-motor burst of ≤10 s**, not continuous.
- **Form factor:** "Nano" is taken as a 30.5 × 30.5 mm stack-in-one board. It is 6-layer, with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes.

**Parameter placeholders.** MOSFET parameters are representative of the 40 V DFN5×6 class with roughly 0.8 mΩ R_DS(on). Replace them with the selected part's datasheet values before release.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 4 × 80 A = **320 A pack current, about 8 kW** through a 30 × 30 mm board. That exceeds an XT60 rating (about 60 A continuous) and requires an XT90-class or direct-soldered lead.
>
> As Section 3 shows, an 80 A burst on all four channels simultaneously is **thermally limited to roughly 2–4 s** at realistic prop-wash cooling. Section 3 also explains why the ~10 s burst holds only with a per-channel load-sharing caveat.
>
> These limits should go into the product spec rather than be hidden.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS,25 (V_GS = 10 V) | 0.80 mΩ |
| T_j (design point) | 125 °C |
| R_DS(125 °C) | 0.80 × 1.5 = **1.20 mΩ** |

### 1.2 Conduction topology in 6-step complementary PWM

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Leg A:** high-side (HS) switches at duty D. The low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1 − D), excluding dead time.
- **Leg B:** the LS is held fully ON (return path).

With phase current I_ph:

$$P_{cond,A} = D\,I_{ph}^2R_{HS} + (1-D-2t_d f_{sw})\,I_{ph}^2R_{LS}$$
$$P_{cond,B} = I_{ph}^2R_{LS}$$

Ignoring the small dead-time fraction and taking R_HS = R_LS = R:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge} \approx 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}\quad\text{(independent of D)}$$

### 1.3 Battery current vs phase current

$$I_{bat} = D\cdot I_{ph}\ \Rightarrow\ I_{ph} = I_{bat}/D$$

The "80 A" rating must state which current it refers to.

- At D = 0.95 and I_bat = 80 A, I_ph = 84 A.
- **I_ph = 80 A** is used as the design point. This is conservative for switching losses and near worst case at full throttle.

### 1.4 Numbers

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{15.36\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper path loss** (bus to FET to phase pad, per channel):

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{t\,w},\quad \rho_{Cu}(100°C)=2.2\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}$$

For 2 oz (70 µm), w = 6 mm, L = 12 mm per layer:

- One layer gives 0.63 mΩ.
- Four paralleled layers give about 0.16 mΩ.
- The path is traversed twice (out and return), so R_Cu ≈ 0.32 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.32\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.0\ W}$$

**Shunt.** The per-channel shunt is 0.25 mΩ in the low-side common return and carries I_bat:

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m} = \mathbf{1.6\ W}$$

Use a 2512 metal-strip shunt rated ≥3 W pulse, or use two in parallel at 0.5 mΩ each.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (switching HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

Rise/fall times are set by the gate driver:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\quad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}}$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 3.5 V, R_g,total = 6.5 Ω → I_g ≈ 1.0 A. With Q_gd = 20 nC, t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$P_{VI} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

> **Check the driver's current capability.** A 1 A gate current needs a driver able to source and sink it. Bootstrap-type drivers such as the FD6288Q class are typically rated well below 1 A source.
>
> If the driver is weaker, t_r/t_f grow roughly in proportion and P_VI scales linearly. For example, at 0.3 A, P_VI ≈ 6.4 W.
>
> **This is the single most sensitive switching-loss parameter.**

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (synchronous LS)

$$P_{dt} = 2\,V_F\,I_{ph}\,t_d\,f_{sw}$$

With V_F = 0.8 V and t_d = 300 ns:

$$P_{dt} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 300\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

Dead time is costly at 48 kHz. Reduce it to about 150 ns (AM32 dead-time setting) once validated against shoot-through. This roughly halves P_dt.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate charge

| Term | Formula | Inputs | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | Q_rr = 60 nC | **0.073 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | Q_oss = 50 nC | **0.060 W** (dissipated in the switching FET) |
| Gate charge | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | Q_g = 60 nC | **0.058 W** (mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET) |
