# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design (Job #22)

## 0. Architecture Constraints

Two points change the design.

**1. The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.**
- ArduPilot is the flight stack. It does not run BLDC commutation firmware.
- The 4-in-1 "ESC" therefore needs four dedicated ESC MCUs. This design uses AT32F421K8U7 parts running AM32, one per channel.
- The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600.
- The "48 kHz" figure is the ESC PWM frequency, generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

**2. "80 A burst" on a nano board (~20×20 to 30×30 mm) is a transient rating, not a continuous one.**
- Section 2 derives how long 80 A can actually be sustained.
- The answer is roughly 1–3 s at the board level, depending on load case.
- Treat that as the honest datasheet number.

**Device values.** All device values are representative of the class (40 V, ~1 mΩ PDFN3.3×3.3 MOSFETs; FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver). Substitute your exact BOM values into the equations. The model is fully parametric.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 1.1 Parameters

| Symbol | Meaning | Value used |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | 25.2 V |
| I | Phase (DC-link) burst current per channel | 80 A |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |
| R_ds,25 | R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.0045 /K (→ ×1.45 at 125 °C) |
| t_r, t_f | Current/voltage transition times | 15 ns each |
| Q_rr | Body-diode reverse-recovery charge | 50 nC |
| C_oss(er) | Energy-related output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| Q_g | Total gate charge @ 10 V | 60 nC |

| t_dt | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |
| R_cu | PCB copper + solder path, per channel | 0.30 mΩ |

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ |

This creates a positive feedback loop: higher loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, which raises loss. Section 2.4 solves it.

### 1.3 Conduction Loss (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

In any 60° commutation sector, current flows through exactly two phases:

- **High-side FET of phase A** is PWM'd at duty D.
- **Low-side FET of phase A** acts as the synchronous rectifier, on for (1−D).
- **Low-side FET of phase B** is on continuously.

During the PWM on-time, current flows HS_A → motor → LS_B. During the off-time, it flows LS_A → motor → LS_B. Either way, exactly two channels are in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\quad\text{(independent of D)}$$

At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

At T_j = 100 °C the same calculation gives 17.2 W.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET is active in 2 of 6 sectors per electrical cycle.

- At flight speeds the electrical frequency is high. Example: 7 pole pairs × 30 kRPM / 60 = 3.5 kHz, which gives a 48 µs sector.
- The die thermal time constant is on the order of ms. So averaging is valid:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx P_{cond,ch}/6 = 3.1\ \text{W}$$

**Exception (stall or startup at high current).** One FET can carry I²R_ds = 9.3 W continuously. Burst current must be limited at low eRPM. AM32 provides this through its stall-protection and current-limit settings.

**Copper loss:**

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Hard-Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high-side FET hard-switches. The low side commutates softly (ZVS-like, through the diode during dead time).

**Linear V-I overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The transition time follows from gate drive:

$$t \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g},\qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g}$$

With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC, V_drv = 10–12 V, plateau ≈ 3 V, and R_g,total ≈ 5 Ω, I_g ≈ 0.8 A and t ≈ 15 ns.
