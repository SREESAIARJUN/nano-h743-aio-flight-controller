# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #43)

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only. It does not drive gates. Each motor channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an STM32G071 or AT32F421. That MCU handles commutation and the bidirectional DShot response. This is the only practical topology, because ArduPilot does not do motor commutation.

**Bottom line up front:** a simultaneous 4 × 80 A burst on a nano-format board is not thermally survivable for more than about 1 s. A single-channel 80 A burst is marginal at about 5 s. Derivation follows.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- On the PWM leg, the high-side (HS) FET conducts during D·T and the low-side (LS) FET conducts during (1−D)·T.
- On the return leg, the LS FET conducts continuously for the 60° sector.

At every instant, therefore, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the phase current, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″ motor (L_ph ≈ 10 µH), ΔI_pp is a few amps. The resulting correction is under 0.5 % at 80 A, so ripple is neglected below.

### 1.3 Temperature coupling (fixed-point equation)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot Z_{\theta}(t)$$

**Thermal runaway criterion.** The loop is stable only if the loop gain is below 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}Z_\theta = I^2\,2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,Z_\theta < 1$$
