## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC)**. It does not commutate the motors. Each 4-in-1 ESC channel (or one shared quad MCU) runs its own commutation firmware, such as AM32 on an STM32G071 or AT32F421, or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The analysis is therefore split:

| Domain | Owner | Analysed in |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction and switching losses at 48 kHz PWM | ESC MCU + gate driver + MOSFETs | §1–§3 |
| Thermal transient and steady state | PCB, MOSFETs, heat spreader | §4 |
| DShot600 timing, timer and DMA mapping | STM32H743 / ArduPilot ChibiOS HAL | §5–§6 |
| Netlists | Both | §7 |

**Engineering finding up front.** On a 20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm "nano" board, **80 A per channel is a transient rating only.** The model below shows that roughly 86 W of dissipation with all four channels at 80 A drives the board to its thermal limit in about **2 s** without a spreader, or about **9 s** with a 10 g aluminium spreader. An 80 A specification is defensible only as a **single-channel, ≤5–10 s burst**. The firmware current limit and thermal derating in §4.5 are mandatory.

## 1. Commutation Model

Each channel uses a three-phase bridge (6 FETs) with trapezoidal 6-step commutation and complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default.

In every 60° sector:
- One phase high-side FET is PWM-switched at duty D, with its low-side partner conducting complementarily during (1–D).
- One other phase's low-side FET is held continuously ON.
- The third phase floats (BEMF sensing).

The motor current I therefore always flows through **exactly two conducting channels in series**:

$$
R_{path} = 2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on),25}
$$

This holds regardless of D, because during the PWM off-time the current freewheels through the synchronous low-side FET rather than its diode, except during dead time.

## 2. Conduction Losses (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Per channel

$$
P_{cond} = I_{pk}^2 \cdot 2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on),25}
= 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega
= \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Per FET

Each FET conducts for a fraction of the electrical cycle. The high-side and complementary low-side split a 120° conduction window by D and (1–D). Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries about 1/3 of the total I² burden:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\cdot 2 = \frac{19.2}{3} = 6.4\ \text{W (total across 6 FETs is } 19.2\text{ W)}
$$

Per-FET average: 19.2 W / 6 = **3.2 W**.

The instantaneous loss while conducting matters for junction ripple:

$$
P_{FET,inst} = I^2 k_T R_{ds,25} = 80^2 \times 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{9.6\ W}
$$

### 2.3 PCB copper loss

This is frequently forgotten and is dominant on nano boards. For a phase path of length l, width w, and copper thickness t (70 µm = 2 oz, ρ_Cu,100°C ≈ 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m):

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, l}{w\, t\, n_{layers}}
$$

Example: l = 15 mm, w = 4 mm, 4 paralleled 2 oz layers:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.004\times70\times10^{-6}\times4} = 0.29\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Two phase paths plus the bus path (~0.3 mΩ shared) give about 0.9 mΩ effective:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{5.8\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

This is 30% of the FET conduction loss. **Plane copper is as important as FET choice.**

## 3. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched leg (one HS/LS pair per channel) incurs switching loss. The hold-on low-side FET does not switch.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time and is hard-recovered when the HS turns on:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I, so treat this as a lower bound. Measure it.
