# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #35)

## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Corrections

These baseline decisions shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not run the motors directly.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs one dedicated motor-control MCU per channel, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs. The "48 kHz" is the ESC PWM switching frequency, not anything the H743 produces.
2. **"80A" means an 80A per-motor burst** on 6S (25.2 V max). The analysis below shows that a nano (30×30 mm) board can sustain this only for seconds, not continuously.
3. **Parameters are representative, not from a specific datasheet.** The model is parametric, so substitute your selected MOSFET's datasheet values. The baseline assumes a 40 V, 5×6 PQFN, single FET per switch position.

| Symbol | Meaning | Baseline value |
|---|---|---|

| I_pk | Burst phase current | 80 A |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases conduct.

- The PWM'd leg alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D of the period) and the complementary low-side FET (fraction 1−D). Its loss is therefore I²R_ds(D + 1 − D) = I²R_ds, independent of duty cycle.
- The return leg is a low-side FET held on continuously, also dissipating I²R_ds.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

At 80 A and 1.38 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.38\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

Each FET conducts roughly ⅓ of the electrical period, so the average per FET is 17.7 / 6 ≈ **2.95 W**. The worst-case instantaneous figure is 8.8 W per FET while it is on.

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Model | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz inner/outer copper | ≈0.4 mΩ per path | 2 paths × I² × R | 2 × 6400 × 0.4 mΩ = 5.1 W |
| Shared current shunt | 0.25 mΩ | Σ I_avg² × R | ≈1.6 W per motor at full throttle |

The copper term is why 8-layer 2 oz construction is mandatory. Copper loss is not a rounding error: it is about 30% of FET conduction loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In six-step drive, only one leg hard-switches per commutation sector.

**(a) V·I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$

The edge time follows from the gate drive:

$$t_{edge} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{g,drv} + R_{g,int})}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With 12 nC, 3 Ω, 10 V and 3.5 V, this gives about 5.5 ns of voltage transition plus current commutation, so 12 ns per edge is conservative for layout parasitics.

**(b) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss,tr} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 2\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

**(c) Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Reducing dead time to 50 ns recovers about 0.3 W per motor, but only if the gate driver's propagation matching allows it.

**(d) Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET)**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 80\text{nC}\times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.08\ W$$

**Switching total:** P_sw ≈ 1.16 + 0.06 + 0.61 + 0.06 = **1.89 W per motor**, plus 0.08 W in the driver.

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.

| f_sw | P_sw per motor |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 0.95 W |
| 48 kHz | 1.89 W |
| 96 kHz | 3.8 W |

At 80A, 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise. Conduction loss dominates about 9:1.

## 3. Total Burst Loss Budget

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (T_j = 110 °C) | 17.7 W | 70.8 W |
| Switching (48 kHz) | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Gate drive and logic | 0.2 W | 0.8 W |
| **Total** | **26.5 W** | **≈106 W** |

At about 2 kW electrical output (4 × 80 A × ~6 V average phase voltage × duty), this is roughly 95% efficient. The problem is not efficiency but the fact that 106 W is concentrated in 9 cm².
