# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): System Architecture & Analysis

## 0. Executive Finding

The math in §2–§4 shows that **an 80 A burst per channel is not survivable on a 20×20 mm nano board** under any realistic burst window and component set. With best-case DFN3.3 MOSFETs:

- Each channel dissipates **≈ 21 W** at 80 A.
- All four channels together dissipate **≈ 90 W**.
- The board reaches **150 °C+ in about 1.7 s** of synchronized four-motor burst.
- The theoretical maximum battery current is **320 A**. No nano-scale XT30/XT60 pad, wire, or plane can carry that.

The model below is complete and parameterized, so it can be re-run for any FET or board size. **My recommendations:**

| Option | Form factor | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| A | 20×20 nano | 35 A continuous / 45 A burst (≤ 5 s) per channel |
| B | 30.5×30.5 | 55 A continuous / 80 A burst (≤ 2 s, 10 s cooldown) per channel, with DFN5×6 FETs |

Everything after this section is engineered so that it is valid for either option.

## 1. System Partitioning

```
 6S LiPo ──► VBAT bus ──┬──► 4× 3-phase bridge (24 FETs) ──► Motors 1-4
                        │        ▲ gate drive (4× FD6288Q-class 3-ph driver)
                        │        │
                        │   4× ESC MCU (AT32F421K8U7, AM32 firmware, 48 kHz PWM)
                        │        ▲ DShot600 bidirectional (single wire, inverted)
                        │        │
                        └──► 5V/3V3 BEC ──► STM32H743VIH6 (ArduPilot) ── IMU/Baro/OSD/UARTs
```

**Rationale for per-channel ESC MCUs (not H743-driven FOC):** ArduPilot has no in-tree motor-commutation driver. Its integration path is DShot plus eRPM telemetry, which feeds the harmonic notch filter (`INS_HNTCH_MODE=3`).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Assumptions

| Symbol | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S full charge (use 26.1 V for HV packs) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Burst phase current |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 class, V_GS = 10 V |
| α_R | 0.0045 /K | Normalized R_ds(on) tempco: ≈1.45× at 125 °C, ≈1.6× at 150 °C |
| R_Cu,path | 0.35 mΩ | Phase path copper: 4-layer 2 oz, ~3 squares paralleled |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | Complementary PWM with synchronous rectification |

### 2.2 Six-step synchronous conduction

At any instant during a 60° commutation sector, current flows through:

- one high-side FET for duty fraction D, or its complementary low-side FET for (1 − D), on the PWM leg, and
- one continuously-on low-side FET on the return leg.

Exactly **two FET channels are therefore always in series** (ignoring dead time). This holds regardless of D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \big[2R_{ds}(T_j)\big] + I^2 R_{Cu}(T)
$$

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R (T_j-25)]
$$

### 2.3 Numerical result at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds | FET conduction | Copper (R_Cu at T_board 100 °C ≈ 0.45 mΩ) | **Total per channel** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.9 W | **15.7 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.9 W | **20.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 2.9 W | **21.5 W** |

**The model has positive feedback.** Higher R_ds raises P, which raises T_j, which raises R_ds further. Thermal runaway threshold condition:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th,ja} \ge 1
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha_R\,R_{th,ja} \ge 1
$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 0.001 × 0.0045 = 0.0576 W/K. Runaway occurs if R_th,ja ≥ **17 K/W** per FET pair. A nano board with no airflow is around 25–40 K/W board-to-ambient, so **steady-state 80 A diverges**. Only transient (burst) operation is possible, as analyzed in §4.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

In six-step operation only **one leg switches per PWM period**, giving one turn-on and one turn-off transition plus two dead-time intervals.

### 3.1 Assumptions

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| t_r + t_f (V-I overlap) | 20 ns total (gate drive ~1 A source/sink, R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω) |
| Q_rr (body diode) | 50 nC |
| Q_oss | 40 nC |
| Q_g | 30 nC at V_GS = 10 V |
| t_dt (dead time) | 200 ns per edge |
| V_F (body diode) | 0.8 V at 80 A, hot |

### 3.2 Loss terms

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss · V · f_sw (approximation) = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8·80·400 ns·48 k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw = 2·30 nC·10·48 k | **0.03 W** |
| **Total per channel** | | **≈ 2.34 W** |

### 3.3 Design levers

- **Dead-time loss exceeds V-I overlap loss.** Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. AM32 dead-time must be tuned against the actual driver propagation skew (≈ ±30 ns for FD6288-class drivers).
- **Switching frequency.** 48 kHz is near optimal here. Going to 24 kHz would save about 1.2 W but raise current ripple and audible noise on low-inductance motors (L_ph ≈ 15–30 µH).
