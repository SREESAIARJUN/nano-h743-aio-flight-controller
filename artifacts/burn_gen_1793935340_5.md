# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These issues determine whether the rest of the design is valid, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an ArduPilot system, the H743 runs the autopilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor is commutated by its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or a BLHeli_32 target) driving a 3-phase gate driver. The 48 kHz PWM lives in the ESC MCUs, not the H743. Everything below follows that partition.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm PCB) is transient-only.** Section 3 shows roughly 100 W of total dissipation at 4×80 A. The board's thermal mass of about 4 J/K limits this to an **~1.5–2 s burst**. The realistic continuous rating is **~20–25 A per motor**, even in prop wash. Marketing "80 A burst" figures on this form factor are optimistic.
3. **All component values below are design assumptions.** Replace them with the datasheet values of the parts you actually select.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel, 80 A burst)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

**Current paths.** In 6-step synchronous operation, exactly two FETs carry I_ph at every instant (ignoring dead time). Each leg spends 1/3 of the electrical period in each state:

| Leg state | Fraction of period | What conducts |
|---|---|---|
| High (PWM) | 1/3 | HS conducts for D; LS conducts the complementary 1 − D |
| Low (static) | 1/3 | LS conducts 100 % |
| Float (BEMF sensing) | 1/3 | Nothing |
