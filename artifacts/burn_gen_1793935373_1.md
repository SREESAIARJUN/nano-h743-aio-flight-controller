## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

### 1.1 Current path

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is high (PWM), one is low (held ON), and one floats.

- **ON-time (D):** current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- **OFF-time (1−D), sync rectified:** current flows through LS(A) → motor → LS(B).

So **exactly two FETs carry I_phase at all times**, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ /K \Rightarrow R_{110} \approx 1.5\ m\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 19.2\ \text{W per motor}$$

PCB copper path: phase pad → via field → bus plane, estimated R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per motor.

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the time, so the average per FET is:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 19.2/6 = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The low-side FETs carry slightly more at low duty, because they conduct during the hold-low sector plus the freewheel period.

**Electrical-frequency check.** At 30k RPM with 14 poles, f_e = 7 × 500 = 3.5 kHz, so the period is 286 µs. That is far shorter than the FET thermal time constant (ms range), so **averaging is valid**. No per-commutation thermal ripple needs to be modelled.

### 1.5 Self-consistency (thermal runaway check)

R_DS rises with temperature, so conduction loss and junction temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}board}, \qquad P(T_j)=I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]\cdot\tfrac{2}{6}\cdot... $$

The solution is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}R_\theta < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; \bar I^2 R_{25}\alpha \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} \cdot R_\theta < 1$$

With R_θ,j-board ≈ 9 K/W: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0059 × 0.33 × 9 = **0.11**. This is stable, with a large margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side switch hard-switches (one per motor at a time). Its complementary low-side soft-switches during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$t_{r}\approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G}} = \frac{10\,nC}{1\,A}=10\ ns$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\times10^{-9})(48000)= \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\times10^{-9})(48000) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery and C_oss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40n(25.2)(48k) = 0.048\ W$$

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.048\ W$$

### 2.4 Gate drive

Most of this is dissipated in the driver and R_G, not in the FET.

$$P_{gate} = 2\cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(30n)(10)(48k) = 0.029\ W$$

### 2.5 Switching total

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 0.97+0.61+0.05+0.05+0.03 = \mathbf{1.71\ W}$$

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it drops to about 0.85 W, but you get more audible noise and higher current ripple. At 96 kHz it rises to about 3.4 W. The 48 kHz choice is reasonable: switching loss is under 10% of conduction loss at 80 A.
