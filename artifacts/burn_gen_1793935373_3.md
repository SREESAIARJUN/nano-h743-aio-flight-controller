# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, DShot Timer Plan, and Reference Netlist

Two things need flagging before the analysis.

1. **This is a model built on stated assumptions, not a verified design.** MOSFET parameters, layout resistances and thermal mass are representative values. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet numbers and validate the result with a thermocouple or IR test on a prototype. The equations are general; the numbers are only as good as the inputs.
2. **80 A burst per motor on a nano-format board is at or beyond physical feasibility.** The model below shows why: about 90 W of heat into roughly 4 J/K of board, and up to 320 A battery current. I give a burst-duration limit rather than claiming it simply works.

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743, ArduPilot, DShot600 bidirectional out to the ESC |
| ESC | 4 × independent ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32), 3 half-bridges each, 24 MOSFETs total. The H743 does **not** run motor commutation. |
| Bus | 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM, f_sw = 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (per switch) | 30–40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN, R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gs2 + Q_gd = 9 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, Q_oss = 40 nC, V_plateau = 3.0 V, V_f(body) = 0.8 V |
| Gate drive | V_drv = 10 V, R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω (external + internal + driver), dead time 50 ns |
| Board | ~30×30 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer copper, no heatsink |

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous PWM

In any commutation sector, one leg is PWM-switched and one leg's low side returns the current continuously:

- **Switched leg:** the high side conducts for D·T_sw, and the low side conducts (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T_sw. Combined, this leg carries I for 100 % of the period.
- **Return leg:** the low side carries I for 100 % of the period.

Per-motor conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045·85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

Phase ripple is small at 48 kHz with typical motor inductance, so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.38\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.7\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Copper and shunt add to this. Assume about 0.5 mΩ of total path resistance per motor (phase pad, current path, shunt):

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\text{ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for roughly 1/3 of an electrical period. The die thermal time constant is about 1–10 ms, which is comparable to or longer than the electrical period at flight eRPM, so average power per FET is:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{17.7}{6} \approx 2.95\text{ W}
$$

Instantaneous power during the return-leg interval reaches 80² × 1.38 m ≈ **8.8 W**. That peak matters at low eRPM: a stalled or locked-rotor motor at 80 A will destroy the FET.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (48 kHz)

### 2.1 Transition times from gate charge

$$
t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_g} = \frac{9\text{ nC}}{(10-3)/4} = 5.1\text{ ns}
$$

$$
t_{off} \approx \frac{9\text{ nC}}{V_{pl}/R_g} = \frac{9\text{ nC}}{0.75\text{ A}} = 12\text{ ns}
$$

### 2.2 Loss components per motor

Only one leg switches hard at a time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·17 ns·48 kHz | 0.82 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
