# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): Design Package

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

**Architecture correction.** On a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 is the **flight controller (FC) MCU** running ArduPilot. It does not commutate motors. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The FC sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. This package covers both domains.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 40 V FETs give about 37% margin over spikes |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mount, 8-layer, 2 oz copper | A 20 × 20 mm "nano" board cannot support 80 A (see §2.6) |
| Burst | 80 A **phase current** per channel, ≤ 2 s | Continuous rating derived in §2.6 |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C/10 V, PDFN 5×6, Q_g ≈ 50 nC, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC, Q_rr ≈ 40 nC | 6 per channel, 24 total |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, ~1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink | Fed from 12 V rail |
| PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), dead time t_d = 100 ns | AM32 typical |
| FC clock | H743 at 400 MHz (ArduPilot default), timer clock 200 MHz | Matters for DShot timing |

**Notation.** "≈" marks a datasheet-dependent value. Recompute it for your actual FET part number.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology

The motor is driven by 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM. At any instant, two phase legs are active:

- The **PWM leg** alternates HS↔LS at 48 kHz.
- The **return leg** holds its LS on.
- Synchronous rectification means motor current *always* flows through exactly **two FET channels** in series. The exception is dead time, when current flows through one body diode.

So the conduction loss per motor channel is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \cdot (1 - 2t_d f_{sw})$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0060\ /\text{K}$$

This gives a factor of about 1.6× at 125 °C, which is typical for 40 V trench FETs.

At the burst design point T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.45 = 1.74\ \text{mΩ}$$

Copper trace/plane resistance per current path is about 0.3 mΩ (phase pad → FET → bus plane, 2 oz, 8 layers paralleled):

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.74 + 0.3)\,\text{mΩ} \times 0.99 = 6400 \times 3.78\,\text{mΩ} \times 0.99 = \mathbf{23.9\ W}$$

Split of that total:

| Element | Loss |
|---|---|
| Each active FET | ≈ 11.0 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W |

### 1.3 Switching loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Use V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case, full charge).

**(a) V-I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off of HS).** Transition times follow from the driver currents:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.67\,\text{A}} \approx 15\,\text{ns}, \quad t_f \approx 15\,\text{ns}$$

The 0.67 A effective figure assumes a 12 V driver into a ~4.5 V plateau through gate resistance.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr can be 2–3× the datasheet value. Budget 0.15 W.

**(c) Output capacitance.** With Q_oss ≈ 50 nC:

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
