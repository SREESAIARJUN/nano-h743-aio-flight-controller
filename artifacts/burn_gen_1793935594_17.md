# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bi-directional DShot600 to **four separate ESC MCUs**. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Each ESC MCU drives a three-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs. All power-stage math below is per ESC channel, then scaled ×4.

### 0.2 The 80A burst spec is the binding constraint

On a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board, 4 × 80 A is a **320 A** battery-side burst. The model shows this is survivable only for roughly 1–3 seconds, and only when the motors burst asymmetrically. It is not a continuous rating. The connector and wiring are not part of the board, but they will saturate first. For example, an XT60 is rated around 60 A continuous.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

Assume six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM, as used by AM32 and BLHeli_32. At any instant, phase current I flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels:

- the PWM-leg high-side (during on-time) or low-side (during off-time, synchronous rectification), **and**
- the static-leg low-side.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

Let r = ΔI_pp / I be the relative current ripple.

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

Motor inductance at 48 kHz typically gives r ≈ 0.2–0.4. Using r = 0.3 gives a correction factor of 1.0075, which is negligible.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 1.4 Conduction loss per channel

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \;-\; \underbrace{(\text{dead-time fraction})}_{\text{moved to diode term in §2}}$$

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6448 × 0.0015 = **19.3 W** | 77 W |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.6 W** | 106 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **29.0 W** | 116 W |

### 1.6 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 120° electrical out of 360°.

- **Static-leg low-side:** 1/3 of the time at full current, giving ≈ I²R/3 averaged per FET.
- **PWM-leg high-side and low-side:** split the remaining third by duty cycle D.

At full throttle (D → 1), the per-FET average is approximately:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \approx 4.8\ \text{W @ } 125\ °C$$

This average is useful for slow thermal mass. However, the instantaneous per-FET loss while conducting is I²R = 14.4 W. **That instantaneous value drives the short-time-constant junction rise (§3.3).**

### 1.7 Copper and PCB losses (often forgotten)

Assume 2 oz inner layers and about 10 mm of 3 mm-wide shared trace per phase path:

- R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ, giving 80² × 0.5 m = **3.2 W per channel**.

The ground and V_bus planes carry the 4-channel sum. I_bat can approach 4 × I × D, so the plane I²R scales with the square of total current. Pour solid planes on at least 2 layers each, and stitch with at least 20 vias per FET pad.
