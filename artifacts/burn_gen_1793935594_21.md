# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and BDShot Design Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and a Feasibility Warning

These assumptions are stated up front because every number below depends on them. Change any of them and re-run the equations.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm mounting, ~36×36 mm board | "Nano" class |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | |
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 at 400 MHz (ArduPilot default) | Runs flight control and DShot only |
| ESC MCUs | 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 | These generate the 48 kHz PWM, not the H743 |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) ×4 | Common in AIO designs |
| MOSFET (example) | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on),25°C = 1.5 mΩ, Q_g = 40 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 50 nC | Substitute your actual part's datasheet values |
| PCB | 6-layer, 2 oz all layers | |

**Feasibility warning.** The model in Section 3 shows that 80 A on all four channels at once is a seconds-scale thermal event on a nano-class board, not a rating. The board can survive a ~1–2 s burst, but its continuous capability is roughly 20–25 A per channel. If marketing requires an "80 A" label, it must be a burst rating with a defined duration and duty cycle, enforced by firmware.

Also clarify which current "80 A" refers to. Battery current and phase current are related by duty cycle:

$$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{phase}$$

An "80 A ESC" rating usually means battery-side current. At partial throttle, phase current (which is what heats the FETs) is higher. This model uses **I_phase = 80 A** as the worst case.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25} \cdot \left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}, \quad \alpha \approx 2.0\text{–}2.3$$

A linear approximation is good enough for design work: R(125 °C) ≈ 1.5 × R(25 °C) = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, exactly two phase legs carry current: one high-side path and one low-side path.

- During PWM-on, the active high-side FET and the opposing low-side FET conduct.
- During PWM-off, the complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectification) and the opposing low-side FET conduct.

In both cases, two channel resistances are in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Distribution across the six FETs

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period (a 120° block):

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \approx \frac{I^2 R}{3} = \mathbf{4.8\ W}$$

The instantaneous loss during a FET's conduction block is I²R = 14.4 W.

**Thermal ripple check.** At 30,000 eRPM the electrical period is 2 ms, so each conduction block is about 0.67 ms. This is comparable to the die-level thermal time constant of 3×3 packages (~1–5 ms). Junction ripple of roughly ±5–10 K is therefore expected, and the average model alone is not sufficient for the worst-case junction temperature. Use Z_th(j-c) at t_p = 0.67 ms from the datasheet:

$$\Delta T_{ripple} \approx 14.4\text{ W} \cdot Z_{th}(0.67\text{ ms}) \approx 14.4 \times 0.4 \approx 6\text{ K}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses

| Element | Estimate | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad / trace (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Bus copper share (per channel) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |
| Shunt (if per-channel, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |

**Copper subtotal: about 3–5 W per channel.** This is often ignored and it should not be.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active high-side FET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side FET commutates softly, through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Assume t_r + t_f = 40 ns. The actual value depends on gate resistance, driver current, and Q_gd.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr can double. Budget **0.1 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.03\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(50\text{ ns}) \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
