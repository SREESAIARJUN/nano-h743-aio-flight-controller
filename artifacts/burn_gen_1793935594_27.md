## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]^{\,},\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design limit T_j = 115°C:

$$R_{hot} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.0055 \times 90) = 1.50\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-sector current path

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D. The synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side conducts for 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{ph}^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The result is independent of D, because synchronous rectification removes the duty dependence.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. At 80A with a typical 2207 motor at ~25k eRPM per pole pair, the electrical period is ~400 µs. That is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~5–20 ms), so per-FET loss can be time-averaged:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \frac{19.2\ \text{W}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The worst instantaneous case is a stalled or locked rotor, where one FET carries the full 80A continuously: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W per FET**. AM32 stall protection must trip in under 200 ms.
