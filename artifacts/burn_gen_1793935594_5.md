# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #35)

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

These assumptions drive every number below. Change any of them and re-run the model.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | H743 FC running ArduPilot, plus a separate 4-in-1 ESC with one MCU per channel (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware) | The H743 cannot sensibly drive 12 FETs directly. It has only two advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8); HRTIM is possible but has no ArduPilot support. |
| Battery | 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V | |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, PDFN5x6, R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ typ at 25 °C / V_gs = 10 V, Q_g = 70 nC, Q_gd = 12 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC | 40 V on 6S leaves about 37% margin. Use 60 V parts if regen braking or long battery leads are expected. |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM (synchronous rectification) | |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, dead time 100 ns | |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel, all 4 channels simultaneously (worst case) | |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 3 oz inner power planes | |

**Feasibility warning:** Truly "nano" (20 × 20 mm) boards cannot sustain 4 × 80 A for more than about 1 s. Section 3 shows that even 30.5 × 30.5 mm supports roughly 5 s, not the 10 s commonly advertised. Treat "80 A burst" as a thermal-time-limited rating and specify the duration on the datasheet.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{ds}(100) = 1.2 \times 1.3375 = 1.605\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path per channel in 6-step drive

During each 60° sector, current flows through two legs:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T and the synchronous low-side for (1−D)·T.
- **Static leg:** the low-side is on 100% of the sector.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\big[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)R_{LS}\big] + I^2 R_{LS} \;\approx\; 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This approximation is independent of D because R_HS ≈ R_LS. Add the copper path (busbar and phase pads) at about 0.25 mΩ per path:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2\,(2\times1.605 + 0.25)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.46\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{22.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Loss distribution per FET

Each FET conducts 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. At normal eRPM, losses therefore average over all 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{2I^2R_{ds}}{6} = 3.42\ \text{W}$$

At very low eRPM (stall or startup), one FET pair carries the full I²R continuously, about 10.3 W per FET. This is the worst-case hot spot. The firmware must enforce a stall current limit below 40 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Loss terms per channel at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f, with t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd/I_g = 12 nC / 0.8 A = 15 ns | ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 50 n·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f | 0.5·60 n·25.2·48 k = **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | 2·70 n·10·48 k = **0.07 W** (dissipated in driver) |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.23 W** |

### 2.1 Design levers

- **Dead time** is the second-largest switching term. Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify against the driver's propagation skew first.
- **PWM frequency:** switching losses scale linearly with f. At 24 kHz they would drop to about 1.1 W, but you would trade more current ripple and motor iron loss.

### 2.2 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu) | 22.1 W | 88.4 W |
| Switching | 2.2 W | 8.9 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if low-side shunt per channel) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| Driver + LDO + MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **26.2 W** | **≈ 105 W** |
