# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #29): Power Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Feasibility Check

Before modelling, the spec has to be interpreted, because the two readings of "80A burst" lead to very different hardware.

| Interpretation | Total battery current | Verdict on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO |
|---|---|---|
| **A: 80 A per motor** (4 × 80 A) | ~320 A | **Not physically achievable.** The board cannot absorb or spread ~120 W of FET loss, and pads, connectors and copper cannot carry 320 A. |
| **B: 80 A total burst** (~20 A per motor) | 80 A | Achievable with good FETs and 2 oz+ copper. |

I model Interpretation A fully below, because it shows *why* it fails, and then give a design for B. Typical nano AIOs are rated 12–25 A continuous / 30–35 A burst per motor. Any "80 A" nano product claim refers to the sum of all motors, if it is real at all.

**Architectural note.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. The 4-in-1 ESC section uses four ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32/Bluejay. Each drives a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is the ESC MCU's switching frequency.

## 1. Loss Model: Definitions

Per motor channel: 3 half-bridges, 6 N-MOSFETs, 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM).

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Phase current (burst) | 80 A (case A) / 20 A (case B) |
| D | PWM duty at burst | ≈1.0 at full throttle (worst-case conduction) |
| R_ds(on),25 | 40 V FET, 3.3×3.3 DFN class | 1.5 mΩ |
| k_T | R_ds temperature factor at T_j ≈ 125 °C | 1.5 → R_hot = 2.25 mΩ |
| t_r + t_f | Switching transition times (driver ~1–2 A) | 20 ns |
| Q_rr | Body diode reverse-recovery charge | 40 nC |
| C_oss | Output capacitance (energy-equivalent) | 1.2 nF |
| t_dead | Dead time | 100 ns |
| V_SD | Body-diode forward voltage | 0.8 V |
| f_sw | PWM frequency | 48 kHz |

## 2. Conduction Loss

In any 6-step sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the low side and the complementary high side share the current over the PWM period. The current is therefore always flowing through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} \cdot 2$$

The per-FET average over a full electrical cycle is P_cond/2 × (1/3), because each leg conducts in 2 of 6 sectors per FET. However, **instantaneous** dissipation within a sector is I²R per conducting FET. That instantaneous value is what matters for transient junction heating at low eRPM.

**Case A (80 A/motor):**
- P_cond = 80² × 2.25 mΩ × 2 = **28.8 W per motor**
- Instantaneous per conducting FET: 14.4 W

**Case B (20 A/motor):**
- P_cond = 20² × 2.25 mΩ × 2 = **1.8 W per motor**

**Iteration for R_ds(on) self-heating.** R depends on temperature, and temperature depends on loss:

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

Solve T_j = T_board + P(R(T_j))·Z_th iteratively. Two passes usually converge.

## 3. Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched FET in each sector hard-switches. With complementary PWM, one transistor hard-switches and the complementary one switches softly (ZVS-like via the body diode).

**3.1 V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

- Case A: 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W**
- Case B: **0.24 W**

**3.2 C_oss loss**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.2\,\mathrm{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.018\ \mathrm{W}$$

**3.3 Reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

The Q_rr scales roughly with I_F. Use 0.1 W as a conservative value for case A.

**3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dead} f_{sw}$$

- Case A: 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**
- Case B: **0.15 W**

**3.5 Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching}$$

With Q_g = 45 nC, V_drv = 10 V, and 2 actively switched gates per sector: about **0.04 W** per motor.

**3.6 Per-motor total**

| Loss | Case A (80 A) | Case B (20 A) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.8 W | 1.80 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 0.24 W |
| C_oss + Q_rr | 0.12 W | 0.07 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| **Per motor** | **≈30.5 W** | **≈2.26 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈122 W** | **≈9.0 W** |

**Conclusions from the loss breakdown:**
- Conduction dominates (~94% in case A).
- Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about +0.8 W per motor at 80 A. It is not the thermal limiter.
- The real lever is R_ds(on) and parallel FETs. Conduction loss scales as 1/N with N paralleled devices, but a nano board has no area for them.

Also add copper loss:
- 1 oz trace is about 0.5 mΩ/square. A realistic phase path is ~1 mΩ including pads and solder.
- Case A: 80² × 1 mΩ × 2 ≈ **12.8 W extra per motor**. This pushes the total to roughly 175 W.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state network (per FET)

```
T_j ──R_θJC──● T_case ──R_θCB──● T_board ──R_θBA──● T_amb
             (1.5 K/W)      (solder+vias ~2 K/W)   (board→air, prop wash)
```

Board-to-air for a 25.5 mm 6-layer board with prop wash (~5 m/s):

$$h \approx 30\text{–}50\ \mathrm{W/m^2K}, \quad A \approx 2 \times 6.5\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 1.3 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m^2}$$

$$R_{\theta BA} \approx \frac{1}{h A} \approx 15\text{–}25\ \mathrm{K/W}\ \text{(whole board)}$$

**Case B steady state:** 9 W × ~18 K/W ≈ 160 K rise.

This means case B is also **not continuous-rated** without airflow beyond prop wash or a heatsink. It is a burst rating. A continuous rating of about 4–5 W total (≈12–14 A per motor) keeps the board near 100 °C at 25 °C ambient.

### 4.2 Burst (transient) model: lumped adiabatic

For bursts shorter than the board time constant (τ = R·C ≈ 18 K/W × 3.5 J/K ≈ 60 s), heat goes mostly into thermal mass:

$$\Delta T_{board} = \frac{P \, t}{m\,c_p}$$

Board plus components: m ≈ 8 g at an effective c_p ≈ 0.45 J/gK, giving C_th ≈ 3.6 J/K.

| Case | P total | 2 s burst | 5 s burst | 10 s burst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (80 A/motor) | 122 W (+copper ≈ 175 W) | +68–97 K | +170–243 K | **+340–490 K** (destroyed) |
| B (80 A total) | 9 W | +5 K | +12.5 K | +25 K |

### 4.3 Junction transient over the board (case A, single FET)

Use the datasheet Z_th(JC) curve. For a 3.3×3.3 DFN at a 10 ms pulse, Z_th ≈ 0.3 K/W; at steady state it is ~1.5 K/W.

$$\Delta T_{j-board}(t) = P_{FET} \cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

- At 1 s: 14.4 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ **+22 K** over the board, which is already near 100 °C+.
- Starting at 80 °C, case A reaches T_j > 150 °C within about 1 s.
- Case B: 0.9 W per FET × 1.5 K/W gives only **+1.4 K**. Board temperature dominates.

### 4.4 Design rule derived from the model

Maximum burst current per motor for burst time t, board start temperature T_0, and limit T_max = 125 °C:

$$I_{max}(t) = \sqrt{\frac{(T_{max} - T_0) - P_{sw}(I)\,Z}{2 R_{hot}\,Z_{eff}(t)}}$$

Here Z_eff(t) ≈ Z_th,JC + t/(4·C_th,per-motor), with C_th per motor ≈ 0.9 J/K.
