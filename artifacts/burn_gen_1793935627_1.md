# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller (Job #13): Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

This system splits into two computing domains, because the H743 does not commutate the motors:

- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It emits bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines.
- **ESC:** 4 independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32, or equivalent), each with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs. Total: 24 FETs.

The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM carrier. Unknown parameters are given explicit placeholder values. Every result below is a closed-form function, so you can substitute datasheet values directly.

**Feasibility:** 80 A burst per channel on a true nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically sustainable beyond about 1 s (see §3.4). A 30.5×30.5 mm board is required for credible 80 A bursts of 5–10 s. I model the 20×20 case and flag where it fails.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology current path
I assume 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, as in AM32).

- During the on-time (fraction D), current flows: high-side of phase X → motor → low-side of phase Y.
- During the off-time (fraction 1−D), current freewheels through the low-side of X (synchronous) and the low-side of Y.

Either way, **exactly two FET channels carry the full phase current** at all times. Conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad k \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow \alpha(125^\circ\text{C}) = 1.5$$

### 1.2 Numbers

| Condition | R_ds | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 2·6400·0.00225 = **28.8 W** | 115.2 W |

### 1.3 Electrothermal coupling (runaway check)
Conduction loss rises with junction temperature, so a stable operating point must exist. Solving self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\cdot 2I^2R_{ds,25}\left[1+k(T_j-25)\right]$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + R_{th}P_0(1-25k)}{1 - R_{th}P_0 k}, \qquad P_0 = 2I^2R_{ds,25}$$

A stable solution exists only if **R_th · P_0 · k < 1**. With P_0 = 19.2 W, the limit is R_th < 10.4 K/W (channel-level, to ambient). A 20×20 board in prop-wash is roughly 8–15 K/W per channel. This sits at the runaway boundary, which is why burst duration must be time-limited (§3).

### 1.4 Per-FET loss
Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The high-side carries D of that interval and the low-side carries the remainder.

- Average per-FET conduction loss is about P_cond/6 ≈ **4.8 W** (at 125 °C).
- The worst-case low-side FET, at low D, approaches **~9.6 W**.

At 80 A, motor eRPM is high, so the electrical period is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). The averaging is therefore valid.

### 1.5 Copper and interconnect
Board I²R is not negligible. Consider a phase pad trace of 2 oz copper with 4 paralleled layers, length 8 mm, effective width 4 mm:

$$R = \rho L / (w\,t) = 1.72\text{e-}8 \cdot 0.008 / (0.004 \cdot 4 \cdot 70\text{e-}6) \approx 0.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That gives ≈ **0.8 W** per phase path at 80 A
