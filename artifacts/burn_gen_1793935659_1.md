# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Technical Design Package

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points change the scope of the request.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz switching PWM.** On every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel runs its own MCU (STM32G071, AT32F421 or EFM8 under AM32 or BLHeli_32/S). That MCU commutates the bridge at 48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This package models both sides but keeps them on separate timing domains.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** On a 20×20 mm board the thermal model below supports only about 1–2 s at 80 A on all four channels. Section 3 derives this. If the requirement is a sustained 80 A, you need a 30.5×30.5 mm board with paralleled FETs, or heatsinking.

3. **The netlists are reference-level, not release-ready.** Verify part numbers, pinouts and alternate-function mappings against the current datasheets, the STM32H743 reference manual (RM0433) and the ArduPilot hwdef documentation before layout.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phases carry current in every sector:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1−D).
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds,hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

The result does not depend on duty cycle, which is the main benefit of synchronous rectification.

Ripple correction: I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12. With motor inductance around 10 µH line-to-line, ΔI is a few amperes. That adds less than 0.1 %, so it is neglected.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}
$$

Copper losses in the same two-path conduction:

$$
P_{cu} = 2 I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{6.4\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The low-side FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at zero voltage.

**Hard-switching overlap:**
$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 27\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.31\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

**Gate drive (two switching FETs).** This loss is dissipated in the driver, not in the FETs.
$$
P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Loss summary

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| PCB copper | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Hard switching | 1.31 W | 5.2 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 W | 0.44 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 33.0 W** | **≈ 132 W** |

Conduction dominates switching by about 19:1 at 80 A. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per channel. R_ds(on) and copper are the real levers.

Efficiency at 80 A: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 0.8 that is about 1.6 kW per channel, giving roughly 98 % efficiency. Efficiency is good. The problem is power density.

### 2.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts 120° electrical, which is one third of the electrical period.

- **Instantaneous dissipation while conducting:** I²R_ds,hot = 12.3 W.
- **Electrical period:** at about 100 k eRPM, the commutation period is about 600 µs. That is much shorter than the die thermal time constant of roughly 5–20 ms, so use the averaged value.
- **Average per FET:** P_FET,avg ≈ (24.6 + 1.97) / 6 ≈ **4.4 W**. High-side FETs carry the switching share, so for them use 4.4 + 0.3 ≈ **4.7 W**.
