# STM32H743 4-in-1 AIO Flight Controller (Job #55): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Feasibility Warning and Assumptions

**80 A burst on a nano AIO is very tight.** Section 3 shows ~26 W of loss per motor at 80 A, or ~105 W with all four motors bursting. A 20×20 or 25.5 mm AIO has roughly 5–7 J/K of heat capacity, so the board can sustain this for **about 2–4 seconds**, not tens of seconds. I give the full model so you can apply it to your actual BOM. My recommendation:
- use a 30.5 mm form factor or parallel FETs, and
- treat "80 A" as a **≤2 s burst rating with firmware enforcement**.

**Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and does not commutate the motors. Each motor has its own ESC MCU and gate driver:
- ESC MCU: 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32.
- Gate driver: 4× 3-phase driver (FD6288Q class).
- Power stage: 24 N-FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Per-FET loss over one commutation cycle

In each of the six sectors:
- one phase is PWM-high, with its high-side FET (HS) on for duty D and its low-side FET (LS) synchronously rectifying for 1−D;
- one phase has its LS on continuously;
- one phase floats.

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical period as source, 1/3 as sink, and 1/3 floating. This gives the per-FET losses:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Per-motor total

At every instant exactly two channel resistances carry I in series, so the duty cycle drops out of the total:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 3(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × 1.6 = **1.6 mΩ**.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Loss | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ | **12.8 W** |
| Hot (125 °C) | 2 × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| Worst single FET (LS, D = 0.9, hot) | (1.1/3) × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ | **3.75 W** |
| Worst HS FET (D = 0.9, hot) | (0.9/3) × 6400 × 1.6 mΩ | **3.07 W** |

The electrical frequency is several kHz, which is far faster than the FET thermal time constant (tens of ms). The sector-averaged values above are therefore valid for thermal analysis.

### 1.5 Copper loss

Assumptions:
- 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers in parallel;
- sheet resistance ≈ 0.245 mΩ/□ per 2 oz layer;
- the phase path (FET → pad) plus the share of the battery path ≈ 0.5 mΩ effective per motor current loop, hot.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 3.2\ \text{W/motor}$$

### 1.6 Option: two FETs in parallel per switch

Paralleling halves the effective R_ds: P_cond,hot drops to **10.2 W/motor**, at the cost of 48 FETs, which is unrealistic on a nano board.
