# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Architecture (Job #11)

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Running ArduPilot plus four 48 kHz six-step or FOC loops on one MCU is not a viable design. The standard and assumed architecture is:

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESCs.
- **ESC MCUs:** 4 × AT32F421 (or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. These generate the 48 kHz PWM and perform commutation.
- **Gate drivers:** 4 × FD6288Q three-phase drivers.
- **Power stage:** 24 × N-MOSFETs, 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3.

**2. 80A per motor on a nano (20×20) board is not thermally credible**, even for bursts. I have modelled a **30.5×30.5 mm power board** with a stacked 20×20 H743 FC. The equations below are parametric, so you can substitute your own MOSFET datasheet values.

**Model parameters:**

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, full charge |
| I_burst | 80 A per motor | Phase current, burst |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step, Complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, two phase legs are active.

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D) and the synchronous low-side FET (fraction 1−D).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously.

Because synchronous rectification is used, the current always flows through exactly one FET in each active leg, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds,hot} + I^2 R_{cu}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.8\text{ m}\Omega) + (80)^2(0.5\text{ m}\Omega) = 23.0 + 3.2 = \mathbf{26.2\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average is 23.0 / 6 ≈ **3.8 W**. The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R = 11.5 W.

**Electrothermal coupling.** Because R_ds(on) rises with temperature, solve iteratively:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

Then iterate T_j = T_board + P_FET · R_th,j-b until it converges. This usually takes about three iterations.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30n·48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dead·f_sw | 0.8·80·400n·48k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2·50n·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | V·Q_oss·f_sw | 25.2·40n·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (driver side) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·60n·10·48k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.85 W per motor** |

**Total per motor:** P_ESC ≈ 26.2 + 2.85 ≈ **29 W**

**4-in-1 board:** P_tot ≈ **116 W**

This is about 1.4% of the 8 kW electrical throughput.

**Sensitivity.** Conduction loss dominates (~90% of the total).

- Halving R_ds(on) saves about 11.5 W per motor.
- Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W per motor.

**Recommendation:** spend the budget on FET R_ds(on) and copper (2 oz minimum, 3 oz preferred on the power layers), not on switching speed.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction to board

Assume R_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W and R_th,c-b ≈ 5 K/W (via array: 12 × 0.3 mm plated vias under the drain pad). The per-FET average loss including the switching share is about 4.8 W.

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = 4.8 \times 6.5 \approx \mathbf{31\ K}$$

### 3.2 Board to air (lumped RC)

**Thermal capacitance.** The board mass is about 9 g (FR4 + Cu + FETs), giving C_th ≈ **8 J/K**.
