# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4‑in‑1 ESC (Job #19): Thermal, Switching and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Corrections

These points affect every number below.

| Item | Assumption / Finding |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst | 80 A **per motor phase**, so up to **320 A total** at the battery |
| Form factor | "Nano" stack, 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, ~37 × 37 mm board, 8‑layer, 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner power planes |
| PWM | 48 kHz, 6‑step with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), as in AM32 / BLHeli_32‑class firmware |
| MOSFET | 40 V, 5×6 mm, R_DS(on) = 0.9 mΩ typ / 1.1 mΩ max at 25 °C, Q_g ≈ 55 nC, Q_gd ≈ 10 nC, Q_rr ≈ 45 nC, V_SD ≈ 0.8 V. Substitute your exact part's datasheet values. |

**Architectural notes:**

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). These read back eRPM. The 48 kHz switching happens on the ESC MCUs. The H743 only owns the DShot timer and DMA problem (Section 4).
2. **80 A × 4 on a nano footprint is not thermally continuous.** The model in Section 3 shows it is a **~1–3 s burst rating**, limited by junction transient impedance. Treat "80 A" strictly as a burst spec. A defensible continuous rating is ~35–45 A per channel with airflow.
3. **320 A through a 37 mm board** requires battery pads rated for that current, ≥4 oz equivalent copper in the battery path, and no single shared current shunt (see Section 3.5).

## 1. Electrical Model of One ESC Channel

In 6‑step operation, two phases conduct at any instant. One half‑bridge is PWM'd at duty D, and the opposite low‑side is held ON.

With synchronous rectification, the current path always contains exactly **two conducting MOSFETs**:

- During t_on: the high‑side of the PWM phase plus the low‑side of the return phase.
- During t_off: the low‑side of the PWM phase (synchronous rectifier) plus the low‑side of the return phase.

The only exception is the dead time, when the body diode conducts instead of one FET.

Over each electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time (120° of conduction), with a role that rotates.

**Switching path (per channel):**

- Q_HS (PWM phase): hard‑switched at f_sw.
- Q_LS (PWM phase): soft‑switched (ZVS after the body diode), plus Q_rr loss.
- Q_LS (return phase): static ON, conduction loss only.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature‑dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0055\text{–}0.007\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

Use R_DS,25 = 1.1 mΩ (max) and α = 0.006. At T_j = 125 °C this gives **R_DS = 1.76 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Conduction loss (per channel)

Two FETs are always in the path, so:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)\cdot\left(1-2\,t_{dt}f_{sw}\right)$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.10 mΩ | 14.1 W |
| 100 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W |
| 125 °C | 1.76 mΩ | **22.5 W** |

The dead‑time correction (t_dt = 200 ns, giving 1.9 %) is negligible for this table.

**Per‑FET worst case** occurs at high throttle (D → 1), where the PWM high‑side carries nearly the full current for its whole 120° window:

$$P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.76\,\mathrm{m} = \mathbf{11.3\ W}\ \text{(instantaneous)}$$

$$P_{FET,avg} = 11.3/3 \approx 3.8\ \mathrm{W}\ \text{(averaged over rotation)}$$

The 11.3 W instantaneous figure only matters at low eRPM. At normal speed the 120° windows are short compared with the junction time constant, so the averaged value applies.

**Copper and trace loss** (additional). For battery path and phase pad resistance R_cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ per channel (effective):

$$P_{cu} = 80^2 \times 0.15\,\mathrm{m} = 0.96\ \mathrm{W\ per\ channel}$$

### 2.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz (hard‑switched high‑side)

Gate‑drive timing, using a gate driver that sources 1.0 A and sinks 1.5 A effective through R_g:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{10+8\ \mathrm{nC}}{1.0\ \mathrm{A}} \approx 18\ \mathrm{ns}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{18\ \mathrm{nC}}{1.5\ \mathrm{A}} \approx 12\ \mathrm{ns}$$

Real layouts add parasitic L, so budget **t_r + t_f = 40 ns**.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 45 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.054 W |
| Dead‑time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 kHz | **1.23 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·C_oss,eq·V²·f_sw (C_oss,eq ≈ 1.5 nF) | 0.023 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw × 2 FETs = 55 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz × 2 | 0.053 W |

**Total switching‑related loss per channel: ≈ 3.3 W.**

**Design levers:**

- Dead time dominates after overlap. Cutting t_dt from 200 ns to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per channel. The AM32 "dead time" parameter must still clear driver propagation mismatch.
- Dropping to 24 kHz halves this 3.3 W but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 2.4 Total burst loss

| Term (per channel, T_j = 125 °C) | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 22.5 |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + C_oss | 3.3 |
| Copper / pads | 1.0 |
| Shunt (if 0.5 mΩ per channel, see 3.5) | 3.2 |
| Gate driver + ESC MCU + LDO | 0.3 |
| **Per channel** | **≈ 30 W** |
| **4 channels** | **≈ 120 W** |

Efficiency check: P_out ≈ 4 × 80 A × 22 V × 0.95 ≈ 6.
