## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility

Before the derivations, here are the architecture decisions everything else depends on.

| Parameter | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Topology | STM32H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each motor has its own ESC MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421) running AM32. The H743 does **not** commutate motors. |
| Battery | 6S LiPo: 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor channel for ≤ 2 s; 45 A continuous target |
| PWM | 48 kHz, center-aligned, synchronous (complementary) rectification |
| MOSFET (representative) | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN. R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, Q_g = 50 nC, Q_gd = 12 nC, Q_rr = 50 nC, Q_oss = 60 nC, R_g,int = 1 Ω, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate driver | FD6288Q (3-phase bootstrap driver), V_CC = 10 V |
| Form factor | 30.5×30.5 mm (see warning below) |

> **Feasibility warning.** The 80 A × 4 burst requirement is physically incompatible with a 20×20 mm "nano" board. Section 1.5 shows roughly 20 W per channel at burst, about 80 W total, dissipated into a few grams of copper. On 20×20 mm, the burst limit falls below about 1 s, and there is not enough room for 24 single FETs plus copper weight. I recommend 30.5×30.5 mm with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper (8-layer), or derating the burst to 55 A on 20×20 mm. All numbers below assume 30.5×30.5 mm.

## 1. Thermal Model: Conduction Losses at 80 A Burst

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous drive

During each commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side switch conducts for duty D. During (1−D), the low-side switch of the same leg conducts synchronously.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side switch is on for the entire sector.

With synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly two channels:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\cdot\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

Conduction loss is therefore **independent of duty**, which is the key simplification. The ripple contribution is negligible here. With motor inductance around 15 µH, the peak-to-peak ripple is:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} \approx 8.75\text{ A}
$$

So:

$$
I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12} = \sqrt{6400 + 6.4} \approx 80.04\text{ A}
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond at 80 A (2 FETs) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 80 °C | 1.25 mΩ | 16.0 W |
| 110 °C | 1.38 mΩ | 17.7 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |

### 1.3 Interconnect losses (often forgotten)

| Element | R | P at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad + trace (2 oz, 3 mm wide, about 8 mm long, 2 layers) | ~0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Shared bus share (per channel) | ~0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |
| Shunt (if used, 0.5 mΩ) | 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |

I recommend dropping per-channel shunts. Use a single bus shunt, or a hall-effect sensor such as the ACS772, to avoid the 3.2 W per channel.

### 1.4 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. There is one turn-on and one turn-off per period.

**Gate current (Miller plateau):**

$$
I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext} + R_{g,int}} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{3 + 1} = 1.63\text{ A}
$$

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} + Q_{gs2}}{I_g} = \frac{12\text{n} + 6\text{n}}{1.63} \approx 11\text{ ns}
$$

Allowing for driver source/sink asymmetry and layout inductance, use t_r = t_f ≈ 18 ns.

**(a) V–I overlap loss:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 36\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 1.74\text{ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (body diode conducts during dead time):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.073\text{ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 100 ns, two edges, V_F = 0.8 V):

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\text{ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in R_g and the driver, not in the FET die):

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.048\text{ W per leg}
$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 2.48 W per channel at 80 A.**

Because the PWM leg rotates through phases A, B and C every electrical revolution, this loss is spread across all six FETs over time.

### 1.5 Total loss budget per channel

| Loss | 25 °C | T_j = 110 °C |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 12.8 W | 17.7 W |
| Switching (all components) | 2.5 W | 2.6 W |
| Copper interconnect | 2.6 W | 3.4 W |
| **Total per channel** | **17.9 W** | **23.7 W** |
| **Board total (4 channels)** | **71.6 W** | **94.8 W** |
| At 45 A continuous, T_j = 90 °C | | ~7.2 W per channel, 29 W total |

Over one electrical revolution, the loss in the hottest FET is:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}+P_{sw}}{3} \approx 6.8\text{ W}
$$

The spatial peak is higher. During a slow-RPM, high-torque sector, one FET carries the full 80 A continuously.
