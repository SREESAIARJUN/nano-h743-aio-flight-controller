**Feasibility flags. Read these before committing to the spec.**

1. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is not thermally credible**, even as a burst. The numbers below assume a 30.5×30.5 ESC footprint. On a 20×20 board, the copper thermal mass drops by about 57%, so the allowable burst duration shrinks in proportion.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the ESC has its own MCU(s) running AM32 or BLHeli_32 (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCU. The 48 kHz PWM and commutation are ESC-firmware parameters, not ArduPilot parameters.
3. **"80 A burst" must be defined with a duration and duty cycle.** Without that, it is not verifiable. Section 2.6 gives you the equation to set it.

## 2. Thermal Loss Model (per ESC channel, then ×4)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. During any sector:

- One phase leg is PWM-switched. Current alternates between its high-side FET (for duty D) and its low-side FET (for 1−D) through synchronous rectification.
- One low-side FET of another phase conducts continuously.

So **two FETs carry the phase current at all times**, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive at burst (flat-top current), I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 100) = **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond}(125^\circ C) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 2.3 Copper and shunt conduction

Assume 0.15 mΩ of phase-path copper, 0.15 mΩ of bus-path copper, and a 0.25 mΩ shunt (if per-phase or per-ESC shunting carries full current):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}) = 6400 \times 0.55\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.5\ W}$$

### 2.4 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each loss term is listed below.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead times per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
