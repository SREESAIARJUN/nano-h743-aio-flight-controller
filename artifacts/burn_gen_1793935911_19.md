# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #39): Loss, Thermal, DShot/DMA Model, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility

**Topology.** The STM32H743 cannot drive four 3-phase bridges with BEMF commutation while also running ArduPilot. This design therefore uses the standard split architecture:

```
[STM32H743 FC, ArduPilot] --4x bidir DShot600--> [4x AT32F421 ESC MCU, AM32] --> [4x gate driver] --> [4x 3-phase bridge, 24 FETs]
```

The rest of this document models that architecture.

**Feasibility warning.** The calculations show that "80 A burst per motor" on a nano board (20×20 mm, or 25.5×25.5 mm) is only a short burst rating:

- **Burst limit:** about 1.5–3 s with all four motors at 80 A, starting from a warm board.
- **Continuous limit:** realistically about 15–25 A per motor, set mainly by board heat rejection rather than by the FETs.

Treat these limits as design constraints, not marketing figures.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° sector, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg (phase A):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D and the low-side FET conducts synchronously for (1 − D). Both carry I_ph.
- **Return leg (phase B):** the low-side FET is held on for the whole sector and carries I_ph.
- **Floating phase (C):** used for BEMF zero-cross sensing; no conduction.

Conduction loss per motor:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This result is independent of duty, because synchronous rectification means some FET in the PWM leg always conducts.

Add the copper path (pads, vias, phase/bus planes), estimated at R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per conduction path on 2 oz inner layers over a nano footprint:

$$
P_{cond,total} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,[R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}]
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | P_FET (2·I²R) | P_Cu | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 3.84 W | 16.6 W |
| 100 °C | 1.375 mΩ | 17.6 W | 3.84 W | 21.4 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.84 W | 23.0 W |

Average per FET: each FET is active for roughly one-third of the electrical cycle, so the long-term average is about P_FET/6 per device. At 125 °C that is 19.2/6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**. The peak instantaneous dissipation during its active interval is about I²R = 9.6 W.

### 1.3 Battery current vs phase current

$$
I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{ph} \quad (\text{lossless approximation})
$$

At D = 0.6, I_bat ≈ 48 A per motor. Specify "80 A" as phase current, otherwise the bus-side numbers below will be over-stated.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In the 6-step scheme, only the PWM leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per motor. The total is:

$$
P_{sw} = P_{VI} + P_{rr} + P_{oss} + P_{dt} + P_{gate}
$$

### 2.1 V–I overlap (dominant term)

Transition time is set by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns}
$$

Allowing for the Q_gs2 contribution and driver droop, use t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$
