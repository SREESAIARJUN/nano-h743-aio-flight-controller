## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology current path

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM):

- **During PWM-on:** current flows through HS(phase A) and LS(phase B).
- **During PWM-off:** current freewheels through LS(phase A) and LS(phase B).

In both states exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current. Therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.55) = **1.55 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Copper path resistance

Take the phase pad plus bus segment as R_Cu ≈ 0.4 mΩ per channel. For 2 oz copper, R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□, so this corresponds to about 1.6 squares effective. Verify the real value with a PDN/IR-drop solver.

### 1.4 Numbers per channel at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Result |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFETs @ 125 °C | 2 × 80² × 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W |
| Copper | 80² × 0.4 mΩ | 2.6 W |
| **Total conduction** | | **22.4 W** |

### 1.5 Per-device average

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about 19.8 W ÷ 6 ≈ **3.3 W**. Peak instantaneous dissipation while conducting is I²R = **9.9 W**.

At a typical eRPM of 30 k–200 k, the electrical period is short compared with the die thermal time constant (~ms). The die therefore sees essentially the average loss. At stall or startup this no longer holds: one FET can sit at 9.9 W for many ms. This is why stall protection is mandatory.

### 1.6 Thermal runaway check

The loss and temperature are mutually dependent:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{th(j\text{-}pcb)}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

Solving gives:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + R_{th}\,I^2 R_{25}(1-25\alpha)/3 \cdot k}{1 - R_{th}\,I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, k/3}$$

where k = 1 (one FET's 1/3 duty share of I²R). The solution is stable only if the denominator is positive. With R_th(j-pcb) = 2 K/W, the loop gain is 2 × 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.0055 / 3 = **0.023**, so the system is stable with a large margin
