## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with **complementary (synchronous) PWM**, two phases carry current at any instant:

- **PWM on-time (D):** HS of phase A and LS of phase B conduct.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** LS of phase A (synchronous freewheel) and LS of phase B conduct.

So outside the dead time, **exactly two MOSFET channels carry the full phase current at all times, regardless of duty.** This gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\left(1 - \frac{2t_{dt}}{T_{sw}}\right)$$

The dead-time window is handled separately as diode conduction (§2.3).

### 1.2 Phase RMS current including PWM ripple

$$I_{ph,rms} = \sqrt{I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Take a typical 5-inch-class motor: L_ph ≈ 15 µH phase-to-neutral (30 µH line-to-line), D = 0.5, and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 12 V at the burst operating point.

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{12 \times 0.5}{30\times10^{-6} \times 48\,000} = 4.17\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{ph,rms} = \sqrt{80^2 + 4.17^2/12} = 80.009\ \text{A}$$

The ripple contribution is negligible at 48 kHz. **Use I_rms ≈ 80 A.** This is one benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz.

The commutation-period current shape (trapezoidal, with a 120° conduction block per phase) is already captured because we sum over the two conducting devices. Per device, the RMS is I·√(1/3) in the HS on its 120° block, apportioned by D.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on), solved self-consistently

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds (mΩ) | P_cond per motor @ 80 A (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 0.950 | 12.16 × 0.992 = **12.06** |
| 85 | 1.178 | **14.96** |
| 100 | 1.235 | **15.68** |
| 125 | 1.330 | **16.89** |

Dead-time correction: 1 − 2(200 ns)(48 kHz) = 0.9808.
