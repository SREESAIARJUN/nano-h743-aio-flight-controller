# STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 80A ESC: Design Model (Job #20)

## 0. Design Review Flags

These constraints shape every calculation below.

1. **80A per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally achievable as a continuous rating.** The model in §2–3 shows it is a *burst* rating of about 2–3 s, even on a 30.5×30.5 mm board. I model a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC layer as the realistic minimum. A true 20×20 board would roughly halve the burst time.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In ArduPilot the H743 sends bidirectional DShot600. Each ESC channel is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or the 4-channel equivalent), and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture is therefore: H743 → DShot → 4× ESC MCU → gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs.
3. **Treat netlists as reference designs, not release data.** Pin alternate functions, part values and footprints must be verified against current datasheets and the specific H743 package (LQFP100 vs. UFBGA) before layout.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through **exactly two FETs**:

- one FET of the PWM leg (high-side for duty D, low-side for 1−D), and
- the low-side FET of the return leg.

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Total conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Averaged over a 360° electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 120°:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$

**At T_j = 100°C:** R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005·75) = 1.375 mΩ

- P_cond = 2 · 6400 · 1.375×10⁻³ = **17.6 W per channel**
- ≈ 5.9 W average per FET

**Thermal runaway check.** Conduction loss rises with T_j, and T_j rises with loss. The loop converges only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,\theta_{ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,\theta_{ja,FET} < 1$$

With 6400 · 0.001 · 0.005 · θ < 1, θ_ja must be below 31 K/W per FET. This is satisfied with proper copper, but it shows why an unheatsinked burst becomes unstable.

### 2.2 Switching Losses

Only the PWM leg's FET pair switches. All terms below scale linearly with f_sw.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value @ 48 kHz |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30n · 48k = **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_d · f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 400n · 48k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver and R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.8 W per channel** |

**Design levers:**

- Dead-time loss is nearly equal to overlap loss. Cutting t_d from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Verify against the gate driver's shoot-through margin first.
- Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.4 Channel and Board Totals at 80A Burst

| | Per channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.6 W | 70.4 W |
| Switching | 2.8 W | 11.2 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **23.9 W** | **≈ 96 W** |

At 25.2 V and full throttle, input power is roughly 4 × 2 kW. The ESC efficiency is therefore about 98.8%, but 96 W in a 30 mm board is the core problem.
