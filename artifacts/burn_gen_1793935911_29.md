# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot Timing and Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

The math below leads to three conclusions that should be settled before layout:

1. **On a 20×20 mm board, "80 A burst" is only a seconds-scale, single-motor rating.** All four motors at 80 A dissipate about 130 W. The board's thermal mass gives roughly **1.8 s** before reaching the junction/solder budget. A realistic continuous rating is **12–15 A per motor** at a 60 K rise. Even on 30.5×30.5 mm, sustained 80 A requires 5×6 mm FETs and forced airflow.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no in-flight-controller 4-motor FOC/6-step driver. The architecture is therefore an H743 flight controller sending bidirectional DShot600 to four per-motor ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32). The DMA analysis applies to the H743. Switching and thermal losses apply to the ESC power stage.
3. **Bus decoupling dominates at 80 A.** On-board MLCCs alone cannot hold ripple (see §2.4). An external low-ESR bulk capacitor (≥1000 µF, 35 V) at the battery pads is mandatory, not optional.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side FET in series. With complementary PWM (as AM32/BLHeli_32 use), freewheeling current flows through the opposite FET's channel rather than its body diode. The series path therefore always presents about 2R regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on),hot}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.0045 = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET RMS current

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period, so:

$$I_{rms,FET} = \frac{I}{\sqrt{3}} = 46.2\,\text{A}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 R = 2133 \times 0.00225 = \mathbf{4.8\ W}$$

Check: 6 FETs × 4.8 W
