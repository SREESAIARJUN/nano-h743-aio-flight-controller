# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Design Model (Job #36)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several points in the brief need correcting before any math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every production AIO and 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It generates bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The usual choices are 4× AT32F421 or 4× STM32G071. I model it that way.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically credible as continuous, or even multi-second, all-motor current.** The model below shows why. I treat 80 A as a **per-motor phase-current burst of ≤1–2 s on a single channel**, and I flag the four-motor case.
3. **Device parameters are placeholders.** They represent a typical 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm-class N-MOSFET. Replace every value marked ⚠ with your actual datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

AM32/BLHeli_32 uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM (synchronous rectification). During each 60° sector, current flows through one high-side (HS) device and one low-side (LS) device in different legs.

The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS and LS switch. Because both conduct the same phase current, the duty cycle drops out of the loss sum:

$$P_{cond,leg} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,D + I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D) = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$$

The static leg (LS held on) contributes another $I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}$. Therefore, per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + 2\,I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$

Temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/\mathrm{K}$$

**At 80 A, hot (T_j ≈ 125 °C):**

- Silicon: 2 × 6400 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**
- Copper: 2 × 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **6.4 W**
- **Total conduction loss per motor ≈ 25.6 W**

Battery current is related to phase current by the duty cycle: $I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph}$. A "80 A" ESC rating is almost always phase current, not battery current.

Ripple correction: with phase inductance L_ph, add $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12$, where $\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L f_{sw}}$. For L = 20 µH this is under 1 %, so it is neglected.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Its HS device hard-switches; its LS device soft-switches through the body diode.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS diode) | Q_rr·V·f | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.060 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| C_oss charge/discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f | **0.023 W** |
| Gate drive (2 devices, mostly in the driver/R_g) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f | **0.048 W** |
| **Total switching-related loss** | | **≈ 1.71 W per motor** |

Sensitivities:

- **Frequency:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is about 0.86 W; at 96 kHz about 3.4 W.
- **Dead time is the second-largest term.** Trimming it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, but it must stay above the driver propagation-delay mismatch.
- **Parasitics:** layout inductance (L_loop ≈ 2 nH) adds overshoot $\Delta V = L\,di/dt = 2\,\mathrm{nH}\times 80\,\mathrm{A}/10\,\mathrm{ns} = 16\,\mathrm{V}$. A 25.2 V bus plus 16 V spike reaches 41 V, which **exceeds a 40 V device rating**. You need either a ≥60 V device or a slower gate (R_g ↑, which raises overlap loss). This trade-off is real and must be resolved on the bench.

### 4.3 Two-node transient (Foster) model

The model has two thermal nodes:

- **Junction to board:** R_jb ≈ 1.5 K/W per device ⚠, with C_j ≈ 0.02 J/K (die + leadframe), giving τ₁ ≈ 30 ms.
- **Board to ambient:** R_ba ≈ 8 K/W with C_board ≈ 2 J/K, giving τ₂ ≈ 16 s.
