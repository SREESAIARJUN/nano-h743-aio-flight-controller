# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Architecture & Analysis Package

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Four points need settling before the math.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design:
   - The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 frames.
   - Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead time and commutation.
   - The model is therefore split: Section 1–3 cover the ESC power stage, Section 4 covers the FC timer/DMA.
2. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A burst is the critical risk.** A 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate 4 × 80 A for any useful duration (proven in §3). I recommend 30.5×30.5 mm with 80 A as a ≤5 s single-channel burst rating. All numbers below are parameterized so you can rerun them.
3. **No MOSFET part was specified.** I use a representative 40 V, 5×6 mm (PDFN) N-FET class device. Substitute your datasheet values.
4. **Everything here is first-order engineering analysis.** Validate with thermocouple/IR testing on a dyno before releasing the rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

AM32/BLHeli use six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° electrical sector:

- One phase leg is PWM-switched: high-side (HS) on for duty D, low-side (LS) on for 1−D.
- One leg has its LS held on.
- One leg floats.

At every instant exactly **two FETs** carry I_ph. Averaged over an electrical cycle, per leg:

- HS conduction fraction: δ_HS = D/3
- LS conduction fraction: δ_LS = (1−D)/3 + 1/3

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

### 1.3 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,path}$$

Results at I = 80 A (I² = 6400):

| T_j | R_ds | FET conduction | Copper | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 8.96 W | 5.12 W | 14.1 W |
| 100 °C | 0.94 mΩ | 12.0 W | 5.12 W | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.02 mΩ | 13.0 W | 5.12 W | 18.1 W |

Copper loss is about 30% of the total. Use 2 oz or heavier inner power planes, and use via arrays under the FET drains.

### 1.4 Per-FET loss at full throttle (D → 1)

$$P_{FET,HS} = P_{FET,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.02\text{ mΩ}) = 2.18\text{ W}$$

At D = 0.5, the LS carries 0.5 vs. 0.167 for the HS, so it is the hot device:

$$P_{LS} = 0.5 \cdot 6.53 = 3.27\text{ W}$$

**Design rule:** at partial throttle and high current (e.g., punch-outs from hover), the low-side FETs are the thermal limit.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg switches at a time.

### 2.1 Gate drive currents

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{7}{4} = 1.75\text{ A}, \qquad I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3}{4} = 0.75\text{ A}$$

### 2.2 Switching transition times

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 8.6\text{ ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 20\text{ ns}$$

### 2.3 Loss terms (per ESC at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz)

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_on + t_off)·f_sw | 1.38 W |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| Body-diode dead time | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 0.61 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.07 W |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Sensitivities:**
- Overlap and dead-time loss scale linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz you save about 1 W per ESC but gain motor current ripple.
- Turn-off dominates. A Schottky-assisted turn-off path (diode across R_g,ext) cuts t_off to about 8 ns and saves about 0.6 W.

### 2.4 ESC totals at 80 A, T_j ≈ 110 °C

$$P_{ESC} \approx 17.4 + 2.2 \approx 19.6\text{ W}, \qquad P_{4\text{-}in\text{-}1} \approx 78\text{ W (all four at 80 A)}$$
