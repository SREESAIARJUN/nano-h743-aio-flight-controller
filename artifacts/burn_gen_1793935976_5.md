# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: Reference System Architecture (Job #60)

> **Scope and honesty statement.** This is an engineering reference derivation, not a release-ready design. All component parameters are representative values. Every number marked **[DS]** must be replaced with the datasheet value of the part you actually procure before layout freeze. The thermal results also challenge the "80A × 4" requirement, so read §3.5 before committing to the form factor.

---

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. The partition is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100), 400–480 MHz | ArduPilot, generates bidirectional DShot600 on 4 timer channels |
| ESC MCU ×4 | STM32G071 / AT32F421 (AM32 firmware) | 6-step or sinusoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry reply |
| Gate driver ×4 | 3-phase half-bridge driver, e.g. FD6288Q class [DS] | Bootstrap high side, ~1.2 A / 0.8 A source/sink |
| Power stage ×4 | 6 × N-MOSFET, 40 V, ~0.7 mΩ class (PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 dual-cool) [DS] | 6S (25.2 V max) bus |

**Why not drive the FETs from the H743?** 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time at 48 kHz plus 4 sensorless BEMF observers is possible on TIM1/TIM8/HRTIM. However, it couples flight-critical attitude loops to commutation faults. It also forfeits the AM32/BLHeli ecosystem and makes ArduPilot certification of the ESC path your problem. A dedicated MCU per motor is the industry-correct choice.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

With complementary (synchronous) PWM in 6-step commutation, phase current at every instant flows through exactly **two** conducting channels:
- the PWM-switched leg (high side during on-time, low side during off-time), and
- the static low side of the return phase.

Because synchronous rectification puts the low-side FET in place of the diode during the off-time, the conducting resistance is always 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle D (apart from dead time). This holds with the motor current I_ph as a ripple-free DC value. At 48 kHz with typical L_ph ≈ 10–20 µH, ripple is ≪ 80 A, so RMS ≈ DC.

### 2.2 Conduction loss with temperature feedback

$$
R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)\right]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_J)
$$

Per-FET split (useful for hotspot analysis):

| FET | Conduction share |
|---|---|
| PWM high side | D · I²R |
| PWM low side | (1−D) · I²R |
| Static low side (return) | 1 · I²R ← **hottest device** |

**Numerical evaluation at 80 A:**

| T_J | R_DS | P_cond (2 FETs) | Static-LS FET alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 8.96 W | 4.48 W |
| 100 °C | 1.015 mΩ | 12.99 W | 6.50 W |
| 125 °C | 1.12 mΩ | **14.34 W** | **7.17 W** |

**Thermal-runaway stability criterion.** The system is stable if the incremental loss per kelvin is smaller than the incremental heat removal per kelvin:

$$
\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_J} \cdot R_{\theta JA,eff} < 1
\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;
2 I^2 R_{25}\, \alpha\, R_{\theta JA,eff} < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2·6400·0.7e-3·0.006 = 0.0538 W/K. The criterion therefore requires R_θJA,eff < 18.6 K/W. That is easily met, so there is **no runaway**. The failure mode is plain over-temperature (see §3), not instability.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on the high-side turn-on, which commutates the current away from the low-side body diode.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, dissipated mostly in the high side):**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$
P_{dt} = 2\, V_F\, I_{ph}\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot 0.8\cdot 80\cdot 100\text{n}\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated in the driver and R_G, not the FET; two switching FETs):

$$
P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot 60\text{n}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ W
$$

**Switching-frequency sensitivity.**

$$
\frac{dP_{sw}}{df} \approx \left[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + Q_{rr}V + Q_{oss}V + 2V_F I t_{dt}\right] = 56.5\ \mu J
$$
