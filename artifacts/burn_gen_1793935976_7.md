# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #62): Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Key Findings

Three constraints shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no ESC commutation firmware. The board is therefore an **H743 flight controller plus four ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8U7 running AM32; or EFM8BB21 running Bluejay). The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to them. The 48 kHz PWM is an **ESC-MCU parameter**, not an H743 timer.
2. **"Nano" and "80 A" conflict.** On a 20×20 mm board, 24 power FETs plus gate drivers cannot dissipate 80 A loads. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board** and treats 80 A as a **transient burst rating**. Section 2 quantifies how long that burst can last.
3. **All component values are representative.** Rds(on), Qg, Qrr and Rθ must be replaced with your selected MOSFET's datasheet values. The equations are general; the numbers are worked examples.

**Key results:**

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Loss per channel @ 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz | ≈ 28.6 W (≈ 92 % conduction) |
| Total @ 4 × 80 A | ≈ 114 W |
| Burst duration, single motor at 80 A | ≈ 10 s |
| Burst duration, all four motors at 80 A | ≈ 2.4 s |
| Continuous rating (strong prop-wash) | ≈ 30–35 A per channel |
| DShot timer allocation | TIM1 CH1–CH4 for M1–M4, one timer, BIDIR on all |

| t_dt | 100 ns | Dead time per edge |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | Reverse-recovery charge |
| Q_oss | 50 nC | Output charge at V_bus |
| Q_g | 50 nC | Total gate charge at 10 V |
| R_cu | 0.4 mΩ | PCB copper path resistance (bus → FET → phase pad → FET → GND) |

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, the current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

- During PWM on-time: the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During off-time: the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Duty cycle therefore cancels out:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]
$$

With α ≈ 0.0055 /K, the 125 °C multiplier is ≈ 1.55, giving R_ds ≈ 1.86 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.86\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.8\ W/channel}
$$

PCB copper loss adds:

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.56\ W}
$$

Current-sense shunt loss, if used (a shared 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying 4 × 80 A), is significant: 320² × 0.25 mΩ ≈ 25.6 W. **Recommendation:** use per-channel 0.5 mΩ shunts sized for telemetry only, or rely on ESC firmware current estimation.

**Electro-thermal coupling.** Since R_ds rises with T_j, solve iteratively:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\,P\!\left(T_j^{(n)}\right)
$$

This converges for the numbers here. If the loop gain dP/dT · Z_th ≥ 1, the design is in thermal runaway.
