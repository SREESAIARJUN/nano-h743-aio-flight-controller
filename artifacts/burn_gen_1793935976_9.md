# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #66)

**Scope:** This document covers power-stage losses (conduction and switching at 48 kHz), the transient thermal model for 80 A bursts, ArduPilot bi-directional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and a reference netlist.

**Status:** Engineering model and reference design. All component parameters are typical-datasheet assumptions and are stated explicitly so the model can be re-run with the final BOM. The netlist is architecture-level. It is not a fab-ready schematic and requires schematic capture, DRC/ERC, and thermal validation (IR camera plus thermocouple) before release.

---

## 0. Architectural corrections and feasibility flags

Three points must be settled before any math, because they change the results.

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only produces the DShot600 command stream and decodes the bi-directional eRPM telemetry. The two domains are modeled separately below.

2. **"80 A burst" needs a definition.**
   - **Case A: 80 A per motor.** Battery current is 320 A. That exceeds any XT60/XT30 connector, 12–14 AWG battery leads, and nano-class shunt and bulk-capacitor ratings by a factor of 3–5×.
   - **Case B: 80 A total.** This is 20 A per motor, which is realistic for a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm board.
   - Both cases are computed. The FET-level math is done at 80 A per channel as the worst-case design point, and the board-level consequences are shown for both.

3. **Footprint.** A 20×20 mm mounting pattern (about 30×30 mm outline) cannot hold 24 FETs plus an H743 on a single board at 80 A/channel. The reference architecture is therefore a **2-board stack**:
   - FC board: H743, IMU, OSD, flash.
   - ESC board: 4× ESC MCU, 4× tri-half-bridge drivers, 24 FETs.

## 2. Conduction loss model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**.

Because conduction loss raises T_j, which raises R_ds, the loss and temperature are solved iteratively (fixed-point iteration, §4.3).

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, current I flows through one phase leg to the bus and returns through a second leg:

- **Non-PWM leg:** the low-side FET is fully on. Loss is I²R.
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the complementary low-side FET conducts for 1 − D.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R\cdot D + I^2R\,(1-D) + I^2R = 2I^2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(1.38\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{17.7\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for about 120° of every 360° electrical cycle. Take a 2207 motor (14 poles = 7 pole pairs) at 30,000 rpm:

- Electrical frequency: f_e = 7 × 500 = 3.5 kHz, so the period is 286 µs.
- Die thermal time constant: about 1–5 ms, which is much longer than 286 µs.
- The die therefore sees the average power.

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{17.7}{6} = \mathbf{2.95\ W}$$
