# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Design Model and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

No component selections were provided for Job #42, so this model uses representative parameters. Every equation is parametric. Substitute your actual MOSFET, gate driver, and stackup values before sign-off.

**Architecture correction.** ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A "STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC" must therefore be split into two domains:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, sends bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC (×4, or ×1 quad-capable) | e.g. STM32G071 / AT32F421 (×4) | AM32 or BLHeli_32 | Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |

The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over 4 DShot lines on the same PCB. The model below covers both domains.

**Baseline parameters (assumed):**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per channel | I_pk | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, PDFN 5×6 or 3.3×3.3, R_ds(on) = 1.0 mΩ @ 25 °C, V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 0.0055 /K (≈ 1.5× at 115 °C) |
| Rise / fall time | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 50 nC |
| Output capacitance | C_oss(eff) | 1.5 nF |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns per edge |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V |
| Commutation | — | Trapezoidal 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 is far beyond typical ratings for that size (≈35–55 A burst). The numbers below are physically consistent, but they show this burst is only survivable for a few seconds. See §3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step current path

At any instant the phase current I flows through one high-side leg and one low-side leg. With synchronous PWM at duty D:

- The active high-side carries I for a fraction D of the period.
- Its complementary low-side carries I for (1 − D) (freewheel).
- The opposite low-side carries I for 100 % of the period.

The conducting path therefore always contains **two** R_ds(on) in series, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Distribution over the six FETs

Each FET is active for 120° electrical (1/3 of the cycle), so the average loss per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{ds}}{3} \cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2}\cdot 3/3 = \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The short-window worst case (low duty, low-side carrying 100 %) is I²R = 9.6 W instantaneous. Thermal mass averages this out above a few hundred Hz electrical.

### 1.4 Electrothermal stability

$$P = 2I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_a + P\,R_{\theta ja}$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j = \frac{T_a + 2I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{\theta}}{1 - 2I^2R_{25}\alpha R_{\theta}}$$

This is stable only if **2I²R₂₅·α·R_θ < 1**. With I = 80 A and R₂₅ = 1 mΩ, the loop gain is 0.0704·R_θ. Thermal runaway therefore occurs if the effective channel R_θ(ja) exceeds about **14 K/W**. That limit is easily exceeded on a nano board in still air, which reinforces that 80 A is a burst-only rating.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching occurs only on the PWM'd leg: one hard-switched pair per channel at a time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| C_oss charging | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = 0.5·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, only PWM pair switching at f_sw) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw = 2·60 nC·10·48 k | 0.06 W (dissipated mostly in gate resistors and driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 1.2 W per channel at 80 A. In exchange it reduces current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a small-motor L_ph of about 20 µH at D = 0.5: ΔI ≈ 6.6 A at 48 kHz, versus 13 A at 24 kHz. The ripple adds an RMS penalty of ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 2.1 Total burst loss

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| PCB copper (est. 0.3 mΩ path, 2× I²R) | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.4 W** | **≈ 102 W** |

PCB copper resistance is significant at this current. A 2 oz outer / 2 oz inner 6-layer stackup with paralleled phase pours is needed to approach 0.3 mΩ.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network per FET (Foster-reduced)

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc 0.8 K/W]──●──[Rθcs 0.3 K/W (solder+via)]──●──[Rθ_board-spread]──●──[Rθ_conv]── T_amb
                       Cj≈0.02 J/K                    C_board                 
```

### 3.2 Steady state, showing why continuous 80 A is impossible

Assume an effective 25×25 mm board with a heatsink-less, two-sided convection area of A ≈ 1.25×10⁻³ m². Under prop wash, h ≈ 80 W/m²K.

$$R_{\theta,conv} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80 \times 1.25\times10^{-3}} = 10\ \text{K/W (whole board)}$$

$$\Delta T_{ss} = 102\ \text{W} \times 10 = 1020\ \text{K}$$

This is not achievable. Continuous ratings must be derived as below.

### 3.3 Transient (adiabatic) burst capability

Assume the effective thermal mass is FR4 plus copper plus FETs plus caps, about 6 g at roughly 0.7 J/g·K, giving C ≈ 4.2 J/K.

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P - (T-T_a)/R_\theta}{C} \approx \frac{102}{4.2} \approx 24\ \text{K/s}$$

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{T_{max} - T_{start}}{dT/dt} = \frac{110 - 45}{24} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ s}$$

Junction-to-board rise per FET, with P_FET ≈ 4.2 W (3.2 W conduction + 0.4 W switching + copper share) and R_θjc + R_θcs = 1.1 K/W:

$$T_j \approx T_{board} + 4.6\ \text{K}$$

The board limit governs, not the junction.

### 3.4 Continuous rating (board limited, T_board ≤ 100 °C, T_a = 40 °C)

$$P_{max} = \frac{60\,\text{K}}{10\,\text{K/W}} = 6\,\text{W total} \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{ds}\cdot4 + \ldots \le 6\ \text{W}$$

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{6 - 4\times0.6}{4 \times 2 \times 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.2}} \approx \mathbf{17\text{–}20\ A\ per\ channel}$$

### 3.5 Recommended firmware protection

- Set the ESC temperature limit (AM32 `TEMPERATURE_LIMIT`) to 100 °C, and place the NTC adjacent to the hottest FET pair.
- Use ArduPilot `MOT_BAT_CURR_MAX` with time-constant limiting (`BATT_*`) to model I²t: cap 80 A at 2 s, then fold back.
- An I²t model in the ESC is preferred: trip when ∫(I² − I_cont²)dt > (80² − 20²) × 2 s = 12,000 A²s.
