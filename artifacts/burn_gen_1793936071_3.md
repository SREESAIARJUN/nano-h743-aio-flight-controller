## 0. Architecture Review and Design Risks

These issues determine whether the rest of the design is viable, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Impact | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **The H743 cannot commutate the motors directly.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCU per motor (or a dual-motor MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 is the flight controller and emits bidirectional DShot600. | Architecture | Two-board stack. The FC (H743) connects over an 8-pin harness to the ESC (4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). |
| R2 | **"80A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is thermally unsupportable beyond about 1 s** (see §3). | Spec | Rate the board at **80A for ≤1 s, 50A for 10 s, and ~25A continuous with prop wash**. Otherwise move to a 30.5×30.5 board with 5×6 mm FETs. |
| R3 | 4 × 80A = 320A bus on 6S is about 8 kW. That is beyond XT60 ratings (60A continuous) and the ripple rating of any bulk capacitor that fits on a nano board. | Spec | Burst is per-motor and non-simultaneous. Budget the bus for 4 × 40A = 160A peak. |
| R4 | DShot DMA buffers placed in DTCM will silently fail, because DTCM is not reachable by DMA1/DMA2. | Firmware | ArduPilot handles this already (`AP_HAL_ChibiOS` uses DMA-safe memory). Do not override it in custom code. |

**Design point:** 6S LiPo (25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal), 48 kHz PWM, synchronous (complementary) 6-step commutation.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step synchronous commutation, two legs are active at any instant:
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between HS (duty D) and LS (1−D).
- **Freewheel leg:** LS is held fully on.

At any instant, exactly **two** FET channels carry the phase current *I*, independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T R_{25}$$

At 80A, Tj = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

The Rds(on) tempco creates a positive feedback loop: loss raises Tj, which raises Rds(on), which raises loss. Solve it iteratively in §3.4.

**PCB copper** (add to the model). A phase path is about 15 mm on 2 oz (70 µm), 3 mm wide, across 3 parallel layers:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times 0.015}{0.003 \times 70\times10^{-6} \times 3} \approx 0.41\ \text{m}\Omega$$

The current path spans two phases plus the bus, about 1.0 mΩ total. That gives P_Cu ≈ 80² × 1.0 mΩ ≈ **6.4 W**. This heat goes directly into the board.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. LS turn-on is soft (ZVS after dead time); HS carries the hard transitions.

**Transition times** (gate-charge method, plateau ≈ 4.5 V, V_drive = 10 V, R_g,total ≈ 3 Ω):

$$I_{g,on} \approx \frac{10 - 4.5}{3} \approx 1.8\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} \approx 6.5\ \text{ns}$$

Use t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 10 ns. These conservative values account for driver current limits and layout inductance.

**(a) V-I overlap loss:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.06\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode, recovered at HS turn-on):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$
Add the Q_rr-induced current overlap at roughly the same magnitude, giving about **0.07 W**.

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{ nC}\times 25.2\times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period):
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs):
$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2_{\text{(PWM leg)}} = 50\text{ nC}\times 10 \times 48\text{ k}\times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This is small, but the FD6288Q supply must deliver it. The bootstrap capacitor sizing in §5 covers this.

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Total Burst Budget

| Loss | Per motor @ 80A | Note |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 20.5 W | Dominant term (≈ 75%) |
| PCB copper | 6.4 W | Into board, not FET |
| V-I switching | 1.06 W | Scales with f_sw and V_bus |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | Reduce t_dt to 50 ns: −0.3 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.12 W | — |
| Gate | 0.05 W | — |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.7 W** | **Efficiency ≈ 98.6%** at 2 kW per motor |

The worst case, with all four motors bursting simultaneously, is **≈ 115 W** into a 20×20 mm board.

**Frequency sensitivity:** switching loss is about 1.8 W per motor at 48 kHz. Moving to 24 kHz saves about 0.9 W. That is under 4% of total loss, so 48 kHz is justified for current ripple and acoustic reasons. **Conduction loss is the lever that matters.** Parallel FETs halve it, but they do not fit on 20×20.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-State (Proves the Continuous Rating Cannot Be 80A)

Thermal network per FET:
$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}\left(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta C\text{-}PCB} + R_{\theta PCB\text{-}A}\right)$$

A 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board in prop wash (~5–8 m/s) achieves R_θ,board-ambient ≈ 6–8 K/W for the whole board. With 115 W in, ΔT is about 800 K, so steady-state 80A is physically impossible.

The continuous rating comes from the allowable board rise (ΔT = 70 K at T_a = 40 °C, Tj ≤ 125 °C with FET-to-board margin):

$$P_{max} \approx \frac{70}{7} = 10\ \text{W (board total)}$$
