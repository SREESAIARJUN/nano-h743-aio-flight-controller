# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan

**Engineering reference, revision A (pre-layout).** Every numeric result depends on the assumed component parameters in §1. Replace them with datasheet values for your selected MOSFETs before freezing the design.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

Two corrections to the scope before the math:

1. **The STM32H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.** On every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel (or the whole board) runs its own MCU, such as AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32 parts running AM32/BLHeli_32/BLHeli_S. The H743 generates DShot600 frames and decodes eRPM telemetry from the ESC MCU. This document models:
   - **Domain A:** H743 timers, DMA and bi-directional DShot (ArduPilot `hwdef`).
   - **Domain B:** ESC power stage. This covers MOSFET conduction and switching losses at f_PWM = 48 kHz, and thermal behaviour.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor is at the edge of physical feasibility.** It is achievable per-channel for short bursts (about 1–5 s) only with low-R_DS(on) parts and heavy copper. All four channels at 80 A simultaneously is thermally infeasible without a heatsink and airflow (shown in §4).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 0.75 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.125 mΩ**.

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, loss also rises. This positive feedback is the root of thermal runaway (§4.4).

### 2.2 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° electrical sector:

- One phase's high side is PWM-modulated at duty D. Its complementary low side conducts during (1 − D).
- A second phase's low side is fully on.

The current therefore always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**, either HS+LS or LS+LS during freewheeling:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2\,(R_{shunt}+R_{Cu})$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

- FETs: 2 × 6400 × 1.125×10⁻³ = **14.4 W**
- Shunt and copper: 6400 × 0.55×10⁻³ = **3.52 W**

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution (worst case: hottest FET)

Each FET is active in 120° of the 360° electrical cycle. The high-side FET of the PWM phase is the hottest:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}\,D \qquad P_{cond,LS,sync} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D)$$

Averaged, each FET sees approximately 14.4 / 6 ≈ **2.4 W**. During a full-throttle burst (D → 1), the HS FETs carry most of it.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated leg switches hard. Its loss averages across the three legs over a rotation, but the total per motor is:

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Output capacitance | V Q_oss f_sw | 25.2 × 50 nC × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V Q_rr f_sw | 25.2 × 40 nC × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2 V_SD I t_dt f_sw | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W** |

**Design observations:**

- At 48 kHz, conduction dominates by about 7:1. Lowering R_DS(on) is the highest-leverage change.
- Dead-time diode loss (0.61 W) is about 40 % of overlap loss. Reduce it by:
  - trimming dead time to around 50 ns (AM32 setting), and/or
  - choosing FETs with an integrated Schottky body diode.
- Switching loss scales linearly with frequency. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per motor but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.

### 3.1 Total loss per motor at 80 A

$$P_{motor} = 14.4 + 3.52 + 2.2 \approx \mathbf{20.1\ W}$$

Efficiency: P_in ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D. At D = 1, this is 2016 W, so the loss is about 1.0 %.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction rise above board (steady-state approximation)

For the hottest FET, taking P ≈ 2.4 W conduction plus about 1 W switching share:

$$\Delta T_{JC} = P\,R_{\theta JC} \approx 3.4 \times 1.0 = 3.4\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-case drop is small. **The board-to-ambient path is the bottleneck.**

### 4.2 Lumped board thermal capacitance (nano 20×20 mm)

| Element | Mass | c_p (J/g·K) | C (J/K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| PCB (FR4, 8-layer, 2 oz) | ~3.5 g | ~0.9 (composite) | 3.2 |
| 24 × DFN FETs | ~1.0 g | 0.7 | 0.7 |
| Solder, caps, connectors | ~1.5 g | 0.5 | 0.75 |
| **Total** | | | **C_th ≈ 4.6 J/K** |

Board-to-ambient resistance with prop wash: R_θBA ≈ 8–15 K/W (take 10 K/W). The time constant is τ = R_θBA C_th ≈ 46 s.

### 4.3 Burst temperature rise

The first-order transient is:

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{\theta BA}\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$$

**Case 1: one motor at 80 A, others at 20 A.** Loss at 20 A ≈ 1.6 W each, so P ≈ 20.1 + 3 × 1.6 ≈ 25 W.

| Burst length | ΔT_board |
|---|---|
| 2 s | 25 × 10 × (1 − e^(−2/46)) ≈ **10.6 K** |
| 5 s | ≈ **25.7 K** |
| 10 s | ≈ **48.8 K** |

**Case 2: all four motors at 80 A.** P ≈ 80 W.

| Burst length | ΔT_board |
|---|---|
| 5 s | ≈ **82 K** |
| 10 s | ≈ **156 K**, which destroys the board |

**Conclusion:** rate the board **"80 A × 5 s burst, single channel"** with a continuous rating of about 35–40 A per channel. All-channel 80 A requires a heatsink, more board area, or lower R_DS(on).

### 4.4 Thermal-runaway stability check

The loop is stable if d(P)/dT × R_θ < 1:

$$\frac{dP}{dT}R_\theta = 2I^2R_{DS,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta BA} = 2(6400)(0.75\times10^{-3})(0.005)(10) = 0.48$$

This is stable but has little margin. At R_θBA = 20 K/W (no airflow), the product is 0.96, which is near runaway. **Require ESC firmware thermal throttling with a NTC sensor at the FET cluster.**
