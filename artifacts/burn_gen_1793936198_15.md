# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Electrothermal and DShot Timing Model

## 0. Architecture Corrections

These issues affect the rest of the model, so they come first.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors directly.** In ArduPilot it runs the flight stack and emits DShot. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running sensorless 6-step firmware, typically AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32. This design uses **4× AT32F421 ESC MCUs** on the same PCB.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally implausible beyond a few seconds.** The model below quantifies this: about 93 W of bridge dissipation into roughly 10 J/K of board heat capacity. The rating should be **80 A for ≤ 2 s, per channel, single channel**, or derated.
3. **The netlist is a reference topology, not a production-validated design.** Pinouts must be checked against the specific datasheet revisions and package variants before layout.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation pattern.** In 6-step complementary PWM, at any instant one high-side FET (HS) and one low-side FET (LS) of different legs carry the phase current. During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET of the switching leg synchronously rectifies, so the current path always passes through **two FETs in series**.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.45 mΩ. With 0.05 mΩ of package and solder, use **R_eff ≈ 1.5 mΩ**.

**Bridge conduction loss per ESC channel:**

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2\,I^2R_{eff}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 120° of 360°:

$$P_{cond,FET}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{eff}=3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Copper loss.** At 2 oz, R_□ = ρ/t = 1.72×10⁻⁸ / 70×10⁻⁶ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□.

- A phase path of about 3 squares, paralleled over 4 layers, gives ≈ 0.19 mΩ.
- Phase copper: P_Cu,phase = I²·R = 6400 × 0.19 mΩ ≈ 1.2 W per channel.
- Battery bus: 320 A peak if all four channels burst together, on a ~0.1 mΩ bus. That is about **10 W** at the pads alone. This is a second reason not to rate simultaneous 4×80 A.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs transition loss.

**Hard-switching overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction loss:**

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8(80)(600\text{ns})(48\text{k})=1.84\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate drive:**

- Reverse recovery: P_rr = Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48k ≈ 0.06 W
- Output capacitance: P_oss = Q_oss · V · f ≈ 0.05 W
- Gate drive: P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f = 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48k ≈ 0.05 W. This load lands on the driver and BEC, not on the FETs.

**Total switching-related loss: ≈ 3.9 W per channel.**

Dead-time loss is nearly equal to overlap loss. Cutting t_dt from 300 ns to 150 ns, which AM32 supports when gate-drive margin allows, saves about 0.9 W per channel.

## 4. Total Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr/Q_oss | 3.9 W | 15.6 W |
| Phase copper | 1.2 W | 4.8 W |
| Bus copper (4-channel) | – | ~10 W |
| **Total** | **~24 W** | **~107 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is roughly 25.2 V × 80 A × duty ≈ 1.8 kW at full duty. Loss is about 1.3% per channel, so the electrical efficiency is fine. The problem is purely thermal density.
