## 0. Architecture Clarification and Review Flags

Before the math, three architectural points need settling. Getting them wrong invalidates everything downstream.

| # | Issue | Resolution adopted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class) with a 3-phase gate driver. | H743 = flight controller (ArduPilot). 4× ESC MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421, running AM32) = commutation at 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to them with bi-directional DShot600. |
| 2 | "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) board. | This is an **all-channels-simultaneous adiabatic burst limit**, not a continuous rating. Section 3 shows continuous 4×80 A is physically impossible on this footprint (≈118 W of dissipation). The rating is defined as **80 A per channel for t_burst ≤ 1–2 s** (computed below). |
| 3 | 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8 kW input. | Battery leads, XT60/XT90 connector, and bulk capacitance must be specified against this. Flagged for system review. |

## 2. Loss Model per Motor Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In six-step drive, every 60° electrical sector has exactly two phases energized:

- One phase leg is PWM-switched. Its high-side conducts for D·T_sw; its low-side synchronously rectifies for (1–D)·T_sw.
- One phase leg has its low-side held fully on.

So at **every instant, the motor current flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle (ignoring dead-time intervals).
