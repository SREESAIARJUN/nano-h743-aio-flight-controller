# STM32H743 Nano AIO: 4-in-1 ESC Architecture, Loss Model and BDShot DMA Plan

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

These two points shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In production 4-in-1 ESCs, each motor channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, here an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. Running 4× sensorless FOC or 6-step at 48 kHz plus EKF3 on one H743 is not supported by ArduPilot and is not proposed here.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is a marketing number unless you bound the duration.** The model below shows that one channel at 80 A dissipates about 22 W. All four channels at 80 A dissipate about 88 W into roughly 2.5 J/K of board. The result is a ≤1–2 s burst limit. I specify it that way.

All component values are **design assumptions**. Replace them with your selected MOSFET datasheet values. The equations hold regardless.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per-channel conduction loss
Over a full PWM period the current path passes through exactly two FETs (HS or LS of the switching leg, plus the static LS):

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with $R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$.

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond @ 80 A | P_cond @ 35 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.45 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 3.28 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.55 W |

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution (worst-case FET)
- The static low-side FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.
- The switching-leg FETs share by duty D.
- The worst-case static LS FET carries $I^2 R$ for 120° electrical, giving an average $P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 2 I^2R$ per FET when commutation is rotated. Peak instantaneous loss is $I^2R$ = 9.3 W at 125 °C and 80 A.
- Because the electrical period is short (≪ τ_th of the FET, ~10 ms), use the **average** for junction temperature and the **peak** for the bond-wire / SOA check.

### 2.3 Thermal-runaway stability criterion
Steady state exists only if loop gain is < 1:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}R_{th} = 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{th,ja,eff} < 1$$

At 80 A with R_th,eff = 12 K/W (whole channel to ambient):

$$2(6400)(0.001)(0.0045)(12) = 0.69$$

The loop is stable but strongly amplified. The steady-state rise is multiplied by $1/(1-0.69) \approx 3.2\times$. This is the formal reason 80 A cannot be continuous.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Transition times
Gate current during the Miller plateau:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,int}+R_{g,ext}+R_{drv}} = \frac{10-3}{1+4.7+5}\approx 0.65\text{ A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot,off}} \approx \frac{3}{1+4.7+2}\approx 0.39\text{ A}$$

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{14\text{ nC}}{0.65}\approx 22\text{ ns},\qquad t_f\approx\frac{14}{0.39}\approx 36\text{ ns}$$

Add an anti-parallel Schottky + 0 Ω on R_g,off, or a turn-off diode, to cut t_f to about 15 ns.

### 3.2 Loss terms (per channel; only one leg switches at a time)

| Term | Equation | @ 80 A | @ 35 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·58n·48k = **2.81 W** | 1.23 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f_sw | 30n·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.048 W** | 0.048 W (Q_rr grows with I) |
| Dead-time body diode | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200n·48k = **0.61 W** | 0.27 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs switching) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 2·45n·10·48k = **0.043 W** (in driver) | 0.043 W |
| **Σ switching** | | **≈ 3.55 W** | ≈ 1.63 W |

### 3.3 Total per channel and board

| Case | P_cond (T_j=100 °C) | P_sw | P_shunt (0.5 mΩ) | **P_channel** | **4 channels** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst | 17.1 W | 3.55 W | 3.2 W | **23.9 W** | **95.6 W** |
| 35 A continuous | 3.28 W | 1.63 W | 0.61 W | **5.5 W** | **22.1 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** $\partial P/\partial f_{sw} \approx 74\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$ at 80 A. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.8 W per channel at 80 A. Above ~35 A, conduction loss dominates, so 48 kHz is chosen for acoustic and current-ripple reasons, not thermal ones.
