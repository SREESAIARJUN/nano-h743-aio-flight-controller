## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Assumptions

**The STM32H743 is not the ESC controller.** The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. AM32 targets include the AT32F421 and STM32G071. ArduPilot's bidirectional DShot driver commands those ESC MCUs and reads eRPM telemetry back.

**80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 is at the edge of physical feasibility.** The model below gives an honest result: roughly **4–5 s burst**, with a realistic continuous rating of **≈30–35 A per channel**. Treat "80 A" as a short-duration rating that firmware current limiting must enforce.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd high-side (complementary low-side) and phase B is held low-side on.

- HS_A conducts for D·T_sw.
- LS_A, the synchronous rectifier, conducts for (1−D)·T_sw. Dead time is handled separately.
- LS_B conducts for 100% of the sector.

The current path therefore always crosses **exactly two R_DS(on)**:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)\,\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$
