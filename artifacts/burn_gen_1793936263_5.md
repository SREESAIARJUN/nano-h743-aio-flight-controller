# Job #61: STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC

## Design Basis, Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and DShot Timer Allocation

---

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points in the brief need correcting before any numbers make sense:

1. **The H743 does not switch the bridges at 48 kHz.** In a 4-in-1 design, each bridge (or the whole ESC) uses a dedicated ESC MCU running the commutation firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to those MCUs. Driving 24 gates directly from the H743 is impractical: it has only two advanced timers (TIM1/TIM8) with complementary outputs and dead-time, plus HRTIM. That architecture would also make DShot pointless.
   - **Chosen architecture:** H743 (ArduPilot), then DShot600 bidirectional, then 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 at 48 kHz PWM, then FD6288Q 3-phase gate drivers, then 24× N-MOSFETs.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm board) is thermally a seconds-scale rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a ≤2 s all-motor burst, with ~10 W total continuous dissipation capability.

3. **Several values below are part-dependent.** These include MOSFET parameters, AM32 timing, and ArduPilot's DShot tick constant. They are stated as assumptions and must be verified against the final BOM datasheets and the ArduPilot source revision you build from.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. The current path at each moment is one high-side (HS) FET plus one low-side (LS) FET. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel current flows through the complementary FET rather than its body diode, so the series path is always ≈2 × R_ds(on).

Hot-channel resistance:

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.4 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.24\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Bridge conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.24\text{m} = \mathbf{28.7\,W}$$

Per FET, each device carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds,hot}}{3} = \frac{6400 \times 2.24\text{m}}{3} = \mathbf{4.78\,W}$$

The hot-R_ds(on) value must be solved self-consistently with T_j. If T_j reaches 125 °C, k_T is about 1.6, giving 32.8 W per bridge.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard (one HS/LS pair at a time).

**Hard-switching overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\,W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\,W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\,W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\,W}$$

**Gate drive** (2 switching FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.038\,W}$$

**Switching subtotal: ≈ 1.72 W per bridge.**

Design note: at 48 kHz, switching is only ~6% of the total. Conduction dominates, so R_ds(on) and copper are the levers that matter. Raising frequency from 24 to 48 kHz costs ~0.85 W per channel. That is worth it for current ripple and acoustic benefit on low-inductance motors.

### 2.3 Shunt and Copper

Shunt loss:

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m} = \mathbf{3.2\,W}$$

Copper loss (≈0.3 mΩ effective path, 2 oz inner pours + phase pads):

$$P_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.3\text{m} \approx \mathbf{1.9\,W}$$
