## 1. Plant Model

### 1.1 PMSM in the rotor (d-q) frame

The electrical angle is θₑ = p·θₘ, where p is the number of pole pairs and θₘ the mechanical angle. The voltage and torque equations are:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2} p \left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q\right] \;\xrightarrow{\;L_d=L_q,\; i_d=0\;}\; \tau_e = K_t\, i_q,\quad K_t = \tfrac{3}{2}p\lambda_m
$$

### 1.2 Mechanics (per axis)

$$
J\dot\omega_m = K_t i_q - B\omega_m - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{dist}
$$

- τ_dist includes cable drag, imbalance torque m·g·r·sin(·), and base-motion coupling.
- Reflected J must include the payload inertia about that axis, including contributions from the outer gimbal frames.

### 1.3 Transforms

**Clarke (amplitude-invariant):**

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**SVPWM via min-max zero-sequence injection** (equivalent to 7-segment SVM):

$$
v_{a,b,c} = \text{invClarke}(v_\alpha, v_\beta),\quad
v_0 = -\tfrac12\left(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\right),\quad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{dc}}
$$

The linear range is |v_αβ| ≤ V_dc/√3. The d-q voltage vector is clamped circularly to this limit, with d-axis priority.

## 2. Cascaded Control Law

```
θ_ref ─►[P + ω_ff]─► ω_ref ─►[PI + J·α_ff]─► i_q,ref ─(+ i_cog,ff)─►[PI_dq + decoupling]─► SVPWM
          ▲ θ (IMU/encoder)        ▲ ω (gyro/encoder)                       ▲ i_d, i_q
```

### 2.1 Current loop (20 kHz): PI with pole-zero cancellation

Set the PI zero at the electrical pole R/L:

$$
C_i(s) = K_{p,i} + \frac{K_{i,i}}{s},\quad K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{ci},\quad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{ci}
\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{i}{i_{ref}} = \frac{\omega_{ci}}{s+\omega_{ci}}
$$

**Bandwidth limit.** Choose ω_ci ≤ 2π·f_s/20 to account for the 1.5-sample delay of computation plus PWM.

- At 20 kHz, f_ci ≈ 1 kHz, giving ω_ci = 6283 rad/s.
- This yields K_p = 12.6 V/A and K_i = 31 400 V/(A·s).

**Decoupling feedforward:**

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

**Discrete form with back-calculation anti-windup** (applies to all PI loops):

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_k &= K_p e_k + I_k + u^{ff}_k \\
\bar u_k &= \text{sat}(u_k) \\
I_{k+1} &= I_k + T_s\left[K_i e_k + K_{aw}(\bar u_k - u_k)\right]
\end{aligned}
$$

Choose K_aw ≈ K_i/K_p, i.e. the tracking time constant equals the integral time T_i.

Many gimbal motors are high-resistance and voltage-driven. If you have no current shunts, set v_q = R·i_q,ref + ω_e·λ_m in open loop. The upper loops below are unchanged.

### 2.2 Velocity loop (4 kHz)

Treat the current loop as unity (valid because ω_cv ≪ ω_ci). The plant is then K_t/(Js).

Use a PI with a symmetric-optimum-style placement:

$$
K_{p,v} = \frac{J\,\omega_{cv}}{K_t},\qquad K_{i,v} = \frac{K_{p,v}\,\omega_{cv}}{N},\quad N\in[4,10]
$$

The crossover is ω_cv. The PI zero sits at ω_cv/N, which gives phase margin ≈ atan(N) minus the delay contribution.
