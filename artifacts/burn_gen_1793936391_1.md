## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flag

Two points need agreement before any math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot (attitude control, EKF, DShot master). Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, such as an AT32F421K8 or STM32G071. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600, using internal board traces in an AIO design or the stack connector in a 2-board design.

2. **"80 A burst per channel" on a 20×20 mm nano board is not physically sustainable beyond about 1 second.** Section 3 shows this from first principles:
   - Total dissipation at 4 × 80 A is about 175 W.
   - The board's thermal mass is about 3 J/K.
   - The battery would also be supplying about 320 A, which exceeds any connector, wire gauge and pad that fits this form factor.

**My recommendation:** rate the product at 80 A for ≤1 s (single channel or diagonal pair), 45–55 A for 10 s, and about 30–35 A continuous with airflow. Marketing "80A burst" figures are usually derived exactly this way. Below I give the model so the number can be defended.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs:
- one high-side and one low-side during the ON portion of the PWM cycle, or
- two low-sides during freewheeling.

So the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j),\qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 2.20 mΩ | 80² × 4.40 mΩ = **28.2 W** |
| 85 °C | 2.93 mΩ | **37.5 W** |
| 125 °C | 3.41 mΩ | **43.6 W** |

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period, so the average dissipation per FET is P_cond / 6, about **4.7–7.3 W per FET**.

**Copper losses are not negligible.** Use 2 oz inner planes with phase pours of roughly 0.25 mΩ round trip, plus the battery feed:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{trace} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6 \text{ W per channel}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge is PWM-switching at any instant in 6-step operation.

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching)**

The switching transition time is set by the Miller charge and the gate drive current:

$$
t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g,src}} = \frac{6\text{ nC}}{1.0\text{ A}}\approx 6\text{ ns (use 10 ns with layout parasitics)},\quad t_f \approx 10\text{ ns}
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times20\text{ ns}\times48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5\times1.5\text{ nF}\times635\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM cycle)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ ns}\times48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (6 FETs per channel; this loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$
P_{g} = N\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot k_{sw}
$$

Here k_sw is about 1/3 for 6-step, because only one leg is PWM-switching at a time:

$$
P_g \approx 6\times30\text{ nC}\times10\text{ V}\times48\text{ k}\times\tfrac13 = \mathbf{0.03\ W}
$$

**Switching subtotal:**

$$
P_{sw} \approx 0.97+0.036+0.023+0.31+0.03 \approx \mathbf{1.37\ W}
$$

**Design insight:** at 80 A, conduction loss is about 30× larger than switching loss. Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 0.7 W per channel. The 48 kHz choice is justified by current ripple, low-inductance motors and acoustics, not by thermals. Spend your effort on R_DS(on) and copper.

### 2.3 Auxiliary Loss Terms

**Current-shunt loss** (if a 0.25 mΩ shunt is used per channel):

$$
P_{shunt} = 6400 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 1.6 \text{ W}
$$
