# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC (Job #30): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (FC). It does not commutate the motors. Each motor's bridge is driven by its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Using 4× AT32F421 or 4× STM32G071 is industry practice for 4-in-1 ESCs, because sensorless 6-step commutation with back-EMF zero-cross detection needs dedicated comparators and timers per motor. The H743 talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600.

> ⚠️ **Reality check up front:** 80 A × 4 motors at high duty means about **300 A of battery current** (~7.6 kW). A nano board, an XT30/XT60 connector, and a single-shunt current sensor cannot carry that continuously. The math below shows that "80 A burst" is only defensible as **per-motor, single-digit seconds**. The spec sheet must say so.
