## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

### 0.1 Architecture

| Item | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | H743 FC + 4× dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421, AM32 firmware) | ArduPilot on the H743 does **not** run motor commutation. It emits DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. Commutation at 48 kHz needs its own MCU per motor (or one MCU per 2 motors). |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge driver (FD6288-class), 1 per motor | Proven in 4-in-1 ESCs, integrated bootstrap. |
| MOSFETs | 40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 or DFN5×6 N-channel, 6 per motor (24 total) | Required for 6S operation. |

### 0.3 Form-Factor Warning

Sustained 4 × 80 A (**320 A total**) is not physically achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" board. Copper cross-section, connector ratings, and heat capacity cannot support it. The model below shows the rating is credible only as a **seconds-scale burst** on a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz** board. If the 20×20 footprint is mandatory, derate to roughly 35–45 A burst per motor.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.70 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 100) = 1.05\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

During any 60° commutation sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side path.

- **High-side switch:** conducts for fraction D.
- **Low-side (complementary) switch:** conducts for fraction (1−D).

The current returns through the opposite phase's low-side switch.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}$$

With complementary PWM, total conduction loss is **duty-independent**. It is redistributed between FETs, not reduced.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

### 1.3 Worst single FET

The worst case is the low-side switch at low duty, where it conducts the full sector:

$$P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R_{DS} = 6.72\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Trace resistance per path:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t_{eff}}, \qquad \rho_{Cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\,[1 + 0.00393\,(T-20)]$$

Assume an effective 0.4 mΩ path per motor (battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad, multi-layer parallel pours, 80 °C copper):

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.56\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In each PWM period, only the PWM-switching leg incurs hard-switching loss. The low side transitions near-ZVS after dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time events occur per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 active FETs, dissipated in driver and R_G)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs, 125 °C) | 13.44 | 53.76 |
| Copper | 2.56 | 10.24 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 | 3.87 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.46 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 | 0.52 |
| **Total** | **17.71** | **70.85** |

Conduction dominates (~76%). The design priority is lowest R_DS(on) and copper. Lowering f_sw buys little: dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only ~0.8 W per motor.

**Efficiency at burst** (output ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW electrical):

$$\eta_{ESC} \approx 1 - 70.9/8064 \approx 99.1\%$$

The efficiency is excellent. The problem is the absolute heat on a tiny board.

### 2.7 Bus capacitor ripple

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 0.5 I = 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per motor (at } D=0.5)$$

With 4 motors and phase-interleaved PWM (ESC firmware dependent), the combined ripple is roughly 40–80 A_rms. This requires:

- Local low-ESR ceramics: ≥ 4 × 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 per motor.
- A 470–1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic at the battery pads, external to the board.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per motor, lumped Cauer approximation)

```
P_FET ──[Rθjc 1.2 K/W]──●──[Rθ_spread 3 K/W]──●──[Rθba,share]── T_amb
                       Cj≈0.02 J/K       C_local≈1.5 J/K      C_board (shared)
```

### 3.2 Junction-to-case rise (fast, ms time constant)

$$\Delta T_{jc} = 6.72 \times 1.2 \approx 8\ \text{K}$$

Reaches steady state in about 10 ms.

### 3.3 Spreading to local copper

Two FETs dissipate about 13.4 W into the local pour:

$$\Delta T_{spread} \approx 6.72 \times 3 \approx 20\ \text{K}$$

### 3.4 Board bulk (adiabatic burst approximation)

Board heat capacity for a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board with FETs and capacitors is about **6 J/K**. Convective resistance in prop wash is R_θba ≈ 6 K/W (about 25 K/W in still air).
