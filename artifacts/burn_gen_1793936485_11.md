# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #36): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Key Findings

**The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** On this product the H743 runs ArduPilot. It sends bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). Those MCUs run 6-step commutation with complementary PWM at 48 kHz.

**Finding 1: 80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a transient rating only.** At 80 A on all four channels the board dissipates about 110 W. With roughly 3.5 J/K of board heat capacity, it heats at about 30 K/s. Continuous capability on this form factor is about 20–25 A per motor. Section 3 derives these figures.

**Finding 2: The DShot600 timer and DMA plan fits the H743 comfortably.** It uses two timers, 4 output and 2–4 input-capture DMA streams, all resolved through DMAMUX1.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step with synchronous rectification)

During any commutation step, current flows through one high-side PWM leg (complementary pair) and one low-side FET that is held fully on.

### 2.1 Conduction loss

- PWM leg: P_HS + P_LS,sync = D·I²R + (1−D)·I²R = I²R. This is independent of duty cycle.
- Static low-side FET: I²R.

> **P_cond = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(on),hot**

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W**

Each conducting FET dissipates about 12.3 W while conducting. Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the average per FET is about 8.2 W.

### 2.2 Switching (V–I overlap) loss

> **P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw**

= 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 kHz = **1.45 W**

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

> **P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw**

= 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W**

Note that Q_rr scales strongly with di/dt and I, so a real value could be 2–3× higher.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

> **P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw**

= **0.024 W**

### 2.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction

> **P_dt = V_f · I_ph · 2t_dt · f_sw**

= 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**

### 2.6 Gate drive

> **P_g = Q_g · V_GS · f_sw**

= **0.019 W per switching FET**. This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### 2.7 Totals at 80 A

| Loss | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.6 W | 98.3 W |
| Switching + Q_rr + C_oss | 1.52 W | 6.1 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 26.7 W** | **≈ 107 W** |

Add about 2–4 W of copper/trace I²R and shunt losses at 320 A aggregate. Bus current is lower than phase current at partial throttle; use I_bus ≈ D·I_ph for battery sizing.

**Design sensitivity:** Conduction dominates by about 94%. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz only adds about 2 W per motor. The levers that matter are R_DS(on) (parallel FETs or 5×6 parts) and T_j (which sets k_T).

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Junction transient (single FET, 12.3 W while conducting)

Assume Z_th,jc ≈ 1.5 K/W (3×3 package, steady state). For pulses under 10 ms, Z_th is about 0.2–0.4 K/W, so commutation-scale ripple is only a few kelvin.

The junction tracks the board plus I·R·Z_th,jc: ΔT_jc ≈ 12.3 × 1.5 ≈ **18 K above case**. The board temperature is the real limit.

### 3.2 Board lumped capacitance (25.5 × 25.5 mm, 6-layer 2 oz)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | 650 mm² × 0.42 mm × 60% fill ≈ 0.16 cm³ × 3.45 J/cm³K | 0.57 J/K |
| FR-4 | 1.04 cm³ × 1.9 J/cm³K | 2.0 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | | ≈ 1.0 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | **≈ 3.5 J/K** |

### 3.3 Steady-state convective resistance (in propwash)

h ≈ 80–100 W/m²K, A ≈ 1.3×10⁻³ m² (both sides)

> **R_th,sa ≈ 1/(h·A) ≈ 7.7–9.6 K/W**

Time constant τ = R·C ≈ 27–34 s.

### 3.4 Burst capability

> ΔT(t) = P·R·(1 − e^(−t/τ)); for t ≪ τ, ΔT ≈ P·t / C_th

Budget: T_amb = 35 °C, T_board,max = 105 °C (leaves about 20 K for T_j ≤ 125 °C), so ΔT_allow = 70 K.

| Scenario | P | dT/dt | Max burst |
|---|---|---|---|
| All 4 @ 80 A | 107 W | 30.6 K/s | **≈ 2.3 s** |
| All 4 @ 60 A | ≈ 61 W | 17 K/s | ≈ 4 s |
| All 4 @ 40 A | ≈ 28 W | 8 K/s | ≈ 10 s (non-linear region; use full exponential) |
| Continuous limit | ΔT/R ≈ 8 W | — | ≈ **20–23 A/motor** |

To derive the continuous limit, solve 4·(2I²R_hot + 2.1·(I/80)) ≈ 8 W, which gives I ≈ 22 A.

**Recommendations:**
- Publish the rating as **"22 A continuous / 80 A 1 s burst"**. Do not claim 80 A without a time qualifier.
- Implement I²t thermal derating in the ESC firmware.
- Use the ESC temperature telemetry (via EDT) in ArduPilot.
