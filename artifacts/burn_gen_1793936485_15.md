# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Switching, Thermal and DShot Model

This is an engineering derivation with explicitly stated assumptions. Every number marked **[A]** is an assumed component parameter. Replace these with values from your selected MOSFET and driver datasheets before release.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Verdict

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage. It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. That MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and six N-FETs.

```
 [H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir x4--> [ESC MCU x4] --> [Gate driver x4] --> [6 FETs x4] --> Motors
     ^                                  |
     +----------- eRPM telemetry (GCR) -+
```

**Verdict.** The headline result of the model (§2–3) is this:

| Interpretation | Power-stage loss | Feasible on 20×20 "nano"? |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A burst per channel, 4 channels simultaneously | ≈ 85 W | **No.** Junction limit is reached in about 1–2 s, and board copper is undersized. |
| 80 A burst per channel, one channel at a time | ≈ 21 W | Marginal. Bursts of ≤ 1 s only. |
| 80 A total (20 A per channel) | ≈ 6 W | Yes. |

Recommendation: rate the board **"20 A continuous / 30 A burst per channel"** on 20×20. Alternatively, move to a 30.5×30.5 board with 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 FETs if you need 80 A per channel.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Six-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases carry I_ph. Assume complementary PWM on one leg and a static low-side on the other. The current path is then:

- **PWM ON (duty D):** HS_a → winding → LS_b
- **PWM OFF (1−D):** LS_a (synchronous) → winding → LS_b

Both paths always pass through exactly two channels of R_ds(on), independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\ \alpha\approx0.005\,/\mathrm{K}
$$

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Distribution between devices:**

- Static LS_b carries 100 % of the time, giving 9.6 W.
- HS_a carries D × 9.6 W.
- LS_a carries (1−D) × 9.6 W.

The worst single device is therefore about 9.6 W conduction plus its share of switching loss. Commutation rotates this load through the 6 legs.

### 2.2 Electro-thermal coupling (R_ds runaway check)

$$
T_j = T_c + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}, \quad P(T_j)=I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
\Rightarrow T_j = \frac{T_c + I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha)R_{\theta JC}}{1 - I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JC}}
$$

The stability condition is I²·R₂₅·α·R_θJC < 1. With the values above: 6400 × 1e-3 × 0.005 × 1.2 = 0.038, so the stage is stable with no runaway. The danger is total heat, not instability.

### 2.3 PCB copper conduction

Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C, and about 0.32 mΩ/□ at 100 °C.

On a 20×20 board, assume each phase path is about 4 squares, and use 2 layers in parallel per net:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx 4 \times 0.32/2 = 0.64\ \text{mΩ} \;\Rightarrow\; P_{Cu}=80^2\times0.64\text{m}\times(\text{2 legs}) \approx \mathbf{8.2\ W}
$$

This is a large hidden loss. A 30×30 board with 6-layer 2 oz copper, plus soldered phase busbars, cuts it by 3–4×.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. It produces one turn-on and one turn-off event per period, under hard switching.

**(a) V–I overlap:**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges):**

$$
P_{DT} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery.** At HS turn-on, the LS body diode recovers:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

This adds overlap time. A common rule is to add t_rr/2 to t_r.

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss(er)}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\cdot 2 = 0.030\ \text{W}
