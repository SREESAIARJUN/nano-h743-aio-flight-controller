# STM32H743 AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis and DShot DMA Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

These findings change the design and should be read before the derivations.

| # | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. In every practical AIO, each ESC channel (or a pair) has its own MCU, typically an AT32F421 running AM32 or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sources bidirectional DShot600 and decodes the replies. | The architecture separates into two domains: the FC (H743) and the ESC (4× AT32F421 with gate drivers). |
| F2 | "80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board cannot be sustained for the typical 10 s marketing burst. The calculation in §3.4 shows an adiabatic board temperature rise of about 34 K/s at 4×80 A. | Specify the rating as 80 A for ≤1.5 s per motor, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with a heatsink. |
| F3 | Device parameters below are representative values for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFET class. They are not taken from a specific datasheet. | Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values into the equations. The model is fully parametric. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step drive with synchronous rectification, two FETs always carry the phase current:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET (synchronous) for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, fully on.

Current ripple from the motor inductance L_ph adds an RMS correction:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

For a racing motor with L_ph ≈ 15 µH at D = 0.9 near full throttle, ΔI ≈ 3.5 A. The ripple term is therefore about 1 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Total conduction loss per ESC, including the board copper and phase-pad resistance R_cu (≈0.15 mΩ per path on a 2 oz, 8-layer board):

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2\,\big[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big]
$$

### 1.3 Electro-thermal fixed point (runaway check)

The junction temperature depends on the loss, and the loss depends on the junction temperature:

$$
T_j = T_b + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JB}
$$

$$
P_{FET} = k\,I^2 R_{ds,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

Solving for T_j:

$$
T_j=\frac{T_b + kI^2R_{ds,25}R_{\theta JB}(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha\,kI^2R_{ds,25}R_{\theta JB}}
$$

**Stability condition:** the loop is stable only if

$$
\alpha\,kI^2R_{ds,25}R_{\theta JB} < 1
$$

**Worst-case FET.** The return-leg low-side FET conducts the full current for 120° of each electrical cycle, so k = 1/3.

$$
kI^2R_{ds,25} = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(0.9\times10^{-3}) = 1.92\ \text{W}
$$

With R_θJB = 3.2 K/W:

- Loop gain = 0.0045 × 1.92 × 3.2 = 0.028. This is far from runaway at the junction level.
- **Runaway happens at the board level.** As T_b climbs (see §3), R_ds tracks T_b.
