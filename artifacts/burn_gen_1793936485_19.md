# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Power Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Design Basis

Three points need settling before any modelling, because they change the numbers.

1. **The H743 does not run the ESCs.** It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency set in that firmware. It has no relation to the H743 timer configuration.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as per-channel phase/motor current, for about 10 s.** Battery current is lower: I_bat = D·I_phase.
3. **The 80 A rating is a transient rating on a nano form factor.** The analysis below shows it cannot be sustained thermally. The design is therefore governed by a thermal-capacitance (adiabatic) budget, not by steady-state Rθ.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At an assumed T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2 \times 1.425 = 1.71\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This assumption is checked by iteration in §3.4.

### 1.2 6-step commutation conduction pattern

In each 60° sector, phase A carries PWM on its high side (duty D) with its low side complementary (1−D). Phase B's low side is the return path and is fully on. Phase C floats.

At any instant, exactly two MOSFETs carry I_ph. The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-device average over an electrical cycle

Each phase spends 120° in each of three states: PWM-high, return-low, and floating. Averaged over a cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2R \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,[1 + (1-D)]\,I^2R$$

At full-throttle burst (D → 1), the loss is evenly shared:

$$P_{HS}=P_{LS}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(1.71\times10^{-3}) = 3.65\ \text{W per FET}$$

The bridge total is 6 × 3.65 = **21.9 W per channel**.

**Worst case for the low side is part throttle.** At D = 0.5:

- P_LS = 0.5 I²R = 5.5 W.
- P_HS = 1.8 W.

Steady 80 A at mid-throttle is a stalled or locked-rotor condition. The ESC current limit must catch it.

**Averaging is valid** because the electrical period is much shorter than the die thermal time constant. For example, a 2000 KV motor on 25 V with 7 pole pairs runs at about 5.8 kHz electrical (~170 µs period), against a die time constant of a few milliseconds.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWMing leg switches hard. The terms below cover one hard-switched transition pair per PWM period.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 30 ns · 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V f_sw | 40 n · 25.2 · 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss V f_sw (½ per device, 2 devices) | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD I (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 n · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | Q_g V_GS f_sw × 2 FETs | **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.2 W per channel** |
