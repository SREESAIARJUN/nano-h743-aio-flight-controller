## 0. Scope and Architectural Corrections

Three points in the brief need clarifying before any analysis.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the whole board) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and runs the commutation loop. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This document models it that way:
   - Sections 1–3: the power stage, owned by the ESC MCU.
   - Section 4: the DShot link, owned by the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A RMS phase current per motor for ≤ 5 s.** The math below shows this is a transient rating on a nano/AIO form factor, not a continuous one.
3. **Battery-side current is the real system limiter.** Four motors at 80 A near full duty implies about 320 A of battery current. That exceeds what an XT60, the pigtail, or a 20×20/30×30 board's bus copper can carry. The bus and connector budget appears in §3.4.

**Commutation scheme:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through one high-side and one low-side FET of two different phases. The PWM'd leg alternates between its HS FET (on-time) and its LS FET (off-time, synchronous rectification).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Per-motor conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, the current path always contains exactly two FET channels, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

With α ≈ 0.0059 /K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.5 at 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}
$$

At T_j = 25 °C the same calculation gives 15.4 W. The temperature-dependent R_DS(on) creates positive electrothermal feedback, which is solved iteratively in §3.3.

### 2.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle (120° HS, 120° LS, 120° floating). Within its HS interval, the PWM'd leg splits current by duty:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R \cdot D, \qquad P_{LS,sync} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R (1-D)
$$

The worst case is D → 1 at burst. The most-stressed FET is any HS FET:

$$
P_{cond,FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 6400 \cdot 1.8\times10^{-3} \cdot 2 \;\to\; \text{averaged} \approx \mathbf{3.84\ W}
$$

This figure is time-averaged over the electrical cycle. Electrical periods (≈ 0.1–1 ms) are far shorter than the die thermal time constant (≈ 5–20 ms), so averaging is valid.
