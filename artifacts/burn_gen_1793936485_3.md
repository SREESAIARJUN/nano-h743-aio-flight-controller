# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #44)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**System partitioning.** This document assumes the standard AIO architecture:

- **The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot.** It generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
- **Each ESC channel has its own MCU** (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), plus a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.
- **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** A 48 kHz, 4×6-step commutation workload does not belong on the flight controller.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths (6-step trapezoidal, complementary/synchronous PWM)

During any commutation sector, two phases are active: say phase A is PWM-high and phase B is low.

- **PWM on-time (fraction D):** current flows through HS_A → motor → LS_B.
- **PWM off-time (fraction 1−D):** current freewheels through LS_A (synchronously rectified) → motor → LS_B.

So **exactly two MOSFETs conduct at all times**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per-device split within a sector:

$$P_{HS_A}=D\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS_A}=(1-D)\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS_B}=I^2R$$

Over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs is active for 1/3 of the time. The average per FET is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R \cdot \{D \text{ (HS)}, (1-D)+1 \text{ (LS)}\}$$

The low side carries more heat at low duty.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (requires an iterative solution)

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on the loss, solve with a fixed-point iteration: T_j → R_DS(T_j) → total loss P → T_j = T_board + P_FET·R_θ → repeat.

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,ESC}=2(80)^2(1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Copper loss (do not ignore)

With 2 oz copper, sheet resistance is ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□. A 10 mm long, 5 mm wide phase trace is 2 squares, giving 0.5 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu}\approx I^2 R_{trace}\cdot n_{paths} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} \times 2 \approx \mathbf{6.4\ W/ESC}$$

**Copper strategy:**
- Use 4–6 layers with stacked power planes.
- Use 2 oz copper on outer layers and inners where possible.
- Put the battery pads adjacent to the FET bank.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses apply to the PWM-switching device (HS_A), with LS_A acting as the synchronous rectifier.

| Loss term | Formula | Value at V=25.2 V, I=80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_d·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | **0.024 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 6·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 6·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.144 W** |
| **Total per ESC** | | **≈ 2.45 W** |

**Interpretation:** Switching loss is only about 11% of conduction loss. At 48 kHz, **conduction loss dominates**. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 2.4 W per ESC. If quieter motors are the goal, that is a viable trade.

Switching loss scales with V_bus. Conduction loss does not, apart from the effect of duty cycle on the HS/LS split.

**Shunt choice matters.** A single total-current shunt at 0.25 mΩ dissipates I²R = 320² × 0.25 mΩ = 25.6 W. Options:
- Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a high-gain amplifier (for example INA186 at 200 V/V): 10 W.
- Use a Hall sensor (for example the ACS7xx family): approximately 0 W.
