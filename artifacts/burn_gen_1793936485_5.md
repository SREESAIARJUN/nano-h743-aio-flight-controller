## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 0.9 × 1.6 = **1.44 mΩ**

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction

In 6-step drive, current flows through one high-side and one low-side path at all times. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel current during PWM off-time goes through the complementary FET, not the diode. Conduction loss is therefore nearly duty-independent:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot \frac{R_{ds}(T_j)}{n_p}$$

where n_p is the number of paralleled FETs per switch.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.44\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{18.4\ W}$$

The peak dissipating FET carries 9.2 W while conducting. Averaged across the six FETs over one electrical cycle, each FET carries about 6.1 W.

### 1.3 Electrothermal convergence

R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Iterate until stable:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\cdot Z_{th,j\text{-}pcb}(t)$$

This converges in about 3 iterations. It runs away if $\partial P/\partial T_j \cdot Z_{th} \geq 1$, i.e. if $I^2 R_{25}\alpha Z_{th} \geq 1$. At baseline: 6400 × 0.9 mΩ × 0.006 × 2.5 = **0.086**, which is stable.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect

Assume about 0.5 mΩ per channel in bus traces, pads and motor-wire solder joints:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ mΩ} = \mathbf{3.2\ W/ch}$$

This is often underestimated.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. The commutating legs switch at the electrical commutation rate, which is negligible compared with f_sw.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | $\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}$ | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 50 ns × 48 k = **2.42 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | $V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$ | 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | $Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | $Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}$ | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver/R_g, not FET) | $2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}$ | 2 × 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 3.8 W/ch** |

**Rise-time estimate:** t_r ≈ Q_gd / I_g(plateau). With Q_gd ≈ 12 nC and about 0.5 A of driver current at the Miller plateau, t_r ≈ 24 ns. Weak gate drivers with 0.2–0.3 A sink capability double this term.

**48 kHz vs 24 kHz trade-off:** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds about 1.9 W per channel, roughly 7.6 W for the whole board. In exchange you get lower current ripple, quieter motors and smoother low-throttle control. Dead-time loss is the second-largest switching term, so trim t_dt to the minimum your driver's shoot-through margin allows.

### 3.2 Two-node network: junction to board to air

```
P_FET ──► [Z_th,j-pcb(t)] ──► T_pcb node (C_pcb) ──► [R_conv] ──► T_amb
                                     ▲
                    P_total (all FETs + copper + caps)
```

**Board heat capacity** for a 30.5 mm 6-layer board plus FETs and capacitors:

$$C_{pcb} = \sum m_i c_i \approx 4\text{ g FR4}\times1.1 + 2\text{ g Cu}\times0.385 + 1\text{ g parts}\times0.7 \approx \mathbf{6\ J/K}$$

**Convection** under prop-wash (h ≈ 100 W/m²K, two faces, 1.86×10⁻³ m²):

$$R_{conv} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx \mathbf{5.4\ K/W}, \qquad \tau = R_{conv} C_{pcb} \approx 32\text{ s}$$

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_{amb} + P_{tot}R_{conv}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right) + (T_{pcb,0}-T_{amb})e^{-t/\tau}$$
