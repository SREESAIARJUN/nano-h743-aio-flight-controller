## 1. Architecture and Reference BOM

The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC microcontrollers, which generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. This is the only practical topology: ArduPilot has no native FOC/6-step commutation for H7 timers, and BDShot telemetry requires ESC firmware such as AM32 or BLHeli_32.

```
VBAT (6S, 25.2V max) ──┬── 4× [ESC MCU → 3-ph gate driver → 6× N-MOSFET] → Motors 1-4
                       ├── 5V/2A buck (FC, RX, VTX-lite)
                       └── 3V3 LDO (H743, IMU, baro)
H743 TIM1 CH1-4 ──DShot600 bidir (single wire, 3V3)──► ESC MCU #1-4 (TIM input)
```

| Function | Reference part | Key parameters used |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | 400 MHz core, 200 MHz timer clock |
| ESC MCU (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 (AM32) | 48 kHz PWM, complementary, dead-time insertion |
| Gate driver (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V) | ~1.5 A sink / 1.0 A source (effective) |
| MOSFET (×24) | 40 V, PG-TDSON-8 (5×6), e.g. ISC/BSC 0.7 mΩ class | R_DS(on),25°C = 0.70 mΩ; Q_g = 95 nC; Q_gd = 14 nC; Q_oss = 60 nC; Q_rr = 50 nC; V_SD = 0.8 V; R_θJC = 0.9 K/W |
| PCB | 30.5×30.5 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz Cu all layers | 1.6 mm finished |
| Bulk capacitance | 1× 35 V 1000 µF low-ESR + 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R per channel | — |

> **Physical-reality flag.** A 24-FET, 80 A/channel power stage does not fit a 20×20 mm "nano" footprint. 30.5×30.5 mm is the minimum, using double-sided placement with 12 FETs per side, about 360 mm² of 930 mm². Section 5 shows that 80 A is a **time-limited burst rating**, not continuous.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS,125} = 0.70 \times 1.60 = 1.12\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM

At any instant, two phases carry the phase current I. Over one PWM period with duty D:

- **Non-PWM'd low-side FET:** conducts I for 100% of the sector.
- **PWM'd phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the complementary low-side FET freewheels for (1−D), minus dead time.

So at every instant exactly two FETs are in the current path:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At I = 80 A:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.12\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{14.34\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase spends 120° electrical driven high, 120° driven low, and 120° floating. Over one electrical revolution each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the cycle. The worst-case FET is the low-side FET of the non-PWM phase:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.12\text{ mΩ} = 7.17\ \text{W (while conducting)}$$

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 7.17 = 2.39\ \text{W (cycle average)}$$

The electrical period at typical speeds (≥ 300 Hz electrical) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (about 10 ms). Junction ripple is therefore under 2 K, and the average value governs.

### 2.4 Copper interconnect loss

Assume each phase/bus path is about 15 mm long and 4 mm wide, on 4 paralleled 2 oz layers (t = 70 µm):

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.015}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}\cdot 4} = 0.23\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Use ρ × 1.39 at 100 °C, giving 0.32 mΩ. With about 3 segments in the current loop:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 3 \times 6400 \times 0.32\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{6.1\ W}$$ **[EST]**

> Copper loss is roughly 40% of the FET conduction loss. This is the most commonly neglected term in nano 4-in-1 designs. Mitigate it with stitched via arrays (≥ 20 vias per 0.3 mm drill per pad) and the shortest possible phase pours. Section 4 conservatively assigns about 3 W per channel to the board itself, since part of this copper loss is shared bus.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per channel, that is one turn-on and one turn-off of the high-side FET per PWM period. The complementary low-side FET switches at about zero voltage, after dead time.

### 3.1 Voltage-current overlap (dominant term)

The Miller plateau is about 3.0 V, with V_DRV = 10 V and R_g,total ≈ 4.7 Ω:

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{7}{4.7}= 1.49\text{ A}\rightarrow \text{driver-limited } \approx 1.0\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = 0.64\ \text{A}$
