# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Power-Stage and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Check

The figures below are engineering estimates. Before building, they must be checked against the datasheets for the parts you actually select.

### 0.1 Topology

A 4-in-1 ESC does not run its motors from the H743. The H743 is the flight controller and runs ArduPilot. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743. All timer and DMA analysis below applies to the H743 side only.

### 0.2 Current Rating

Reaching 80 A burst on all four channels of a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically credible. The analysis in §2.6 shows about 110 W of dissipation into roughly 6 J/K of thermal mass. That is about 18 K/s of adiabatic temperature rise.

This design therefore assumes:
- **Form factor:** 30.5×30.5 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner copper.
- **Rating:** 80 A per channel for 2 seconds or less, or 80 A on one channel transiently.
- **Continuous:** about 45–55 A per channel.

On a 20×20 board, derate to roughly 35–40 A burst.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-Channel Conduction

In 6-step commutation, current always flows through one high-side and one low-side FET in series. Under complementary PWM with duty D:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side FET conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- **Return phase:** the low-side FET conducts for 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle to first order. Dead-time diode intervals are handled separately in §2.

### 1.2 Thermal Feedback (Fixed Point)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. The coupled equations are:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta jc}, \qquad R_{DS} = R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solve iteratively. Thermal runaway occurs if:

$$2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta,total} \ge 1$$

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond for 4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 61 W |
| 100 °C | 1.70 mΩ | **21.8 W** | 87 W |
| 125 °C | 1.86 mΩ | **23.8 W** | 95 W |

### 1.4 Per-FET Split

Each phase leg conducts high-side for 1/3 of the electrical period and low-side for 1/3. At the 125 °C point, average loss per FET is about 23.8 W / 6 ≈ **4.0 W**.

The instantaneous peak per FET is about 12 W. This peak occurs at the electrical commutation frequency. For example, at 30 krpm with 14 poles, the electrical frequency is about 3.5 kHz. The die thermal time constant (roughly 1–10 ms) averages this ripple partially.

### 1.5 PCB Copper Loss

Assume 10 mm of 4 oz-equivalent copper (2+2 oz parallel layers), 3 mm wide, per path.

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\times0.01}{0.003\times140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.41\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That is 0.41 mΩ per path, with about 3 paths per channel. At 80 A this gives approximately **+7.9 W per channel**, which is significant. Use solid pours, stacked vias, and solder-reinforced phase pads.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Use V = 25.2 V (worst case), I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V·I Overlap (High-Side FET Only)

The synchronous low-side FET turns on with zero-voltage switching (ZVS) after its body diode conducts.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
