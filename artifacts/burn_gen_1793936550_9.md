## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

These four points shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run ESC commutation firmware. The architecture is an H743 flight controller plus **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071, running AM32) on the same assembly. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. AM32 is preferred because it supports a configurable 48 kHz PWM, bidirectional DShot, and extended DShot telemetry (EDT).
2. **80 A on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is a burst rating only, measured in about 1 second.** Section 3 proves this. For a meaningful burst window, the minimum credible format is 30.5×30.5 mm with 8-layer 2 oz copper.
3. **"80 A" is defined as motor phase current**, the worst case for the FETs. Bus current is I_bus = D·I_ph.
4. **Bus voltage:** 6S LiPo, 25.2 V maximum, with 40 V FETs. That gives about 37% margin for regenerative and ringing spikes, which is marginal. Section 5 adds a TVS and bulk capacitance for this reason.

## 2. Loss Model (Per ESC Channel, 6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current flows through **exactly two FETs** at every instant:

- the PWM leg, through either its high-side FET (on-time D) or its low-side FET (sync-rectifying, 1−D), and
- the low-side FET of the steady-on leg.

This holds independent of D, so:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Averaged over an electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET}=\frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}{3}$$
