# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Notes

The physics forces a few clarifications before the math.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm "nano" stack (FC board + ESC board) |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| ESC topology | 4 independent 3-phase bridges, 24 MOSFETs total |
| ESC MCU | Dedicated per-motor MCU (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32). The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. |
| FC→ESC link | Bidirectional DShot600, one wire per motor |
| "80 A burst" | Per-motor phase current, short duration (≤ 1–2 s), not all four motors simultaneously at steady state |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, 48 kHz |

**Feasibility warning.** Four motors at 80 A simultaneously means 320 A from the battery. That is about 100 W of dissipation in a 20×20 mm stack. Section 3 shows this is survivable only for roughly 1 s. A credible "80 A burst" rating at this size means one or two motors peaking briefly while the average stays well below 40 A. If the requirement is genuinely 4×80 A sustained, the design must move to 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 MOSFET parameters (representative 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient α (normalized) | ≈ 0.0045 /K |
| Q_g (total) | 40 nC |
| Q_rr | 30 nC |
| C_oss @ 25 V | ≈ 1 nF |
| R_θJC (bottom) | 1.5 K/W |
| Body diode V_f @ 80 A | ≈ 0.8 V |

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 \approx 1.74\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Use 1.8 mΩ for margin.

### 1.3 6-step conduction

At any instant one high-side FET is PWM-modulated (duty D) and one low-side FET is fully on. With synchronous rectification, the opposite device of the PWM leg (the low-side FET in the same leg) carries current during (1−D). Current always flows through exactly two channels, so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left[ R_{HS} D + R_{LS,sync}(1-D) + R_{LS,on} \right] \approx 2 I^2 R_{DS(on)}$$

At I = 80 A and R = 1.8 mΩ:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0018 = 23.0\,\text{W}$$

Add copper and shunt resistance. A 0.5 mΩ path for the phase traces, bus, and connectors adds 6400 × 0.0005 = 3.2 W.

$$\boxed{P_{cond,motor} \approx 26.2\,\text{W}}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

- **Instantaneous** while conducting: 6400 × 0.0018 = 11.5 W
- **Average**: 23.0 / 6 = 3.84 W

Check whether the die averages over the cycle:

- At 30,000 eRPM, f_elec = 500 Hz, so T = 2 ms.
- Die thermal time constant τ_die ≈ 1–5 ms, so averaging is only partial.
- Peak junction ripple: ΔT_ripple ≈ (11.5 − 3.84) × Z_θ(0.67 ms) ≈ 7.7 × 0.3 ≈ **2–3 K**, which is acceptable.

At low eRPM under high current (stall or startup), the ripple approaches the full 11.5 W × Z_θ. The ESC firmware must enforce stall/desync current limits.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

All values use V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap), high-side PWM FET

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

The gate driver is FD6288Q-class (≈ 1.0 A source / 1.5 A sink), with R_g ≈ 2.2 Ω external. The Miller plateau gives t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 0.97\,\text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Dead time t_dt = 200 ns per edge, two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 1.23\,\text{W}$$

Dead-time loss is **larger than switching loss**. Reducing dead time to 100 ns (if the gate-driver propagation matching allows) recovers about 0.6 W per motor.

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48000 = 0.036\,\text{W}$$

Recovery current also adds turn-on overlap. Include about 1.5× margin: 0.05 W.

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 10^{-9} \times 635 \times 48000 = 0.015\,\text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and R_g, not the FET)

Two switching FETs per motor:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\,\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48000 \times 2 = 0.038\,\text{W}$$

### 2.6 Total per motor @ 80 A

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 23.0 |
| Conduction (copper, shunt) | 3.2 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.07 |
| Gate drive | 0.04 |
| **Total** | **≈ 28.5 W** |

**Efficiency check.** At D ≈ 0.8, P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.8 × 80 ≈ 1613 W, so η_ESC ≈ 98.3 %.

**All four motors at 80 A: P_total ≈ 114 W.**

### 2.7 Frequency tradeoff

f_sw-dependent losses are about 2.3 W at 48 kHz, or 0.048 W/kHz. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.15 W per motor. The cost is higher current ripple in low-inductance quad motors.

Motor ripple:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V(1-D)D}{L f}$$
