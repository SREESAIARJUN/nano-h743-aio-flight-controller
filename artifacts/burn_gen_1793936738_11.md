### 0.3 ⚠️ Feasibility Flag: Read Before Proceeding

"80 A burst" on a nano-class board, if applied to **all four channels simultaneously**, implies:

- 320 A from the battery
- About 7.1 kW at 22.2 V

That load is not survivable on a 20×20 mm or 30.5×30.5 mm PCB for more than a few seconds. This applies to the battery pads, XT60/XT90 leads, bus capacitors and the shunt as much as to the MOSFETs.

The analysis below therefore treats the rating as follows:

- **80 A per channel, burst ≤ 10 s, all four channels loaded (worst case).** The model shows where this breaks.
- **Continuous rating** is derived separately in §2.5.

Marketing ratings should be derived from §2.5, not the reverse.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

AM32 uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- At any instant, phase current I flows through **two** conducting paths:
  1. The PWM leg: high-side for duty D, then low-side synchronous rectifier for 1−D.
  2. The static low-side of the return phase.
- With synchronous rectification, the PWM leg always has exactly one FET conducting. Dead-time intervals are handled separately in §1.4.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent Rds(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_J) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)\right]
$$

At a design-point T<sub>J</sub> = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS}(110) = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.006 \times 85] = 1.2 \times 1.51 = 1.81\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Conduction Loss per Motor Channel

Assume DC motor-phase current (high-inductance assumption: ripple is treated in §1.5):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_J) \cdot \underbrace{\left[D + (1-D)\right]}_{\text{PWM leg}} + I^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_J) \cdot \underbrace{1}_{\text{static LS}} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_J)
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.81\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET average.** Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for about 1/3 of the cycle (120°). The HS/LS split depends on D:

$$
\bar{P}_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} \approx 3.9\ \text{W}
$$

**Instantaneous peak** (the most important number for junction transients, since τ<sub>JC</sub> is about 1–5 ms, comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM):

$$
P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_J) = 80^2 \times 1.81\,\text{m}\Omega = 11.6\ \text{W}
$$

> **Low-RPM hazard:** At startup or stall, the commutation period can exceed τ<sub>JC</sub>. A single FET then sees 11.6 W quasi-steady, giving ΔT<sub>JC</sub> = 11.6 × 1.2 ≈ 14 °C above case. AM32 stall protection and the startup current limit **must** be enabled.

### 1.4 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2(50\,\text{ns}) \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}
$$
