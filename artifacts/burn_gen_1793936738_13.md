# Job #46: STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC

## Architecture Note: 80A Burst on a Nano Board

The thermal model below shows that **80A on all four channels at once is a 2–3 second event** on a nano-class (20×20 to 30×30 mm) board. It is not a continuous rating. The design is valid only if:

- **"80A burst"** means ≤2 s per motor at a ≤10% duty over any 30 s window.
- **The continuous rating** is about 35–45A per channel with prop-wash airflow.

The numbers that drive this conclusion are in Section 3. All device parameters are **stated assumptions**. Replace them with your selected parts' datasheet values, and every equation will still hold.

### System Architecture

| Block | Function |
|---|---|
| STM32H743VIT6 (FC) | Runs ArduPilot. Sends bidirectional DShot600 on 4 channels to the ESC MCUs (Section 4). |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8BB51 running Bluejay. Drive 3-phase gate signals. |
| 3-phase gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q or equivalent. |
| MOSFETs | 6 per motor, 24 total. |

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors directly. Each motor gets its own ESC MCU. This is standard practice and is required for ArduPilot bidirectional DShot (eRPM telemetry originates in the ESC firmware).

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, motor current always flows through exactly **two** MOSFETs:

- **During the on-time (duty D):** high-side of phase X, then low-side of phase Y.
- **During the off-time (1 − D):** synchronous low-side of phase X, then low-side of phase Y.

So the channel conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot [1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85] = 1.244\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.244\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each of the 6 FETs is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The average per-FET loss is therefore 15.9/6 × 2 ≈ 5.3 W for low-side devices and less for high-side devices. However, each FET sees **8.0 W instantaneous** for the duration of its sector. This matters for junction transients (§3.2).

### 2.2 PCB Copper Conduction

Assume 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers in parallel. The phase and bus path is about 12 mm long and 4 mm wide.

- Sheet resistance for 2 oz copper: ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.
- Path ≈ 3 squares per layer × 4 layers in parallel, giving ≈ 0.19 mΩ.
- Include the current-return path and the solder pads: R_cu ≈ 0.45 mΩ total per conduction loop. Hot copper adds +0.39%/K.

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \cdot 0.45\,\text{m}\Omega \cdot 1.25 \approx \mathbf{3.6\ W}$$

This loss is easy to underestimate. On a nano board it is ~18% of total dissipation.

### 2.3 Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM period: the high-side turns on/off against the synchronous low-side.

**Gate current during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{DRV}-V_{pl}}{R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}+R_{drv,src}} = \frac{10-3.6}{4.7+1.2+5} \approx 0.59\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{G,ext}+R_{G,int}+R_{drv,snk}} = \frac{3.6}{4.7+1.2+2} \approx 0.46\ \text{A}$$

**Rise and fall times:**

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G,on}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{0.59} = 25.4\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_f = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{0.46} = 32.6\ \text{ns}$$

**Overlap loss:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 58\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\,000 = \mathbf{2.81\ W}$$
