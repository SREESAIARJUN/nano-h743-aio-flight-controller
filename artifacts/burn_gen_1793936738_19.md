> ⚠️ **Feasibility warning.** A true "nano" form factor (20 × 20 mm) **cannot** carry 4 × 80 A. Copper cross-section, FET footprint area, and thermal mass are all insufficient. 80 A per channel is only credible as a **short burst** on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board with 2 oz × 8 layers and 5×6-class FETs. The math below shows why.

> **Architecture note.** The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It sends DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz 6-step PWM. This keeps the H743 free for the ArduPilot scheduler and avoids latency coupling between the attitude loop and commutation.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side FET of phase A and one low-side FET of phase B.

- During the ON interval (duty D), the conducting pair is HS_A and LS_B.
- During the OFF interval (1 − D), the synchronous freewheel pair is LS_A and LS_B.

So at every instant **two FETs carry the full phase current I**. The per-channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D (to first order):

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS,HS}\,D + I^2 R_{DS,LS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{DS,LS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond per channel at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 2·6400·0.0010 = **12.8 W** |
| 85 °C | 1.36 mΩ | **17.4 W** |
| 110 °C | 1.51 mΩ | **19.3 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | **20.5 W** |

### 1.3 Electrothermal convergence

The loss depends on temperature, and temperature depends on loss. Solve the coupled equations iteratively:

$$
T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}, \qquad P_{FET} \approx I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

At 80 A, with T_board = 100 °C and R_θJC = 1 K/W:

1. Start with P_FET ≈ 6400 × 1.45 mΩ = 9.3 W, giving T_j = 109.3 °C.
2. Then R_DS = 1.50 mΩ, P_FET = 9.6 W, giving T_j = 109.6 °C.
3. This has **converged**. The junction-to-case rise is about 10 K.

**Thermal runaway check.** The system is stable when the loop gain is below one:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta} = 6400 \cdot 0.001 \cdot 0.006 \cdot 1.0 = 0.038 \ll 1
$$

The junction is stable. **The board, not the junction, is the limiting element** (see §3).

### 1.4 Copper (PCB) conduction loss

Phase and bus traces add real loss. A 2 oz layer has a sheet resistance of about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C.

Assume an effective path per channel of 4 squares in parallel across 4 layers, i.e. 1 □ effective:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx 0.25\text{ mΩ} \times 1.3\ (\text{at }100^\circ\text{C}) \approx 0.33\text{ mΩ}
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.33\text{ m} \approx 2.1\text{ W per channel}
$$

Add a shared battery-input path carrying 320 A at an estimated 0.1 mΩ: about **10 W**. This is why the XT60 pads and input copper must be massive. An XT60 is itself rated about 60 A continuous, so a 4 × 80 A burst exceeds it. Use XT90 or AS150 pigtails on solder pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

In each channel, only the PWM'd leg switches: one HS/LS complementary pair. V_bus = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switching) loss

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = 0.968\text{ W}
$$

###
