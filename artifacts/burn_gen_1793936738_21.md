# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Analysis

This is a **reference-level engineering derivation**, not a released design. All component values rest on stated assumptions. The netlists are architecture-correct starting points. They must go through ERC/DRC, datasheet verification against the parts you actually source, and bench validation (thermal camera, current probe, DShot scope captures) before fabrication.

---

## 0. Architecture Reality Check

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32) that generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs. This document uses that split:
   - **FC domain:** STM32H743, sensors, DShot output.
   - **ESC domain:** 4 × AT32F421 (AM32), 4 × 3-phase gate driver, 24 × MOSFETs.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano-format board is a transient rating, not a thermal steady state.**
   - At 4 × 80 A, the battery path carries **320 A**. That is far beyond XT60 (≈60 A continuous) and beyond typical nano-board pad and trace capacity.
   - The math below shows a bounded burst duration of a few seconds. Treat 80 A as a per-motor transient rating with non-coincident peaks.
3. **Assumed operating point:** 6S LiPo (25.2 V full charge, 22.2 V nominal), 30.5 × 30.5 mm board, 8-layer 2 oz copper.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Assumed MOSFET (PDFN 3.3×3.3, 40 V class)

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V (typ / max) | R₀ | 1.0 / 1.3 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient (normalized) | k(T) | ≈ 1 + 0.0055·(Tj − 25) → 1.55× @ 125 °C |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC |
| Output charge @ 25 V | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Body-diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_F | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | Rθ_JC | 1.5 K/W |

### 1.2 Commutation geometry (six-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

Over one 360° electrical cycle, each phase leg spends:
- 120° **PWM-driven high:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction D; the complementary low-side FET conducts for (1 − D).
- 120° **held low:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.
- 120° **floating:** used for BEMF sensing; no conduction.

Time-averaged conduction loss per FET, with phase current I:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\, D$$

$$P_{LS} = \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,(1-D) + \frac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per motor, summed over 6 FETs, this reduces to:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This holds for any D, because exactly two FET positions carry I at every instant.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A

I² = 6400 A².

| Case | R_DS(Tj) | P per conducting FET | P_cond per motor | P_cond × 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Typical, Tj = 100 °C | 1.0 × 1.41 = 1.41 mΩ | 9.0 W instantaneous | **18.0 W** | 72 W |
| Worst, Tj = 125 °C | 1.3 × 1.55 = 2.02 mΩ | 12.9 W | **25.9 W** | 103 W |

The hottest single FET is the low-side FET at low D, which conducts about 2/3 of the cycle:
- Worst case: P_LS,max ≈ (2/3)(6400)(2.02 mΩ) ≈ **8.6 W**.
- At D → 1: about 4.3 W per FET.

### 1.4 Additional copper losses (per motor)

- Phase traces and pads: about 0.3 mΩ effective.
  - 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **1.9 W** per motor, distributed in the PCB copper.
- Bus copper at 320 A aggregate: about 0.1 mΩ effective.
  - 102,400 × 0.1 mΩ ≈ **10 W**.
  - This number shows why simultaneous 4 × 80 A is not a design point.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET soft-switches through its body diode during dead time. Per motor, one leg is switching at any instant.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

Assume a gate driver with about 1.5 A source/sink and 5 Ω gate resistors, giving t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
