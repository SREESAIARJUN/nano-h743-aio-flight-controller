## 0. Architectural Corrections and Design Assumptions

Three issues in the brief affect everything downstream.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- Four sensorless 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time insertion.
- The H743 has only two advanced timers with complementary outputs (TIM1, TIM8), which is enough for two motors. HRTIM can be pressed into service, but it is impractical alongside ArduPilot's real-time load.
- Standard practice, and this design: the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32, one per motor).
- This is also the only configuration in which "bidirectional DShot" is meaningful, since the eRPM telemetry is returned by the ESC MCUs.

**2. 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous one.**
- The thermal math in §3 quantifies this.
- Realistic continuous capability is roughly 20–30 A per channel, and only with prop-wash airflow.

**3. Every number below depends on the MOSFET and gate-driver datasheets.**
- I use representative parameters for a 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN MOSFET.
- Substitute your selected part's values into the equations; the models themselves are general.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1+\alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.6\times(1+0.0055\times 85) = 2.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

- At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs in series: one high-side (or its complementary low-side during PWM-off) in the driven phase, and one low-side in the return phase.
- With synchronous rectification, the PWM-off interval conducts through the complementary FET's channel rather than its body diode.
- Therefore the per-channel conduction loss is essentially independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Distribution across the six FETs

The average per FET is

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 5.0\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET is a high-side device at D → 1. It carries full current for one-third of the electrical period:

$$P_{HS,max} = I^2 R_{DS} \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot D \approx 5.0\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

- PCB copper path: assume 0.5 mΩ (2 oz outer layers, 20 mm path). This dissipates 6400 × 0.0005 = **3.2 W per channel**.
- DC-link shunt (shared, 0.25 mΩ, at 320 A total): 320² × 0.00025 = **25.6 W**.
  - This shows a single shared shunt is not viable at 4×80 A.
  - Use per-channel 0.2 mΩ shunts, which dissipate **1.3 W each**.
  - Alternatively, use the FET R_DS(on) sense provided by AM32's current estimation.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-active leg switches hard. Its low-side partner turns on softly, after its body diode has already picked up the current.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times30\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
