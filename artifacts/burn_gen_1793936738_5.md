# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #13): Loss Models, Thermal Budget, Bi-directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility Warning

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel uses its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This document assumes 4× STM32G071 parts. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600, and the 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs.

**Feasibility warning:** 80 A *per channel* on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally sustainable.

- This model shows about 82 W of total dissipation at a 4×80 A burst.
- An adiabatic board heats at roughly 50–60 K/s under that load.
- Treat "80 A" as a **≤1 s burst rating** with a firmware-enforced I²t limit, not a continuous rating.
- If the requirement is 80 A *total* (4×20 A), every loss figure below drops by about 16×, and the design is comfortable.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### Topology
Six-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active phase pair.

- During PWM-on, current flows HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During PWM-off, current freewheels through LS(A) → motor → LS(B), using the synchronous rectifier.
- Therefore, at every instant, **exactly two FETs carry I**, whatever the duty cycle D.

### Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.49 mΩ | 19.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.65 mΩ | 21.1 W |

**Thermal runaway check.** Because P_cond depends on T_j, solve the loop self-consistently:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta JA}\,P(T_j)$$

This converges only if

$$R_{\theta JA}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

- At 80 A: 2·6400·1e-3·0.0065 = 0.083 W/K.
- Therefore R_θJA must stay below about 12 K/W per channel. This is easily met, so the system is stable, but the loss level is the real problem.

**Per-FET share.** Each FET conducts during roughly 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{FET,avg}\approx I^2R/3 \approx 3.2\text{ W at }100\,°C$$

The peak instantaneous loss is I²R ≈ 9.6 W. This peak matters for the die's transient Z_th.

### Copper and shunt losses (often the hidden dominant term)

| Element | Assumption | Formula | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase trace, 2 oz outer + 2 oz inner paralleled | ~3 squares of copper, ~0.12 mΩ/sq effective | I²·R_trace | ≈ 2.3 W per channel |
| Low-side shunt | 0.25 mΩ | I²·R_shunt | 1.6 W |

For the shunt, an alternative is to use LS R_ds(on) sensing or a single bus shunt. Doing so saves about 6 W at the board level.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The high-side FET hard-switches. The low-side FET soft-switches, commutating ZVS via its body diode during dead time.

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (HS) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.968 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction (LS) | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·100 ns·48k = **0.307 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 30 nC·25.2·48k = **0.036 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f | **0.024 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs, mostly dissipated in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching per channel** | | **≈ 1.37 W** |

Design levers:

- **Frequency:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be about 0.69 W, but 48 kHz gives lower current ripple and quieter low-throttle behavior.
- **Dead time:** dead time is the second-largest term. Trim it toward 30 ns after verifying shoot-through margin with the actual gate driver propagation skew.

### PWM resolution on the ESC MCU
For a 64 MHz G071 with center-aligned PWM:

$$ARR = \frac{64\,\text{MHz}}{2\cdot 48\,\text{kHz}} = 666 \;\Rightarrow\; \approx 9.4\text{ bits}$$

### Transient (burst) model
Use a Cauer RC ladder:

**die → R_θJC → case → R_θCB → PCB (C_pcb) → R_θBA → air (with prop wash)**

|---|---|---|

| FR4 | ~1.6 g × 0.9 J/gK | 1.44 J/K |
| 24× FETs + capacitors | — | ~0.3 J/K |
