# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #14): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Corrections

I don't have Job #14's BOM, stack-up, or layout files. Every number below comes from the stated assumptions, so substitute your actual datasheet values into the equations. The equations themselves are general.

There are three architectural points to settle before the math.

1. **The STM32H743 does not generate the 48 kHz MOSFET PWM.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU does commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below is split along this boundary.
2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 board is limited by thermal capacitance, not steady-state thermal resistance.** The analysis in §3 shows the burst lasts about 1–2 s with all four channels loaded. A spec sheet should state the burst duration.
3. **Concurrency matters.** Four motors at 80 A each at 22 V is about 7 kW. That is unrealistic for a nano airframe. The usual rating means one channel, short duration. I model both cases.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology

In 6-step complementary PWM, two phases are active in each 60° sector.

- The **PWM leg** alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D of the period) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The **return leg** has its low-side FET on continuously.

So at every instant, exactly two FETs carry the phase current I in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I_dc,phase. Sinusoidal FOC would change this to $P = \tfrac{3}{2} I_{pk}^2 R$ per three-phase set.

### 1.2 Electrothermal Fixed Point

Loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. You solve this iteratively:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)}) \cdot (R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CB})$$

**80 A, T_j = 100 °C (converged value, see §3):**

- R_DS(on) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = 1.34 mΩ
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 6400 × 1.34 × 10⁻³ = **17.1 W per channel**

**Distribution across the six FETs:** each FET conducts about one-third of the electrical cycle on average. So the average per FET is 17.1 / 6 ≈ **2.9 W**, with instantaneous peaks near 8.6 W during its conduction interval. The die's thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) is longer than one electrical period at flight eRPM (≈0.2–1 ms), so averaging is valid at speed. **This averaging does NOT hold at stall or startup**, where one FET pair can carry DC current.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches in a given sector. Each sector has one hard-switched high-side transition pair per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard Switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (Low-Side Diode into High-Side Turn-On)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

The diode recovery current also adds to the high-side turn-on current. This is often the dominant EMI source.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

Gate drive power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.45 + 0.61 + 0.06 + 0.02 + 0.05 \approx \mathbf{2.2\ W per channel}$$

**Design sensitivity:**

- **Frequency:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going 48 → 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel at 80 A, but increases current ripple on low-inductance nano motors.
- **Dead time:** dead-time loss is about 28% of switching loss. Trimming t_dt from 100 to 50 ns saves about 0.
