# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 cannot drive the power stage. It runs ArduPilot (attitude control, EKF, DShot master). Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. A small Cortex-M0+/M4 such as the AT32F421 or STM32G071 is typical. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM, does BEMF commutation, and returns bidirectional DShot eRPM telemetry.

```
 VBAT ─┬───────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │                                               │
   [FC section]                                 [4-in-1 ESC section]
   STM32H743 ── DShot600 bidir (×4) ──────────▶ ESC MCU ×4 ─▶ 3φ gate driver ×4 ─▶ 6 FETs ×4
       ▲                                               │
       └──── CURR (shunt amp), VBAT divider ◀──────────┘
```

All numbers below use the stated assumptions. Swap in your actual datasheet values. The equations are general.

## 1. Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, DFN3.3×3.3 class) R_ds(on) @25 °C | R₂₅ | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_ds(on) temp coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K |
| Rise+fall time (effective) | t_r + t_f | 20 ns |
| Body-diode reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output-capacitance energy at 25 V | E_oss | 0.3 µJ |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 40 nC @ 10 V |
| Dead time (each edge) | t_dt | 100 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Motor phase inductance (for ripple) | L_ph | 15 µH |
| PWM duty at burst | D | 0.9 |

Commutation is 6-step trapezoidal (120° conduction) with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant one high-side and one low-side FET carry I_ph. In the PWM leg, the high-side and low-side FETs alternate.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × (1 + 0.0045·75) = **2.01 mΩ**

### 2.2 RMS current including ripple

The ripple is worst-case near D = 0.5. Evaluated at D = 0.9:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}/2)\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}} \;\;\text{(two series phases → 2L)}$$

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.9 \cdot 0.1}{2 \cdot 15\,\mu \cdot 48\,k} = 1.58\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 0.21 \approx 6400\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple is negligible at 80 A.

### 2.3 Per-channel conduction loss

Two FETs are always in the current path. The PWM leg alternates its high-side and low-side, but the sum is still one R_ds:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.01\,m = 25.7\ \text{W per motor channel}$$

### 2.4 Copper and shunt losses

These are often ignored but are significant.
- 2 oz copper, ~4 mm wide × 8 mm phase path: ~0.5 mΩ per path, giving 6400 × 0.5 m ≈ **3.2 W**.
- 0.25 mΩ shunt: **1.6 W**.

Distribute this into the board node of the thermal model, not the FET junctions.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM-leg FETs switch. The high-side switches hard; the low-side switches with soft ZVS-like transitions after dead time.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 n·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 30 n·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | E_oss·f_sw | 0.3 µ·48 k = **0.014 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | 2·0.8·80·100 n·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·40 n·10·48 k = **0.038 W** |
| **Total switching (per channel)** | | **≈ 1.67 W** |

**Insight:** At 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 15:1. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per channel. Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel.

### 3.1 Totals at 80 A, T_j = 100 °C

| | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 25.7 W | 102.8 W |
| FET switching | 1.67 W | 6.7 W |
| Copper + shunt | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| **Total** | **32.2 W** | **128.7 W** |

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{25.7 + 1.67}{6}\cdot 2 \approx 9.1\ \text{W}$$

This is a time-averaged value over an electrical revolution while the FET is in rotation. Instantaneous peaks are double.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady state (shows why "80 A" can only be a burst)

DFN3.3 on an 8-layer board has R_θJA ≈ 35–45 K/W. Take 40 K/W.

$$\Delta T_{j} = 9.1 \times 40 = 364\ \text{K} \;\Rightarrow\; \textbf{destroyed}$$
