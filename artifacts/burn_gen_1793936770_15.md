# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC (Job #36)

**System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, DShot DMA Plan and Reference Netlist**

> **Scope note:** The H743 runs ArduPilot only. The four ESC channels each need their own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot run ArduPilot and also commutate four motors at 48 kHz. This document covers both domains and the bidirectional DShot link between them.
>
> All numbers below are design estimates built on stated assumptions. Validate them against your chosen MOSFET datasheets and against IR-camera burst testing before you release the board.

---

## 0. Executive Findings

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Loss per channel at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz, Tj ≈ 125 °C | **≈ 22.3 W** (18.6 W FET conduction, 1.9 W copper, 1.8 W switching and dead-time) |
| Board loss with all 4 channels at 80 A | **≈ 89 W** |
| Safe burst duration, all 4 at 80 A, starting at 40 °C ambient with prop-wash | **≈ 3 s** (to a 110 °C board hotspot) |
| Single-motor 80 A burst | ≈ 12–15 s |
| Realistic continuous rating (all 4 channels, 30×30 mm board) | **≈ 27 A per motor** |
| DShot600 bidirectional frame cycle | ≈ 90 µs, giving roughly 8 kHz theoretical headroom. ArduPilot at 400 Hz–2 kHz is fine. |
| DMA streams used, H743 (DMA1 + DMA2 = 16) | 11 of 16, with no forced sharing on motor timers |

**Key conclusion:** "80 A" is only defensible as a burst rating of about 3 s with all four channels loaded. On a true 20×20 mm "nano" footprint, the heat capacity is roughly 45% of the 30×30 figure, so the burst window shrinks to about 1.4 s. Recommendation: a **30.5×30.5 mm** board with 8-layer copper, or derate the label to **"55 A burst / 25 A continuous"** at 20×20 mm.
