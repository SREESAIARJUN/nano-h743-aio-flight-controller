## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Some parameters were not specified, so the design assumptions are fixed here. Every number below follows from them. Change any assumption and re-run the equations.

**Architecture clarification.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller (ArduPilot). Commutation is handled by four dedicated ESC MCUs, one per motor, running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on AT32F421 or STM32G071-class parts. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU. Running 4× FOC/6-step commutation at 48 kHz on the H743 alongside ArduPilot is possible in principle, but no supported firmware path exists. This split is the industry-standard partition.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** A nano-format board cannot dissipate the burst losses of four channels at 80 A in steady state. As shown in Section 3, the design is **thermal-capacitance limited**. The 80 A rating is valid only as a time-bounded burst, and the ESC firmware must enforce it with current limiting and thermal derating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current distribution in 6-step with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector:
- Phase X is the PWM-high phase. Its high-side FET conducts for D·T. Its low-side FET conducts (synchronous rectification) for (1−D)·T.
- Phase Y is the low phase. Its low-side FET is on for 100% of the sector.
- Phase Z floats and sees back-EMF sensing only.

In every sub-interval, the motor current I flows through exactly two FET channels in series. The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on) and self-consistency

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$
