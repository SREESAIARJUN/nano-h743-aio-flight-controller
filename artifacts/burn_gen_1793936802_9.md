# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #62): Power-Stage Model, DShot Allocation & Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Warning

**A note on architecture.** The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. In any practical 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU that does commutation and runs AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those four MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The model below follows that split.

**Baseline parameters.** The numbers are representative. Swap in datasheet values for your actual BOM. Every equation is kept parametric so you can do that.

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag (see §3):** 80 A burst on all four channels of a *nano* board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) is not thermally realisable for more than about 1–3 s. The numbers below show why. I recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm minimum footprint, or a separate ESC stack, if 80 A is a hard requirement.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During each 60° commutation sector, one leg's high-side and another leg's low-side carry the phase current I. Assume synchronous rectification, which is the AM32 default. During the PWM off-time, the freewheel current moves from the high-side FET to its complementary low-side FET instead of the body diode.

The result is that, apart from dead time, exactly two R_DS(on) are always in series with the current, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \big(2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{sh}\big)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

Inductor ripple ΔI adds RMS heating:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}/2)\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}\ \text{(worst case at } D=0.5)$$

For a typical 7-inch motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH line-line:

ΔI = 12.6 · 0.25 / (15 µH · 48 kHz) ≈ **4.4 A**

The correction term ΔI²/12 = 1.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent resistance (electro-thermal coupling)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot Z_{th,jb}(t)$$

These two equations are solved iteratively. They also give a thermal-runaway stability criterion:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{th} = I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1$$

At 80 A, 1 mΩ, and α = 0.0045, the left side is 0.0288 · R_th. Runaway therefore occurs if the effective R_th,ja exceeds about **35 K/W per FET**. A small FET in still air on a hot board can approach this, so this is a real failure mode, not a theoretical one.

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each leg conducts for 240°. The PWM'd 120° is shared between HS (fraction D) and LS (fraction 1−D). The worst-case single FET (the HS at D→1) sees:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}, \qquad P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS}$$

For thermal transients shorter than one electrical period (around 1 ms at speed), use the **instantaneous** value.

### 1.5 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS | 2·R_DS·I² | Copper / shunt (R_Cu ≈ 1.0 mΩ) | **P_cond/ESC** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 6.4 W | 19.2 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 6.4 W (±T_coef Cu) | 23.5 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 7.0 W | **25.6 W** |

The R_Cu figure is an estimate: phase pad, bus planes, and vias for about 8 mm of 4 × 2 oz layers. Copper has a temperature coefficient of 0.0039 /K, so copper loss climbs with temperature too.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period: the HS turns on against the LS diode/channel, then turns off.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

$$t_{r} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,on}} = 20\text{ ns},\quad t_{f} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{g,off}} = 10\text{ ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(30\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$
