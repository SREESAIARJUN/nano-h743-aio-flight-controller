# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth and Feasibility

Three constraints shape the whole design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sees the ESC only through the DShot line and the eRPM telemetry that comes back on it.
2. **"80A" on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 board is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** The model below shows that 80A on all four channels at once makes roughly 90 W of heat. That is survivable only for a few seconds. Treat the 80A figure as a per-channel transient rating and design the firmware current limit around it.
3. **All device parameters below are representative assumptions.** Typical values for 30–40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm MOSFETs are used. Before release, substitute the actual datasheet values for Rds(on)(T), Qg, Qoss, Qrr, and Rθ. Everything is written parametrically so you can re-run it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows through two phase legs.

- **PWM leg:** the high side conducts for duty D and the low side (synchronous rectifier) conducts for 1−D.
- **Static leg:** the low side conducts for the full sector.

The PWM leg therefore always contributes one Rds(on) in the current path, and the static leg contributes another. This gives:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The temperature dependence is modelled as:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad k \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

This gives α ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

**At 80A:**

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C (cold start) | 1.0 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.0010 = **12.8 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 2 · 6400 · 0.0016 = **20.5 W** |

**Per-device average.** Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, either as the static low side or as part of a PWM pair. The per-FET load at 125 °C is:

$$
P_{FET} \approx \frac{20.5}{6}\cdot\frac{6}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2} \approx 3.4\ \mathrm{W\ average}
$$

Peaks during the FET's conduction sector reach up to 10 W.

**Copper parasitics must be included.** On a nano board, the trace and plane resistance from the battery pad to each phase pad is typically 0.3–0.6 mΩ. At 80A that adds:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 6400 \times 0.0004 \approx 2.6\ \mathrm{W\ per\ channel}
$$

This is not negligible next to a ~1 mΩ FET.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Each PWM period contains one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off.

**(a) Voltage/current overlap**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

**(b) Output capacitance (Coss)**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery.** The low-side diode conducts during dead time, then the high side turns on into it.

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time diode conduction**

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive.** This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET. All 6 gates are counted as an upper bound.

$$
P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 6\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.115\ W}
$$

**Switching total:** about **1.8 W per channel**.

Two points follow from this:

- At 48 kHz, switching loss is under 10% of conduction loss at 80A. Raising f_sw for smoother current hurts very little at burst current.
- At cruise current (~15A), conduction loss drops by a factor of about 28, while P_VI only drops linearly. At low current, 48 kHz is the dominant loss term.

## 4. Total Loss Budget

| Term | Per channel (T_j = 125 °C) | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| Copper | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Switching (VI + Coss + Qrr) | 1.08 W | 4.3 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Gate drive | 0.12 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.9 W** | **≈ 99.6 W** |

Efficiency at the burst point, with output power ≈ 25.2 V × 80A × 4 ≈ 8 kW (the actual value is lower because duty is below 1): **η ≈ 98.8%**.

The efficiency is fine. The problem is that roughly 100 W must leave a 4–6 cm² board.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Steady-state network (per FET)

The heat path is a series chain:

$$
T_j \xrightarrow{R_{\theta JC}\approx1.2\,K/W} T_{case/pad} \xrightarrow{R_{\theta CB}\approx3\,K/W\ \text{(vias, solder)}} T_{board} \xrightarrow{R_{\theta BA}} T_{amb}
$$

**Board-to-ambient resistance.** Assume a 4-layer board with 2–3 oz copper, 20×20 mm (≈ 8 cm² of two-sided area), and propwash at h ≈ 60–100 W/m²K:

$$
R_{\theta BA} \approx \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{80\cdot 8\times10^{-4}} \approx 15.6\ \mathrm{K/W}
$$

This is for the whole board. Adding an aluminium heat-spreader or a 30.5 mm form factor brings it to roughly 5–8 K/W.

**Steady-state check at 100 W:**

$$
\Delta T_{BA} = 100 \cdot 15.6 \approx 1560\ \mathrm{K}
$$

This is physically impossible. **Continuous 4×80A cannot exist on this board.** Sustainable continuous dissipation at a board rise of ΔT = 70 K is:

$$
P_{max} = 70/15.6 \approx 4.5\ \mathrm{W\ total} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{4.5/4}{2\cdot1.4\text{m}+0.4\text{m}}} \approx 18\ \mathrm{A\ per\ channel}
$$

This range of roughly 15–25A continuous is typical of real 20×20 mm 4-in-1 ESCs.

### 5.2 Transient (burst) model: Foster/Cauer lumped

Thermal capacitance of the board (FR4 plus copper plus components):

- FR4: ~1.0 g × 1.1 J/gK ≈ 1.1 J/K
- Copper: ~2.5 g × 0.385 ≈ 1.0 J/K
- FETs, solder, and other components: ≈ 0.6 J/K
- **C_th,board ≈ 2.7 J/K**
