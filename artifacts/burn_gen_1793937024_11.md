# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #6): 80 A Burst, 48 kHz Switching, Bidirectional DShot600

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Three points affect the rest of the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only produces DShot600 frames and decodes the eRPM telemetry. The thermal and switching models below apply to the **ESC board**. The DMA and timer model applies to the **FC**.
2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is burst-only.** The model shows roughly 25–27 W of dissipation per channel at 80 A. On a 30×30 mm-class board, that limits bursts to about **1.5–2.5 s**. Continuous 80 A requires 5×6 mm FETs and at least a 30.5×30.5 mm board with a heatsink.
3. **MOSFET and driver parameters are representative assumptions.** Values are typical of 40 V, ~1.2 mΩ PDFN3.3 parts. Substitute the datasheet values for your chosen part. The equations remain valid.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.60 = **1.92 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current paths during one commutation step

During each 60° step:

- **Active (PWM) leg:** current alternates between HS (duty D) and LS (1−D, synchronous rectification). The sum is one FET conducting 100 % of the time.
- **Return leg:** the LS conducts 100 % of the time.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore equivalent to two FETs in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.92\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-FET average

Each FET is engaged for 120° of 360° electrical:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 4.1\ \text{W}$$

The worst case is a return-leg LS: 12.3 W for one third of the period.

The die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms) exceeds the electrical period above ~3 000 eRPM, so averaging is valid in flight. At stall or startup this is not true. The ESC firmware stall protection must handle that case.

### 2.4 Self-consistent junction temperature

Junction temperature and resistance depend on each other, so iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_b + \bar P_{FET}(T_j^{(k)})\,(R_{\theta JC}+R_{\theta CB})$$

This converges in 3–4 iterations. Section 4 gives the result.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the **active leg** hard-switches. The HS turns on and off once per PWM period.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½(25.2)(80)(32 ns)(48 k) | **1.55 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·600 ns·48 k | **1.84 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½(1 nF)(635)(48 k) | 0.015 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_drv·f_sw | 0.048 W* |

\* Gate-drive loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.55+1.84+0.05+0.02 = \mathbf{3.46\ W}$$
