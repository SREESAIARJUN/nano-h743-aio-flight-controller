## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Flags

The requirements contain two issues that need resolving before layout.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the flight controller sends DShot to *dedicated ESC MCUs*, which run the 48 kHz PWM and BEMF commutation. ArduPilot has no in-tree ESC firmware. This design therefore uses:
   - **FC:** STM32H743VIH6, running ArduPilot.
   - **ESC:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), running AM32. These handle 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot telemetry.

2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm nano board is not physically credible.** As Section 3 shows, the board cannot absorb 4×80 A for more than a few seconds, even on a 30×30 mm footprint. You need two things:
   - Define "80 A burst" precisely: per channel or total, for how many seconds, and at what duty cycle.
   - Accept a minimum 30.5×30.5 mm footprint, because twelve 5×6 mm 40 V MOSFETs plus gate drivers will not fit on 20×20.

   The model below assumes **80 A per channel, 6S, 2 s burst**, which is the most demanding interpretation.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

During any 60° commutation sector, the current path is:

- **PWM'd phase:** high-side FET on for duty D, then the complementary low-side FET for (1−D), via synchronous rectification.
- **Return phase:** low-side FET on 100% of the sector.

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous 6-step drive.

$$
R_{hot} = k_T R_{25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Copper loss.** Add the phase and bus copper. Assume about 0.3 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel, with 2 oz outer and internal pours stitched in parallel:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.0003 \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Coss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 0.05 W (dissipated in the driver) |
| **Total switching loss** | | **≈ 2.7 W** |

The overlap term scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise.

### 2.3 Totals at 80 A Burst

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction loss | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper loss | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching loss | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| **Total** | **23.8 W** | **≈ 95 W** |

Bus power is 4 × 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW, so ESC efficiency at burst is about 98.6%. Efficiency is not the problem. Heat density is.

### 2.4 Per-FET Distribution (Hottest Device)

Over one electrical cycle, each phase spends three equal intervals:

- **1/3 PWM-active:** the high-side FET carries I²R·D plus switching loss, and the low-side FET carries I²R·(1−D).
- **1/3 return:** the low-side FET carries I²R continuously, which is 9.6 W instantaneous.
- **1/3 floating:** near-zero loss.

The worst case is a **low-side FET**:

$$
P_{LS,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}(9.6) + \tfrac{1}{3}\left(9.6(1-D)\right)
$$

At D = 0.5 this gives about **4.8 W**. The high side at D = 1 gives about 3.2 W + 0.9 W ≈ 4.1 W.

The electrical period at typical eRPM is under 1 ms, while the junction thermal time constant is over 10 ms. Using cycle-averaged power is therefore valid.

## 3. Thermal Calculations

### 3.1 Junction Rise Above Local Board

| Element | Thermal resistance |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (bottom), 5×6 PQFN | ≈ 0.8 K/W *(assumed)* |
| Spreading resistance, case to local copper (8-layer, 2 oz, filled via array under the drain pad) | ≈ 8 K/W *(estimate; requires FEA or measurement)* |

$$
\Delta T_{j-board} \approx 4.8\ \text{W} \times 8.8\ \text{K/W} \approx \mathbf{42\ K}
$$

This local rise reaches steady state in roughly 1 s because the local thermal mass is small.
