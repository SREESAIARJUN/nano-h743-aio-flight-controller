## System Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architectural Correction: The H743 Does Not Commutate the Motors

A 4-in-1 ESC cannot be run directly from an STM32H743. Each sensorless BLDC channel needs 3 complementary PWM pairs (6 outputs) with hardware dead-time and break input, so four channels need 24 outputs. The H743 has only:

| Resource | Complementary pairs |
|---|---|
| TIM1 | 3 (+CH4 without complement) |
| TIM8 | 3 |
| HRTIM (5 units × 2) | ~5 usable pairs, no BLDC comparator/zero-cross integration |

That is not enough. The only coherent architecture, and the one assumed in the rest of this document, is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                                  └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per channel
```

**Design baseline (stated assumptions, to be replaced with datasheet values at part selection):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max |
| Burst current | 80 A per channel (phase current, trapezoidal) |
| f_sw | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary (synchronous rectification) |
| MOSFET (generic 40 V, 3.3×3.3 DFN) | R_ds(on)@25 °C = 1.0 mΩ, temp coeff ×1.6 @125 °C, Q_g = 60 nC, Q_rr = 40 nC, Q_oss = 50 nC, R_θJC = 1.2 K/W |
| Gate driver | 3-phase half-bridge with bootstrap (FD6288Q class), 10 V gate |
| Effective t_r + t_f | 35 ns (driver plus gate-resistor limited) |
| Dead time | 100 ns per edge |
| Board | 30.5 mm mount, ~36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz power inner layers |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous)

### 1.1 Current path analysis
In each 60° commutation sector, one phase's high side PWMs with duty *D*, another phase's low side is held fully on, and the third phase floats.

Within a sector:
- PWM-on phase: current flows through HS_x for fraction *D* and through LS_x (synchronous freewheel) for fraction (1−D).
- Static phase: current flows continuously through LS_y.

Each leg spends 120° as the "PWM leg" and 120° as the "static low leg" per electrical cycle. The time-averaged conduction loss per leg is:

$$
P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
\qquad
P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

$$
P_{leg} = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of } D\text{)}
$$

Summing three legs gives the per-channel loss:

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)}
$$

This is physically consistent: the current always flows through exactly two switches.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.6 mΩ.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Copper and interconnect loss
- Battery pads to bus: ~0.3 mΩ shared.
- Phase pours: ~0.25 mΩ per phase, with 2 phases conducting.

$$
P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

Shared bus loss uses I_bus = D·I_phase. With 4 channels at D≈0.95: I_bus ≈ 304 A, so P_bus ≈ 304² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **27.7 W**. This is a serious finding: **the battery entry path is the single largest hotspot** on a nano AIO at this current.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high side hard-switches. The low-side complement switches softly (ZVS after its body diode conducts during dead time).

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 35\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.69\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, at HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 40\text{nC} \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}
$$
