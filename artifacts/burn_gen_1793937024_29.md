## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two points need to be settled before the math, because they change every result below.

**A. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation, and running four 48 kHz six-step or FOC loops beside the flight stack is poor practice. The architecture is therefore:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600 ×4 (on-board traces)──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 on AT32F421K8U7)
                                                              └─► FD6288Q 3-ph gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per channel
```

The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU PWM frequency. DShot600 is an on-board digital link.

**B. "80 A burst" is defined here as 80 A per channel for no more than about 2 s, on a single channel or with a total-current cap.** Section 3 shows that four simultaneous 80 A channels cannot be sustained on a nano-format board (20×20 or 30×30 mm). Expect marketing pressure on this point and resist it with the numbers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step current path

With trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM on the active leg), motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

- the PWM leg, where the high side conducts for D and the low side for 1−D, and
- the low side of the return leg, which conducts continuously for 120°.

The instantaneous conduction loss per channel is therefore:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$
