# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz gate PWM.** In every production AIO of this class, each motor bridge (or the whole 4-in-1) is run by dedicated ESC MCUs, such as AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 only emits DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. Putting 4× 3-phase commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection, and ArduPilot on one H743 is not supported by ArduPilot. The model below therefore has two domains:
   - the **ESC domain**: 48 kHz switching, conduction loss, and thermal behavior;
   - the **FC domain**: DShot timers and DMA.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 30.5×30.5) board is a burst figure, not a continuous rating.** The math below shows it lasts only a few seconds. Treat any "80 A" label as a 1–5 s rating with a defined starting temperature.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths
With 6-step trapezoidal drive and complementary PWM, two phases conduct at any instant.

- **PWM on-time (D):** high-side of phase A and low-side of phase B carry I_ph.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** synchronous low-side of phase A and low-side of phase B carry I_ph.

In both states, exactly **two FET channels** carry I_ph. The total is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (electro-thermal coupling)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.81 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per ESC | P_cond, 4 ESCs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 61.4 W |
| 110 °C | 1.81 mΩ | **23.2 W** | **92.7 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution
Over one electrical revolution, each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the time.

- High-side FETs conduct only during D.
- Low-side FETs conduct during both their own commutation slots and freewheeling.
- At a typical high-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.9), loss spreads approximately evenly. Each FET sees about 23.2 / 6 ≈ **3.9 W average**.

**Stall or low-RPM worst case:** a single FET pair can hold the full 11.6 W each for tens of milliseconds. Desync protection must catch this.
