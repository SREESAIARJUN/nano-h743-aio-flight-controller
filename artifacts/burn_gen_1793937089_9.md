## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Several points in the brief need clarification before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600 frames. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU that runs the 48 kHz commutation PWM. This design uses an AT32F421K8 running AM32, chosen because AM32 supports 48 kHz PWM and EDT/BDShot telemetry. The 48 kHz loss model therefore applies to the ESC power stage, not the FC.
2. **Interpretation of "80 A burst."** I model it as 80 A RMS phase-path current on one channel for a time-limited burst. I also give the all-4-channels-simultaneous case, which is the binding constraint.
3. **Form factor.** True nano boards (20×20 mm) cannot dissipate the losses derived below for any useful duration. The model is therefore built on a 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board. Section 2.6 shows the 20×20 numbers so the tradeoff is explicit.
4. **FET parameters are placeholders.** I use a representative 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 class FET. Replace these with the selected part's values.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current-path topology

In trapezoidal six-step commutation, each 60° electrical sector has:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET (HS) is on for duty D. The complementary low-side FET (LS) is on for 1−D (synchronous freewheel).
- **Return leg:** the LS is fully on.
- **Third leg:** floating, used for BEMF sensing.

Over 360°, each leg spends:
- 120° as the PWM leg,
- 120° as the return leg,
- 120° floating.

### 1.2 Per-device conduction loss

With R_j = k_T · R_ds(on),25 and phase current I:

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_j \cdot \frac{D}{3}$$

$$P_{LS} = I^2 R_j \cdot \left(\frac{1-D}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right) = I^2 R_j \cdot \frac{2-D}{3}$$

Summing over 3 legs gives the per-motor total:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 3\,(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\,I^2 R_j \quad\text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

This is physically intuitive: exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the motor current at every instant.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Condition | R_j | Per FET (HS, D→1) | Per FET (LS, D→1) | **Per motor** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.5 mΩ | 3.2 W | 3.2 W | **19.2 W** |
| Hot (125 °C) | 2.4 mΩ | 5.12 W | 5.12 W | **30.7 W** |

At D ≈ 0.5 the distribution shifts. HS falls to 2.56 W and LS rises to 7.68 W hot, so **the LS FET is the hottest device at part throttle with high current.** This is typical during punch-outs from hover.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

The model for copper resistance is:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho_{Cu}(T)\cdot \frac{L}{w\,t\,n_{layers}}$$

with ρ = 1.72 µΩ·cm at 20 °C and +0.393 %/K.

For the phase path from the FET to the motor pad (L ≈ 12 mm, w ≈ 4 mm, 70 µm, 4 paralleled layers): R ≈ 0.18 mΩ.

The battery bus is shared across all four motors. With two paths in series plus a bus share of about 0.4 mΩ effective:

$$P_{Cu,motor} \approx 80^2 \times (2\times0.18 + 0.1)\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{2.9\ W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Losses are evaluated per motor, with I = 80 A and V = 25.2 V.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (LS, two transitions per cycle)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw}\times2 = 45\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$
