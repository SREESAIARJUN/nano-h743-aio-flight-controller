# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Upfront Flags

I have no prior design files for "Job #32," so everything below rests on stated assumptions. Swap in your actual BOM values. The equations are parameterized for that purpose.

**Flag 1: Architecture.** The STM32H743 should be the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU. The 48 kHz PWM and commutation run on four dedicated ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. The model below follows that split.

**Flag 2: Feasibility.** 80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm nano board is not thermally sustainable. It is viable only as a short burst. Section 2.6 quantifies the time limit, and the result should drive the marketing spec. I'd recommend labeling it "80 A burst (≤ ~1–2 s), 35–45 A continuous with airflow." Do not label it "80 A."

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 DFN, R_DS(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ @ V_GS = 10 V |

## 2. Thermal / Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss with temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

With complementary 6-step drive, current always flows through exactly two FETs in series (one high-side, one low-side, or their synchronous partner). That holds regardless of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

Phase-current ripple adds a small term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}
$$

Using L_ph = 10 µH and D = 0.5 at worst case:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2·0.25 / (10 µ·48 k) ≈ **13.1 A**
- Ripple term: 13.1²/12 ≈ 14.3 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

Self-consistent junction temperature (iterate T_j):

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond (W) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 |
| 100 | 1.34 | 17.1 |
| 125 | 1.45 | 18.6 |

Design value at T_j = 125 °C: **P_cond ≈ 18.6 W per motor** (≈ 9.3 W per conducting FET).

### 2.2 Switching (V–I overlap) loss

Only the hard-switched FET in the active leg incurs this loss:

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Capacitive, reverse-recovery, dead-time, and gate losses

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Coss | Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 50n·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200n·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2·60n·10·48k = **0.06 W** (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |

### 2.4 Total at 80 A burst

$$
P_{motor} \approx 18.6 + 0.97 + 0.05 + 0.06 + 0.61 + 0.06 \approx \mathbf{20.4\ W}
$$

$$
P_{4\text{-}in\text{-}1} \approx \mathbf{81.5\ W} \quad (+\ \sim 2\text{–}4\ \text{W copper/shunt/connector } I^2R)
$$

- **Efficiency at 80 A × 22.2 V (1776 W per motor):** ≈ 98.9 %.
- **Key insight:** conduction dominates at about 91 %. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per motor. Lowering R_DS(on), or paralleling FETs, is the lever that matters.

### 2.5 Junction temperature rise (per hot FET)

The hottest FET is the high-side device carrying conduction plus switching plus dead-time loss:

$$
P_{FET,max} \approx 9.3 + 0.97 + 0.3 \approx 10.6\ \text{W}
$$

Thermal path:

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta,solder+via})
$$

With R_θJC = 1.2 K/W and the solder plus via array at about 2.5 K/W, the junction sits **ΔT_j-pcb ≈ 39 K above the local copper**. So with T_j,max = 125 °C, the board must stay **≤ ~85 °C** under each FET.

### 2.6 Burst duration (lumped thermal capacitance)

The board's heat capacity determines how long the burst can last:

$$
t_{burst} = \frac{C_{th}\,(T_{pcb,max} - T_{pcb,0})}{P_{total} - (T_{pcb}-T_{amb})/R_{\theta,conv}}
$$

Nano board estimate:
- 6-layer, 2 oz, about 4 g copper and FR4, plus 24 FETs and an optional 2 g aluminum spreader.
- C_th ≈ 3.5–5 J/K.
- R_θ,conv with prop wash ≈ 4 K/W, which is negligible during the burst.

From T_pcb,0 = 45 °C to 85 °C (ΔT = 40 K):

$$
t_{burst} \approx \frac{4 \cdot 40}{81.5 - 10} \approx \mathbf{2.2\ s}\quad (\text{all four motors at 80 A})
$$

This is optimistic because lateral spreading resistance creates hot spots. **Validate with IR thermography and a thermocouple under a phase FET.** Steady-state continuous capability is roughly:

$$
P_{cont} = \frac{85-45}{4} \approx 10\ \text{W total} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{2.5}{2 \cdot 1.3\text{m}}} \approx \mathbf{31\ A/motor}
$$

That figure is with prop-wash cooling. A heatsink or a larger board raises it.

### 2.7 Firmware protection to enforce the burst limit

- **ESC side:** AM32 temperature limit at 85 °C board-NTC with current limit derating. Use a burst-integral (I²t) limit:

$$
\int (I^2 - I_{cont}^2)\,dt \le (80^2 - 31^2)\cdot 2\ \text{s} \approx 10{,}900\ \text{A}^2\text{s}
$$

- **FC side:** ArduPilot `BATT_MAX_AMPS` / ESC-telemetry-based throttle limiting as a backstop.

## 3. Bidirectional DShot600 Timer and DMA Model

### 3.1 Bit timing

The H743 runs at SYSCLK 480 MHz. TIM1 is on APB2, so the timer clock f_TIM = 240 MHz.

ArduPilot uses 8 ticks per bit by default (bit-0 = 3 ticks, bit-1 = 6 ticks, approximately):

$$
\text{PSC}+1 = \frac{f_{TIM}}{600\text{k}\cdot 8} = \frac{240\text{M}}{4.8\text{M}} = 50 \;\Rightarrow\; \text{exact, 0 \% baud error}
$$

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Bit period | 1.667 µs |
| Frame (16 bits) | 26.7 µs |
| Telemetry turnaround | ~25–30 µs |
| eRPM reply | 21-bit GCR at 5/4 × 600 k = 750 kbit/s → 28 µs |
| Full transaction | ≈ 85 µs |
| Max loop rate | well above 2 kHz; ArduPilot schedules at loop rate (e.g. 1–2 kHz) or `SERVO_DSHOT_RATE` |

### 3.2 DMA requirements

**Output path:**
- One DMA stream on **TIMx_UP**, writing through **TIMx_DMAR** (burst mode).
