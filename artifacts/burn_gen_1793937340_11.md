## 3. Conduction-Loss Model

### 3.1 Temperature-corrected on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045\times 85) = 1.2 \times 1.383 \approx 1.66\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 3.2 Per-motor conduction loss

In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli complementary PWM), exactly two FETs carry the phase current at any instant:
- one high-side FET, or its complementary low-side during the off-time;
- one low-side FET on the return phase.

So the loss is:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}$$

For FOC/sinusoidal drive, use instead $P_{cond} = 3\,I_{rms,ph}^2 R_{DS}$ with $I_{rms,ph} = I_{pk}/\sqrt2$.

### 3.3 Per-FET instantaneous vs. average loss

- **Instantaneous loss** in a conducting FET: $I^2 R_{DS} = 10.6$ W.
- **Average loss** per FET: each FET conducts for ~1/3 of an electrical cycle, so the average is ≈ 21.2 / 6 ≈ 3.5 W.

The electrical period is short compared with the die thermal time constant (~1–10 ms). At 1.5 kHz electrical frequency (≈ 26k rpm on a 14-pole motor), the die effectively sees the average loss plus a small ripple. At low rpm under high load (stall, punch-out from hover), the die sees closer to the full 10.6 W per FET. This is the worst case.

## 4. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 4.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

The in-circuit edge time comes from the Miller plateau:

$$t \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g}, \qquad I_g = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_g}$$

Example: with Q_gd = 8 nC and I_g ≈ 0.5 A, t ≈ 16 ns, consistent with the assumption above.
