# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Two points need settling before the math, because they change where each loss and timing constraint lives.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** On a 4-in-1, each ESC channel has its own MCU (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU drives the gate driver at 48 kHz.
   - The H743 (ArduPilot) only emits DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.
   - The switching-loss model below therefore belongs to the ESC firmware and power stage. The DMA model belongs to the FC.
2. **"80 A burst" is interpreted as 80 A per channel**, with all four channels loaded simultaneously as the worst case. The thermal section shows this is a seconds-scale transient on a nano-format board, not a continuous rating.

**Baseline parameters.** These are representative values. Replace them with datasheet values from your actual BOM, since every result scales directly from them.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The commutation is 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous (complementary) PWM:
- During any commutation step, current flows through two conducting devices.
- One device is the low-side FET of the "sink" phase, which is on continuously.
- The other is either the high-side FET (during D·T_sw) or the complementary low-side FET (during (1−D)·T_sw) of the PWM'd phase, apart from dead time.

So, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left( 2\,k_T\,R_{ds(on)} + R_{Cu} \right)
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \cdot (2 \cdot 1.5 \cdot 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega + 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = 6400 \cdot 3.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.4\ W}
$$

Of this, 19.2 W is in silicon and 3.2 W is in copper.

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each of the six FETs conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle.

- **Instantaneous dissipation while conducting:** I²·k_T·R_ds = 9.6 W.
- **Electrical-cycle average for the continuously-on low side:** 9.6 / 3 = 3.2 W.
- **Worst device:** the PWM'd leg's high or low side carries extra switching and dead-time loss (Section 2).

### 1.3 Averaging validity

The electrical frequency is f_e = (poles/2) · RPM / 60.

- For a 14-pole motor at 30,000 rpm: f_e = 3.5 kHz, so the 120° on-window is about 95 µs.
- The die-level thermal time constant of a DFN3.3 FET is about 1–5 ms, so cycle averaging is valid.
- **Exception:** at stall, start-up, or desync, f_e approaches 0. One FET then carries the full 9.6 W or more continuously, and Z_th(t) must be used instead of averages. This is the most dangerous operating case on small AIOs.
