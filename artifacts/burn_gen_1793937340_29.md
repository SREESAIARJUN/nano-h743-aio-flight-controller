## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path
With 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly **one high-side and one low-side channel**:

- The PWM leg alternates between its HS FET (on-time, D) and its LS FET (off-time, 1−D).
- The fixed leg's LS FET is on continuously.

The series resistance is therefore always ≈ 2·R_ds, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu,path}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left(\frac{T_j + 273}{298}\right)^{2.3}$$

| T_j (°C) | Factor | R_ds (mΩ) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 1.20 |
| 100 | 1.68 | 2.02 |
| 125 | 1.89 | 2.27 |

The factor at 125 °C is approximately 1.9. Many datasheets show 1.6–1.8, so use your datasheet's normalized R_ds curve.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction at I_ph = 80 A
- FETs at T_j = 125 °C: 6400 × 2 × 2.27 mΩ = **29.1 W**
- Copper path (phase trace + bus + via fields), estimated at 0.4 mΩ: 6400 × 0.4 mΩ = **2.6 W**
- **P_cond,ch ≈ 31.7 W.** All four channels at burst: **≈ 127 W.**

### 1.4 Per-FET share
Over one electrical revolution, each FET carries current for 120°, i.e. 1/3 of the cycle. The die thermal time constant (ms) is longer than the commutation interval above roughly 2 krpm electrical, so averaging is valid:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = \tfrac{1}{3}(6400)(2.27\,\text{m}\Omega) = 4.84\ \text{W}$$
