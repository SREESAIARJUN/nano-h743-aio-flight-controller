## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** drive the power stage directly. Bidirectional DShot is a protocol between the flight controller and separate ESC MCUs. Each ESC channel has its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and returns eRPM telemetry.

```
VBAT (6S, 25.2 V max)
   │
   ├─► 4× [ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421, AM32 FW)] ─► 3-ph gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET ─► Motor
   │            ▲  │ DShot600 bidir (single wire, open-drain-ish, pulled up)
   │            │  ▼
   └─► Buck 5V/3V3 ─► STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ── TIM3/TIM5 + DMA ── M1..M4
```

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current paths

Commutation is trapezoidal (6-step) with synchronous rectification. In every PWM state, exactly one high-side or low-side FET in the PWM leg conducts, plus one low-side FET in the return leg.

- **ON interval (D):** HS_a and LS_b conduct.
- **OFF interval (1−D):** LS_a (synchronous rectifier) and LS_b conduct.

This means two FET channel resistances are always in series with the motor current, independent of duty cycle. The only exception is the dead time, which Section 3.3 covers separately.

### 2.2 Equation

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 4.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W}
$$

The heat splits as follows:
- The return-leg LS FET carries the full 14.4 W.
- HS_a and LS_a share the other 14.4 W in proportions D and (1−D).
- The **worst-case single FET is 14.4 W**. This loss lands on the low-side FETs, so the layout must give them the best thermal path.

### 2.3 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, critical at 80 A)

Copper resistance per square is

$$
R_{\square} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}}{70\,\mu m} = 0.246\ \text{m}\Omega/\square
$$

- **Phase path:** pad to FET to pad, about 3 squares, paralleled across 3 layers. That gives about 0.25 mΩ, so P = 80² × 0.25 m ≈ **1.6 W per channel**.
- **Battery bus:** carries 320 A total, so a 0.1 mΩ effective resistance dissipates about **10 W**.
  - Use solid pours on all inner planes.
  - Use stitched vias, with at least 1 A per 0.3 mm via as a rule of thumb.
  - Use direct-solder XT60 pigtail pads.
- **Current sense:** a 0.1 mΩ shunt at 320 A dissipates 10.2 W and produces 32 mV.
  - **Recommendation:** use per-channel 0.25 mΩ low-side sensing inside the ESC (1.6 W each), or a coreless Hall sensor on the battery lead.
  - Report summed current to the FC via ESC telemetry rather than a main shunt.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The return leg is static within each 60° electrical sector. All losses below are per channel.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, recovered at HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = 0.614\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance (hard turn-on of the HS)

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eq} V^2 f = 0.5\times1\text{n}\times635\times48\text{k} = 0.015\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

With 2 switching FETs per channel (HS_a + LS_a) at 10 V:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f = 2\times40\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.038\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.6 Summary

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 28.80 W | 115.2 W |
| Phase copper | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| V-I overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.5 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.06 W | 0.25 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |

| **Total** | **~32 W** | **~138 W** |
