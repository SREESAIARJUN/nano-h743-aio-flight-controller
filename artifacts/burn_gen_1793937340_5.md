## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility

Two points need clarifying before the derivation, because they drive everything else.

**1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly.** Bi-directional DShot implies a digital link between the flight controller and *separate* ESC microcontrollers, each running a commutation firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32). The topology is therefore:

```
[STM32H743 FC] --DShot600 bidir (4 lines)--> [4x ESC MCU: STM32G071 / AT32F421, AM32]
                                                   |
                                          [3-phase gate driver] --> [6x MOSFET] --> Motor
```

**2. "Nano" and "80 A burst per channel" are in tension.**
- 4 × 80 A from a 20×20 mm board is not physically achievable. Copper current capacity and thermal mass both rule it out.
- This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** with a stacked 20×20 or 30.5 mm FC.
- "80 A burst" is defined here as **≤ 2 s, with airflow**. Section 3 shows why that limit is a physics constraint rather than a conservative choice.

## 1. Power Stage Operating Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Burst phase current | I_ph | 80 A (per motor) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET | — | 40 V N-ch, PDFN 5×6 class |
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R₀ | 0.90 mΩ |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | ≈ 0.5 %/K, so ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 60 nC |
| Reverse recovery charge | Q_rr | 40 nC |
| Output charge | Q_oss | 50 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Gate drive voltage | V_drv | 10 V |
| Effective rise + fall time at 80 A | t_r + t_f | 40 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns (per edge) |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, complementary (synchronous) PWM |

**Why 40 V parts on 6S:** a 30 V part leaves less than 5 V margin against regenerative braking spikes. 40 V is the minimum safe rating.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step operation, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The **PWM phase** alternates its high-side FET (duty D) with its low-side FET (duty 1−D, synchronous rectification).
- The **return phase** holds its low-side FET on for 100 % of the sector.

The motor current therefore always passes through exactly two channels in series.

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS} \right] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

The result is **independent of D** when all FETs are identical.

**Temperature correction:**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_0\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 0.90 × 1.5 = **1.35 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

**PCB copper loss.** Assume a 0.3 mΩ effective path (bus + phase pad + motor pad) in 4 oz inner layers:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM phase hard-switches. Its low-side FET turns on at near zero voltage because the body diode is already conducting after dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap, high side:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$
