## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Finding

**Partitioning.** ArduPilot on the STM32H743 does not commutate MOSFETs. The defensible architecture is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIH6 (400 MHz) | ArduPilot ChibiOS | Attitude control, sends bi-directional DShot600 |
| ESC × 4 | AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) per motor | AM32 | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |
| Power stage × 4 | 6 × N-MOSFET + 3-phase gate driver | n/a | 12 MOSFETs total |

**Key finding.** "80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is only achievable for about 1–3 seconds if all four channels burst at once. The board's heat capacity, not the die, is the limit. This is quantified in §3.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation mode.** Six-step trapezoidal control with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32 default. In each 60° sector:

- One leg PWMs: the high-side conducts for D, and the complementary low-side for (1 − D).
- One low-side is held fully on.

Because synchronous rectification gives a complementary path, there are always exactly two MOSFETs in series with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

**Temperature dependence.**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**Self-consistent iteration.** R_DS and T_j depend on each other, so solve iteratively. From §3, T_j ≈ 115–125 °C at the end of a burst:

$$R_{DS}(125^\circ C) = 1.2 \times 1.6 = 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W}$$

**Copper loss.**

$$P_{cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 3.8\ \text{W}$$

**Per-device average.** Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the loss spreads across all 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 24.6 / 6 \approx 4.1\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Per PWM period, one hard-switched transition pair occurs on the PWM leg.

**V–I overlap.**

$$E_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 22\,\text{ns} = 22.2\ \mu\text{J}$$

**Reverse recovery.** This loss lands in the high-side FET at turn-on.

$$E_{rr} \approx V_{bus}\, Q_{rr} = 25.2 \times 30\,\text{nC} = 0.76\ \mu\text{J}$$

**Output capacitance.**

$$E_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 V_{bus} Q_{oss} = 0.32\ \mu\text{J}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction.** There are two dead-time intervals per period.

$$E_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I_{ph}\, t_d = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\text{ns} = 12.8\ \mu\text{J}$$

**Total switching loss.**

$$P_{sw} = f_{sw}\sum E = 48\,\text{kHz} \times 36.1\ \mu\text{J} = \mathbf{1.73\ W}$$

**Gate drive loss.** This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

> Dead time accounts for about 35% of switching loss. Tightening t_d from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Verify against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch before doing so.

### Loss Summary at 80 A Burst

| Component | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| MOSFET conduction (hot) | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| PCB copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching | 1.73 W | 6.9 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 30.2 W** | **≈ 121 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is about 25.2 V × 0.95 × 80 A ≈ 1.9 kW per channel.
