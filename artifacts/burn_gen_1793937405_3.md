# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #52): Power-Stage Model, Bidirectional DShot DMA Plan, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These three findings shape the rest of the design.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz switching PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 sends DShot600 commands. Each motor's 48 kHz commutation PWM comes from a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. This document models it that way. If you intend the H743 to commutate the motors directly, ArduPilot cannot do that, and the architecture changes completely.

2. **Specifying "80 A burst" requires two clarifications:**
   - Whether it is **phase current or battery current**. Battery current equals duty × phase current.
   - **How long** the burst lasts.

   The math below assumes 80 A phase current per motor, all four motors at once, and a 6S supply.

3. **On a 20 × 20 mm "nano" board, 4 × 80 A is thermally infeasible beyond about 1 second.**
   - The numbers in §3 show about 110 W of dissipation against about 2.5 J/K of board heat capacity.
   - The options are:
     - Move to a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board.
     - Derate to about 45 A continuous / 60 A for 2 seconds.
     - Accept a roughly 1-second single-event burst with hard current limiting.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and complementary PWM, phase current always flows through exactly two FETs:

- **PWM on:** HS(a) + LS(b)
- **PWM off:** LS(a) acting as the synchronous rectifier + LS(b)

**Total conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

With α ≈ 0.0053 K⁻¹, which gives k_T ≈ 1.5 at 120 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.8\text{ m}\Omega) = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Loss per individual FET.** Averaged over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for one third of the time:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R = 3.84\text{ W}$$

The duty-dependent split between HS and LS is:

- P_HS = D·I²R (during its 120° window)
- P_LS,sync = (1 − D)·I²R

At low duty, the low-side FETs run hottest.

**Copper and solder loss:**

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

**Thermal runaway check.** R_DS(on) rises with temperature, which raises loss, which raises temperature. The coupled equation is:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{th}\,[P_{sw} + 2I^2R_{25}(1+\alpha(T_j-25))]$$

It converges only if:

$$R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}\alpha < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; R_{th} < 1/(2\cdot6400\cdot1.2\text{e-}3\cdot0.0053)=12.3\text{ K/W}$$

The nano board's steady-state R_th from board to air is about 12.5 K/W (see §3). **The design therefore sits at the edge of thermal runaway in steady state.** This is a second reason the 80 A rating can only be a burst rating.
