# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture (Job #51)

**Scope.** This covers the conduction and switching loss models at an 80 A per-motor burst and 48 kHz PWM, a transient thermal model, the ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan on the H743, and reference netlists.

**Assumptions.** The component parameters below are representative placeholders. Before release, replace them with values from the datasheets of the parts you actually procure. Every equation is written so you can substitute directly.

## 0. Architecture Decision

On a "4-in-1 ESC," the H743 does not commutate the motors. The partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (LQFP100), 480 MHz | ArduPilot. Generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | STM32G071 or AT32F421 | AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. 6-step or FOC commutation at 48 kHz. |
| Gate drivers (×4) | 3-phase half-bridge driver with bootstrap (FD6288Q class) | 6 FETs per motor |
| Power stage | 24 × N-MOSFET, 40 V (for 6S) | 4 motors × 3 legs × 2 FETs |

Reason for the split: a single H743 cannot run four sensorless 48 kHz commutation loops alongside ArduPilot's scheduler with acceptable determinism. It also runs short of advanced-control timers, which are the ones with complementary outputs and dead-time (TIM1 and TIM8 only, giving 2 of the 4 motors needed).

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor (320 A aggregate) on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is achievable only as a time-limited burst. Section 3 quantifies the limit at about 3–4 s.
