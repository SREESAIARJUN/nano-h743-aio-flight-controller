# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture Model

## 0. Assumptions and Constraints

I don't have the Job #14 specification, so the values below are labeled assumptions. Replace them with your BOM data. Three issues need resolving before the math matters.

**1. The H743 cannot also be the ESC controller.**
ArduPilot on the H743 cannot also run four 48 kHz sensorless FOC/6-step commutation loops with BEMF zero-cross detection. The standard architecture, and the one modeled here, is:
- STM32H743 runs ArduPilot.
- Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32).
- The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back.

**2. "80A burst" on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not physically credible.**
- With four channels at 80 A, the battery input must carry up to 320 A peak.
- The board dissipates about 85 W (derived below).
- On a 20×20 mm board this works only for sub-second bursts, and pad, trace and connector current density then dominates.

I model the electrical/thermal physics honestly and recommend 30.5×30.5 mm minimum for an 80 A burst rating. Alternatively, keep 20×20 and redefine the burst as a single channel at 80 A.

**3. "80 A" is interpreted as motor phase current per channel** at near-full duty, the worst case for conduction loss.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.5) = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step bridge with synchronous rectification

In any 120° commutation sector, current flows through exactly two conducting devices at every instant. These are either:
- the PWM'd high-side FET plus the static low-side FET, or
- during off-time, the synchronous-rectifying low-side FET plus the static low-side FET.

The total bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Per-FET RMS current at D → 1, where each FET conducts 1/3 of the electrical period:

$$I_{rms,FET} = I_{ph}\sqrt{1/3} = 80 \times 0.577 = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 R = 2133 \times 1.5\text{m} = 3.20\ \text{W}$$

**Per-channel bridge loss:** P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**

At partial duty D, the loss redistributes rather than changing in total:
- PWM'd high-side FET: P = (D/3) I² R
- Its complementary low-side FET: P = ((1−D)/3) I² R

### 1.3 Additional resistive paths per channel (often forgotten)

| Element | Assumed R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ) | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Low-side shunt (if used, 0.25 mΩ) | 0.25 mΩ (I_rms) | ~0.5–1.6 W |
| Solder/pad interfaces | ~0.1 mΩ | 0.6 W |

**Realistic per-channel conduction total: about 22 W.**

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In each sector only one half-bridge is PWM-switched. It undergoes one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per PWM period.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The low-side body diode conducts during dead time, then the high-side FET turns on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Add the induced turn-on loss in the high-side FET, roughly the same magnitude again: about **0.06 W**.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total per channel

$$P_{sw} \approx 1.45 + 0.12 + 0.02 + 0.31 = \mathbf{1.9\ W}\ \text{(FETs)} + 0.06\ \text{W (driver)}$$

At true 100% duty, switching loss vanishes. The worst case is high phase current at high but not full duty, which occurs during punch-outs and current-limited acceleration. Use the full 1.9 W for burst design.

**Sensitivity:** P_VI scales linearly with t_r + t_f. If the gate driver is a weak FD6288-class part, which is common, t_r + t_f approaches 60–80 ns and P_VI rises to 3–4 W per channel. Driver selection matters more than f_sw here.
