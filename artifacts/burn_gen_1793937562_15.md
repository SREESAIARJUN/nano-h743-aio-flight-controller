## 0. Architectural Correction (Read First)

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It does not commutate the motors. A correct AIO architecture looks like this:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (4 lines)──► ESC MCU(s) (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class)
                                                    e.g. 4× AT32F421 or 4× STM32G071
                                                    ──► gate drivers ──► 24× N-MOSFET (4×3 half-bridges)
```

Two consequences follow from this:

- **48 kHz PWM** is an *ESC firmware* parameter (for example, AM32 `PWM frequency = 48 kHz`). It is not an H743 timer setting.
- The H743 timer and DMA work covers only DShot generation and eRPM telemetry capture.

**Feasibility flag.** "80 A burst × 4" on a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) dissipates roughly 85 W of heat (see §3). The board can survive that only as a seconds-long burst. Continuous rating will be about 30–40 A per motor with prop-wash cooling. §3.4 quantifies this so the datasheet claim can be stated honestly.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel = one 3-phase bridge)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with synchronous complementary PWM. At any instant, motor current flows through **exactly two MOSFET channels in series**: the active-leg high-side (or its synchronous low-side during off-time) plus the return-leg low-side. Therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_J)
$$

The resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_J-25)]
$$

Using a hot design point of T_J = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.0\,(1+0.005\cdot100) = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Electrothermal feedback.** Conduction loss and temperature depend on each other, which creates a fixed point. If T_J is driven by P through an effective resistance R_θ,eff:

$$
T_J = T_a + R_{\theta,eff}\cdot 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_J-25)]
$$

Solving for T_J:

$$
T_J = \frac{T_a + k(1-25\alpha)}{1 - k\alpha}, \qquad k = 2I^2R_{25}R_{\theta,eff}
$$

Thermal runaway occurs when kα ≥ 1. At 80 A with R_25 = 1 mΩ, this happens at R_θ,eff ≥ 15.6 °C/W, so it is not the limiting case here. It does matter for small FETs.

**PCB copper and shunt.** These are added in series and are not negligible. For 4 mΩ total path at 70 °C, use about 0.6 mΩ trace plus 0.25 mΩ shunt per channel:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot 0.85\,\text{m}\Omega = 5.4\ \text{W}
$$

**Design rule:** keep the phase path below 0.5 mΩ. This requires 2 oz outer layers plus paralleled inner planes.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

In 6-step, only **one leg PWM-switches** at a time. That leg hard-switches the full current once on and once off per period.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot40\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery (body diode of the synchronous FET):**

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 0.05\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive (two FETs per switching leg).** This is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}
$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching terms scale linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz, P_sw roughly doubles to about 4.7 W. At 24 kHz it is about 1.2 W. The 48 kHz point is a good compromise for low-inductance racing motors: current ripple scales as ΔI ≈ V·D(1−D)/(L·f).

### 2.3 Per-Channel and Board Totals (80 A, T_J = 125 °C)

| Loss | Per ESC channel | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 5.4 W | 21.6 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.4 W | 9.5 W |
| **Total** | **27.0 W** | **107.9 W** |

The 108 W total assumes all four motors at 80 A simultaneously, which is the worst case. A realistic punch-out has an asymmetric mix, so about 85 W is a reasonable design burst figure.

### 2.4 Per-FET Distribution

Each high-side FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. Peak dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 9.6 W. The average over a cycle is about 3.2 W plus its share of switching loss.

Junction-to-case rise is therefore ΔT_JC ≈ 9.6 W × 1.0 °C/W = **9.6 °C instantaneous peak**. This is small. The bottleneck is **case → board → air**, not the die.

### 2.5 Bus Capacitor Ripple

This is frequently missed. Input RMS ripple per channel is:

$$
I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}
$$

At D = 0.5 this is **40 A_rms per channel**. That is impossible for a single electrolytic, so the design needs:

- **Local:** 4–6× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 ceramic per bridge, placed directly at the high-side drains.
- **Bulk:** an off-board low-ESR electrolytic (1000 µF/35 V) at the battery pads. This should be specified in the user manual as mandatory.
