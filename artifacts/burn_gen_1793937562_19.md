## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Architectural Correction

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production 4-in-1 ESC uses one dedicated MCU per channel, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four ESC MCUs.

Running FOC or 6-step for four motors directly on the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot. This design keeps the industry-standard split:

```
[6S LiPo] ──► [4-in-1 ESC: 4× AT32F421 + 4× FD6288Q + 24× MOSFET] ◄── bidir DShot600 ── [H743 FC]
                                   └── telemetry / current sense ──────────────────────► [H743 ADC/UART]
```

**Reference assumptions.** No MOSFET was specified, so this model uses typical parameters for a 40V, ~1 mΩ PDFN5x6 part (the class of BSC010N04LS or TPHR8504PL). Replace these with the parameters of your selected part before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology insight

The drive uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At every instant, motor current flows through **exactly two MOSFETs** in series:

- During PWM-on: high-side (HS) of phase A, then low-side (LS) of phase B.
- During PWM-off: LS of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then LS of phase B.

This holds regardless of duty cycle D. The bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

with the temperature dependence:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per bridge | Per FET (instantaneous) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 8.6 W |
| 135 °C | 1.50 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 9.6 W |

**Per-FET average.** The motor electrical frequency is about 2.3 kHz (a 14-pole motor at 20k RPM). The die thermal time constant is on the order of ms, so the die sees the electrical-cycle average. Each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the cycle:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,bridge}}{6}\cdot 2 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W (hot)}
$$

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt (add to R_ds)

| Element | Resistance |
|---|---|
| Phase pad + 2 oz pour (~10 mm) | ~0.3 mΩ |
| Shared current shunt | 0.25 mΩ (carries DC bus current only, ≈ D·I) |

The copper adds about 2·I²·0.3 mΩ ≈ 3.8 W per channel. **This is often ignored and should not be.** Use 4 to 8 layers of paralleled copper and dense via arrays under the drain tabs.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches: one HS/LS pair per channel.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS turn-on and turn-off)

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_g} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}\quad(\text{each edge})
$$

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.04\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead times per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel. Measure shoot-through margin first.

### 2.5 Gate-drive loss (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g)

$$
P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80A burst, T_j = 135 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction, FETs | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Conduction, copper | 3.84 W | 15.4 W |
| V–I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.16 W | 0.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

**Key conclusion: at 48 kHz, conduction loss dominates (~93%).** Raising f_sw to 96 kHz costs only about +1.7 W per channel. Choosing a lower-R_ds FET buys far more than optimizing switching.
