# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal & Bidirectional DShot Architecture (Job #15)

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

Several points need settling before the math, because they change the results.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600 to the ESC. Each ESC channel uses a dedicated commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. In this design the 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency, set in AM32 firmware.
2. **80 A burst per channel is not credible on a true 20×20 mm "nano" board.** Section 3 shows roughly 75 W of dissipation across four channels at burst, versus about 3.5 J/K of board heat capacity. This design therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC** paired with a nano or 20×20 FC, rated 80 A for ≤2 s bursts and about 45–50 A continuous. If the 20×20 ESC is mandatory, derate to about 40 A burst.
3. **All component values are representative of a parameter class**, such as a 40 V, ~0.9 mΩ MOSFET in a 5×6 mm package. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] = 0.9\,(1 + 0.005\cdot 85) = 1.28\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector, one leg is PWM'd at duty D, one low-side is held fully on, and one leg floats. Averaged over one electrical cycle, each leg spends:

- 120° in the high-side PWM state: the high-side (HS) FET conducts for D and the low-side (LS) FET for (1−D),
- 120° with the LS held fully on,
- 120° floating.

Per-FET average conduction loss:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}$$

Total per ESC channel (6 FETs). This result is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 3\,(P_{HS}+P_{LS}) = 2\, I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}$$

### 1.3 Ripple correction at 48 kHz

The ripple is worst at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{30\mu\cdot48k} = 4.38\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 1.6 \approx 6401.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.025 %). This is one real benefit of 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where the ripple term would be 4× larger: still small, but motor iron losses and acoustic noise also fall.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 6401.6 \cdot 1.28\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

At full-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.95), per FET:

| FET | Duty factor | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| High-side | D/3 = 0.317 | 2.60 W |
| Low-side | (2−D)/3 = 0.350 | 2.87 W |

**PCB copper loss** is additive. For a 2 oz, 4 mm wide, 15 mm phase path:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho L/(w t) = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015/(0.004\cdot70\times10^{-6}) = 0.92\ \text{m}\Omega$$

This is comparable to the FET resistance. Parallel the phase path across ≥4 layers to bring it to about 0.25 mΩ, which costs about 1.6 W per phase-pair path at 80 A.
