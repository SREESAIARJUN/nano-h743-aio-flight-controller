# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Power-Stage Model, DShot Allocation and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points affect every number below.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel, or a shared multi-channel die, runs its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU produces the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives bidirectional eRPM telemetry. The switching-loss model applies to the ESC MCU's PWM; the timer/DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally viable only for a few seconds.** The math below quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a time-limited rating (≈2–3 s), not a continuous one.
3. **Device parameters are assumed representative values** for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm power MOSFET. Replace them with your chosen part's datasheet values and rerun. All formulas are parameterized for that purpose.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Topology.** The bridge is three-phase, uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, and runs with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, current I flows through one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel path is a MOSFET channel rather than a diode. To first order, two R_ds(on) are therefore always in series regardless of duty D.

**RMS current with ripple.** For peak-to-peak ripple ΔI:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1 + \frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2\right)$$

For ΔI/I ≤ 0.3, the correction is under 0.75%. It is neglected below.

**Hot on-resistance.**

$$R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.6 \times 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Per-channel conduction loss.**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

**Per-device average.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle, and the electrical frequency is far above the thermal time constant. The average loss per FET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{20.5}{6} \approx 3.4\ \text{W}$$

**Electro-thermal feedback.** R_ds(on) rises with T_j, so the loss must be solved iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + P(T_j)\,R_{th},\quad R(T) \approx R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T-25)],\ \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}$$

Runaway occurs if $\alpha \cdot P \cdot R_{th} \ge 1$. With P = 3.4 W and R_th(j-board) = 5 K/W, the product is 0.1. The design is stable with margin.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step commutation, only one leg is PWM-switching at a time. All terms below are per channel.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Rise and fall times can be derived from the gate loop:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{plateau})/(R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int})}$$

For example, with Q_gd = 8 nC, V_plateau = 3 V, and R_g,total = 3.2 Ω, the gate current is about 2.2 A and t ≈ 3.6 ns. In practice, small-gate-driver current limits (≈1–1.5 A for FD6288-class drivers) and loop inductance push the effective value toward 10–15 ns per edge. That is why 30 ns total is used.

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output-capacitance energy

This term covers two devices per switching event:

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is large at 80 A. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it. Do so only after verifying shoot-through margin on the bench.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Totals

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 20.5 W | 82.0 W |
| V-I switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.09 W | 0.37 W |
| **FET total** | **≈23.3 W** | **≈93 W** |
| Gate (driver) | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ @ 80 A) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |

**Frequency sensitivity.** Every term except conduction scales linearly with f_sw. The fsw-dependent loss is about 2.8 W per channel at 48 kHz, so moving to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per channel. Conduction dominates (88%), so FET R_ds(on) and copper are the levers, not PWM frequency.
