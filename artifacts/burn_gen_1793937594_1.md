**Reality check up front.** An 80 A rating on a 30.5 mm board is a *burst* rating governed by thermal mass, not by steady-state dissipation. The model below quantifies how long the burst can last.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R_DS ≈ 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Per-FET duty distribution (6-step, synchronous)

Each half-bridge spends 120° in each of three states: PWM-high, held-low, and floating.

| State (120° each) | High-side FET | Low-side FET |
|---|---|---|
| Phase PWM-driven | conducts for D | conducts for (1−D) (sync rectification) |
| Phase held low (return) | off | conducts 100% |
| Floating (BEMF sense) | off | off |

$$P_{cond,HS} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{D}{3},\qquad P_{cond,LS} = I^2 R_{DS}\,\frac{(1-D)+1}{3}$$

**Consistency check:** at every instant exactly two FETs carry I, so the per-ESC total is

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}.$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, D = 0.9, T_j = 125 °C

- I²R = 6400 × 0.0024 = 15.36 W
- P_HS = 15.36 × 0.9/3 = **4.61 W** per high-side FET
- P_LS = 15.36 × 1.1/3 = **5.63 W** per low-side FET
- P_cond,ESC = 2 × 15.36 = **30.7 W**

**Low-throttle trap.** At D = 0.2 the low-side FET's share rises to 1.8/3 of I²R. High phase current at low duty (stall, prop strike, aggressive braking) therefore loads the low-side FETs hardest.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (per ESC)

- Phase and power path, ≈0.5 mΩ effective with 2 oz × 8 layers: 80² × 0.5 m = **3.2 W**
- Current shunt, if per-ESC (0.2 mΩ): 80² × 0.2 m = **1.28 W**

Most 4-in-1 designs use a single shared shunt on the battery return instead. See §5.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-driven half-bridge hard-switches, so per ESC one half-bridge switches at any instant.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Edge times are estimated from the gate charge:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_g} = \frac{5\,\text{nC}}{(10-3)/5} \approx 3.6\ \text{ns (ideal)}$$

$$t_f \approx \frac{5\,\text{nC}}{3/5} \approx 8.3\ \text{ns (ideal)}$$

With layout inductance, I use t_r + t_f ≈ 25 ns:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

Nearly all of this falls on the **high-side FET**, because the low side switches at near-zero voltage after its body diode conducts.
