# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #50): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

Two constraints shape the whole design.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors.** ArduPilot does not run BLDC commutation or FOC. Bi-directional DShot also implies that a separate ESC MCU replies with eRPM telemetry. The architecture is therefore:
   - **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot.
   - **ESC:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071), each running AM32 firmware.
   - **Gate drivers:** 4× FD6288Q-class three-phase drivers.
   - **Power stage:** 24× 40 V N-MOSFETs.
2. **80 A cannot be continuous on a "nano" board.** In the model below, 80 A on all four channels dissipates about 110 W. A 20×20 mm board cannot reject that heat. I specify a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer board with 2 oz copper**. 80 A is treated strictly as a **burst rating with a computed time limit**. If the board must be 20×20, use the same equations with roughly 40% lower thermal capacitance and expect about 1.6× shorter burst windows.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step (Trapezoidal) Current Path

With complementary (synchronous) PWM, current flows through one high-side and one low-side MOSFET at every instant.

- During ON time: HS(A) → winding → LS(B).
- During OFF time: LS(A, synchronous) → winding → LS(B).

Two channel-resistances are therefore always in series, so the duty cycle cancels out of the per-channel total:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At a target T_j of 100 °C:

$$
R_{ds} = 1.2 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 75) = 1.65\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Ripple Correction

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad
\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\, D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH) at 48 kHz, ΔI ≈ 8–12 A. The ripple term adds less than 2% at 80 A, so it is neglected.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 6400 \times 1.65\times10^{-3} = 10.56\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 21.1\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{Cu,ch} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.5 Thermal Runaway Check

Conduction loss rises with temperature, which raises temperature further. Solve the loop self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\, P(T_j)
$$

The loop is stable when:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\, R_{th} < 1
\;\Rightarrow\;
2 I^2 R_{ds,25}\, \alpha\, R_{th} < 1
$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1.2×10⁻³ × 0.005 × R_th < 1, which requires **R_th < 13 K/W**. Both the junction path and the board path satisfy this, so there is no runaway.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side MOSFET hard-switches. The synchronous low-side MOSFET soft-switches (ZVS after its body diode conducts).

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40n × 48k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 0.06 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_f·I·t_dt·f_sw | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (×2 FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.67 W per channel** |

### 2.1 Sensitivity

- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.3 W, but audible noise and current ripple both rise.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise.
- Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W
