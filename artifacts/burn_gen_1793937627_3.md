# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Engineering Model (Job #62)

This document covers the loss and thermal model, the bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA plan, and reference netlists.

> **Scope:** No MOSFET part number, board stack-up, burst duration or cooling condition was specified. Every number below comes from **stated, parameterized assumptions** so you can substitute datasheet values. The netlists are **reference topologies, not a released, DRC-checked design**.

---

## 0. Architecture Check (Read First)

Three points shape the rest of the analysis:

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.** A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s), typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 generates DShot. The 48 kHz figure is the **ESC's** PWM frequency, set in AM32 firmware.
2. **"Nano" + 4 × 80 A is thermally inconsistent.** Section 3 shows that a 20 × 20 mm board can survive 80 A per channel only for roughly **1–2 s bursts**. Use a minimum 30.5 × 30.5 mm board with a heatsink if bursts are longer.
3. **Assumed operating point:** 6S battery, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge), 24 MOSFETs (6 per channel), synchronous complementary PWM, dead time 100 ns.

## 2. Loss Model (per channel, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step commutation and synchronous rectification, **two FETs are always in the current path**: one high-side or its complementary low-side, plus the static low-side.

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 0.8\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.6 = 1.28\,\text{mΩ}$$

**Ripple correction.** I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12. The worst case is D = 0.5. Assuming a line-to-line inductance L_LL = 20 µH:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.6\,\text{A}_{pp}$$

This gives ΔI²/12 = 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6404 \times 1.28\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{16.4\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses

One hard-switched leg per PWM period.

| Term | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 20 ns × 48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f | **0.024 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2·t_d·f | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f | **0.058 W** (dissipated in driver/R_g) |

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{1.70\ W}$$

### 2.3 PCB Copper Loss

Assumptions:
- 2 oz copper, 0.25 mΩ/sq at 25 °C, about 0.36 mΩ/sq when hot.
- Roughly 1.5 effective squares across paralleled layers, giving about 0.5 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{mΩ} \approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.4 Totals

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 16.4 W | 65.6 W |
| Switching + dead time | 1.7 W | 6.8 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| **Total** | **21.3 W** | **≈ 85 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Conduction loss dominates (77 %). Halving R_ds(on) by paralleling FETs or using 0.5 mΩ parts gains far more than lowering f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 0.8 W per channel and increases ripple and audible noise.

### 3.2 Board-to-Air Resistance

Assume prop-wash convection with h ≈ 100 W/m²K over both faces (8 cm²):

$$R_{\theta,BA} \approx 12.5\ \text{K/W}, \qquad \tau = R\,C \approx 33\ \text{s}$$

### 3.3 Transient Response

$$T_{pcb}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{\theta}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Because the burst is much shorter than τ, the board heats almost adiabatically at P/C = 85/2.6 ≈ **33 K/s**.
