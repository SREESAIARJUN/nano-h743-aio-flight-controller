## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and One Architectural Correction

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at production quality. Each motor needs its own sensorless BEMF commutation loop, with zero-cross detection at roughly 1 µs granularity. Every commercial 4-in-1 design delegates this to a dedicated ESC MCU per motor (AT32F421, STM32G071, or EFM8 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The partition used throughout this document is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir──▶ 4× AT32F421 (AM32) ──▶ 4× 3-ph driver ──▶ 24× MOSFET
```

The "48 kHz switching" figure is the PWM frequency inside the ESC MCU. The H743's job is DShot generation and eRPM capture.

**Assumptions.** No MOSFET part number or board stack was specified, so the parameters below are representative values. Replace them with datasheet values before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation model

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

At any instant, two phase legs carry I_ph:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET conducts for (1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw), minus dead time.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side FET is held on continuously.

Each leg therefore always has exactly one channel conducting, except during dead time.

### 1.2 Equations

Hot on-resistance:

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS,hot} = 2 (80)^2 (1.5\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Copper loss also matters. Assume 15 mm of 2 oz phase trace, 4 mm wide, on 4 parallel layers, giving about 0.3 mΩ per path with two paths:

$$P_{Cu} = 2\, I^2 R_{trace} = 2(6400)(0.3\times10^{-3}) \approx 3.8\ \text{W}$$

> **Design note:** On a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), copper loss is comparable to a full FET's loss. Use 6–8 layers, 2 oz or heavier, and via-stitched phase pours.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The switching node is the high-side FET of the PWM leg (hard turn-on and turn-off). The low-side FET switches at roughly zero voltage after the body diode conducts.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = (50\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} \approx 0.5(40\text{n})(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors. Two FETs switch per PWM cycle:

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.20 | 76.8 |
| Copper | 3.80 | 15.2 |
| V-I switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + Q_g | 0.14 | 0.6 |
| **Total** | **25.7** | **≈ 103 W** |

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** Frequency-dependent loss is about 2.7 W per motor at 48 kHz.
- At 24 kHz it would be about 1.35 W per motor.
- Conduction loss dominates by roughly 7:1, so raising f_sw for smoother current (less torque ripple, quieter motors) is cheap.
- Reducing R_DS(on) is the high-leverage action.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction rise per FET (Foster network, short timescale)

Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs per motor conducts about 1/3 of the time. Average per-FET loss is roughly 21.9 W / 6 ≈ 3.65 W. Instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R = 9.6 W.

With a typical PQFN transient thermal impedance Z_th(jc) of about 0.15 K/W at a 1 ms pulse:

$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}c,peak} \approx 9.6 \times 0.15 \approx 1.5\ \text{K}$$

**Conclusion:** junction-to-case ripple is negligible. The limit is the bulk board temperature.
