# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #55)

## 0. Feasibility Findings

I am stating these first because they constrain everything else.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** It runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Commutation runs on dedicated ESC MCUs. This design uses 4× AT32F421 running AM32, one per channel. One MCU per channel is the standard choice because it isolates faults and runs a mature, proven firmware.
2. **80 A on all four channels at once is a 320 A pack draw, about 7.1 kW at 22.2 V.** That exceeds the XT60 and XT90 connectors and the battery leads. I have interpreted the spec as **80 A per channel, burst ≤ 5 s, ambient 40 °C**, with a 45 A continuous rating.
3. **A true nano form factor (20×20 mm) cannot dissipate this.** The calculation below (§3) shows an 80 A ×4 burst is only survivable on a 30.5×30.5 mm board with a bonded heat spreader. Even then it is time-limited to about 6–8 s. This must be enforced in firmware through AM32 temperature and current limits plus an ArduPilot battery current limit.
4. **Treat all component parameters as representative assumptions.** Examples are Rds(on), Qrr and Rth. Re-run the model with final datasheet values. The netlist is a reference topology, not a fab-ready design. Pin numbers on the gate drivers and MCUs must be checked against the datasheets and footprints before layout.

## 2. Loss Model (per channel)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses trapezoidal six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

In each 60° sector:
- **High-side FET of phase A:** PWM at duty D.
- **Low-side FET of phase A:** conducts during (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Low-side FET of phase B:** fully on.

Each FET is active for one third of the electrical cycle.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

Within a sector, current always flows through exactly two channel resistances:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}
$$

Here the bracketed term is the PWM leg (high-side plus synchronous low-side), and the final term is the fully-on low side.

The result is independent of D:

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.86\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{23.8\ W}
$$

Rds(on) is temperature dependent, which makes conduction loss a self-heating feedback loop:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

The fixed point between T_j and P converges if

$$
2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta} < 1
$$

where R_θ is the effective junction-to-ambient resistance.

### 2.3 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches.

**Gate current during the Miller plateau:**

$$
I_g = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10 - 3.5}{6.2} = 1.05\ \text{A}
$$

**Edge time:** Q_gd/I_g ≈ 9.5 ns. Adding the Q_gs2 and layout inductance contribution gives t_r ≈ t_f ≈ 15 ns.
