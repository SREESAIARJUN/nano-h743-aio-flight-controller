## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0047\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS}(110^\circ C) \approx 1.2 \times 1.40 = 1.68\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with complementary PWM

In any 60° sector, one phase is the PWM leg and one is the return leg (low side held on); the third floats.

- On the PWM leg, the high-side FET conducts for D·T and the synchronous low-side FET for (1 − D)·T. Current therefore always flows through exactly one FET in that leg.
- On the return leg, the low-side FET conducts 100% of the time.

Total instantaneous conduction loss per motor is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS} + R_{Cu}\right)$$

Here R_Cu is the copper path: pads, vias, phase pad and bus. I estimate about 0.3 mΩ per conduction path on 2 oz copper.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 1.68 + 0.3)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.4\ W}$$

Of this, 21.5 W dissipates in the FETs and 1.9 W in copper.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle: 1/3 as the PWM leg and 1/3 as the return leg.

- High-side FET: $P_{HS} = I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\frac{D}{3}$
- Low-side FET: $P_{LS} = I^2 R_{DS}\cdot\left[\frac{1-D}{3} + \frac{1}{3}\right]$

At D = 0.9 (typical burst):

- P_HS = 10.75 × 0.30 = 3.2 W
- P_LS = 10.75 × 0.367 = 3.9 W

**The low-side FETs are the hottest devices.** Give them the most copper and thermal vias.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Its low-side FET soft-switches through dead time, so hard-switching loss lands on the high-side FET.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

Gate plateau about 4.5 V and gate resistance R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω give t_r + t_f ≈ 20 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (driver loss, not FET loss)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2\ \text{FETs switching} = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Switching total

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 0.97 + 0.05 + 0.61 + 0.04 + 0.05 = \mathbf{1.72\ W}$$

The 48 kHz switching frequency contributes only about 7% of the loss at 80 A; conduction dominates. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add about 1.7 W per motor, which is acceptable if you want quieter low-throttle operation.

### 2.7 Totals at 80 A

| Item | Per motor | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 21.5 W | 86.0 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.6 W |
| Switching | 1.7 W | 6.9 W |
| **Total** | **25.1 W** | **≈ 100 W** |

Input power at 80 A, D ≈ 0.9 and 22.2 V is about 4 × 1.6 kW. Stage efficiency is therefore about 98.4%, but 100 W on a 9 g board is a thermal problem, not an efficiency problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

```
P_FET ─[R_θJB = 1.5 K/W]─ T_board ─[R_θBA]─ T_ambient
                              │
                          C_board
```

- Board heat capacity: C_th ≈ m·c_p ≈ 9 g × 0.8 J/g·K ≈ **7.2 J/K**
- Board-to-air resistance, with prop wash at about 5 m/s: R_θBA ≈ **3 K/W**
- Time constant: τ = R·C ≈ **21.6 s**

### 3.2 Junction-to-board rise (hottest low-side FET)

$$\Delta T_{JB} = 3.9 + 0.4\ (\text{share of switching loss}) \approx 4.3\ \text{W} \times 1.5 = 6.5\ \text{K}$$
