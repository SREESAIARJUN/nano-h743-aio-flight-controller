# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #32): Power, Thermal & Timing Design

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-controller firmware, not ESC firmware. In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical ESC MCUs are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. The ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM and drive the gate drivers. Everything below follows that partition.

| Parameter | Value used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max | Use 40 V MOSFETs (≥1.5× margin on ringing) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path
In 6-step commutation, two legs are active at any moment. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

- one high-side FET in the "+" leg, and
- either the high-side FET (PWM on) or the low-side FET (PWM off) in the other leg.

Body diodes conduct only during dead time.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.3 Conduction loss per motor channel
$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2\,\big[2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\big]$$

At 80 A with T_j = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ:

| Contributor | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| FETs | 80² × 3.0 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |

Each of the 6 FETs conducts about one-third of the electrical cycle. Average FET loss is therefore 19.2 / 6 ≈ **3.2 W per FET**. Peak FET loss is **9.6 W** while it is conducting.

### 1.4 Partial-throttle note
Phase current is I_ph = I_batt / D. At 50% duty with 40 A from the battery, the phase current is 80 A. Conduction loss depends on phase current, not battery current. If the current-limit firmware senses battery current, it under-protects the FETs at low duty.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard. Each PWM period contains one turn-on and one turn-off transition.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f}_{\text{V–I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2 V_f I\, t_{dt} f}_{\text{dead-time diode}}$$

Evaluated at V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48k | **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | 25.2 × 40 nC × 48k | **0.05 W** |
| C_oss | 0.5 × 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k | **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time diode | 2 × 0.8 V × 80 × 100 ns × 48k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (two FETs, dissipated in the driver) | 2 × Q_g × V_gs × f = 2 × 30 nC × 10 V × 48k | 0.03 W |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.1 W per motor** |

**Design levers:**
- Dead-time loss is about 30% of switching loss. Tightening dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per channel, but you must verify shoot-through margin on a scope.
- Raising PWM to 96 kHz doubles the 2.1 W to 4.2 W per motor.

## 3. Total Burst Dissipation

| Item | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| FET switching | 2.1 W | 8.4 W |
| PCB copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Shunts, LDOs, MCU (estimate) | — | ~2 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 100 W** |

Electrical efficiency at full power: about 100 W lost out of roughly 4 × 80 × 25 ≈ 8 kW, which is about 98.8%. Efficiency is fine. **Heat density is the problem.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction to case (not the bottleneck)
The average junction rise above case is:

ΔT_jc = 3.2 W × R_θjc (≈ 3 K/W) ≈ **10 K**

The peak during each 120° conduction interval is lower than 9.6 W × 3 K/W, because Z_θ(t) at millisecond pulse widths is well below R_θjc.
