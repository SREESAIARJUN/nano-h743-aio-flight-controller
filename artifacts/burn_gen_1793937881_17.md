**Architectural note.** In a practical 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller only. Motor commutation runs on dedicated ESC MCUs, typically running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware on STM32G071, AT32F421, or similar parts. The H743 talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot. I model it that way. Having the H743 commutate four motors directly is not compatible with ArduPilot.

**Feasibility flag.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 footprint is at or beyond the physical limit. The math below shows it is a **~1–2 second burst rating** under realistic conditions, not a sustained one. Total pack current at full throttle would be up to ~320 A, which also stresses the XT60/XT90 connector, the bulk capacitors, and the battery.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous/complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current path

At every instant in 6-step commutation, current flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels:
- During PWM on-time: high-side of phase A, then motor, then low-side of phase B.
- During PWM off-time with synchronous rectification: low-side of phase A, then motor, then low-side of phase B.

Body-diode conduction occurs only during dead time. That is treated in §2.

So, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts 120° high-side-equivalent and 120° low-side-equivalent per electrical cycle:
- **Low-side FETs** carry current for 1/3 of the cycle as a static low-side, plus (1−D)·1/3 as a synchronous rectifier.
- **High-side FETs** carry current for D·1/3 of the cycle.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{DS},\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\, I^2 R_{DS}$$

Check: 3·(P_HS + P_LS) = 2 I² R_DS ✓.

**Implication:** low-side FETs run hotter at partial throttle. At D = 1 the split is equal.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R = 1.6 mΩ)

$$I^2R = 6400 \times 0.0016 = 10.24\ \text{W per conducting FET (instantaneous)}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 10.24 = 20.5\ \text{W}$$

| D | P_HS (each) | P_LS (each) | Per motor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 3.41 W | 3.41 W | 20.5 W |
| 0.7 | 2.39 W | 4.44 W | 20.5 W |

Add PCB copper loss. A typical phase path is ~0.3 mΩ (2 oz, short, multi-layer), giving 6400 × 0.0003 × 2 ≈ **3.8 W per motor**. This is a frequently ignored term.

**Electro-thermal coupling:** R_DS depends on T_j, so solve iteratively (§3.4).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Per motor, only one leg is PWM-switching at a time (HS plus its synchronous LS). That leg makes two hard transitions per PWM period.

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard-switched turn-on and turn-off)

The transition time is set by the charge that must move through the Miller region:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,plateau}},\qquad I_{g,plateau} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv}}$$

With 8 nC at ~0.4 A, t ≈ 20 ns per edge.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\,\text{ns} \cdot 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
