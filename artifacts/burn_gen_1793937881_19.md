## 0. Architecture Corrections and Feasibility Notes

Three points need settling before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC.** ArduPilot does not commutate BLDC motors. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to **four dedicated ESC MCUs**. These are typically AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, or STM32G071 / EFM8BB21 running BLHeli_32/S. The ESC MCUs drive gate drivers and the MOSFET bridges. The netlist in §5 reflects this.

2. **"80A per motor" on a nano board is a burst rating only, not a continuous one.** Four motors at 80A means 320A from the battery. The model in §3 shows the board copper heats at roughly 15–25 K/s at full burst. The defensible spec is **80A for no more than about 2 s, from a board starting at 40°C or below**, with a realistic continuous rating of about 25–35A per motor.

3. **The 48 kHz PWM is the ESC's bridge switching frequency, not the DShot rate.** AM32 supports 24/48/96 kHz. DShot600 is the FC→ESC command link.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default.

- In each 60° sector, one phase sources current through its high-side FET (HS) at duty D.
- Its complementary low-side FET (LS) carries freewheel current for (1 − D).
- Another phase sinks current through its LS FET, which is held fully on.

So at every instant, **exactly two channels conduct I_ph** in series.

$$P_{cond,motor}(T_j) = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET split

Each phase conducts positive current for 120° out of 360°.

| Position | Duty-cycle share | Fraction of the electrical period |
|---|---|---|
| HS of a sourcing phase | D | ⅓·D |
| LS of a sourcing phase (sync rectifier) | 1 − D | ⅓·(1 − D) |
| LS of a sinking phase | 1 | ⅓ |

The average loss per FET depends on its position:

$$P_{HS}= \tfrac13 D\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS}=\tfrac13(2-D)\,I^2R$$

**The low-side FETs run hotter.** At D = 0.9 (burst), each LS FET carries 0.37·I²R and each HS FET 0.30·I²R. Place the LS FETs closest to the thermal pad and widest copper.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80A

| T_j | R_ds,on | P_cond,motor | Hottest FET (LS, D = 0.9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.35 W |
| 100 °C | 1.45 mΩ | **18.6 W** | 3.40 W |
| 125 °C | 1.60 mΩ | 20.5 W | 3.75 W |

### 1.4 Copper loss (non-negligible)

A 2 oz (70 µm) trace that is 5 mm wide and 15 mm long has resistance:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{w t}=\frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.005\cdot70\times10^{-6}} = 0.74\ \text{m}\Omega$$

That is comparable to a FET. Over the phase path and the half-share of the bus path, budget about 0.5 mΩ per motor current loop after layer paralleling:

$$P_{Cu,motor}\approx 2\cdot I^2\cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega\approx 6.4\ \text{W}$$

Adding the battery-lead pad and bus plane (320A total, about 0.1 mΩ) gives **+10 W** for the whole board. **Use ≥3 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers, and parallel the power planes on 4 or more layers.**
