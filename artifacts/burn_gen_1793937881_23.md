# STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture (Job #6)

## 0. Critical Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

Three issues shape every number below.

**1. The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.**
- ArduPilot cannot run sensorless 6-step commutation for 24 half-bridge gates.
- Bi-directional DShot also implies separate ESC MCUs, because the telemetry is generated by ESC firmware.
- The architecture is therefore:
  - The H743 (ArduPilot) sends DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware).
  - Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver, which drives 6 FETs.
- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

**2. 80 A per motor is a burst rating, not continuous, especially on a "nano" form factor.**
- Commercial 20×20 4-in-1 ESCs are typically rated 25–45 A continuous.
- 80 A burst is normally 30.5×30.5 mm with 5×6 mm FETs or paralleled 3.3×3.3 mm FETs.
- Section 3 quantifies how long the burst can actually be sustained.

**3. Component parameters are placeholders.** The device values below represent a typical 40 V, 6S-capable trench MOSFET class. Replace them with datasheet values for the actual BOM before sign-off.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9 \times [1 + 0.004 \times 85] = 1.206 \text{ mΩ}$$

Use **R_hot ≈ 1.2 mΩ**. The model is self-consistent: iterate T_j until it converges, as described in Section 3.4.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification

Within each 60° sector, two phases carry current:
- **PWM phase:** the high side conducts for fraction D; the complementary low side conducts for 1−D (synchronous rectification).
- **Return phase:** the low side conducts for the full sector.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

Notes:
- The result is independent of D because of synchronous rectification.
- Ripple adds a factor of $(1 + \frac{1}{12}(\Delta I/I)^2)$. This is under 1% at 48 kHz with typical 2207 motor inductance (about 10 µH phase-to-phase), so it is ignored
