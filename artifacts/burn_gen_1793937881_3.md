## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)] , \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.006 × 85) = **1.51 mΩ**

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary PWM

At any instant, two phase legs conduct:

- **PWM leg:** The high-side FET conducts for duty D. The low-side FET (synchronous rectification) conducts for (1 − D).
- **Static leg:** The low-side FET conducts 100% of the interval.

$$P_{cond,PWM\,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}$$
$$P_{cond,static\,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}$$
$$\boxed{P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

The result is independent of D, to first order with synchronous rectification.

At 80 A and T_j = 110 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.51\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.3\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

### 1.3 Current ripple correction

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{2L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Example: L_ph = 10 µH line-to-line (5 µH per phase), V_bus − V_bemf = 4 V at full throttle, D ≈ 0.95:

ΔI_pp ≈ 4 × 0.95 / (2 × 5 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 7.9 A, so the correction is ≈ +5 A² (**< 0.1%**, negligible at 48 kHz).

Low-inductance motors at partial throttle can see a much larger ΔI. Re-evaluate for motors with L_ph < 2 µH.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

- **Phase trace / pad:** 3 mm, 2 oz, 15 mm path ≈ 0.4 mΩ, so P = 6400 × 0.4 m ≈ 2.6 W per active pair. Use parallel layers and via stitching to reach ≤ 0.2 mΩ.
- **Shunt:** A single 0.1 mΩ shunt at a 320 A aggregate burst gives P = 320² × 1e-4 = **10.2 W**. Use 2 × 0.2 mΩ 2512 in parallel, or per-channel 0.25 mΩ shunts (1.6 W each).

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

All terms are per ESC (one active PWM leg).

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw, with t_r = t_f = 20 ns | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40e-9 × 48e3 = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50e-9 × 25.2 × 48e3 = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½ · C_oss,er · V² · f_sw | 0.5 × 1.5e-9 × 635 × 48e3 = **0.023 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw, with V_f = 0.8 V, t_dt = 100 ns | 0.8 × 80 × 200e-9 × 48e3 = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw (dissipated in driver/R_g, not FETs) | 2 × 40e-9 × 10 × 48e3 = **0.04 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈ 2.67 W** |

**Rise time from gate charge:**

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}} = \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 10 nC, R_total = 20 Ω, and V_drv − V_pl = 6.5 V: t_r ≈ 31 ns. Size R_g to land in the 15–25 ns range. Faster edges cut overlap loss but raise ringing and EMI on the gyro rails.

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it is ≈ 1.3 W; at 96 kHz it is ≈ 5.3 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable trade between ripple and loss for this FET class.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

| Item | Per ESC | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.3 W | 77.2 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| Copper traces | 2.6 W | 10.4 W |
| Shunt(s) | — | 6–10 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.6 W** | **≈ 105–109 W** |

### 3.2 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries load for about 1/3 of the time. The low-side FETs also carry synchronous rectification current.

- **Average per FET:** P_FET,avg ≈ (19.3 + 2.7) / 6 ≈ **3.7 W**
- **Instantaneous on a conducting FET:** P_FET,inst ≈ I²·R_DS ≈ **9.7 W**

```

                                            │
                                           C_board
```
