# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Architecture Boundary and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates the bi-directional DShot600 frames. The 48 kHz PWM and commutation run on a separate MCU on the ESC (e.g., AM32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071), one per motor. The power-stage math below therefore belongs to the ESC side. The DMA math belongs to the H743.

**Assumptions.** No MOSFET or board stackup was specified, so every number below rests on these values. Substitute your datasheet values into the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I | 80 A |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3 class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.5 mΩ (max) |
| R_ds tempco | α | ≈ 0.0045 /K (R ≈ 1.5× at 125 °C) |
| Gate-drive totals | Q_g, Q_gd + Q_gs2 | 40 nC, 12 nC |
| Reverse recovery / output charge | Q_rr, Q_oss | 50 nC, 40 nC |
| Rise / fall time (gate driver ≈ 1–1.5 A) | t_r = t_f | 15 ns |
| Dead time | t_dt | 50 ns |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Current path.** In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant.
- In the PWM leg, either the high-side FET (fraction D of the time) or the low-side FET (fraction 1−D) carries the current, because rectification is synchronous.
- In the static leg, one FET carries the current continuously.

So at every instant, exactly two R_ds(on) are in series with the motor current.

**Equations.**

$$P_{cond,motor}(T_j)=2\,I^2\,R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \;+\; I^2R_{cu}(T)$$

**Distribution per FET.** Each FET conducts for at most 120° of the electrical cycle. The average loss per FET is therefore approximately P_cond/6. The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is approximately I²R_ds.

**Numbers at 80 A:**

| Condition | Per-motor bridge | Copper | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 2·6400·1.5 mΩ = 19.2 W | 3.2 W | 22.4 W | 89.6 W |
| T_j ≈ 100 °C (R ≈ 2.0 mΩ) | 25.6 W | ≈ 4.2 W | 29.8 W | 119 W |

**Per-FET at 100 °C:**
- Average loss is about 4.3 W.
- Instantaneous loss is about 12.8 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches, so these terms are counted once per motor.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·30 ns·48 k | 1.45 W |
| Body diode during dead time | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 k | 0.31 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss·V·f | 0.05 W |
| Gate charge (dissipated mostly in the driver) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f = 2·40 nC·10 V·48 k | 0.04 W |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.9 W** |
| **Total ×4** | | **≈ 7.6 W** |

**Takeaways.**
- At 80 A, conduction loss dominates switching loss by about 15:1.
- Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1 W per motor.
- Dead time is the cheapest knob: dropping from 50 ns to 25 ns saves about 0.15 W per motor.
