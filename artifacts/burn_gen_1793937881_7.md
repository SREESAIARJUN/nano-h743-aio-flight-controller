# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture Model

This document is a **parametric design model** built on stated assumptions. Every number traces to an equation, so you can substitute real datasheet values when the BOM is frozen. Items marked ⚠ are design risks.

---

## 0. Architecture Decision

The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate four BLDC motors directly. That would require 12 complementary PWM pairs, and the H743 has only TIM1 and TIM8 (3 complementary pairs each) plus HRTIM.

The standard, validated topology is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir ×4──► 4× AT32F421 (AM32) or EFM8BB21 (BLHeli_S/Bluejay)
                                                │
                                                ├─ 3-phase gate driver (e.g. FD6288Q)
                                                └─ 6× N-MOSFET per motor (24 total)
```

The **48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs**. The H743 only produces DShot frames and decodes eRPM telemetry.

⚠ **Form-factor reality check.** 80 A per motor means 320 A from the battery, which at 6S is roughly 8 kW. A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot handle this beyond sub-second bursts. The model below assumes a **30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board**, and even that is burst-limited (see §3).

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step commutation, current always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:

- **PWM ON:** HS_x + LS_y
- **PWM OFF (synchronous rectification):** LS_x + LS_y

So the conducting path is always 2·R_ds(on), independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on),hot} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.25\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period. The electrical frequency (≈2–3 kHz at 7 pole pairs, 20–25k rpm) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (ms scale), so the per-FET average is:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx 28.8/6 = 4.8\text{ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Loss

Only the active high-side FET switches hard. Using a linear V–I overlap model:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 30\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time (Body-Diode) Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery, Output Capacitance and Gate Drive

| Loss | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr · V · f = 30 nC · 25.2 · 48k | 0.036 W |
| C_oss (two nodes) | P_oss = 2 · ½ C_oss V² f | 0.046 W |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f | 0.038 W |

### 2.5 Copper Loss

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

### 2.6 Totals

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 28.8 W | 115.2 W |
| Switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W
