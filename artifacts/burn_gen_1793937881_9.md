## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The architecture is two-tier:

| Tier | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (ArduPilot) | Attitude control, emits bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines, decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 class MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32) | Commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, generates DShot reply |
| Power stage (×4) | 3-phase gate driver + 6 MOSFETs | 80A burst per motor |

So Sections 1–3 model the ESC power stage, and Section 4 models the H743 timer/DMA subsystem.

**Feasibility flag:** 80A burst ×4 on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally credible. Section 3 shows why. I base the design on a **25.5×25.5 mm (whoop/AIO) or 30.5×30.5 mm** footprint and state burst duration limits explicitly.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

With 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, at any instant current flows through **one high-side path and one low-side path**:

- **Static leg:** the low-side (or high-side) MOSFET is held on for the 60° electrical sector.
- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the HS FET (duty D) and the synchronous LS FET (1−D).

Ignoring dead time, two R_ds(on) are always in series:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K} \Rightarrow \times1.6 \text{ at } 125\,°\mathrm{C}$$

Add copper/trace resistance R_cu (phase pad, shunt if present, bus planes), estimated at 0.3 mΩ per path:

$$P_{cond} = I^2(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu})$$

**At 80A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \cdot (2 \times 1.28\,\text{m} + 0.3\,\text{m}) = 6400 \times 2.86\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.3\ W}$$

(FET-only share: 16.4 W; copper share: 1.9 W.)

**At 45A continuous, T_j = 100 °C (R_ds ≈ 1.16 mΩ):** 2025 × 2.62 mΩ = **5.3 W**

### 2.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg, inductive load)

Only one leg switches per PWM period. With linear V–I overlap approximation:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With R_g,tot = 3 Ω (driver + external + internal), V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{12\,\text{nC} \times 3}{5.5} \approx 6.5\ \text{ns (gate-limited)}$$

Real overlap is dominated by commutation-loop inductance and driver current limits, so use **t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns** as the practical design value, to be validated by double-pulse test.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

Two dead-time intervals per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

*Lever:* cutting dead time to 100 ns halves this, but needs shoot-through margin verification.

### 2.4 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive Loss (dissipated mostly in driver + R_g)

$$P_{gate} = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Output Capacitance Loss

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} \approx 0.023\ \text{W}$$

(Partly included in the overlap model, so it is kept as a separate upper bound.)
