**Feasibility.** The analysis below concludes that 80 A per channel on a 20×20 board is sustainable only for about 1–2 seconds. It is not a continuous rating. Specify it as a timed burst rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

With 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry the phase current at any instant.

With complementary PWM at duty D:
- The active high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- Its synchronous low-side partner conducts for (1−D).
- The opposite-phase low-side FET conducts for 100% of the commutation step.

The total conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on),hot}\cdot\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 \cdot R_{DS(on),hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on),hot}$$

### 1.2 Temperature correction

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

Add PCB copper resistance. For a phase-to-pad trace plus a shared bus at about 0.3 mΩ:

$$R_{path} = 2(2.25) + 0.3 = 4.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Losses at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 4.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

- The FET-only share is 28.8 W.
- The copper share is 1.9 W.

The individual devices are not loaded equally:

$$P_{LS,static} = 80^2 \times 2.25\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{14.4\ W}\ \text{(the 100\%-on low-side, worst device)}$$

This hot-spot device rotates every 60° electrical, but at low eRPM it dwells long enough to matter thermally. Section 4 uses it as the worst case.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Use V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f_sw = 48 kHz.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching)

Rise and fall times are set by Q_gd and the gate drive current. With about 1 A source and 1.5 A sink, t_r ≈ 12 ns and t_f ≈ 8 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, during dead time)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 30\text{nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and current, so de-rate this term by about 2× at 80 A: roughly 0.07 W.

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(900\text{p})(25.2^2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.014\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (the driver dissipates this, not the FETs)

Two FETs switch per channel:

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(35\text{n})(12)(48\text{k}) = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction, FETs | 28.8 |
| Conduction, copper | 1.9 |
| V–I switching | 0.97 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 |
| **Total per channel** | **≈ 32.4 W** |
| **Board, 4 × 80 A** | **≈ 130 W** |

At 80 A, conduction dominates switching by about 30:1. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per channel. Lowering R_DS(on) is the lever that matters.

Efficiency at a 22.2 V × 80 A = 1776 W per-channel input: 32.4 / 1776 ≈ 1.8% loss, or 98.2% efficient. The efficiency is fine; heat density is the problem.

## 3. Steady-State Thermal Check

$$T_j = T_a + P\cdot R_{\theta JA}$$

For the worst low-side FET at 14.4 W, R_θJA = 25 K/W, and T_a = 40 °C:

$$T_j = 40 + 14.4(25) = 400\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

**Continuous operation at 80 A is impossible.**

The continuous current limit for T_j,max = 150 °C with 20% margin, so T_j = 120 °C, is:

$$I_{cont} = \sqrt{\frac{(120-40)/25}{2.0\text{m}\Omega}} \approx \mathbf{40\ A}$$

This assumes no airflow. Prop-wash airflow typically cuts R_θJA by about 30–50%, which yields roughly **50–55 A continuous** in flight.
