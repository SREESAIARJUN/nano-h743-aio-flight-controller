# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #64): Engineering Model

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

All results depend on the component values assumed in §1. Swap in datasheet values for your chosen parts before committing.

**Finding 1: architecture.** The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly. Use the standard AIO architecture:

- The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600.
- Four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) do the 48 kHz commutation.
- Driving 12 half-bridges from the H743 would cost DShot/RPM telemetry decoupling, fault isolation and timing determinism, with no gain.

**Finding 2: an "80 A burst" on a nano board is thermally bounded to about 1–2 s.**

- 4 × 80 A from 6S is about 320 A, or about 8 kW.
- An XT60 connector, a 20×20/25.5×25.5 mm PCB, and the battery leads cannot sustain that.
- §4 quantifies the limit. Specify the burst rating as **80 A for ≤1 s, ≥10 s off, per channel, not all four simultaneously**, and use a realistic continuous rating of about 25–35 A per channel.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

With AM32 complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM in six-step commutation, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at every instant, independent of duty cycle D:

- During the D interval: the high-side FET of leg A and the low-side FET of leg B conduct.
- During the (1−D) interval: the low-side FET of leg A (synchronous) and the low-side FET of leg B conduct.

Per channel:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot \big(2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + 0.006\,(T_j - 25)]$$

The 0.006 /K coefficient matches the 1.6× factor at 125 °C.

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | Per FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 A, T_j = 25 °C | 1.10 mΩ | 6400 × (2.2 + 0.3) mΩ = **16.0 W** | 7.0 W |
| 80 A, T_j = 125 °C | 1.76 mΩ | 6400 × (3.52 + 0.3) mΩ = **24.4 W** | 11.3 W |
| 30 A, T_j = 90 °C | 1.53 mΩ | 900 × 3.36 mΩ = **3.0 W** | 1.4 W |

Two corrections:

- **Ripple correction.** Phase current ripple at 48 kHz (motor L ≈ 15–30 µH) adds I²_rms ≈ I² + ΔI²/12. This is under 1 % at 80 A and is neglected.
- **Thermal runaway check.** Because R_DS(on) rises with T_j, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta JC} \cdot P_{FET}(T_j)$$

The loop converges if R_θJC · I² · R_DS,25 · 0.006 < 1. At 80 A: 1.5 × 6400 × 1.1e-3 × 0.006 = 0.063, which is stable.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM leg switches. Each period has one hard turn-on and one turn-off.

| Term | Formula | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss charge | C_oss · V² · f | 1 nF × 635 × 48 k = **0.030 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2 t_dt · f | 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate charge (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f | 2 × 40 nC × 12 × 48 k = **0.046 W** |
| **Total P_sw per channel** | | **≈ 2.37 W** |

Interpretation:

- Switching is about 9 % of conduction at 80 A. Burst losses are conduction-dominated, so lowering f_sw buys little at burst. It helps more at partial throttle.
- Reducing t_r + t_f below about 30 ns raises dV/dt above roughly 1 V/ns. That makes BEMF zero-cross detection noisy on the ESC MCU comparator. Keep R_g at 2.2–4.7 Ω.

### DC-link capacitor ripple

This term is often overlooked:

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)} \;\Rightarrow\; 40\text{ A}_{rms}\text{ at } D = 0.5,\ 80\text{ A}$$

- With an effective ESR of 3 mΩ (for example 4 × 10 µF X7R per channel plus a shared bulk electrolytic): P_cap ≈ 4.8 W per channel at worst case.
- Mandate per-channel local MLCCs within 3 mm of each half-bridge.
- Add an external 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic or polymer on the pigtail.

### Current shunt (shared)

Total current is 4 × 80 = 320 A. A 0.25 mΩ shunt dissipates 320² × 0.25 mΩ = **25.6 W**, which is not viable. Instead:

- Use a 0.1 mΩ shunt with an INA-class amplifier (gain 100+), or a Hall sensor (ACS-class or similar).
- Alternatively, rely on per-ESC telemetry current via EDT.

### 4.2 Lumped RC model

The thermal path is a two-node network: each FET junction connects through R_θJC to the PCB node, and the PCB node connects through R_θPA to ambient.

- **R_θPA, board to ambient:** about 12 K/W in still air; about 3–4 K/W in prop wash at around 8 m/s across a 30×30 mm board.
- **C_PCB:** an 8-layer 2 oz board of about 30×30 mm with 24 FETs, caps and pads.
  - Copper: about 4 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.6 J/K.
  - FR4 and components: about 4 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 4.4 J/K.
  - **C ≈ 6 J/K.**
- **τ_PCB** = R × C ≈ 3.5 × 6 ≈ **21 s** in prop wash.
