# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #17): System Architecture Package

**Scope:** 80A burst conduction and switching loss model, 48 kHz PWM, thermal transient analysis, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation, and reference netlists.

> **Assumptions.** Several parameters were not specified, so I assumed them. Every assumed value is tagged **[A]**. Replace these with your chosen MOSFET datasheet values before signing off. The model is written so you can substitute values directly.

---

## 0. Executive Findings (Read First)

1. **An 80A-per-motor burst on a 20×20 mm nano board is thermally limited to about 0.3–0.6 s**, starting from a 40 °C board. The board has roughly 2 J/K of usable heat capacity, and at 80A × 4 motors it must absorb about 120 W of dissipation. This is a physical limit, not a tuning problem. There are two viable paths:
   - Re-rate to **80A total (≈20A/motor continuous, 35–40A/motor burst)**, which is realistic for 20×20 and 6S.
   - Move to a **30×30 or 30.5×30.5 board** with 5×6 mm PDFN FETs and a heatsink pad.
2. **The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs (AM32 / BLHeli_32), not the H743.** The H743 only emits DShot600 and captures eRPM telemetry. Its timer and DMA budget is unaffected by the ESC PWM frequency.
3. On 6S at 80A, **conduction loss dominates (~85%)**. Switching at 48 kHz adds about 1.7 W per channel. Dead-time body-diode conduction is the largest switching-related term, so dead-time tuning is your best lever.
4. **Recommended motor timer:** TIM1 CH1–CH4 on PE9/PE11/PE13/PE14. This uses one timer, one UP-DMA burst stream, and per-channel CC DMA for bidirectional capture. The H743 DMAMUX makes stream assignment collision-free if SPI IMU DMA is prioritised.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path
With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous (complementary) rectification, at any instant exactly **one high-side and one low-side FET** carry the phase current:
- During PWM on-time: HS(A) + LS(B).
- During off-time: LS(A) freewheel + LS(B).

Either way there are two devices in series. Conduction loss is therefore essentially **independent of duty cycle** under synchronous rectification:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_J)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_J) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)]
$$

For RMS precision with ripple, replace I² with I_RMS² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. At 48 kHz with typical 5–20 µH motor inductance on 6S, ΔI²/12 adds less than 2%, so it is neglected here.

### 2.2 Per-device split
Over one electrical cycle each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time on average, but **thermally it is the instantaneous device that matters during a burst.** The worst-case FET during a commutation sector:

$$
P_{FET,inst} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_J) = 6400 \times 1.5\text{m}\Omega \times k_T
$$

### 2.3 Electro-thermal convergence
R_DS rises with T_J, so solve iteratively:

| T_J (°C) | k_T | P per active FET (W) | P_cond per channel (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 9.6 | 19.2 |
| 75 | 1.225 | 11.8 | 23.5 |
| 100 | 1.34 | 12.8 | 25.7 |
| 125 | 1.45 | 13.9 | 27.8 |

**Design point (T_J ≈ 100 °C): P_cond ≈ 25.7 W per channel.**

### 2.4 PCB copper loss
Phase and bus copper path is about 15 mm effective at 2 oz. With parallel layers, assume an effective R_cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ per channel **[A]**:

$$
P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\text{ W/channel}
$$

The battery-side bus carries the sum of the per-motor battery currents, and that copper is shared. For a 25 mm bus at 0.3 mΩ, 4 motors at about 60A battery-side each (duty 0.75) gives 240A × 240A × 0.3 mΩ ≈ 17 W. **This is a major hidden loss.** The XT pads and bus copper must be massive.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ed leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = 0.968\text{ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output-capacitance loss
$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\text{ W}
$$
This is an energy-based approximation. Use the datasheet E_oss if available.

### 3.3 Reverse recovery
The body diode conducts in dead-time, so Q_rr applies:
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\text{ W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 0.614\text{ W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive
This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs:
$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 2 = 30\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.029\text{ W}
$$
