# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #4): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot DMA Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These results drive the rest of the document:

1. **80 A/channel on a 20×20 mm nano board is not thermally sustainable.** With realistic 40 V MOSFETs, the board dissipates about 135 W at a 4×80 A burst. The adiabatic temperature rise of a nano-sized PCB is about 40–50 K/s, so "80 A burst" means **≤1–1.5 s** before the MOSFETs reach their junction-temperature limit, even when starting at 40 °C. Section 4 derives this.
2. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every real 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel (or a shared ESC MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware on a dedicated MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and commutation belong to the ESC MCU. I have partitioned the design that way.
3. **The component values below are representative, not datasheet-verified.** They describe a generic 40 V, ~1.5 mΩ PDFN5×6 MOSFET and an FD6288-class 3-phase gate driver. Re-run every equation with the datasheet numbers of the parts you actually select. Every formula is written so you can substitute them directly.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (6-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 2.1 Bridge state

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched, one is held low, and one floats. With synchronous rectification:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D·T_sw and the low-side for (1−D)·T_sw. The current is continuous through one switch at all times.
- **Return leg:** the low-side is fully on.

Per ESC channel, the instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

### 2.2 Per-device average

Each phase spends 120° high, 120° low, and 120° floating. Averaged over an electrical revolution, the six FETs share the load roughly equally:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{30.7}{6} \approx 5.1\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak in an active FET is I²R = **15.4 W**. This peak matters for Z_th at low eRPM, where sectors last milliseconds.

### 2.3 Self-consistent R_ds(T_j) correction (optional iteration)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\text{K}$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + \bar P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,jb}$$

Iterate until it converges. If the loop diverges (dP/dT · R_th ≥ 1), you are in thermal runaway territory.

### 2.4 PCB copper loss (frequently overlooked)

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), ρ_Cu(100 °C) ≈ 2.2×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, and a 6 mm wide × 15 mm long current path:

$$R_{trace} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.015}{0.006\times70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.79\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Paralleling 3 layers gives ≈0.26 mΩ. Phase plus bus paths total about 2 path-lengths:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.26\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 3.3\ \text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per ESC channel there is one hard-switched commutation cell: the HS turns on with hard switching, and the LS acts as a synchronous rectifier with body-diode dead-time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times40\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery (LS body diode, transferred into HS at turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 3.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is large at 80 A. Trimming dead-time from 200 ns to 100 ns, if the driver propagation-delay matching allows it, saves about 0.6 W per channel.

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 3.6 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (hot) | 30.7 W | 122.9 W |
| V–I switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + Q_g | 0.16 W | 0.6 W |
| PCB copper | 3.3 W | 13.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈37.3 W** | **≈149 W** |

**Efficiency check:** P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A × D_avg. At full throttle the losses are about 7% of input power, which is plausible for an ESC but enormous in absolute terms for a nano PCB.

**Sensitivity:** Conduction dominates (~82%). Switching frequency is not your lever. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.6 W per channel. **R_ds,on and copper are the levers.**
