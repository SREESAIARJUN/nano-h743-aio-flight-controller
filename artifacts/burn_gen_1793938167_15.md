## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design-point junction temperature of T_j = 115 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 90) = 2.11\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Worked example for a typical 5"-class motor (L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5:

- ΔI_pp ≈ 25.2 × 0.25 / (20 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 6.6 A
- This adds only 3.6 A² to I² = 6400 A², i.e. less than 0.1 %.

Ripple can be neglected at burst current. It matters only at low throttle with low-inductance motors.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution over the 6-step cycle

Each phase leg is high-side active for 1/3 of the electrical period and low-side active for 1/3. With complementary PWM:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R_{ds}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\, I^2 R_{ds} + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}$$

The first low-side term is synchronous freewheeling. The second is the low-side conducting the return current.

At full throttle (D → 1):

$$P_{HS} = P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(80^2)(2.11\times10^{-3}) = 4.50\,\text{W per FET}$$

### 1.4 Per-channel conduction loss

Two FETs are in series with the current at every instant:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{ds} = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.11\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{27.0\ W}$$

### 1.5 Copper and board losses

Assume about 0.4 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel (pads, vias, and 2 oz inner planes):

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.4\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 2.6\,\text{W}$$

The shared battery-input traces carry the sum current, 4 × 80 × D_avg. Their loss lands on the battery pads and **must be modeled separately**: at 320 A, even 0.1 mΩ dissipates about 10 W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Per PWM period there is one turn-on transition and one turn-off transition at current I.
