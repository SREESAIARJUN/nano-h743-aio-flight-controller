# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Power-Stage and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design Model

**Job #40: Architecture and Analysis Package, Rev A**

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Two points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 architecture, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel (or a shared quad MCU) runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an AT32F421, STM32G071, or similar, and that MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The model below keeps this split. The 48 kHz switching analysis applies to the ESC side, and the DMA and timer analysis applies to the H743.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" board cannot be sustained.** Section 3 shows about 90 W of total dissipation at 4 × 80 A. A 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board cannot reject that. The target is valid only as a **burst rating of a few seconds**, with an explicit thermal budget. Recommendations:
   - Use a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint at minimum.
   - Specify 80 A as a burst of 2 s or less (at ~40 °C ambient, ~70 % thermal headroom; see §4.4).
   - Specify continuous current at about 45–55 A.
   - Consider a split stack so ESC heat does not reach the IMU.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Current distribution per FET (6-step, complementary PWM)

In each 60° sector:
- One phase high side is PWM'd at duty D, and that phase's low side conducts the freewheel current (1−D).
- One other phase low side is held fully ON.

Each FET takes part in two of the six sectors per electrical cycle, which is 1/3 of the time. This gives:

$$
\overline{I^2_{HS}} = \frac{D}{3}\,I^2 \qquad
\overline{I^2_{LS}} = \frac{1}{3}\,I^2 + \frac{1-D}{3}\,I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3}\,I^2
$$

$$
P_{cond,HS} = \frac{D}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) \qquad P_{cond,LS} = \frac{2-D}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

**Check:** two FETs always carry I in series, so the total per motor is $P_{motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}$, independent of D. Summing the per-FET terms reproduces this: 3·D/3 + 3·(2−D)/3 = 2. ✔
