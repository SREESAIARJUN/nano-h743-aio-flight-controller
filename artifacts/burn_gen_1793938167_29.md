**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction and switching loss model, 48 kHz PWM, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, reference netlists, and the thermal budget.

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

The brief combines two subsystems, so the boundary needs to be clear first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. It receives eRPM telemetry back on the same wire (bidirectional DShot). Driving 12 half-bridges with deadtime and BEMF sensing directly from the H743 is possible in principle. ArduPilot does not support it, however, so this design uses the standard partitioning.
2. **80 A on a "nano" footprint is burst-only.** The calculations below show this quantitatively. Continuous capability is set by convection and is much lower.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with synchronous (complementary) PWM. At any instant, two phases are active:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) switch conducts for a fraction D of each PWM period. The low-side (LS) switch on the same leg conducts for the remaining (1 − D), acting as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the LS switch is on continuously for the full 120° commutation window.

Each switch therefore conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical period. Exactly one path, HS or LS, is always active in the PWM leg, and the return-leg LS is always on. As a result, two R_ds(on) are always in series with the motor current, regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple correction

The peak-to-peak ripple current is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\, D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}}$$

The worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{40\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 3.28\ \text{A}$$

The RMS current including ripple is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 0.90 = 6400.9\ \text{A}^2$$
