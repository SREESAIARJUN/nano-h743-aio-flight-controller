# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture & Loss Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have access to the Job #37 requirements package, BOM, or stackup. Every number below comes from the **assumed parameters in §0.1**. The equations are general, so you can substitute datasheet values and the results will rescale. Values marked **[VERIFY]** must be checked against real component datasheets or the ArduPilot source before tape-out.

### Architecture

- **FC MCU:** STM32H743 (480 MHz, APB timer clock 240 MHz, or 200 MHz in ArduPilot's default 400 MHz config). It generates bi-directional DShot600 for 4 motors.
- **ESC:** four independent channels, each with its own MCU. AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed; BLHeli_32-class MCUs work the same way. Each channel has a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-MOSFETs, with 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- **Form factor:** "Nano" is assumed to mean a 20×20 mm mount on a ~30×30 mm board (FC/ESC stack or AIO).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

During any 60° commutation sector:
- One phase leg is PWM'd: the high-side (HS) conducts for D, and the low-side (LS) synchronous-rectifies for (1−D).
- One phase leg has its LS held on.
- One phase floats (BEMF sensing).

The motor current I flows through exactly **two channel resistances at all times**:

$$
P_{cond,leg\,A} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{hot}
$$
$$
P_{cond,leg\,B} = I^2 R_{hot}
$$
$$
\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on),25}\,k_T}
$$

With synchronous rectification, this loss is **independent of duty cycle**, which is the key simplification.

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
R_{hot} = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 2.08\,\text{m}\Omega
$$
$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.08\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.6\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

**Self-consistent R_DS(on).** Tj and R_DS(on) depend on each other. Solve by iteration:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006/\text{K}
$$
$$
T_j = T_{pad} + P_{FET}(T_j)\,R_{\theta JC}
$$

The 1.6 factor corresponds to T_j ≈ 125 °C. If the board is held cooler, conduction loss drops roughly in proportion.

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Over one electrical revolution each leg spends 120° HS-sector, 120° LS-sector, and 120° floating. With D ≈ 1 at full throttle, conduction is approximately uniform:

$$
\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 4.4\ \text{W (average)},\qquad \hat P_{cond,FET} = I^2R_{hot} = 13.3\ \text{W (peak, during its sector)}
$$

The electrical period is short (≈1–3 ms at full RPM) compared with the junction time constant (≈10s of ms), so **T_j follows the average plus a small ripple**.

### 1.4 Copper Losses (Often Larger Than Expected)

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\,\ell}{w\,t},\qquad \rho_{Cu}(100°C)=2.2\times10^{-8}\,\Omega\text{m}
$$

**Example: phase output path.** Length 8 mm, width 4 mm, 4 layers paralleled of 2 oz (70 µm) copper:

$$
R = \frac{2.2\times10^{-8}\times0.008}{0.004\times(4\times70\times10^{-6})} = 0.157\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

The battery bus and ground return have similar magnitude. A budget of ~0.4 mΩ total gives:

$$
P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.6\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches hard: one HS FET hard-switches, and the LS FET commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V-I Overlap (Hard Switching, HS FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output Capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 25\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.030\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery (LS Body Diode → HS Turn-On)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

At 80 A, Q_rr increases with di/dt and I_F. Plan for 2–3× this value: ~0.1 W.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

This is the second-largest switching term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns halves it, but first verify shoot-through margin on the scope.

### 2.5 Gate Drive (Dissipated in Driver + R_g)

$$
P_{g} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 30\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k}\times2 = 0.029\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.6 Switching Total

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 1.94 + 0.03 + 0.10 + 0.61 + 0.03 \approx \mathbf{2.7\ W}
$$

**Frequency scaling:** P_sw ∝ f_sw. At 24 kHz it would be ≈1.35 W. At 96 kHz it would be ≈5.4
