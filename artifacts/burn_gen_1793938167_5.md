## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 General form

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. During the PWM on-time the current path is high-side FET (phase A) → motor → low-side FET (phase B). During the off-time, synchronous rectification freewheels the current through the complementary low-side FET. So at every instant, exactly **two FETs carry I_ph**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{25}
$$

The distribution across FETs depends on D. Over one electrical cycle each of the 6 FETs is in the active pair for 1/3 of the time:

$$
I_{rms,HS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{rms,LS} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}\left(1 + (1-D)\right)}\ \text{(commutated LS + freewheel share)}
$$

Under the dominant (worst-case) assumption, per-FET loss is:

$$
P_{FET,max} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)\cdot 2 \;\;\text{(low side at low D carries the most)}
$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

$$
R_{hot} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 2.3 Copper (PCB trace and via) loss

Assume an effective phase-path resistance of 0.4 mΩ per channel (planes, vias, motor pad):

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} \approx 5.1\,\text{W}
$$

This is shared, so I'll fold about half of it into board heating. The **DC-link bus**, with 320 A total through the input pads, adds roughly 320² × 0.1 mΩ ≈ 10 W if all four channels burst together. This is frequently underestimated.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With trapezoidal complementary PWM, only **one half-bridge switches per commutation sector**. That gives one hard-switched turn-on/turn-off pair plus one synchronous-rectifier transition per PWM period.

### 3.1 V-I overlap (hard switching)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 3.2 Output capacitance

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.307\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors)

$$
P_{g} = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 50\text{n}\times 10 \times 48\text{k}\times 2 = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

### 3.6 Total per channel

$$
P_{sw,ch} = 0.968 + 0.036 + 0.048 + 0.307 + 0.048 \approx \mathbf{1.41\ W}
$$

**Observations:**
- At D = 100% (full throttle), switching losses vanish. The worst combined case is D ≈ 0.85–0.95 at near-peak current.
- Dead time is the second-largest term. Tightening t_dt from 50 to 30 ns saves about 0.12 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation mismatch first.
- Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw, but this is a minor lever compared with conduction loss.
