# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #47)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Note on Architecture

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU. It runs ArduPilot and drives four ESC channels over bi-directional DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32, because commutation, BEMF sensing and 48 kHz PWM generation do not belong on the H743 when ArduPilot owns it.

The 80 A figure is analysed as a **burst** rating. The thermal model in §3 shows that no nano-format board can sustain it continuously.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In six-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The **PWM phase** passes current through either its high-side FET (on-time, D) or its low-side FET (off-time, 1−D, synchronous freewheel).
- The **return phase** passes current through its low-side FET continuously.

So, ignoring dead time, there are always exactly **two FETs in series** with the motor current:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

| Quantity | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET | I²R | 9.28 W |
| Average per FET over one electrical revolution | 18.56 / 6 | 3.09 W |
| Worst single FET (low side at D → 0 during a commutation sector) | I²R × (1/3 duty) × 2 sectors | ~6.2 W short-term |

### 1.4 PCB copper conduction

Assume 0.5 mΩ of effective trace and via resistance per motor path (2 oz, 8-layer, phase pads plus the bus plane):

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ W$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Each PWM period contains one hard-switched turn-on and one turn-off on the PWM phase.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 25\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.21\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.4 C_oss charge and discharge

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.015\ W$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two complementary FETs switch per period:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.048\ W$$

Most of this is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.
