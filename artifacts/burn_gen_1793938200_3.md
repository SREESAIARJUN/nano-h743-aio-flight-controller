# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #29)

**Scope:** Loss model at 80 A burst and 48 kHz, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 timer/DMA allocation, reference netlists, and thermal budget.

**Status:** Engineering derivation. All component values are design-intent assumptions that must be verified against current datasheets before layout.

---

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility

Two points need to be settled before any calculation, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no sensorless BLDC/FOC motor-control path. The working architecture is:
   - **FC side:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
   - **ESC side:** 4× AM32-class MCUs (STM32G071 or AT32F421), each with a 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

2. **80 A on a nano (20×20 mm) board is burst-only, measured in seconds.** Section 3 shows the board's thermal mass gives roughly **1.5–2 s** at full 4×80 A before limits. Realistic continuous capability is **about 20–30 A per channel**. The firmware current limit and ArduPilot `MOT_*` / battery-failsafe settings must enforce this.

**Recommended form factor:** stacked 20×20 mm, two boards.
- **Top board:** FC (H743 in TFBGA100, IMU, baro, OSD, regulators).
- **Bottom board:** 4-in-1 ESC.

A single-board AIO would place a 480 MHz MCU and the IMU next to more than 100 W of transient dissipation. That causes gyro thermal drift and exceeds the H7's ambient limit.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, exactly **two MOSFETs** (one high-side, one low-side) are in the current path at any instant. Each MOSFET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period.

For one MOSFET, the RMS current (ignoring ripple) is:

$$I_{RMS,FET} = I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} \;\Rightarrow\; P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{6400 \times 0.0024}{3} = 5.12\ \text{W}$$

For the whole channel (always two FETs in the path):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0024 = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

During complementary PWM, the low-side FET replaces the high-side FET for the off-interval. The current magnitude is the same, so the expression holds independent of duty cycle.
