## 0. Scope and Assumptions

This baseline rests on four architectural points.

1. **The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC commutation MCU.** The 48 kHz power-stage PWM is generated by a per-motor ESC MCU, for example AT32F421 running AM32 or an STM32G071 running BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600. Running FOC/6-step for four motors directly on the H743 is possible but unsupported by ArduPilot, so it is not assumed here.
2. **Topology.** Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification and complementary PWM on the active leg.
3. **"80 A burst"** means 80 A RMS phase current per motor for at most 5 s, with all four motors loaded simultaneously as the worst case.
4. **I have no prior context for Job #68.** All MOSFET and driver parameters below are representative placeholders. Substitute your BOM datasheet values into the equations; the model structure holds regardless.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In six-step operation with synchronous rectification, the current path always contains exactly two conducting FETs: one high-side and one low-side, in different legs. The only exception is the dead-time interval, which is handled in §2.3.

**Junction-temperature-dependent resistance:**

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + k·(T_j − 25)], with k ≈ 0.006 /K, so α_R ≈ 1.6 at 125 °C.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

P_cond,motor = I_ph² · (2·R_ds(T_j) + R_pcb)

At 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

- FETs: 6400 × (2 × 1.2 mΩ) = **15.36 W**
- PCB copper: 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.20 W** (deposited into the board, not the junctions)

**Per-FET distribution.** Each leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical period. On the PWM leg, the HS/LS share splits by duty D. At full-throttle burst (D → 1), the steady-state hottest FET is a high-side device:

- Instantaneous: I²·R_ds = **7.68 W** while conducting
- Average: P_FET,avg ≈ 7.68 × (1/3) ≈ **2.56 W**

The electrical frequency is roughly 1–3 kHz, which is far shorter than the junction-to-case thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms). The averaged value is therefore valid for computing T_j ripple, and the residual ripple is about 2–4 K.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-Switching (V·I Overlap) Loss

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

- Turn-on: I_g,on = (V_drv − V_pl) / (R_drv,src + R_g,ext + R_g,int) = 6.5 / 5 = **1.30 A**
- Turn-off: I_g,off = V_pl / (R_drv,snk + R_g,ext + R_g,int) = 3.5 / 3.5 = **1.00 A**

Transition times:

- t_on = Q_sw / I_g,on = 15 nC / 1.30 A = **11.5 ns**
- t_off = 15 nC / 1.00 A = **15.0 ns**

Overlap loss:

P_VI = ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw
   = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 26.5 ns × 48 kHz = **1.28 W**

This loss lands on the active (control) FET of the PWMed leg.

### 2.2 Capacitive and Reverse-Recovery Loss

- Reverse recovery: P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W**
- Output capacitance: P_oss ≈ ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw ≈ **0.024 W**

### 2.3 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

P_dt = V_SD · I_ph · (2·t_dead) · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 400 ns × 48 k = **1.23 W**

This term is comparable to the overlap loss. **Tightening dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor.** Validate shoot-through margin first, using the driver's minimum dead-time and the FET's t_d(off) at hot temperature.

### 2.4 Gate-Drive Loss

Per switching FET: P_g = Q_g · V_drv · f_sw = 60 nC × 10 × 48 k = **0.029 W**

This is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g. Two FETs are active per motor, giving about 0.06 W.

### 2.5 Switching Total per Motor

P_sw,motor ≈ 1.28 + 0.048 + 0.024 + 1.23 + 0.06 ≈ **2.64 W**

### 2.6 Frequency Sensitivity

P_sw ∝ f_sw. For comparison:

| f_sw | P_sw per motor | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| 24 kHz | ≈ 1.3 W | More current ripple and acoustic noise |
| 48 kHz | ≈ 2.6 W | Baseline |
| 96 kHz | ≈ 5.3 W | |

| Contributor | Per motor | ×4 motors |

| Switching + dead-time | 2.64 W | 10.6 W |

| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if inline per motor) | 1.60 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.8 W** | **≈ 91 W** |

Converter efficiency is about 96 % at full output. At 91 W of heat, the problem is entirely thermal, not electrical.
