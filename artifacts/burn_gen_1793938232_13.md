# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architecture Corrections

This document contains derived engineering estimates. Every number depends on the stated assumptions. Replace the placeholder MOSFET and driver parameters with datasheet values from your actual BOM before freezing the design.

Three architecture points need correcting before the math is useful.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In any 4-in-1 ESC, each channel is driven by its own ESC MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32) through a 3-phase gate driver. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to those MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The switching-loss model therefore belongs to the ESC side.
2. **"80 A" must be defined.** I model **80 A phase (motor) current per channel**. This is the worst case for conduction. Battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I_ph.
3. **An 80 A, 4-channel stage on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is a burst-only rating of roughly 1 to 2 seconds.** Section 3 proves this. It is a physics limit, not a design-margin issue.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any 60° sector, phase *a* is PWM'd high and phase *b* is held low:

- **On-interval (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a and LS_b.
- **Freewheel interval (1 − D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous) and LS_b.
