# Job #53: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC Power Stage, Thermal, Switching and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Design Assumptions

Several things in the brief need clarifying before any math is meaningful.

### 0.1 Partitioning: the H743 does not commutate motors

An STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. It does not commutate the motors. A "4-in-1 ESC" contains four separate ESC MCUs, one per motor, each running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 or GD32E230. Each ESC MCU drives a three-phase gate driver and six MOSFETs.

The design is therefore split into two domains:

```
[ STM32H743 + ArduPilot ] --4x bidir DShot600 (single-wire, open-drain-ish w/ pull-up)--> [ 4x ESC MCU (AM32) ]
        |                                                                                      |
   IMU/Baro/OSD/etc.                                                       4x (gate driver + 6 MOSFET bridge)
```

The 48 kHz figure is the ESC PWM frequency, which is set in AM32. It has nothing to do with the H743 timers.

### 0.2 "80A burst" on a nano form factor

I interpret "80A burst" as peak motor phase current per channel. The math below shows this is thermally achievable only for short bursts (seconds) on a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board.

Battery-side current at 80 A phase and high duty would also be very large: 4 × ~64 A ≈ 256 A at the XT connector. No nano connector, wire gauge or bulk capacitor can carry that. Treat 80 A as a per-channel transient rating. Size the continuous rating separately (§2.6).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, current at every instant flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

- **PWM on-time:** high-side of the PWM leg, then the low-side of the "sink" leg.
- **PWM off-time:** low-side of the PWM leg (synchronous rectification), then the low-side of the sink leg.

So, neglecting the dead-time intervals handled in §2:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

For trapezoidal drive at constant torque, I_ph,rms ≈ I_ph during conduction. Use 80 A as the worst case.
