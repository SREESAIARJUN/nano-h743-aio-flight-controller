**Red flags to resolve before layout:**

1. **80 A on a 20×20 "nano" footprint is a transient-only rating.** This model shows about 100 W total dissipation at 4×80 A, against a board heat capacity of only about 4–5 J/K. Burst duration will be measured in seconds (§2.5). Market it as "80 A burst (≤1 s)" and design for 40–50 A continuous at most.
2. **Battery current.** 4×80 A phase current at high duty is about 250–320 A from the battery. That exceeds what XT60 connectors, pads and 12 AWG leads can handle, so the battery side will limit before the FETs do.
3. **Architecture.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel (or a single ESC MCU running 4 channels) runs its own firmware (AM32 or BLHeli_32-class) and generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 only produces bidirectional DShot600. These two clock domains are modeled separately below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (AM32/BLHeli_32 default, complementary PWM on the active leg):

- One leg is PWM-switched (high-side and low-side alternate with duty D).
- One leg is held low-side ON.
- One leg floats (used for BEMF sensing).

At every instant the phase current flows through **exactly two FETs in series**: one in the PWM leg (HS or LS, depending on PWM state) and one in the static low leg.

Per-channel conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.71\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Worst-case single-FET loss

This determines the hot spot. The static low-side FET carries the full current for its 120° commutation interval:

$$P_{FET,LS,static} = I^2 R_{DS} = 6400 \times 1.71\text{ m}\Omega = 10.9\text{ W (during its 1/3 window)}$$
