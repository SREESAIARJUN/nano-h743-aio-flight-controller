# Job #7: STM32H743 Nano AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC), Architecture & Math Model

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Two findings change the scope of this job:

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC commutation controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743, and the H743 sends bidirectional DShot to the ESCs. Each motor channel needs its own dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7, which is AM32-supported. The 48 kHz figure is the AM32 PWM frequency on those MCUs, not on the H7.
2. **80 A burst per motor on a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally non-viable** beyond about 1–2 s, as shown in §2.4. The model below quantifies this and derives a defensible rating.

**Unverified assumptions:** All MOSFET parameters are representative values for a 40 V DFN3.3×3.3 device (Rds(on) ≈ 1.5 mΩ @ 25 °C). Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before sign-off.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

AM32 uses 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phase legs carry the current I, and one FET conducts in each leg: the high-side for duty D, the low-side for 1 − D. The duty terms sum to 1:

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D)}
$$

$$
R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.4\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Add copper trace and plane resistance: about 0.3 mΩ per current path → 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ = **3.8 W**.

### 2.3 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The other active leg is held static for the commutation step.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48k = **0.97 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f | 50 nC·25.2·48k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f ×2 FETs | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f | 0.8·80·200 ns·48k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | Q_g·V_GS·f ×2 | **0.04 W** (in driver) |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 1.7 W per motor** |

Dead-time loss is about 36 % of switching loss. Tuning AM32 dead-time down to the minimum safe value is worthwhile.

**Total per motor at 80 A:** 30.7 + 3.8 + 1.7 ≈ **36 W**
**4 motors at 80 A:** **≈ 145 W**

### 2.4 Transient Thermal Model (Burst Duration)

Use a lumped two-node model: junction → board (Zth_jc) and board → ambient.

Board heat capacity for 25.5×25.5×1.6 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper:
- FR4: 1.04 cm³ × 1.9 J/cm³K ≈ 2.0 J/K
- Cu: 8 × 6.5 cm² × 70 µm ≈ 0.36 cm³ × 3.45 J/cm³K ≈ 1.25 J/K
- FETs, caps and solder: about 0.5 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 3.8 J/K**

Board-to-ambient resistance with prop-wash airflow: R_th,ba ≈ 4 K/W, giving τ = 15 s.

$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) = P\, R_{th}\,(1 - e^{-t/\tau})
$$

At P = 145 W, the board initially heats at dT/dt ≈ 145 / 3.8 ≈ **38 K/s**. Starting at 40 °C ambient with a 110 °C board limit (70 K headroom):

$$
t_{max} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{70}{145\cdot4}\right) \approx \mathbf{1.9\ s}\ \text{(all 4 motors)}
$$

The junction sits above the board by P_FET·Zth_jc. Per FET this is about 15 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ 23 K, which pushes T_j to about 133 °C. That is at or above typical derating limits.

### 2.5 Derived Rating

Steady-state limit with ΔT = 70 K and R_th = 4 K/W is P_total = 17.5 W, or 4.4 W per motor. Solving 2I²(2.4 mΩ + 0.3 mΩ) + 0.02·I ≈ 4.4 W gives:

| Rating | Value |
|---|---|
| Continuous, all 4 motors | **~28 A per motor** |
| Burst, all 4 motors | **~45 A for 5 s** (I ∝ √P scaling with the transient factor) |
| 80 A | Only as a ≤1 s single-motor transient, if the board is cool beforehand |

**Recommendation:** Rate the board as 30 A continuous / 45 A burst. To reach 80 A, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board with 2 FETs in parallel per switch. Doubling the FETs halves Rds(on) per switch, which halves conduction loss, and also adds area and thermal mass.

### 2.6 Input Capacitor Ripple

Per motor, I_cap,rms = I·√(D(1−D)), which peaks at D = 0.5: 80 × 0.5 = **40 A rms**. Four channels running asynchronously add as root-sum-square: **≈ 80 A rms worst case**. This calls for:
- **20× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1206 ceramics**, about 3 A each, placed adjacent to each half-bridge.
- **One external low-ESR 470 µF/35 V electrolytic** on the pads for the inductive surge from battery leads.
