## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This value of α is typical for 40 V trench FETs (R_ds roughly ×1.45–1.5 at 125 °C).

### 1.2 Six-step current path with complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A (PWM phase):** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw, and the low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Phase B (sink phase):** the low-side FET is on for 100 % of the sector.
- **Phase C:** floating (used for BEMF sensing).

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM phase and 1/3 as the sink phase. Averaged over a full electrical cycle, the per-FET losses are:

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R\,D}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R\,[(1-D) + 1]}{3} = \frac{I^2R\,(2-D)}{3}$$

Summing over three legs:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge} = 3\left[\frac{I^2RD}{3} + \frac{I^2R(2-D)}{3}\right] = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)}$$

The total is independent of duty cycle, because exactly two channels are always in the current path. What duty cycle changes is how the loss is distributed between high-side and low-side FETs. At low throttle the low-side FETs run hottest, approaching 2/3 of I²R each as D approaches 0.

### 1.3 Worked example (80 A, T_j = 110 °C)

$$R_{ds}(110) = 1.2\,[1 + 0.005\cdot 85] = 1.71\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.71\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Copper path loss** (often forgotten). Assume 2 oz copper (≈0.25 mΩ/sq at 100 °C), roughly 3 squares effective from pad to FET to phase pad, and 4 paralleled layers:

$$R_{cu} \approx 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega \cdot 3 / 4 \approx 0.19\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cu} \approx 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.19\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 2.4\ \text{W per motor}$$

Add solder-joint and battery-lead resistance separately.

### 1.4 Shunt loss (if using a per-ESC shunt)

$$P_{shunt} = I_{bus,rms}^2\,R_{sh}$$

The bus RMS current is approximately I·√D. At D = 0.95 with a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$P_{shunt} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.95 \cdot 0.25\ \text{m}\Omega = 1.5\ \text{W}$$
