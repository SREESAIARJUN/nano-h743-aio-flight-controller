# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #28): Architecture, Loss Model and Timer Allocation

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules

Three assumptions shape everything below.

1. **The H743 runs ArduPilot only.** Each ESC channel uses its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Commutation at 48 kHz with BEMF zero-cross detection cannot share a core with ArduPilot's scheduler. A single AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor is the industry norm.
2. **80 A is treated as a short burst, not a continuous rating.** On a 20×20 or 30.5×30.5 board, the thermal analysis below shows 80 A on all four channels is sustainable for about **2–3 s**, not continuously. The rating should be published as a burst figure.
3. **Electrical baseline:**
   - 6S supply (25.2 V max)
   - Trapezoidal 6-step drive with synchronous rectification
   - Center-aligned PWM at f_sw = 48 kHz
   - 40 V N-channel MOSFETs in PDFN3.3×3.3

**Reference MOSFET parameters** (substitute the datasheet values of the chosen part):

| Param | Value | Param | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) max @25 °C, V_GS=10 V | 1.3 mΩ | Q_g total | 50 nC |
| Temperature coefficient α | ~0.0045 /K | Q_gs2 + Q_gd (Q_sw) | 12 nC |
| V_plateau | 3.5 V | Q_rr (body diode) | 40 nC |
| R_g internal | 1.0 Ω | Q_oss | 30 nC |
| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V | R_θJC (bottom) | 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-corrected resistance

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 1.3\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1+0.0045(125-25)] = 1.885 \approx 1.9\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Add a PCB copper and solder allowance of about 0.1 mΩ per FET, giving an effective **R_eff ≈ 2.0 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in 6-step drive with synchronous rectification

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase (A) is PWM'd high and one phase (B) is held low:

- **On-state (duration D):** current flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- **Freewheel (duration 1−D):** current flows through LS_A (synchronous rectification) and LS_B.

Two R_DS(on) are therefore in series at all times, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{eff} = 2(80)^2(0.002) = \mathbf{25.6\,W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts about 1/3 of the time. The PWM'd leg splits its share between HS and LS by D.

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{25.6}{6} \approx 4.3\,\text{W}$$

The instantaneous loss in a conducting FET is I²R = **12.8 W**.

**Ripple check at 30k eRPM:**
- Electrical period: 2 ms
- Each FET conduction window: ~667 µs
- Die thermal time constant: ~1–5 ms

The die therefore partially follows the ripple. Add about **+5–8 K of junction ripple** on top of the average-based estimate.

### 1.4 Phase-current ripple correction

Phase inductance for a typical 5" motor is about 15 µH. The worst-case ripple occurs at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V(1-D)D}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{15\mu \times 48k} \approx 8.75\,\text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 6.4 \Rightarrow +0.1\,\%$$

This correction is negligible at 48 kHz, which is one justification for the high switching frequency.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each switching event is one hard-switched turn-on (HS) plus one turn-off.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Gate drive is 10 V through R_g,ext = 4.7 Ω:

$$I_{g} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}} = \frac{10-3.5}{5.7} = 1.14\,\text{A}$$

$$t_{sw} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.14\,\text{A}} = 10.5\,\text{ns}\ \ (\text{each edge}) \Rightarrow t_r+t_f \approx 21\,\text{ns}$$
