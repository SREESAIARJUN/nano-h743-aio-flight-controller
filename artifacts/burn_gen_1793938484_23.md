### Architectural flag (read before layout)

The H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It should not also commutate the four motors. Each ESC channel needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 firmware, which supports bidirectional DShot and EDT. A common choice is an STM32G071 or AT32F421.

The H743 talks to those ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This split is required for the DShot model in §5 to make sense, and it is the industry-standard partition.

### Physical-feasibility flag

80 A per motor on a 30.5 mm board can only be a **burst** rating (≤ ~5 s, see §4). Continuous ratings on this footprint are realistically 25–35 A per channel. Market it accordingly, or derate.

## 1. Commutation Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

AM32 uses trapezoidal six-step commutation. Each 60° electrical sector has three legs:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** HS switches at duty D; the complementary LS synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Leg B (return leg):** LS is held fully ON.
- **Leg C (floating leg):** OFF; BEMF zero-cross sensing happens here.

Every leg spends 120° as a PWM leg, 120° as a return leg and 120° floating. That gives the per-FET conduction duty fractions:

$$
\delta_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}, \qquad \delta_{LS} = \frac{(1-D)}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{2-D}{3}
$$

At any instant exactly **two** channels are in the current path, so per motor:

$$
\sum \delta = 2 \quad\text{(FET-equivalents conducting)}
$$

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 General form

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \cdot \delta
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow 1.5\times \text{ at } 110^\circ\text{C})
$$

Phase current ripple at 48 kHz is small relative to 80 A for typical motor inductance
