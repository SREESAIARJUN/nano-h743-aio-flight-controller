# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Truth

Three facts constrain the whole model.

1. **The H743 cannot commutate the motors under ArduPilot.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It emits DShot commands and does not run FOC or 6-step commutation. The realistic topology is an H743 flight controller plus four independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421-class running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class), all sharing one PCB stack. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency. The H743 only handles DShot600 and the bidirectional eRPM return.
2. **80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" form factor is a burst-only rating.** The model below shows it is thermally sustainable for about 1 s with all four motors loaded. Continuous capability is an order of magnitude lower. A 320 A aggregate draw also implies battery leads and pads that a 20×20 board can barely accommodate.
3. **All device parameters below are stated assumptions** (a representative 40 V, ~1 mΩ-class DFN MOSFET). Replace them with your BOM's datasheet values. Every equation is parametric so you can re-run it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- In any 60° sector, two phases conduct. One high-side FET is PWM'd with its complementary low-side synchronous rectifier (SR). One low-side FET is held fully ON.
- The current therefore always passes through **two series FET channels**. Either path is HS(D) + LS_on or SR(1−D) + LS_on.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2\,\big(2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{cu}\big)$$
