## 0. Architectural Correction and Scope

The H743 should not drive the power stage directly. ArduPilot does not run sensorless BLDC commutation, and 48 kHz six-step control for four motors is the job of dedicated ESC MCUs. The architecture below is the only one that is both realistic and supported by ArduPilot:

```
                     ┌──────────────── FC domain (3V3) ────────────────┐
 IMU(SPI)  Baro(I2C) │  STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz  — ArduPilot           │
 OSD/SD/UARTs        │  TIM3/TIM4 → 4× bi-dir DShot600 (single wire)    │
                     └───────┬────────┬────────┬────────┬──────────────┘
                             │M1      │M2      │M3      │M4   (33 Ω series, 3V3)
                     ┌───────▼──┐ ┌───▼────┐ ┌─▼──────┐ ┌▼───────┐
 ESC domain          │AT32F421  │ │AT32F421│ │AT32F421│ │AT32F421│  AM32 firmware
 (VBAT, 4–6S)        │48 kHz PWM│ │        │ │        │ │        │  (or BLHeli_32 class)
                     └───┬──────┘ └──┬─────┘ └──┬─────┘ └──┬─────┘
                     3-ph driver   …         …         …
                     6× N-FET      …         …         …
```

**Feasibility flag (stated up front).** As shown in §3, 80 A per motor on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 nano footprint is a burst rating measured in seconds. It is not a continuous rating. The math below quantifies that limit precisely, so marketing numbers do not outrun physics.

## 2. Loss Model: Single ESC, Six-Step Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In six-step commutation, two phase legs carry current during each 60° sector:

- One leg is held "on" (high-side or low-side, static).
- The other leg is PWM'd complementarily: the HS for duty D, the LS for (1−D).

At any instant, exactly two FETs are in series with the current path. Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical period (split HS/LS on the PWM leg).

**Temperature-corrected resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

**Total ESC conduction loss** (current path always passes through 2 FETs, independent of D with sync rectification):

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

**Numbers at T_j = 110 °C** (factor 1.383):

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.383\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.7\ W}$$

**PCB copper.** Phase and bus traces, about 0.3 mΩ effective per path in 2 oz inner plus outer layers on a nano board:

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

> **Sensitivity:** conduction loss scales with I² and is ~90 % of total loss. Halving R_ds (paralleled FETs) beats any switching-frequency optimization.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Hard switching occurs on one FET per transition (HS turn-on/off against LS body diode).

**V–I overlap:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs; dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.19\ W}$$

### 2.3 Per-ESC and board totals at 80 A burst

| Term | Per ESC (W) | 4× ESC (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.7 | 70.8 |
| Copper | 1.9 | 7.6 |
| Switching + dead time + gate | 2.2 | 8.8 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ shared bus sense) | — | 1.6 (at 80 A total; 25.6 W if 320 A!) |
| **Total** | **≈21.8** | **≈87–110** |

Two implications follow:

- **Shunt sizing.** A single bus shunt at 4×80 A is untenable on a nano board. Use per-ESC 0.25 mΩ shunts (1.6 W each at 80 A, 2512 or 3-terminal metal strip). Alternatively, use phase-current estimation from the ESC MCU.
- **Frequency trade.** Dropping f_sw from 48 → 24 kHz saves about 1.0 W per ESC (≈4.5 %). It is a marginal gain with an acoustic and current-ripple penalty, so 48 kHz is justified.

**Per-FET average dissipation** (6 FETs, conduction split evenly over the electrical cycle):

$$P_{FET} \approx \frac{17.7 + 2.2}{6} \approx \mathbf{3.3\ W}$$

The switching FET at the moment of commutation sees up to 1.45 W more transiently.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Cauer-reduced to 3 nodes)

```
P_FET ──► [Tj] ──R_jc 1.2 K/W── [Tc] ──R_cb 3.0 K/W── [T_board] ──R_ba── [Ta]
             │C_j 0.02 J/K          │C_c 0.15 J/K           │C_board
```

- **R_cb:** FET case to local copper through vias. Use ≥ 9× 0.3 mm filled vias under the drain pad.
- **R_ba:** board to ambient for the whole 4-in-1. Values: ≈ 6 K/W still air, ≈ 1.5–2.5 K/W in prop wash at ~10 m/s.
- **C_board:** 25.5×25.5 mm, 1.6 mm, 6-layer 2 oz, plus 24 FETs and caps. Estimate ≈ 4.5 g × 0.7 J/(g·K) ≈ **3.2 J/K**.

### 3.2 Steady state (proves 80 A is not continuous)

Board rise in prop wash at R_ba = 2 K/W:

$$\Delta T_{ba} = 87\text{ W} \times 2 = 174\text{ K}$$

Junction temperature:

$$T_j = 40 + 174 + 3.3 \times (1.2 + 3.0) \approx \mathbf{228\ °C}$$

**Result: not viable.** For T_j ≤ 125 °C the continuous capability is:

$$P_{board,max} \approx \frac{125 - 40}{2 + 4.2/24 \cdot \text{(local)}} \Rightarrow \approx 35\ \text{W total}$$

$$I_{cont} \approx 80 \sqrt{35/87} \approx \mathbf{\approx 50\ A\ per\ ESC}$$

This assumes all four motors are loaded equally and the board sits in good prop wash. A realistic continuous label is **≈ 40–45 A**.
