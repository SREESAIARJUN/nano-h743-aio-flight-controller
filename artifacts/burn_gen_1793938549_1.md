**Design-review flag.** 80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 is at the edge of physics. The analysis below shows it is survivable only as a short burst (≈2–3 s), and only with ≥ 2 oz copper on most layers. It is not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side Q_AH conducts for D·T_sw. The low side Q_AL freewheels synchronously for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Leg B (static leg):** the low side Q_BL is on 100% of the sector.

With phase current I (flat-top, ripple ΔI):

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

$$P_{cond,AH} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS,hot} D \qquad P_{cond,AL} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS,hot}(1-D) \qquad P_{cond,BL} = I_{rms}^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,channel} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS,hot} \quad \text{(independent of } D\text{)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature coefficient of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,\left(1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right), \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS,hot = 1.0 × 1.5 = 1.5 mΩ.

This creates a positive-feedback (thermal runaway) loop. Solve iteratively in Section 4.

### 1.3 Ripple current

Motor phase inductance L_ph ≈ 15 µH, so line-to-line inductance is 30 µH. Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{LL}\, f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{30\,\mu\mathrm{H} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz}} = 4.4\ \mathrm{A}$$

$$\frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 1.6\ \mathrm{A^2}$$

This is negligible against 6400 A², so I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

| Element | Equation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Static low side (Q_BL), worst FET | 80² × 1.5 mΩ | **9.6 W** |
| PWM leg pair (Q_AH + Q_AL) | 80² × 1.5 mΩ | 9.6 W |
| MOSFET conduction per channel | | **19.2 W** |
| PCB copper (≈0.5 mΩ total path: pads, vias, phase pad) | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| **Conduction total per channel** | | **22.4 W** |

**Mitigation lever:** two FETs in parallel per switch halve R_DS, and therefore halve FET conduction loss to ≈9.6 W per channel. That requires 24 FETs on the board. On a 20×20 board this is usually impossible; on a 30.5×30.5 board it is standard.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg. Q_AH hard-switches; Q_AL commutates via its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V·I overlap (Q_AH)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 1.45\ \mathrm{W}$$

Transition times, from the Miller plateau:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,tot}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With R_g,tot = 5 Ω and V_plateau = 4.5 V, this gives t_r ≈ 9 ns intrinsic. Add di/dt limiting from about 2 nH of loop inductance to reach ≈15 ns.

### 2.2 Output capacitance (Q_AH turn-on discharges C_oss of both FETs in the leg)

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 2\,\mathrm{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.06\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Body-diode dead-time conduction (Q_AL)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_d\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\,\mathrm{ns} \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.31\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery (diode Q_rr dumped into Q_AH)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\,\mathrm{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.05\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver plus R_g, not in the FET die)

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g\, V_{drv}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\,\mathrm{nC} \times 10 \times 48\,\mathrm{kHz} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$
