## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in six-step commutation

In any commutation sector, exactly two phases carry the full phase current I. Under complementary PWM with duty D:

- **PWM leg (e.g., phase A):** the high side conducts for fraction D. The low side conducts (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D.
- **Return leg (e.g., phase B):** the low side is held ON for the whole sector.

So at every instant, current passes through **two** R_DS(on) in series, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}
$$

### 1.2 Hot R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\cdot\left(\frac{T_j+273}{298}\right)^{\alpha}\approx R_{DS,25}\cdot k_T,\qquad \alpha\approx 2.3
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, k_T ≈ 1.5, so R_DS,hot = 1.5 mΩ.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\text{ mΩ} = 19.2\text{ W}
$$

**Worst single-FET stress.** This is the return-leg low side, or the PWM high side at D → 1:

$$
P_{FET,max} = I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 9.6\text{ W}
$$

Commutation rotates this stress around the six FETs, so time-averaged per-FET loss is about 1/3 of the per-motor total. Size junction checks for the instantaneous 9.6 W, because one electrical period at low eRPM can be several ms.

### 1.4 PCB copper and interconnect loss

Assumed path resistance per motor (battery pad → bridge → motor pad and return): R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ for 2 oz with parallel layers.

$$
\rho_{Cu}(T)=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.00393(T-20)]
$$

$$
P_{Cu,motor}=I^2 R_{Cu}=6400\times0.3\text{ mΩ}\approx 1.9\text{ W}
$$

Shared battery-input copper carries 4 × I_avg. At 4 × 80 A = 320 A with R_bus ≈ 0.1 mΩ, that adds about 10 W. Note that the battery current is D × I_phase, not I_phase, so this is a worst case at full throttle.

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM-leg pair hard-switches. The return leg is static within a sector.

| Loss term | Equation | Value @ 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard turn-on and turn-off) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | 0.5·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.97 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD · I · 2·t_dead · f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | 2 · ½ · C_oss(er) · V² · f_sw | 1 nF·635·48 k = **0.03 W** |
| Gate drive (two switching FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_DRV · f_sw | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.05 W** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_G) |
| **Total P_sw per motor** | | **≈ 1.72 W** |

**Sensitivity notes:**

- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 96 kHz it becomes about 3.4 W per motor.
- If you double R_G to reduce EMI and ringing, t_r and t_f roughly double, so overlap loss reaches about 1.9 W.
- The 48 kHz choice means switching loss is about 8 % of total. Conduction dominates, so effort spent on R_DS(on) and copper pays off far more than effort spent on switching speed.

Efficiency check: at 80 A, D ≈ 0.95, and 25.2 V, input power is about 4 × 1.9 kW ≈ 7.7 kW. Losses are about 1.2 % of that. Efficiency is fine; the problem is heat density.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady-state network per FET

```
Tj ──[Rθjc ≈ 1.5 K/W]── Tc ──[Rθ_pad→PCB ≈ 8 K/W *]── T_pcb ──[Rθ_pcb→air]── T_amb
```

\* Thermal-via array under the exposed pad: 16 × 0.3 mm vias, 25 µm plating, roughly 8 K/W effective into the inner planes.

Board-to-air resistance, with convection plus radiation:

$$
R_{\theta,pcb-air}=\frac{1}{h\,A_{eff}},\quad A_{eff}\approx 2\times(30.5\text{ mm})^2=1.86\times10^{-3}\text{ m}^2
$$

| Condition | h (W/m²K) | R_θ,board→air |
|---|---|---|
| Still air (bench) | 10 | 54 K/W |
| Prop wash, ~10 m/s | 80–120 | ~5.4 K/W (at h = 100) |

### 4.2 Allowable continuous dissipation

For ΔT_board = 60 K (40 °C ambient, 100 °C board), using R ≈ 5.4 K/W in flight:

$$
P_{cont,total}\approx\frac{60}{5.4}\approx 11\text{ W}
\Rightarrow P_{motor}\approx 2.8\text{ W}
$$

Solve for the current that gives 2.8 W per motor:

$$
2I^2(1.35\text{ mΩ}) + 0.3\text{ mΩ}\cdot I^2 + P_{sw}(I) = 2.8\text{ W}
\Rightarrow I_{cont}\approx 28\text{–}32\text{ A per motor (all four loaded)}
$$

With a 45 A continuous label, you are relying on unequal motor loading or forced airflow. This is normal marketing practice, but the label should be documented honestly.
