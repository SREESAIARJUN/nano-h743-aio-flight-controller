## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

Read this section before the math. It changes how the rest should be interpreted.

### 0.1 Architecture Assumed

The STM32H743 cannot practically run four sensorless six-step commutation loops while also running ArduPilot. No shipping ArduPilot target does this. The architecture assumed here is the standard AIO (all-in-one) split:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) at 400 MHz | ArduPilot. Generates bi-directional DShot600. |
| ESC MCUs (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (QFN32) | AM32 firmware, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drivers (×4) | FD6288Q (3-phase, 250 V, bootstrap) or equivalent | 12 gate outputs per channel |
| Power stage | 24 N-MOSFETs, 30–40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3 | 6 per motor |

### 0.2 Reality Check on "Nano + 80 A Burst"

- **A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 80 A per motor in any meaningful sense.** Twenty-four FETs in 3.3×3.3 packages, plus four gate drivers and four MCUs, barely fit on 20×20. The copper cross-section and thermal mass are insufficient.
- **80 A × 4 motors is roughly 320 A of phase current.** That implies on the order of 150–250 A of battery current. A 20×20 board's XT60 pads and bus copper cannot carry that.
- **The model below is therefore built for a 30.5×30.5 mm board.** It also shows the 20×20 result. The math shows that "80 A" is a **seconds-scale burst rating**, not a continuous one, on either form factor. Treat the burst-duration numbers as the actual specification to publish.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path in Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

At any instant one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry I_ph:

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through HS_x and LS_y.
- During the off-time, with complementary sync rectification, the freewheel path is LS_x and LS_y. That is still two FETs in series.

So the current always sees **2 × R_ds(on)**, regardless of duty cycle.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Because R_ds(on) depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, this is a coupled problem. Solve it by fixed-point iteration:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + R_{\theta} \cdot P\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)$$

| T_j (°C) | R_ds(on) (mΩ) | P_cond per channel (W) | P_cond, 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.00 | 12.8 | 51.2 |
| 100 | 1.375 | 17.6 | 70.4 |
| 125 | 1.50 | **19.2** | **76.8** |

Use **T_j = 125 °C** as the design point.

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution

Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Each FET in a leg carries current for roughly 1/3 of the cycle, with the high/low split depending on duty D:

$$P_{HS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R \cdot D, \qquad P_{LS} \approx \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R\,(2 - D)$$

At D ≈ 0.8, which is typical for a punch-out:

- P_HS ≈ 0.27·I²R ≈ 2.6 W
- P_LS ≈ 0.40·I²R ≈ 3.8 W

**The low-side FETs are the hottest parts on the board.** Give them the most copper.

### 1.4 PCB Copper Loss (Commonly Ignored, Not Negligible)

Phase pad to motor pad, with 2 oz outer layers plus two 2 oz internal planes in parallel, gives about 0.25 mΩ effective per path:

$$P_{Cu,ch} \approx 80^2 \times 0.25\text{ m}\Omega \times 2 \approx 3.2\text{ W}$$
