# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #4): Loss Model, Thermal Model, Bi-Directional DShot DMA Plan and Netlist

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Several architecture points need settling before any math.

1. **The H743 does not switch the bridges at 48 kHz.** Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM and does commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The switching-loss math below therefore belongs to the ESC MCUs. The DMA math belongs to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not physically achievable** for more than a fraction of a second. The thermal model in Section 3 proves this quantitatively. It derives the real burst-time limit and recommends a form factor that can carry it.
3. **The silicon and board parameters below are assumptions**, representative of a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 DFN MOSFET. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

| Symbol | Meaning | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 6S nominal, full charge | 25.2 V |
| I_ph | Burst phase current per ESC | 80 A |
| R_ds,25 | R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 1.0 mΩ |
| α | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | 0.006 /K |
| t_r, t_f | Effective voltage rise/fall time (incl. layout L) | 15 ns each |
| Q_g | Total gate charge | 60 nC |
| Q_rr | Body diode reverse-recovery charge | 30 nC |
| C_oss,er | Energy-related output capacitance | 1.5 nF |
| V_SD | Body diode forward voltage at 80 A | 0.8 V |
| t_dead | Dead time per edge | 100 ns |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Conduction topology
The ESC uses trapezoidal 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, one bridge path carries I_ph through two FETs:

- **On-time (D):** the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.
- **Off-time (1−D):** the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectifier) and the low-side FET of phase B conduct.

So exactly two R_DS(on) are always in series with the phase current, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.006 × 100) = **1.6 mΩ**

### 1.3 Ripple correction
For a motor with phase inductance L, the peak-to-peak current ripple is ΔI = V_bus·D(1−D)/(L·f_sw). The RMS current is then:

$$I_{rms}=I_{ph}\sqrt{1+\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\tfrac{\Delta I}{I_{ph}}\right)^2}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L ≈ 15 µH) at D = 0.5:

- ΔI = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) ≈ 8.75 A
- ΔI / I = 0.11, so the RMS factor is 1.0005, which is negligible. Use I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 1.4 Result per ESC channel

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 2 · 80² · 1.6 mΩ | **20.5 W** |
| Copper path | 80² · 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Per FET (2 conducting) | 6400 · 1.6 mΩ | 10.24 W |

### 1.5 Electro-thermal convergence
R_ds depends on T_j, and T_j depends on P. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\!\left(R_{ds}(T_j^{(k)})\right)\cdot R_{\theta JA}$$

The solution diverges (thermal runaway) when:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta JA} = 2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JA} \ge 1$$

At 80 A: 2 × 6400 × 1 m × 0.006 = 0.0768 W/K. The runaway threshold is therefore **R_θJA ≥ 13 K/W per channel**. A nano board easily exceeds this, so **there is no steady state at 80 A on a nano**; only transient (burst) operation is possible.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switched FET pair incurs switching events. These are the high side and sync low side of the active phase.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, high-side FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ W$$

### 2.3 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

(The induced extra turn-on current in the high side roughly doubles this. Budget 0.07 W.)

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in driver and gate resistors, not the FETs)

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ W$$

### 2.6 Switching total

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.45 |
| C_oss | 0.02 |
| Q_rr (incl. induced) | 0.07 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 |
| **FET switching subtotal** | **2.15** |
| Gate driver | 0.06 |

**Sensitivity:** P_VI and P_dt scale linearly with f_sw. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.0 W per channel at 80 A. It costs higher ripple and more motor iron/copper loss and acoustic noise. For burst-limited designs, 24–32 kHz is the better trade.
