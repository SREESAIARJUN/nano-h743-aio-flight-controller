# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #25)

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

The model is only as good as these assumptions. Swap in the real datasheet values before release.

On a real 4-in-1 stack, the **STM32H743 is the flight controller**. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to **four ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), and those MCUs drive the gate drivers. The H743 does not commutate motors directly. Everything below assumes that split.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Current path.** With complementary PWM, exactly two FETs carry the phase current at all times:
- During the on-time (D): high-side A and low-side B conduct.
- During the off-time (1−D): low-side A and low-side B conduct (synchronous freewheel).

**Per-FET RMS current**, with ripple ΔI included:

$$I_{rms,HS}=\sqrt{D\left(I^2+\tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)},\qquad I_{rms,LS,A}=\sqrt{(1-D)\left(I^2+\tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)}$$

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Per-motor conduction loss** (ripple neglected, ΔI ≪ I with typical motor inductance):

$$P_{cond}=2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)+I^2R_{Cu}$$

At T_j = 100 °C, R_ds = 0.7 × 1.5 = **1.05 mΩ**:

$$P_{cond,FET}=2(80^2)(1.05\times10^{-3})=\mathbf{13.4\ W}$$

**Copper loss.** Assume R_Cu ≈ 0.15 mΩ (pads, planes, vias). This adds 80² × 0.15 mΩ ≈ **0.96 W**.

**Worst single FET.** At D → 1 (high side) or D → 0 (low side), one FET carries nearly the full current:

$$P_{FET,max}\approx I^2R_{ds}=6.7\ \text{W}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V-I overlap** (high-side commutating device):

$$P_{sw}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz})=\mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (body diode of the low side):

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}\approx\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.04\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_d)\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** Each FET dissipates Q_g × V_gs × f_sw = 60n × 10 × 48k = 0.029 W, for **0.17 W** across the 6 FETs. This loss lands mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

### Per-motor loss budget at an 80 A burst

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FET, 100 °C) | 13.40 |
| Copper | 0.96 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Body diode, dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.10 |
| Gate drive | 0.17 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 17.2 W** |
| **4 motors simultaneously** | **≈ 69 W** |

ESC efficiency at the burst point is 1 − 17.2 / (25.2 × 80) ≈ **99.1 %**. The efficiency is fine; the heat density is the problem.

**Sensitivity of switching loss to f_sw:**

$$\partial P/\partial f_{sw}\approx(1.94+0.61+0.1+0.17)/48\text{k}=58.8\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.4 W per motor. Raising to 96 kHz costs about 2.8 W per motor.
