This document covers the reference design for an STM32H743 nano flight controller with an integrated 4-in-1 ESC.

## 0. Assumptions and Architectural Decisions

Every number below depends on these inputs. Replace them with your actual BOM values. The equations remain valid.

**Architecture.**
- The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller.
- Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071, running AM32) over bidirectional DShot600.
- Running 4× FOC/6-step commutation on the H743 itself is not recommended:
  - It couples flight-critical code to motor-timing jitter.
  - The H743 has only two advanced-control timers (TIM1/TIM8), not four.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in synchronous 6-step

In each 60° commutation sector, current flows from the PWM-ed (active) leg, through two motor phases, to the static low-side FET of the sinking leg. Within a PWM period:

- **ON interval (duty D).** The current path is HS_a → motor → LS_b.
- **OFF interval (1−D, synchronous).** The current path is LS_a (sync rectification) → motor → LS_b.
- **Dead time (2·t_d per period).** Current flows through the body diode of LS_a, then LS_b.

Two FET channels are therefore always in series with the phase current, independent of D:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \cdot \left(1 - 2 t_d f_{sw}\right) \approx 2 I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

R_ds(on) rises with temperature, and loss in turn raises temperature. This is an electrothermal feedback loop:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha = \frac{1.6 - 1}{100} = 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta \cdot P(T_j)
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0 (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_0 \alpha}
$$

Here P_0 = I²R_25 per device. The loop is stable only if:

$$
R_\theta \, P_0 \, \alpha < 1
$$

This is the thermal-runaway criterion. Check it in Section 4.

### 1.3 Numerical values at 80 A

The per-switch average conduction loss divides by three because each FET conducts for 120° out of 360°. Commutation frequency (several kHz) is far faster than the thermal time constants, so averaging is valid.

| T_j | R_ds(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond per FET (avg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 3.2 W |
| 100 °C | 2.18 mΩ | 27.9 W | 4.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.40 mΩ | 30.7 W | 5.1 W |

**Copper loss must be added.**
- A 2 oz trace has about 0.25 mΩ/sq.
- A phase path is roughly 4 squares, plus vias, giving about 0.5 mΩ per phase path ×2.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 6.4\ \text{W per motor}
$$

On a nano board, PCB copper loss is the same order as FET loss. Do not ignore it.
