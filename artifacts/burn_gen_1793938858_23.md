# STM32H743 AIO Nano Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis, DShot DMA Allocation, and Netlist

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Design Flags

I have no prior specification for "Job #47," so every parameter below is stated explicitly and kept symbolic until the worked example. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values.

**Flag 1: Architecture.** The STM32H743 cannot run the motor commutation itself in a practical 4-in-1 design. The standard and recommended architecture is:

- H743 (ArduPilot) → 4× bidirectional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32, or equivalent) → 3-phase gate drivers → 24 MOSFETs.
- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.

The H743's job is flight control, the DShot/DMA timing, and telemetry decoding.

**Flag 2: Physics.** "Nano" (20×20 mm) together with "80 A burst per motor" is at or past the physical limit. Section 3 shows the board can absorb roughly 90 W of loss for only a few seconds, and the continuous rating lands near 40–45 A per motor. Treat 80 A as a transient rating with a defined duration, enforced in firmware (Section 3.5). A 30.5×30.5 mm board is strongly recommended if 80 A is a real requirement.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC runs 6-step (trapezoidal) BLDC drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, exactly two MOSFET channels carry phase current I:

- **PWM on:** high-side of phase A plus low-side of phase B.
- **PWM off:** low-side of phase A (synchronous rectification) plus low-side of phase B.

Because the current always flows through two channels, conduction loss is first-order independent of duty cycle.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)
