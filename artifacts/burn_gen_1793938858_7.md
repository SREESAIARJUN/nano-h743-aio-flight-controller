**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not switch the MOSFETs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the four ESC MCUs. The H743's role is:

- generating bi-directional DShot600 frames, and
- capturing the GCR-encoded eRPM telemetry.

Treating these as two clock domains is essential to the DMA plan.

## 1. Electrical Operating Point

Per motor, in 6-step operation, two phase legs conduct at any instant:

- one high-side (HS) FET is PWM-switched at duty D;
- the complementary low-side FET in the same leg conducts during (1−D) (synchronous rectification);
- one low-side FET in another leg is held on for the full 60° sector.

Phase current I_ph ≈ I_bus / D (motor current exceeds battery current at partial throttle). The **worst case for conduction loss is I_ph = 80 A at any D**, because synchronous rectification keeps the current path through two FETs at all times.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

This matches the typical datasheet trend of about 1.5× at 125 °C.

At design T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.2\,(1 + 0.005 \cdot 85) = 1.71\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.2 Per-motor conduction loss

The current path always passes through two FETs. HS and sync-LS share the switched leg as D and (1−D), so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[ D R_{HS} + (1-D) R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,static} \right] = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

With trapezoidal flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I_pk. Then:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.71\times10^{-3} = 21.9\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Self-consistent junction temperature (fixed-point iteration)

Because R_DS depends on T_j, solve:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}\cdot P_{FET}(T_j)$$

$$P_{FET}(T_j) = \frac{I^2 R_{DS,25}(1+\alpha(T_j-25))}{3} \cdot 2$$

- Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle on average across the 6 FETs.
- At ≥10k eRPM, the electrical period is ≪ the FET thermal time constant (~5–20 ms), so averaging is valid.

Closed form:

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + R_\theta k (1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta k \alpha},\quad k = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{DS,25}$$

With k = (2/3)·6400·1.2e-3 = 5.12 W, R_θ,j-pcb = 2.0 K/W (exposed pad plus via array), and T_pcb = 90 °C:

$$T_j = \frac{90 + 10.24(0.875)}{1 - 0.0512} = \frac{98.96}{0.9488} = 104.3\ ^\circ\text{C}$$

This sits safely below 150 °C. **The PCB temperature is the real limiter, not R_θ,jc.**

### 2.4 Copper/trace conduction loss (often forgotten)

The phase pad to the motor wire and the battery bus add resistance:

| Path | Resistance |
|---|---|
| Bus pours, 3 oz outer + 2× 2 oz inner in parallel, ~15 mm path | ≈ 0.25 mΩ |
| Phase pours | ≈ 0.15 mΩ |
| Solder joints and wire pads | ≈ 0.1 mΩ |

Per motor:

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \cdot 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd HS FET has hard-switching loss. The sync LS switches at near-zero V_DS (body-diode clamped), leaving only diode reverse-recovery (Q_rr) and output-capacitance (C_oss) losses.

### 3.1 Hard-switching overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

Rise and fall times scale with the driver:

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{drive}},\quad I_{drive} \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g}$$

For Q_gd = 6 nC, R_g,total = 10 Ω, V_drv = 12 V, V_plateau = 4.5 V: I_drive = 0.75 A, so t_r = t_f ≈ 8 ns. Add di/dt and loop-inductance effects for a practical t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns.

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\times10^{3} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$
