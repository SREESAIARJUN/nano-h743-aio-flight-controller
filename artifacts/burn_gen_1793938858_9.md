# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Model, Thermal Analysis, Bidirectional DShot Timer Allocation, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Correction and Stated Assumptions

**The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** It runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 frames to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and generates the 48 kHz PWM itself. The power-stage math below therefore applies to the ESC section. The DMA and timer math applies to the H743.

Every equation below is exact. The component values are representative assumptions, not tested parts. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values before you sign anything off.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max |
| Burst current per motor | I_pk | 80 A |
| ESC PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, PDFN 5×6 class) | R_DS(on) | 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 1.6× at T_j = 125 °C |
| Rise + fall time (R_g ≈ 2.2 Ω) | t_r + t_f | 20 ns |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme.** With trapezoidal 120° commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, exactly two phase legs carry the motor current at any instant.

- The PWM'd leg conducts through the high-side FET for D·T and the low-side FET for (1−D)·T.
- Either way, one FET in that leg is always conducting.
- The return path is one fully-on FET in the second leg.

So the current always flows through 2 × R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + k\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

**At 80 A, hot (T_j = 125 °C, R = 1.6 mΩ):**

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ ESC\ channel}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the loss averages over the 6 FETs:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx 20.5 / 6 \approx 3.4\ \text{W}$$

The high-side/low-side split depends on D. At low duty, the low-side FETs carry most of the loss.

**Copper loss.** Add the PCB copper loss: P_Cu = I²·R_trace. A 2 oz, 5 mm wide, 10 mm long path is about 0.5 mΩ, which adds roughly 3.2 W per channel at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each loss term below is per channel.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.97 W |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw = ½·1.5 nF·635·48 k | 0.023 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 2·V_F·I·t_dt·f_sw = 2·0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 1.23 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 0.08 W (dissipated in R_g and driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.36 W** |

**Sensitivity.** Dead time costs more than the V·I overlap here. Tuning AM32 dead time from 200 ns down to about 100 ns, after checking for shoot-through on a scope, saves about 0.6 W per channel.

## 3. Total Burst Loss

| Case | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A per motor, hot | 20.5 + 3.2 + 2.4 ≈ **26 W** | **≈ 104 W** |
| 20 A per motor (80 A total) | 1.28 + 0.2 + ~0.8 ≈ **2.3 W** | **≈ 9 W** |

At 20 A, the conduction term uses I² scaling. The switching term scales linearly with current.

### 4.2 Convection in prop wash

- Convection coefficient h ≈ 100 W/m²·K.
- Area A ≈ 8×10⁻⁴ m², counting both faces.
- Board-to-air resistance: R_θ,BA = 1/(hA) ≈ 12.5 K/W.
- Time constant: τ = R·C ≈ 27 s.

### 4.3 Transient board temperature rise

$$\Delta T(t) = P\,R_{\theta}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The junction adds its own rise on top of the board: P_FET × (R_θJC + R_θ,spread) ≈ P_FET × (1 + 15) K/W for one FET on a small pour.

| Scenario | 1 s | 3 s | 10 s | Steady state |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 104 W (80 A × 4) | +45 K | +130 K | +410 K* | +1300 K* |
| 9 W (20 A × 4) | +4 K | +11 K | +35 K | +113 K |

\* Physically unreachable. The board fails first.

### 4.4 Engineering verdict

**80 A per motor on a 20×20 board is not survivable, even as a 1-second burst.**

- **Junction overload.** The hottest FET sees about 5 W of local dissipation through roughly 16 K/W of spreading resistance. That puts it more than 80 K above the already-hot board within milliseconds.
- **Solder reflow.** SAC305 solder melts at 217 °C. Burst currents this high routinely reflow FET pads.
