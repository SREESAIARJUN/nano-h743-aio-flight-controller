## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the *flight controller*. It does not commutate motors directly. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated commutation MCU per channel, because sensorless BEMF zero-crossing timing is hard real-time. The baseline is:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIH6, 480 MHz, running ArduPilot.
- **ESC:** 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, each with:
  - FD6288Q 3-phase gate driver
  - 6× N-channel MOSFETs
  - 48 kHz complementary PWM
- **Link:** FC to ESC over bi-directional DShot600, one signal per motor.
- **Form factor:** single 8-layer PCB, 30 × 30 mm outline, 20 × 20 mm mounting pattern.

**Key findings.**

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Loss per ESC at 80 A, 6S | ≈ 25.6 W |
| Total board loss, all 4 at 80 A | ≈ 103 W |
| Max burst duration, all 4 at 80 A (40 °C ambient → 105 °C board) | ≈ 3.0 s |
| Realistic continuous rating | ≈ 20 A per motor |
| DShot600 timer prescaler (240 MHz timer clock) | PSC = 49 → 4.8 MHz, 8 ticks/bit |
| DMA streams for 4× bidir DShot | 6 (2 timers × [UP + 2 CH]) |

**Verdict:** an "80 A" label on a 30 × 30 mm board is a *few-second burst* rating only. The spec sheet should say "80 A burst (≤ 3 s), 20 A continuous" or similar. If the 80 A figure is non-negotiable, use parallel MOSFETs or move to a 30.5 × 30.5 mm or larger board (§2.6).
