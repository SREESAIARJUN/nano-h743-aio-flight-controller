# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Package (Job #71)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor channel on the 4-in-1 ESC has its own small MCU (e.g., an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the **48 kHz** complementary PWM and answers the H743's bidirectional DShot600 frames with eRPM telemetry.

The derivation therefore splits into three domains:

| Domain | Silicon | Concern |
|---|---|---|
| Power stage (×4) | ESC MCU, gate driver, 6 MOSFETs | Conduction and switching losses, thermal |
| Control link | H743 timers and DMA ↔ ESC MCU | Bidirectional DShot600 |
| Thermal envelope | Shared PCB and copper | Burst duration limits |

No MOSFET was specified, so the parameter set below is representative of the 40 V, 5×6 mm / 3.3×3.3 mm class used on 6S 4-in-1 ESCs. **Replace every value marked [A] with your selected part's datasheet values.** All equations are kept symbolic so you can re-run them.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged (worst case) |
| I_pk | 80 A | Burst **phase** current per channel |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | PWM frequency |
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ [A] | At V_GS = 10 V |
| α | 0.005 /K [A] | R_DS(on) temperature coefficient |
| t_r, t_f | 20 ns each [A] | Effective switching transitions with the selected gate resistor |
| Q_rr | 50 nC [A] | Body diode reverse recovery |
| C_oss | 1.5 nF [A] | Output capacitance |
| Q_g | 60 nC [A] | Total gate charge |
| V_F | 0.8 V | Body diode forward voltage |
| t_dt | 100 ns [A] | Dead time per edge |
| V_GS | 10 V | Gate drive |

**Phase current vs. battery current.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive, I_batt ≈ D · I_phase. An "80 A burst" spec printed on a product usually means *battery-side* current. This model uses **80 A phase current**, the stricter thermal case, because it is what the FETs actually see.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths with synchronous complementary PWM

In any 6-step commutation sector, two phases conduct. Say phase A is high and phase B is low:

- **Phase A high-side (Q_AH):** conducts for fraction D of each PWM period.
- **Phase A low-side (Q_AL):** synchronous rectification, conducts for 1 − D (excluding dead time).
- **Phase B low-side (Q_BL):** conducts 100% of the sector.

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is an important property of synchronous drive.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[\,1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\,]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.005 \times 100) = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

| Condition | P_cond per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 2 × 6400 × 0.0010 = **12.8 W** | 51.2 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |

The hottest single device is the continuously conducting low-side FET:

$$
P_{Q_{BL}} = 6400 \times 0.0015 = 9.6\ \text{W}
$$

Because the motor rotates, the 100%-conduction role rotates between phases, which spreads this load over one electrical revolution.

### 1.4 Thermal runaway criterion

Combining the self-heating loop T_j = T_a + R_θ · P(T_j) with the R_DS(on) model gives a closed-form solution:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta\, k\,(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta\, k\, \alpha},
\qquad k = I^2 R_{DS,25}
$$

The device is **stable only if R_θ · k · α < 1**. For a single FET carrying 80 A (k = 6.4 W), runaway occurs at R_θ ≥ 31 K/W. That is far above any realistic soldered-FET value, so runaway is not the limiting factor. **Board-level heat capacity is the binding constraint** (Section 4).

### 1.5 PCB copper losses

These are not negligible on a nano board. The resistance of 2 oz (70 µm) copper is:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}, \qquad \rho = 1.72\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\cdot\text{m}
$$

For a 10 mm × 5 mm phase pour with 4 parallel 2 oz layers:

$$
R_{Cu} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \times 0.01}{0.005 \times 280\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.12\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

At 80 A this adds about **0.8 W per conduction path** (≈1.5 W per channel, including the bus pours). Include it in the budget.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-modulated half-bridge switches; the 100%-on low-side FET does not.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}
$$

$$
P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

During dead time, the low-side body diode conducts. When the high side turns on, that diode must recover:

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\times10^{-9} \times 48000 = 0.060\ \text{W}
$$

The Q_rr-induced current spike also lengthens the effective turn-on time. If the scope shows ringing, budget about +20% on P_sw.
