# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model (Job #57)

**Document scope:** loss model, thermal model, DShot600 bidirectional timer/DMA allocation (ArduPilot), and reference netlist.

**Status:** Engineering estimate. All component parameters below are representative values for 40 V-class 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs. Substitute datasheet values for your selected parts before layout freeze.

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Finding

### Architecture

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, either one per motor or one quad-capable device.

The H743 does three things:
- Runs ArduPilot.
- Emits DShot600 frames.
- Captures the bidirectional eRPM telemetry.

So the system has two clock domains and one isolated signal interface per motor.

### Headline result

> **80 A per motor on a nano (20×20 mm) board cannot be thermally sustained. It is survivable only for roughly 1–2 s bursts, even with ideal layout.** If "80 A burst" means **80 A aggregate (≈20 A/motor)**, the design is comfortable. Both cases are computed below so you can make the call.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Derivation for 6-step synchronous PWM

In each 60° commutation sector:
- Two phase legs carry current I_ph and one leg floats.
- The PWM'd leg alternates current between its high-side (HS) and low-side (LS) FET. With synchronous rectification, the current always flows through exactly one FET of that leg.
- The non-PWM'd leg holds one FET fully on.

Each leg therefore carries current for 2/3 of the electrical period, always through one channel resistance:

$$
P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{2}{3}\, I_{ph}^2 \,(R_{DS,hot} + R_{cu})
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 3 \cdot P_{cond,leg} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\,(k_T R_{25} + R_{cu})
$$

With R_eff = 1.5 × 1.6 + 0.4 = **2.8 mΩ**:

| Case | I_ph | P_cond per motor | Per FET (avg over 6) | Instantaneous per FET while on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 80 A | 2 × 6400 × 2.8 m = **35.8 W** | 6.0 W | I²R_DS = **15.4 W** |
| B | 20 A | **2.24 W** | 0.37 W | 0.96 W |

> **Note:** R_DS,hot itself depends on T_j. Iterate R(T_j) → P → T_j until convergence. At 80 A the iteration diverges unless the burst is time-limited. That divergence is the formal statement of thermal runaway risk.
