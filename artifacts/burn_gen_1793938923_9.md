I don't have Job #72's design files, so this model uses the stated assumptions below. Each one is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

| Parameter | Assumed value | Notes |

| Form factor | 20×20 mm "nano" AIO, 6-layer, 2 oz copper | |

**Feasibility warning.** Four motors at 80 A simultaneously is 320 A and roughly 7.1 kW at 22.2 V, through a 20×20 mm board. The math below shows this is only survivable for about 1 s from a warm start. Likewise, the battery pads and bus copper alone dissipate tens of watts at that current.

"80 A burst" ratings are normally specified on 30×30 boards with 5×6 FETs, or refer to total board current. Confirm which definition the spec intends before committing to the BOM.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.004\ /^\circ\text{C}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_DS = 1.8 mΩ × 1.40 = **2.52 mΩ**

### 1.2 Bridge conduction during one commutation sector

In each 60° sector, phase A is PWM-switched (HS on for D, its LS synchronously rectifying for 1−D) and phase B's low-side is held fully on. The current therefore always passes through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS} = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is a property of synchronous rectification.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.52\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{32.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, with some duty-weighting on the switched leg. The average is about 32.3 / 6 ≈ **5.4 W per FET**. The worst-case FET is a low-side that is both fully on in its own sector and synchronously rectifying in another, at roughly 1.3× the average (≈ 7 W) at low throttle duty.

### 1.4 Parasitic copper

| Path | R (est.) | Current | Loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battery pads + main bus (shared) | 0.3 mΩ | 320 A | **30.7 W** |
| Phase traces/pads per motor (2 conducting) | 2 × 0.4 mΩ | 80 A | 5.1 W ×4 = 20.5 W |
| Current-shunt (if used, 0.5 mΩ, per motor) | 0.5 mΩ | 80 A | 3.2 W ×4 = 12.8 W |

Copper loss is comparable to FET loss. Prioritize thick bus pours, stacked vias, and solder-filled pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

With complementary PWM, only one half-bridge switches per sector. For that leg:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\!\cdot\!I\ overlap} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f_{sw}}_{reverse\ recovery} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw}}_{output\ cap} + \underbrace{2 V_F I\, t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{body\ diode} + \underbrace{2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}}_{gate\ (driver)}$$

Using V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, t_r + t_f = 25 ns (≈1 A sink/source driver), V_F = 0.8 V:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·25e-9·48e3 | **1.21 W** |
| Q_rr | 30e-9·25.2·48e3 | 0.036 W |
| C_oss | 0.5·1.5e-9·25.2²·48e3 | 0.023 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2·0.8·80·100e-9·48e3 | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC, not FET) | 2·40e-9·10·48e3 | 0.038 W |
| **Total per motor** | | **≈ 1.92 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 0.9 W per motor but increases current ripple and audible noise. Cutting dead time to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor, if the driver and FET shoot-through margin allow it.
