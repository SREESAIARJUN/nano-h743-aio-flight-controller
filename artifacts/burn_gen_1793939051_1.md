# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #41): Systems Architecture Model

I have no prior context for Job #41, so every input parameter below is an explicit assumption. Swap in datasheet values and the equations still hold. Before the math, two findings change the design direction.

## 0. Critical Findings

**Finding 1: The H743 cannot be the ESC controller.**
ArduPilot does not commutate motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCU per motor (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or EFM8 running BLHeli_S). The H743 is the flight controller and talks to those MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The architecture below assumes this split.

**Finding 2: "80A burst" and "nano" (20×20 mm) are physically incompatible if 80A is per motor.**
The math in §2 shows about 86 W of dissipation at 4×80A. That would take a 20×20 board from 40°C to 125°C in about 2 seconds, and it exceeds the current rating of the DFN3.3×3.3 MOSFET packages that fit on a nano board. I model two cases:
- **Case A:** 80A per motor phase. This is the spec as literally written, and it fails.
- **Case B:** 80A total battery burst, about 20A per motor. This is realistic for a 20×20 board, though still aggressive.

**Resolution needed:** decide which case is intended. If it is Case A, the product needs a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger ESC board, which is no longer nano.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction losses (6-step, synchronous PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched and a second phase is held with its low side on. At duty cycle D:

- High-side FET of the PWM phase conducts for a fraction D of each period.
- Low-side FET of the PWM phase (synchronous rectification) conducts for 1−D.
- Low-side FET of the return phase conducts 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

The current always flows through exactly two FETs, so conduction loss does not depend on duty cycle. Here $R_{DS,hot} = k_T R_{25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$.

| Case | I per motor | P_cond per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 80 A | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| B | 20 A | 2 × 400 × 0.0015 = **1.2 W** | 4.8 W |

Add PCB copper loss separately. For example, 1 mΩ of phase-plus-bus path gives 6.4 W at 80A versus 0.4 W at 20A.
