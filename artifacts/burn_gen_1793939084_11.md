## 1. FOC Current Loop (20 kHz)

### 1.1 Transforms

Electrical angle from the mechanical encoder angle θ_m:

$$\theta_e = p\,\theta_m - \theta_{off}$$

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, with i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)$$

Park transform:

$$\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}$$

### 1.2 Plant (SPMSM, L_d = L_q = L)

$$v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m$$

The back-EMF constant is λ_m = 2Kt / (3p). Torque is τ = (3/2) p λ_m i_q = Kt i_q.

### 1.3 PI with Pole-Zero Cancellation

Choose C(s) = K_p + K_i/s with K_p = L ω_c and K_i = R ω_c. The open loop then becomes ω_c/s, so the closed loop is first-order with bandwidth ω_c.

The bandwidth is limited by the PWM delay of about 1.5 T_s. Keep ω_c ≤ 1/(5 · 1.5 T_s):

$$\omega_c = 2\pi\cdot 1500\ \text{rad/s}\Rightarrow K_p = 20.7\ \text{V/A},\ K_i = 52{,}800\ \text{V/(A·s)}$$

### 1.4 Decoupling and Back-EMF Feedforward

$$v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(e_d) - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(e_q) + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m$$

### 1.5 Delay Compensation

The voltage is applied about 1.5 T_s after sampling. Rotate the inverse Park transform by:

$$\theta_e' = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s$$

### 1.6 Voltage Limit and Anti-Windup

The linear SVPWM region is a circle of radius V_bus/√3.

- **Limit:** prioritize d, then clamp q to the remaining voltage: $|v_q| \le \sqrt{V_{max}^2 - v_d^2}$.
- **Anti-windup:** use back-calculation:

$$x_{I}[k+1] = x_I[k] + T_s\big(K_i e[k] + K_{aw}(u_{sat}-u)\big),\qquad K_{aw}= K_i/K_p$$

### 1.7 SVPWM (Min-Max Zero-Sequence Injection)

$$v_{a,b,c}\ \text{from inverse Clarke},\quad v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big),\quad d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}$$

This is mathematically identical to sector-based SVPWM and cheaper to compute.

## 2. Velocity Estimation (PLL Observer, 20 kHz)

Raw differentiation of a 14-bit encoder at 20 kHz gives noise of about 7.7 rad/s per LSB step, which is unusable. Use a type-2 PLL:

$$e = \operatorname{wrap}(\theta_{meas} - \hat\theta),\quad \hat\omega \mathrel{+}= T_s k_i e,\quad \hat\theta \mathrel{+}= T_s(\hat\omega + k_p e)$$

Set k_p = 2ζω_n and k_i = ω_n². With ω_n = 2π·400 rad/s and ζ = 0.8, the velocity loop sees about 2 kHz of bandwidth and essentially no phase lag below 200 Hz.
