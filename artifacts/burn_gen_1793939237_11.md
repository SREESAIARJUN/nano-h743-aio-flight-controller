# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Electro-Thermal Model and DShot Allocation

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Two points about the architecture need correcting before any numbers make sense.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each ESC channel (or each pair) has its own small MCU, such as an AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8, running AM32 or BLHeli_32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and handles back-EMF commutation. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The model below follows that split.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board must be defined.** The results differ by a factor of about 16 depending on whether 80 A is per channel or total. I model 80 A per channel phase current, as the worst case, and then show the 20 A per channel case (80 A total). On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, the per-channel interpretation lasts only seconds before thermal runaway (§3).

## 1. Conduction Losses

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification

In every 60° commutation sector, exactly one high-side and one low-side FET carry the phase current I_ph:

- **PWM on-time (fraction D):** current flows through HS_a, the motor, then LS_b.
- **PWM off-time (1 − D):** current flows through LS_a (synchronous freewheel), the motor, then LS_b.

So at every instant, two FET channels are in series with the winding. Ignoring dead time (handled in §2), the per-channel conduction loss is independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) + I_{ph,rms}^2 R_{trace}$$

**Ripple correction.** The current ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E)\,D}{L f_{sw}},\qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

The worst case occurs at D = 0.5 with E ≈ 0.5·V_bus:

$$\Delta I = \frac{12.6 \times 0.5}{15\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 8.75\ \text{A}$$

At 80 A this adds only 6.4 A² (+0.1 %), so it is negligible at high current but matters at idle.

**Temperature dependence.**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C this gives R_ds = 1.80 mΩ.

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution (needed for junction temperature)

For the hottest FET (HS, PWM side) over one electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{ds}$$

For the LS of the same leg:

$$P_{cond,LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R_{ds} + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{ds}$$

The second term is the "return" sector. The LS FETs carry about 2× the HS conduction loss at high throttle. This is why LS FETs fail first in 4-in-1 boards.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing high-side FET hard-switches. The complementary LS FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time, but pays the body-diode and reverse-recovery cost.

$$
P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\text{-}I\ \text{overlap}}
+ \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}}
+ \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}}_{C_{oss}}
+ \underbrace{2V_f I\,t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead time}}
+ \underbrace{2Q_gV_{gs}f_{sw}}_{\text{gate}}
$$

| Term | 80 A | 20 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap: 0.5·25.2·I·30 ns·48 k | 1.45 W | 0.36 W |
| Q_rr: 40 nC·25.2·48 k | 0.048 W | ~0.03 W |
| C_oss: 0.5·1 nF·25.2²·48 k | 0.015 W | 0.015 W |
| Dead time: 2·0.8·I·100 ns·48 k | 0.61 W | 0.15 W |
| Gate: 2·50 nC·10 V·48 k (in driver) | 0.048 W | 0.048 W |
| **P_sw per channel** | **2.17 W** | **0.60 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 45\ \mu\text{W/Hz at } 80\ \text{A}$$

Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 1.1 W per channel but doubles ripple (ΔI ≈ 17.5 A). At 80 A, conduction dominates (93 %), so lowering f_sw does not rescue the burst case.
