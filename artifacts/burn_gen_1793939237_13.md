## 0. Architecture Decisions and Constraints

Three issues shape the whole design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no native ESC firmware. It acts as the flight controller and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. For this design that is 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071). This MCU allocation is assumed throughout.
2. **"80 A" can only be a short burst rating at nano size.** On a 20×20 or 25×25 mm stack, 80 A per motor generates about 90 W of heat. Section 3 shows that sustaining this is physically impossible. The rating must be specified with a duration: about 1–2 s from a warm start.
3. **Component parameters are placeholders.** I have used representative values for the parts below. Replace them with the datasheet numbers for the parts you actually procure before releasing the design.

| Gate driver | FD6288Q-class 3-phase driver, ~1.5 A source / 1.8 A sink |
| PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time t_dt = 100 ns, 6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 1.2\times(1+0.0045\times85) = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Bridge conduction

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two switch positions are always in series with the motor current: one high side and one low side. This holds during both the PWM-on and freewheel intervals.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2\times80^2\times1.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}$$

Each FET conducts one third of the electrical cycle, so the average conduction loss per FET is 21.2 / 6 ≈ **3.5 W**.

### 1.3 Thermal runaway check

Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, the loss and the temperature feed each other. The loop gain is:

$$G = \frac{dP}{dT}\,R_\theta = P_{cond}\,\alpha'\,R_\theta, \qquad \alpha' = \frac{\alpha}{1+\alpha(T_j - 25)}$$

The design is stable only if G < 1. With a local R_θ(j-amb) of about 15 K/W per FET under burst conditions, G ≈ 3.5 × 0.0033 × 15 ≈ 0.17. This is stable, but the loop amplifies the loss by 1 / (1 − G) ≈ 1.2×.

### 1.4 Other conduction paths

| Element | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|
| Copper traces (target ≤ 0.3 mΩ per phase path, 2 oz, multilayer) | ≈ 2 × 80² × 0.3 m ≈ 3.8 W per motor |
| Current shunt (0.25 mΩ, if fitted) | 80² × 0.25 m = 1.6 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd high-side switch hard-switches. Its complementary low side handles the dead-time and reverse-recovery losses.

### 2.1 V–I overlap

The switching time is set by the gate charge the driver must deliver:

$$t_{r} \approx t_f \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_g} = \frac{12\ \text{nC}}{1.5\ \text{A}} = 8\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times16\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.77\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

There are two switching nodes per transition (one per FET in the leg):

$$P_{oss} \approx 2\times\tfrac12 Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\,(2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Dead time is the second-largest loss term. Reducing t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Characterize the driver propagation skew before doing so.

### 2.5 Gate drive

This power is dissipated in the driver and gate resistor, not the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W per switching FET}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A

| Term | Power (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 21.2 |
| Copper and shunt | 5.4 |
| V–I overlap | 0.77 |
| Dead time | 0.61 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.13 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 28.1** |
| **4-in-1 total** | **≈ 112 W** |

Switching losses are under 6% of the total. At this current, **conduction dominates**. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz would add only about 1.5 W per motor, while doubling the FET count (paralleling) would halve the 21 W conduction term.
