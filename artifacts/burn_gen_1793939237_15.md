# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #39): Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, BDShot600 DMA Plan & Reference Netlist

## 0. Architecture Correction and Executive Findings

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own small MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071 / EFM8) running AM32 or BLHeli_32/S. That MCU performs 6-step or FOC commutation and generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends **bidirectional DShot600** to those four MCUs. The design below follows that split.

**Key findings:**

| Item | Result |
|---|---|
| Loss per channel @ 80 A, 6S, 48 kHz | ≈ 28 W (≈ 24 W conduction, ≈ 2 W switching/dead-time, ≈ 2 W copper) |
| Total ESC loss, all four motors @ 80 A | ≈ 113 W |
| Adiabatic burst time (40 → 120 °C board) | **≈ 3–4 s** |
| Realistic continuous rating (prop-wash cooling, 30×30 board) | **≈ 25 A per motor** |
| BDShot600 frame, command + telemetry | ≈ 85 µs, so the update ceiling is ≈ 8–10 kHz |
| DMA streams for motors | 6 (2× TIMx_UP + 4× TIMx_CHy). This is trivial on H7 with DMAMUX. |

**80 A is a ~3-second burst figure, not a sustained one.** Any datasheet claim should state the duration and cooling condition. "Nano" form factors (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) are even worse than the 30×30 numbers used here.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current always flows through **two conducting devices in series**: the PWM'd leg (HS or its complementary LS) plus the low-side "on" leg of the return phase.

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.6 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 3.84\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\,W}
$$

$$
P_{cu,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{1.9\,W}
$$

**Per-device average.** Each of the 6 FETs conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle, but HS and LS share unequally as a function of duty D. A worst-case low-side FET carries:

$$
P_{LS,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds,hot}\left[\tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\right]
$$

At D = 0.5 this gives ≈ 6400·1.92m·0.5 ≈ **6.1 W** in the hottest device.

**Positive-feedback check.** R_ds rises with T_j. The stability condition is

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot \theta_{ja} < 1
$$

With a tempco of ≈ 0.5 %/K, P ≈ 6 W, and θ_ja,eff ≈ 15 K/W, the loop gain is 0.005·6·15 ≈ 0.45. This is stable, but marginal if θ_ja degrades (poor solder voids, no airflow).

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

**Transition time:**

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 8\,\text{ns}\ \ (\times2\text{ edges} = 16\,\text{ns})
$$

**Per-channel loss components** (one actively switching leg at a time):

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·16n·48k | 0.77 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw (Q_oss≈40 nC) | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_f·I·2t_dead·f_sw = 0.8·80·400n·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw·2 = 50n·10·48k·2 | 0.05 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 2.1 W** |

**Insight: dead time is the biggest switching term.** Cutting t_dead from 200 ns to 100 ns saves ~0.6 W per channel. Do this only after verifying shoot-through margin on the scope with your actual gate resistors. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz roughly doubles everything except conduction loss.

**Total per channel:**

$$
P_{ch} = 24.6 + 1.9 + 2.1 \approx \mathbf{28.6\,W}
\qquad
P_{ESC,4ch} \approx \mathbf{114\,W}
$$

**Bus-capacitor RMS ripple** (worst case D = 0.5):

$$
I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)} = 40\,\text{A per channel}
$$

The four channels are not phase-locked, so the RSS gives ≈ 80 A_rms worst case at the board level. No on-board MLCC/polymer bank on a nano board can absorb this. **Specify an external ≥ 1000 µF low-ESR electrolytic at the pads (mandatory).** Also place ≥ 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R + 1× 100 µF/35 V polymer per channel pair, directly across each half-bridge loop.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-board, per device (steady averaged)

$$
\Delta T_{jb} = P_{FET} \cdot \theta_{jb} \approx 6.1\,\text{W} \times 1.5\,\text{K/W} \approx 9\,\text{K}
$$

The die is not the bottleneck. **The board is.**

### 4.2 Board lumped thermal mass (adiabatic burst)

The 30×30 mm, 6–8 layer, 2 oz board plus 24 FETs, caps, and solder has an effective heat capacity of about C_th ≈ 5 J/K. (FR-4 ≈ 1.1 J/gK, Cu ≈ 0.385 J/gK; total ~8–10 g.) Measure this by step-heating a prototype.

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} = \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{114}{5} \approx 23\,\text{K/s}
$$

$$
t_{burst} = \frac{T_{max} - T_0}{dT/dt} = \frac{120 - 40}{23} \approx \mathbf{3.5\,s}
$$
