## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, motor current always flows through two conducting devices in series:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET for duty D, then the low-side FET (synchronous rectification) for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET, on for the whole 60° sector.

The resulting conduction losses are:

$$P_{cond,PWM\,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,return} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)}$$

Dead time is excluded here; the body diode carries current then, and that loss is counted in §2.

Each FET conducts for one-third of the electrical cycle. Averaged over one revolution, the per-FET loss is:

$$P_{FET,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}\cdot 2 I^2 R_{DS} \;\;\text{(shared across 6 FETs, 2 at a time)}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (requires iteration)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At an assumed T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 0.9\,(1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85) = 1.24\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Worked numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.24\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{15.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often larger than designers expect)

| Element | R | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pour, 2 oz, ~4 squares (0.24 mΩ/□ at 70 °C) | ≈ 1.0 mΩ | 6.4 W |
| Battery pads to bus, shared ÷4 | ≈ 0.2 mΩ | — |
| Current shunt (shared per board), 0.25 mΩ | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W per channel-equivalent |
| Motor wire solder joints (2×) | ≈ 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

**Design rule:** on a nano form factor, copper loss is about 40–60% of FET conduction loss. Use 2 oz outer layers, stitch inner layers on phase nets, and place many vias in parallel.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Switching loss is incurred only by the PWM leg, which has one hard-switched transition pair per period.

### 2.1 V·I overlap loss

$$t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{g}},\quad I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{g,int}}$$

Inputs: V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 4.5 V, R_g = 10 Ω + 1.5 Ω internal.

- I_g ≈ 0.48 A, so t_r ≈ 25 ns.
- Assume t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns, since the driver's sink current is stronger than its source.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (body diode of the synchronous FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot600\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.84\ W}$$

> **This is the main lever at 48 kHz.** Cutting dead time from 300 ns to 100 ns saves about 1.2 W per channel. AM32 exposes dead time per target, but verify shoot-through margin on a scope (V_GS of HS and LS on the same leg).

### 2.5 Gate drive

$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}\cdot2 = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

This is dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Per-channel total at 80 A burst

| Loss term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (2 FETs) | 15.9 |
| V·I switching | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.84 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 |
| Gate | 0.06 |
| **Silicon total** | **19.9** |
| Copper, shunt, joints | ~9.3 |
| **Channel total** | **~29 W** |
| **Board, 4 × 80 A simultaneous** | **~117 W** |

**Switching-frequency sensitivity:**

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx \frac{1.94 + 1.84 + 0.19}{48\text{k}} \approx 83\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves about 2 W per channel at 80 A, at the cost of current ripple and audible noise.
