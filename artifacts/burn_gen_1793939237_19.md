# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarifications

These points change the math, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, each motor channel (or the whole 4-in-1) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071 and GD32E230. That MCU produces the 48 kHz complementary PWM and handles commutation. The H743 only sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. The two domains below are therefore modelled separately.
2. **80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) footprint is thermally marginal.** Section 3 shows it is survivable only for about 1–2 s with all four channels loaded. Treat "80 A" as a single-channel, short-burst rating unless the board grows to 30.5×30.5 mm or gains a heatsink.
3. **The component parameters are representative, not from a specific BOM.** Every number derives from the parameter table, so you can substitute datasheet values for your chosen MOSFET and recompute.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the current always passes through exactly two MOSFET channels: one high-side and one low-side, in either the PWM-on or freewheel state.

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.34 mΩ**

**MOSFET conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.34\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.2\ W}$$

**Copper loss per channel:**

$$P_{cu} = I_{ph}^2\,R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

**How the loss splits across devices (worst case):**

- Each phase conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical cycle.
- During its active 120°, a phase acts as the PWM leg for 60° and the static leg for 60°.
- On the PWM leg, the high-side conducts for a fraction D of the time and the low-side for 1 − D.
- The worst-case single FET is the static-leg low-side at high duty. Its peak instantaneous conduction power is I²·R_DS ≈ **8.6 W** while conducting, averaging about **2.9 W** over the electrical cycle.

**Thermal-runaway check.** Because R_DS depends on T_j, the conduction loss is coupled to junction temperature:

$$P(T_j) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)], \qquad T_j = T_a + R_\theta P$$

This has a stable solution only if:

$$R_\theta \cdot 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

At 80 A that requires R_θ < 1/(12.8 × 0.0045) = **17.4 K/W**. That holds at the junction-to-board level but **fails at the board-to-ambient level** (Section 3.1). Steady-state operation at 80 A is therefore impossible, and only burst operation is physical.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

The PWM leg hard-switches once on and once off per period. The synchronous low-side commutates softly, through body-diode conduction during dead time.

**Gate currents during the Miller plateau:**

$$I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,on}} = \frac{6.5}{10} = 0.65\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,off}} = \frac{3.5}{6} = 0.58\ \text{A}$$
