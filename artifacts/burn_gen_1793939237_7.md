# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #44): Power-Stage Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Finding

**80 A per motor on a 20×20 mm stack cannot be sustained.** It is possible only as a time-limited burst, and the model below shows how long that burst can last. With all four motors at 80 A, the electro-thermal loop is unstable at any realistic board-to-air thermal resistance. The firmware must therefore enforce I²t limiting. The math in §2 and §3 sizes that limiter.

All device parameters below are stated assumptions. Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually procure before releasing the design.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous PWM)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In each 60° commutation sector, two phase legs carry the full phase current I.

- In the PWM'd leg, current alternates between the high side (duty D) and the low side (1−D). Their weighted sum is 1.
- In the static leg, one FET conducts continuously.

This gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

For I = 80 A, the base term is 2·80²·0.70 mΩ = **8.96 W**. Scaling with junction temperature:

| T<sub>j</sub> | R<sub>DS</sub> | P<sub>cond</sub> per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.70 mΩ | 8.96 W |
| 100 °C | 1.015 mΩ | 13.0 W |
| 125 °C | 1.12 mΩ | 14.3 W |

Add PCB copper loss. With 2 oz copper and roughly 0.3 mΩ effective phase-path resistance on each of 2 paths: 2·80²·0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**. This term is frequently ignored and it matters.

### 2.2 Switching Losses at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz (one hard-switched leg active)

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t<sub>r</sub>+t<sub>f</sub>)·f = ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k | 0.97 W |
| C<sub>oss</sub> | ½·C<sub>oss</sub>·V²·f | 0.015 W |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> | Q<sub>rr</sub>·V·f | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode | V<sub>f</sub>·I·2t<sub>dt</sub>·f = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (driver dissipation) | Q<sub>g</sub>·V<sub>gs</sub>·f·2 = 50 nC·10 V·48 k·2 | 0.048 W |
| **Total P<sub>sw</sub>** | | **≈ 1.68 W** |

### 2.3 Total at 80 A, T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C

| Scope | Breakdown | Total |
|---|---|---|
| Per motor | 14.3 + 3.8 + 1.68 | ≈ 19.8 W |
| Board (4 motors) | 4 × 19.8 | **≈ 79 W** |
| Plus shunt, if a single 0.1 mΩ shunt carries 320 A | 0.1 mΩ × 320² | **+10.2 W** |

Recommendation: use per-ESC phase sensing or a 50 µΩ shunt, or accept that total current sensing saturates during bursts.

Efficiency check: input power is 25.2 V × 320 A ≈ 8 kW, so the ESC is about 99 % efficient. The problem is not efficiency. The problem is dissipating 80 W in roughly 4 cm² of board.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Junction-to-Board (fast node)

Average power per die is 19.8 W ÷ 6 ≈ 3.3 W. This simplification holds because only 2–3 dies conduct per sector, and a sector lasts tens of microseconds, much shorter than the die thermal time constant.

$$\Delta T_{jc} \approx 3.3 \times 1.5 \approx 5\ \mathrm{K}$$

The junction runs only slightly above the board. The board is the bottleneck.
