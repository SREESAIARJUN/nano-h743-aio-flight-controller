## 0. Architecture Correction

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCUs.

| Function | Device |
|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot600 generation, bidirectional eRPM capture | STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) |
| Commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, dead-time, BEMF sensing | 4× AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (AM32), or 1× quad-capable MCU |
| Gate drive | 3× half-bridge drivers per motor (e.g., FD6288Q is 3-phase in one package) |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFETs (6 per motor) |

The loss model (Sections 1–3) applies to the ESC stage. The timer/DMA model (Section 4) applies to the H743.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology
The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phase legs carry current I. In each active leg, either the high-side (HS) or the low-side (LS) FET conducts. With synchronous rectification there is always exactly one FET channel conducting per active leg.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution
- Each leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle.
- During its active time, the HS FET conducts for fraction D and the LS FET for (1−D).

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}\, D, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D)$$

The worst single FET is the LS at low D, or the HS at D → 1. At full throttle (D ≈ 1), HS conduction loss approaches I²R/3. A rigorous treatment also accounts for the third "commutating" leg, which adds roughly 5–10 %.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Case | R_DS | P_cond per motor | Worst FET (D=1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2·6400·0.001 = **12.8 W** | 2.13 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 3.2 W |

**Copper loss is not negligible.** A 1 oz trace segment of 10 mm × 3 mm has about 0.57 mΩ. Add a PCB copper term:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu},\quad R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}, \quad \rho_{Cu}(T) = 1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.0039(T-20)]$$

With 0.3 mΩ effective path per motor: P_Cu = 6400 × 0.0003 = **1.9 W**.

**DC-link input current** (all 4 motors at 80 A phase, D ≈ 1) is up to **320 A**. No nano XT60 path survives that, so the burst must be phase-current limited per motor rather than simultaneous. Realistically, the battery-side burst is about 120–160 A. The model therefore defines I_bus = D·I_phase.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per commutation step is hard-switched (the PWM leg). Its complementary FET commutates through the body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched FET)
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

Rise and fall times from gate charge:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+ Q_{gs2}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}}$$

Example: Q_gd + Q_gs2 = 15 nC, V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau = 4 V, total R = 3 Ω. This gives I_g = 2 A and t_r ≈ 7.5 ns. The 20 ns assumption is therefore conservative and covers driver current limits; FD6288-class drivers are typically 1.2 A/1.5 A sources.

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.036\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction
$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g)
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2\times45\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.043\ \mathrm{W\ per\ leg}$$

### 2.6 Per-motor loss budget at 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C)

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.20 |
| Copper | 1.90 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.08 |
| Gate | 0.04 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 23.8 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 95 W** |

**Efficiency check.** Output power is about 25.2 × 80 × D. At D = 1 this is 2016 W per motor, giving η ≈ 98.8 %. Efficiency is not the problem; power **density** is.

**Sensitivity**
- ∂P_cond/∂I = 4·I·R_DS = 0.48 W/A. Loss is quadratic in current and dominates everything.
- ∂P_sw/∂f_sw = 40 µW/Hz. Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1 W per motor.
- **Conclusion:** R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not switching frequency.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (per FET, Cauer-to-ambient)
```
P_FET → [R_jc ≈ 1.2 K/W] → case/pad → [R_via-array ≈ 4 K/W] → inner Cu planes
      → [R_spread] → board bulk C_board → [R_conv(h, A)] → ambient
```

### 3.2 Board-level lumped transient (burst behaviour)

$$C_{board} = \sum m_i c_i$$

For a 30.5 mm 8-layer board:
- FR4: 30.5² mm × 1.6 mm × 1.85 g/cm³ ≈ 2.75 g, × 1.1 J/gK = 3.0 J/K
- Copper (approx. 8 oz total): 30.5² mm × 0.28 mm × 8.96 g/cm³ × ~0.6 fill
