# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #54): Power-Stage Thermal, Switching-Loss, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Feasibility Finding

**An 80 A burst per motor on a nano-format (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) 4-in-1 ESC is only thermally survivable for about 3–4 seconds without a heatsink.** Section 3 derives this limit. Continuous ratings will be much lower and are set by airflow.

This is common in the industry: most "80 A" nano ESC ratings are marketing burst figures. This package derives the real envelope so the datasheet rating is defensible. All component parameters below are **design-class assumptions**. Before release, replace them with the values from the datasheets of the parts you actually select.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Synchronous PWM

During each 60° commutation sector, exactly two phases carry current:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** the high-side FET conducts during D·T_sw. The low-side synchronous FET conducts during (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Phase B (return leg):** the low-side FET is continuously on.

At every instant, therefore, the motor current flows through **exactly two R_DS(on) elements in series**:

$$
P_{cond,ch}=I^2 R_{hs}D + I^2 R_{ls,sync}(1-D) + I^2 R_{ls,ret} = 2I^2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This holds independent of D, as long as all FETs are identical.

Over a full electrical cycle, each FET is part of the current path 1/3 of the time, so:

$$
P_{cond,FET}=\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$
