As Section 3 shows, 80 A on all four channels at once is thermally feasible only for about 1 s on a nano (20×20 mm class) board. It is not a continuous rating.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path analysis

In six-step commutation, two phases conduct during each 60° electrical sector.

- One leg is PWM-modulated at duty D.
- The other leg holds its low-side (or high-side) FET fully on.

With complementary PWM, the current path during the on-time is HS(A) → motor → LS(B). During the off-time, the current freewheels through LS(A), which is the synchronous rectifier, → motor → LS(B).

In both states the current passes through exactly two channels. The conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence, linearized:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow k_T = 1.5\ @\ 125^\circ\text{C})$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each leg conducts for 240° of the electrical cycle (two sectors high, two sectors low, or a split depending on scheme). Averaged over one electrical revolution:

$$\bar{P}_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}\quad\text{(average)}$$

The instantaneous peak in the "held-on" FET during its 120° interval is I²R.
