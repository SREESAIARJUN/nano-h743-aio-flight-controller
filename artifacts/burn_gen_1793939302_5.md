## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not commutate motors. The "4-in-1 ESC nano FC" is therefore two compute domains on one board (AIO) or a stacked pair:

| Domain | MCU | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, emits bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (one per motor) | AM32 | 6-step/sinusoidal commutation, 48 kHz PWM, eRPM telemetry |

The H743 cannot run ArduPilot and also drive 12 half-bridges at 48 kHz with BEMF zero-cross detection. Do not attempt this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path in 6-Step Synchronous Drive

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (HS/LS complementary) and one phase is held low (LS on). The phase current always flows through **exactly two MOSFETs**:

- PWM on-time (D): HS(A) + LS(B)
- PWM off-time (1−D): LS(A) [sync rectifier] + LS(B)

Per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

This assumes flat-top current with negligible ripple. Ripple adds a factor of $(1 + \tfrac{1}{12}(\Delta I/I)^2)$, which is under 1% for typical motor inductance at 48 kHz.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 0.0015 = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.06 mΩ | **26.4 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | **28.8 W** |

### 1.4 Per-Device Distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current for an average of 1/3 of the time.

- Per-FET average at 125 °C: 28.8 / 6 × 2 ... more precisely, total loss / 6 = **4.8 W average per FET**.
- Per-FET instantaneous peak while conducting: 6400 × 0.00225 = **14.4 W**.

At low eRPM (e.g., a stalled or punch-out startup), a single FET can sit in conduction for milliseconds. Size the thermal design for the 14.4 W instantaneous figure over the electrical period, not just the 4.8 W average.

### 1.5 Copper and Shunt Losses

Assume a phase-path PCB resistance (pad, via, plane) of R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ round-trip:

$$P_{cu} = I^2 R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.0003 = 1.9\ \text{W per motor}$$

A shunt (if fitted, e.g. a 0.25 mΩ common shunt per ESC) adds 1.6 W at 80 A. A Hall sensor (e.g. ACS-type IC) is recommended instead to save this loss.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. The held-low leg is static within the sector.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V–I Overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, use the datasheet curve; real values can reach 2–3× this.

### 2.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.02\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.04\ \text{W per active leg}$$

### 2.6
