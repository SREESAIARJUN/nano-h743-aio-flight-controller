## 0. Architectural Clarifications

These points constrain everything that follows.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In an ArduPilot FC+4in1 design, the H743 emits bidirectional DShot600 commands. A dedicated ESC MCU switches the bridges at 48 kHz. Typical choices are an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The 48 kHz switching analysis therefore applies to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA analysis applies to the H743 domain.

2. **"80A burst" is defined here as 80 A phase current per channel**, at near-full duty (D ≈ 0.95), on 6S (25.2 V max). Battery current per channel is approximately D·I_ph ≈ 76 A.

3. **Physical feasibility warning.** A 20×20 mm ("nano") board cannot reject 4×80 A of losses continuously. The thermal model in §4 shows the burst is time-limited to seconds. This is the single most important design finding. Your ESC firmware current/temperature limits must be set from it.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- **Phase A (switching):**
  - High-side conducts for D·T.
  - Low-side conducts for (1−D)·T as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B (return):** the low-side conducts for 100% of the sector.
- **Phase C (floating):** off.

### 2.2 Temperature-corrected R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] = 0.70\,[1+0.0055\cdot 85] = 1.03\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Because loss depends on R_ds(T_j), the calculation is solved by fixed-point iteration. Start at T_j = 25 °C, compute P, compute T_j from §4, then repeat. It converges in 3–4 passes. The value above is the converged figure at the 110 °C design point.
