# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #21): Power Stage Losses, Thermal Model, and Bidirectional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Scope and Key Findings

This is a parametric engineering model, not a validated design. All component values are placeholders, labeled as assumptions. Replace them with the datasheet curves of the MOSFETs and drivers you actually select. Then validate with thermocouple or IR measurements on a prototype.

Three findings change the design:

1. **The H743 does not drive the bridges.** Commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs, for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The architecture and netlist below follow this split.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" board is a seconds-scale transient rating, not a continuous one.** The thermal model shows a sustainable time of roughly 1 s from a warm start on a 20×20 board. Section 3 quantifies this.
3. **Timer and DMA mapping on the H743 is constrained by stream count, not request routing.** DMAMUX lets any request use any stream. The real constraints are the number of free streams and ArduPilot's BIDIR pairing rules.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two MOSFETs carry I_ph at any instant:
- one in the leg tied to the rail, and
- either the high-side or the synchronous low-side of the PWM leg.

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]\approx k_T R_{25}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 (80)^2 (1.2\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6) = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.92\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{24.6\ W}
$$

Rotating through six sectors, this averages to about 4.1 W per FET. Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period, plus its share of the sync-rectification interval.

**Copper and shunt losses.** Use 0.3 mΩ of trace/via resistance per current path and a 0.25 mΩ shunt:

$$
P_{Cu} \approx I^2(2R_{trace}+R_{shunt}) = 6400(0.6+0.25)\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{5.4\ W}
$$

This term is often forgotten, and on a nano board it is significant.

**Electrothermal feedback.** R_DS(on) depends on T_j, which depends on loss. Solve self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + Z_{th}\, P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + Z_{th}P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - Z_{th}P_0\alpha}
$$

Here P_0 = I²R_25 and α ≈ 0.006 K⁻¹. Thermal runaway occurs if Z_th·P_0·α ≥ 1. For a single FET (P_0 = 7.7 W while conducting) the margin is large. For the board-level node it is not, as Section 3 shows.

### 2.2 Switching losses (only the PWM leg switches)

**Hard-switching V–I overlap.** One device per edge pair:

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

**Reverse recovery.** This occurs when the high-side turns on into the conducting low-side body diode:

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}
$$

Recovery also adds an overshoot current spike that increases the turn-on energy. Budget +20% on P_VI if Q_rr is large.

**Output capacitance.** E_oss ≈ ½·Q_oss·V, for two devices:

$$
P_{oss} \approx 2\times\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}
$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction.** Two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$

**Gate drive.** This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET:

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = 0.058\ W
$$

### 2.3 Summary at the 80 A burst

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Copper + shunt | 5.4 W | 21.6 W |
| V–I switching | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.43 W |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | 0.23 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 32.2 W** | **≈ 129 W** |

**Design insight.** At 48 kHz with 80 A, conduction dominates at about 93%. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz costs only about 2.2 W per motor more. The real lever is R_DS(on) and copper resistance. Paralleling FETs halves conduction loss per path, but board area makes that impractical on a 20×20.
