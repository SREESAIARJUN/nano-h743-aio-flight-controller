## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at 48kHz alongside ArduPilot in any practical way. The standard and assumed architecture is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──bidir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / AM32 or BLHeli_32)
                                              └─► 4× 3-phase gate driver ─► 4× 6 MOSFET bridges
```

The 48kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The H743 only handles DShot and telemetry.

**Feasibility flags. Read before proceeding:**

1. **"Nano" form factor with 4×80A is not thermally sustainable.** Section 3 shows roughly 100W of dissipation at 4×80A simultaneously. A 20×20 or 30×30 board can survive this for only about 2–4 s.
2. **Spec "80A burst" as per-channel and time-limited** (for example 80A for 5 s on a single channel, with a lower simultaneous 4-channel limit). Section 3.4 gives the numbers to set those limits.
3. **6S assumed** (V_bus = 25.2V max). At 8S, switching loss scales linearly with V and Coss loss with V². Use 60V FETs in that case.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

**Commutation and PWM mode.** With six-step trapezoidal commutation, two phases conduct at any instant. Assume complementary PWM with synchronous rectification, which is the AM32 and BLHeli_32 default. Then:

- The PWM'd leg alternates between high-side (duty D) and low-side sync-rect (1−D).
- The opposing leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

**Current path resistance.** The motor current always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \left[ D \cdot R_{HS} + (1-D) \cdot R_{LS,sync} + R_{LS,return} \right] \approx 2 \, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

**Hot R_ds(on):**

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right] = 1.0 \times [1 + 0.007 \times 100] = 1.70\ \text{m}\Omega \quad (T_j = 125°C)$$

**Ripple contribution.** Phase current ripple adds to RMS current:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf}) \cdot D}{L_{ph} \cdot f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor, line-to-line L ≈ 15–25 µH. At burst near full throttle (D ≈ 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 20V), with L = 20 µH:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(25.2 - 20)(0.9)}{20\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 4.9\ \text{A} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} \approx 2\ \text{A}^2 \;(\text{negligible vs. } 6400)$$

**FET conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.70\text{m} = \mathbf{21.8\ W}$$

**Copper conduction loss.** The PCB copper must be included. 2 oz copper has a sheet resistance of 0.245 mΩ/□ per layer. Assume each phase/bus path is about 3 squares long, paralleled over 4 layers (2 oz each), at 100°C (×1.30):

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{3 \times 0.245}{4} \times 1.30 \approx 0.24\ \text{m}\Omega \text{ per path}, \quad 2\text{ paths}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.24\text{m} = \mathbf{3.1\ W}$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses @ 48kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each loss term:

**(a) V–I overlap, high-side FET hard switching:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**, sync FET body diode during dead time. This loss is dissipated in the high-side FET:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**(c) C_oss charge/discharge**, both FETs in the leg:

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$
