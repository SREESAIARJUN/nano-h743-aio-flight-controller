## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. A production-viable AIO looks like this:

```
 6S LiPo ──┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
           │                                             │
     [5V/10V/3V3 regs]                    ┌──────────────┴───────────────┐
           │                              │ 4× ESC channel (identical)   │
   ┌───────┴────────┐  DShot600 bidir     │  ESC MCU (STM32G071, AM32)   │
   │  STM32H743     │────────────────────►│  → 3-ph gate driver          │
   │  ArduPilot     │◄──── eRPM GCR ──────│  → 6× N-MOSFET half-bridges  │
   │  TIM1 CH1-4    │                     │  48 kHz center-aligned PWM   │
   └────────────────┘  ESC telem (UART)◄──│  shunt + amp → V/I telemetry │
                                          └──────────────────────────────┘
```

- **The 48 kHz switching frequency lives in the ESC MCUs.** The H743 only generates DShot600 frames and decodes the bidirectional eRPM replies.
- **"80 A burst" means 80 A per motor channel** for ≤ 2 s (assumption).
  - That is 320 A aggregate, roughly 8 kW at 25.2 V.
  - On a "nano" board this is thermally and mechanically extreme. Section 4 quantifies how extreme.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, the motor current always flows through **two conducting devices in series**:
- **ON-state:** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- **Freewheel:** LS(A) and LS(B), or HS/HS depending on scheme.

Two key consequences:
- Every 60° electrical, exactly 2 of the 6 FETs carry I_pk.
- Each FET conducts for 120° of 360°, so per-FET duty is 1/3. The *instantaneous* channel loss, however, is always 2 × I²R.

### 2.2 RMS current including ripple

Phase current ripple from PWM (inductor-volt-second balance):

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph,LL}\, f_{sw}}, \qquad
I_{rms} = I_{pk}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_{pk}}\right)^2}
$$

Example: L_LL = 15 µH, D = 0.5, V_bus − V_bemf = 12 V
→ ΔI_pp = 12 × 0.5 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) = **8.3 A**
→ I_rms = 80 × √(1 + 0.0009) ≈ **80.04 A**.

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz for typical motors. **Low-inductance motors (< 5 µH) need this term recomputed.**

### 2.3 Temperature-dependent conduction loss

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch}(T_j) = I_{rms}^2\,[\,2R_{DS}(T_j) + R_{Cu}\,]
$$

| T_j | R_DS | P per conducting FET | P_cond channel (2 FETs + Cu) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.90 mΩ | 5.76 W | 11.5 + 2.56 = **14.1 W** |
| 100 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 7.70 W | 15.4 + 2.56 = **18.0 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.31 mΩ | 8.35 W | 16.7 + 2.56 = **19.3 W** |

**Per-FET time-averaged loss** (1/3 conduction) at T_j = 100 °C ≈ 7.70 / 3 = **2.57 W**. The *instantaneous* 7.7 W matters for short thermal time constants: R_th,jc is reached in about 10 ms, while one electrical period at 10 krpm with 14 poles is 0.86 ms.

### 2.4 Thermal-runaway stability criterion

Because R_DS rises with T_j, the junction loop is stable only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{th,ja} < 1, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = I^2 R_{DS,25}\,\alpha_R
$$

Per conducting FET: 6400 × 0.9 mΩ × 0.0045 = **0.026 W/K**.

- Stable for R_th,ja < **38 K/W**. This is easily met, so there is no electrothermal runaway at the device level.
- The real constraint is absolute T_j, not loop gain.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Switching times from gate charge

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10-3}{3.2} = 2.19\text{ A}, \qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = 0.94\text{ A}
$$

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{14\text{ nC}}{2.19} = 6.4\text{ ns}, \qquad
t_f = \frac{14\text{ nC}}{0.94} = 14.9\text{ ns}
$$

The turn-off asymmetry is why a **separate, lower turn-off resistance** (diode + 0 Ω path) is recommended.
