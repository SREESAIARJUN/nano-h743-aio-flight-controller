## 0. Architectural Assumptions and Issues to Resolve First

Three things need settling before the math is useful.

**A. The H743 should not commutate the motors.**
ArduPilot runs on the H743 as the flight controller. Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. Reasons:
- ArduPilot has no in-tree sensorless BLDC/FOC commutation for this use case.
- 48 kHz PWM plus BEMF zero-cross detection at 4 motors × eRPM rates is a hard-real-time load that conflicts with EKF3 and scheduler timing.

The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over **bidirectional DShot600** on board traces. This is the architecture modeled below.

**B. "80 A burst" on a nano form factor is thermally transient-only.**
Section 3 shows the board's heat capacity supports roughly **1–2 s of simultaneous 4 × 80 A**, not the 10 s often printed on spec sheets. The 80 A figure must be specified with a duration and duty.

**C. Parameter values are design placeholders.**
Values below are representative of 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 mm PDFN MOSFETs used in 20 × 20 / 25.5 × 25.5 mm AIOs. Substitute datasheet values for your selected FET. The equations are the deliverable; the numbers are a worked example.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.5\,\text{mΩ} \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 100) = 2.18\,\text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, phase A high-side PWMs and phase B low-side is held on.

| Interval | Current path | FETs conducting |
|---|---|---|
| ON (fraction D) | HS_A → motor → LS_B | 2 |
| OFF (fraction 1−D) | LS_A (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_B | 2 |

So **two channels always carry I** (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of D)}$$

Per FET, averaged over one electrical revolution:
- High-side FET: conducts I for 1/3 of the revolution at duty D, plus sync-rectification conduction (1−D).
- Low-side FET: conducts I as the held-on device for 1/3 of the revolution.

$$\bar P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}\text{(if SR shared)}]$$

In practice, use the motor total divided by 6 for averaged thermal purposes. Use the 2-FET instantaneous figure for die-level transient checks.

### 1.3 Worked example at 80 A

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.18\,\text{mΩ} = 27.9\,\text{W}$$

**Add PCB copper loss.** Phase/bus path length is about 15 mm effective at 5 mm width across 2 oz × 2 layers paralleled:

$$R_{Cu} = \rho \frac{L}{A} = 1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot\frac{0.015}{0.005 \times 140\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.37\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.37\,\text{mΩ} \times 2 \text{ (supply + return)} \approx 4.7\,\text{W}$$

**Add shunt loss** (0.25 mΩ shared bus shunt per motor):

$$P_{shunt} = I_{bus,rms}^2 R_{sh}$$

At D ≈ 0.9, I_bus,rms ≈ √0.9 × 80 = 75.9 A, so P_shunt ≈ 1.44 W.

> If your 4-in-1 uses a single shunt for all four motors, total bus current reaches up to 320 A. In that case, use ≤ 0.1 mΩ, or a Kelvin-sensed copper-trace shunt, and recompute.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWMing high-side FET hard-switches. The sync-rectifier low side soft-switches (ZVS after dead time) but contributes Q_rr and dead-time diode conduction.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\,\text{W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$
