**Architectural clarification.** The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. It sends DShot600 commands to four independent ESC MCUs, which perform 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. These are two separate timing domains and are modelled separately below.

**Headline feasibility finding.** An 80 A burst on a 20×20 mm, single-FET-per-switch board is not thermally sustainable beyond roughly 1 s per FET under realistic conditions. The math is in §3. Continuous ratings for this form factor land around 15–20 A per motor. A credible 80 A burst rating requires 30×30 mm or larger, paralleled FETs, and a heatsink. The battery connector and leads also cannot carry 4 × 80 A = 320 A (XT30 is rated for about 30 A continuous).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step complementary PWM

At any instant, phase current flows through exactly two conducting devices:
- During the PWM on-time: the high-side FET of the active phase plus the low-side FET of the return phase.
- During the off-time: the synchronous low-side FET replaces the high-side FET.

The path therefore always contains 2·R_DS, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) + I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$
