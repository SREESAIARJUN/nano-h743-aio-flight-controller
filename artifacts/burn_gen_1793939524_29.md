# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model, Job #8

**Scope:** 80 A burst conduction and switching losses at 48 kHz PWM, thermal transient model, ArduPilot bidirectional DShot600 DMA and timer allocation, and a reference netlist.

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller**. It does not commutate the motors. Each motor is driven by its own ESC MCU (an STM32G071 running AM32 is assumed), which generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back.

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Board | 30.5 × 30.5 mm mounting, ~36 × 36 mm outline, 8-layer, 2 oz outer and inner copper |
| Commutation | Six-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM) |
| Switches | 2 × 40 V N-MOSFET in parallel per switch position. 24 FETs total (6 per motor). See the correction in §1.3. |
| Representative FET | 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 mm PDFN. R_DS(on) = 1.4 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V. Q_g = 25 nC, Q_gd = 6 nC, Q_rr = 30 nC, V_SD = 0.8 V |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class), V_CC = 10–12 V, ~1 A source / 1.5 A sink |
| PWM | f_sw = 48 kHz, dead time t_dt = 100 ns |

> **Feasibility warning.** At "80 A per motor", four motors draw **320 A** from the battery. A nano-class board cannot sustain this. The models below show the board reaching its thermal limit in a few seconds. Treat 80 A as a **≤ 5 s burst rating per motor**, and assume an aggregate battery current far below 4 × 80 A in practice. A true 20 × 20 mm "nano" format cannot meet 80 A at all. The analysis below assumes the 30.5 mm format.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design hot point T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110) = 1.4 \times 1.383 = 1.94\ \text{m}\Omega \;(\text{per FET})$$

With two FETs in parallel per switch, R_sw = 0.97 mΩ. Add an allowance for PCB copper and solder per conduction path of R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ (two switch positions plus phase pad):

$$R_{path} = 2R_{sw} + R_{cu} = 2.24\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current distribution in six-step commutation

In each 60° sector, exactly two phases carry the motor current I (one high-side, one low-side). Each switch position is active for 120° of 360°, which is 1/3 of the electrical period.

With synchronous rectification at duty D, the PWM-ing leg's high-side conducts D and its low-side conducts (1−D) during freewheeling. Either way, current always flows through exactly one device per active leg. The total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of D (to first order):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot R_{path}$$

Per switch position (time-averaged, worst case at D → 1):

$$P_{cond,sw} = \frac{I^2 R_{sw}}{3}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

| Quantity | Equation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Per switch position | 6400 × 0.97 mΩ / 3 | 2.07 W |
| Per FET (½ of position) | | **1.03 W** |
| Device conduction per motor | 6400 × 1.94 mΩ | 12.4 W |
| Copper loss per motor | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| **Total conduction per motor** | | **14.3 W** |

*Correction to the assumption table:* with 2 FETs per position the count is 6 positions × 2 × 4 motors = **48 FETs**, not 24. A 24-FET layout (single FET per position) roughly doubles device conduction loss, to 24.8 W per motor at 110 °C, and is not viable at 80 A.
