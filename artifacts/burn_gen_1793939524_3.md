## 0. Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors in a production design. The standard and robust partition is:

| Domain | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot | Attitude control. Generates 4× bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 | AM32 (or BLHeli_32) | Commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, current limiting. |
| Power stage | 24× N-MOSFET, 4× 3-phase gate driver | n/a | 80A burst per channel |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is therefore an **ESC firmware parameter** (the AM32 PWM frequency setting), not an H743 timer function. The H743's timer and DMA work is limited to DShot generation and capture (Section 4).

**Physical feasibility warning.** "Nano" boards (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) cannot sustain 4×80A simultaneously in any meaningful way. Section 3 shows the burst time is a few seconds at best. A rating of "80A burst" must specify duration, duty cycle, airflow, and whether it applies to one channel or all four. Section 3 quantifies this.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, current enters through one phase and exits through another:

- **During PWM on-time (D):** the current path is the high-side FET of phase A plus the low-side FET of phase B.
- **During PWM off-time (1−D):** with synchronous rectification, current freewheels through the low-side FET of phase A plus the low-side FET of phase B.

In both states, exactly two FETs are in series, so the conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Here α ≈ 0.006/K, which gives k_T ≈ 1.5 at 110 °C. The PWM-phase FETs split the loss D : (1−D). The return low-side FET carries the full I²R continuously, making it the hottest device.

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

- Hottest single FET (return low-side): 6400 × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W**.
- Because 6-step rotates conduction among the six FETs, each FET averages roughly one-third of this over an electrical cycle. This only holds at sufficient eRPM; at stall or low speed the full 11.5 W sits on one die.

The model must be solved iteratively because R_ds(on) depends on T_j:

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

### 2.2 Shunt and Copper Loss

$$P_{sh} = I^2 R_{sh} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

Plane and trace resistance adds roughly 0.3 mΩ per path, or about **1.9 W** more. On a 2 oz board this term is not negligible.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched PWM leg only)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is why dead time should be tuned to the minimum safe value. Halving it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W.

### 2.5 Reverse Recovery, C_oss, and Gate Drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} \times 2\ (\text{switching FETs}) = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

Gate-drive power is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET.

### 2.6 Loss Budget at 80A Burst

| Term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Shunt + copper | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Switching | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 31.4 W** | **≈ 125 W** |
| Efficiency (≈ 2 kW per channel input) | ≈ 98.4 % | n/a |

**Frequency sensitivity:** only switching, dead-time, and Q_rr/C_oss/gate losses scale with f_sw. Together they are 3.27 W at 48 kHz, so they cost ≈ **68 mW per kHz per channel**. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.6 W per channel. In return it gives lower current ripple and quieter, smoother low-throttle behavior.
