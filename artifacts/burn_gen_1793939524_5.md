# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Format): Engineering Model

**Document:** Job #0, Rev A (architecture-level)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three premises need correcting before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack (and in ArduPilot's model), the STM32H743 is the flight controller. It emits DShot600 to four ESC MCUs (typically STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32E230 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). These ESC MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 never sees 48 kHz. This document models both sides with that split.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally infeasible for more than about 1–2 s.** Section 3 proves this quantitatively. Real 80 A-class 4-in-1s are 30.5×30.5 or 40×40 mm, with 8–12 FETs per phase leg or heatsinks.
3. **The netlists below are reference-design netlists.** They cover one channel's power stage, the FC↔ESC interface, and the DShot timer pins. A production netlist (1,000+ nets) requires frozen part selection and stack-up. Every assumption below is parameterized so you can substitute real datasheet values.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

The hot on-resistance is:

R_ds(T_j) = R_ds,25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)] = 0.90 mΩ × 1.45 = **1.305 mΩ**

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly two FET channels in series. During the PWM on-time this is the HS of phase A plus the LS of phase B. During the off-time it is the LS (sync-rect) of phase A plus the LS of phase B. The path resistance is therefore 2·R_ds regardless of duty:

**P_cond,FET = I² · 2R_ds(T_j) = 80² × 2 × 1.305 mΩ = 16.70 W**

Copper and shunt losses add to this:

- P_cu = I² · R_cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = 3.20 W
- P_sh = I² · R_sh = 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = 1.60 W

The per-FET distribution sets the hot-spot. Each phase leg carries current for 120° of 360°. At burst throttle (D → 1), the HS FET of a conducting phase carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period:

P_FET,avg ≈ I² R_ds · (1/3) = 6400 × 1.305 mΩ / 3 ≈ **2.78 W per FET** (6 FETs per channel, with the LS split by duty)

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches.

**Hard-switching (V–I overlap):**

P_sw = ½ · V_bus · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz = **1.935 W**

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**

P_dt = V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz = **0.614 W**

**Reverse recovery:**

P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz = **0.060 W**

**Output capacitance (HS + LS):**

P_oss = 2 × ½ C_oss V² f_sw = 1 nF × 635 × 48 kHz = **0.030 W**

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistor, 2 switching FETs):**

P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw = 2 × 50 nC × 10 V × 48 kHz = **0.048 W**

### 2.3 Totals

| Loss term | Per channel (W) | 4 channels (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 16.70 | 66.8 |
| Copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| Shunt | 1.60 | 6.4 |
| Hard switching | 1.94 | 7.7 |
| Dead time | 0.61 | 2.5 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.14 | 0.5 |
| **Total** | **≈24.2** | **≈96.8** |

Total motor power at burst is about 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW in. Conversion efficiency is therefore about 98.8%, which looks good electrically. The problem is purely heat density.

**Sensitivity:**

- ∂P_cond/∂I = 2I·2R_ds ≈ 0.42 W/A. Losses are I²-dominated, so 60 A burst gives 0.56× the conduction loss.
- Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.3 W/channel. **
