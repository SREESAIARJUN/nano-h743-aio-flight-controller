# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

This is an engineering baseline. Every number derives from the stated assumptions, so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values and re-run the equations. Netlists are reference designs and need schematic review and SI/PI validation before layout release.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

ArduPilot does not commutate motors. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, which handles commutation and the bi-directional DShot telemetry. The baseline is:

```
                 ┌──────────── FC (STM32H743VIT6, 480 MHz) ────────────┐
 Battery 6S ──►  │ ArduPilot: DShot600 BIDIR x4 → TIM1 CH1..CH4         │
                 └──────┬──────────┬──────────┬──────────┬─────────────┘
                     M1_SIG     M2_SIG     M3_SIG     M4_SIG   (33 Ω series, 100 kΩ pull-up)
                 ┌──────▼──┐ ┌─────▼───┐ ┌────▼────┐ ┌───▼─────┐
                 │AT32F421 │ │AT32F421 │ │AT32F421 │ │AT32F421 │  ← AM32, 48 kHz PWM
                 │+3ph drv │ │+3ph drv │ │+3ph drv │ │+3ph drv │
                 │6× FET   │ │6× FET   │ │6× FET   │ │6× FET   │
                 └─────────┘ └─────────┘ └─────────┘ └─────────┘
```

**Form factor reality check.** On a "nano" board (20×20 or 30×30 mm), 80A per channel is achievable only as a short burst. Section 3 quantifies how short.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

In 6-step drive, two phases are active. With complementary PWM, current flows through exactly one FET in each active leg at all times: the high side during t_on and the low side during t_off. The conducting path is therefore always **2 × R_ds(on)**, independent of duty cycle (dead time is ignored here and handled in §2.2).

$$R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 6400 \times 3.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\,W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu} = I^2 \cdot R_{cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\,W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase leg is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that, the PWM'd leg splits current between high and low side by duty cycle D, while the non-PWM'd leg conducts continuously in its low-side (or high-side) FET.

- Worst-case single FET (continuously-on side, 1/3 of cycle at full current): I² · R_ds,hot · (1/3) ≈ **3.8 W average**, with 11.5 W instantaneous during its conduction interval.
- Six-FET average: 23.0 / 6 ≈ 3.8 W.

### 2.2 Switching Losses (PWM'd leg only)

**Hard-switching V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\,W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\,W$$

**Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\,W$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two dead-time intervals per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\,W}$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):**
$$P_g = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times 2 = 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.048\,W$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.68\,W}$$

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it drops to about 1.34 W. P_cond is f-independent. At 80A, conduction loss dominates roughly 9:1, so a lower PWM frequency buys little. Lower R_ds(on), or paralleled FETs, is the real lever.

### 2.3 Totals

| Item | Per channel | 4 channels (all bursting) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Copper conduction | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.7 W | 10.7 W |
| ESC MCU, LDO, gate-drive bias | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **~29.2 W** | **~117 W** |

Efficiency at 80A × ~22 V average (≈1.76 kW per channel) is about 98.3%. That sounds good, but 117 W concentrated in roughly 9 cm² is the core problem.
