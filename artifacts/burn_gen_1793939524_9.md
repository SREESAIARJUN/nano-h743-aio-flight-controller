# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model and Reference Design

**Job #28, Revision A (Preliminary)**

---

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Early Findings

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 cannot practically commutate 4 × 3-phase bridges (24 FETs) while also running ArduPilot. That would need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time and 4 × BEMF comparator chains. The industry-standard and assumed partition is:

| Block | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, bi-directional DShot600 master |
| ESC × 4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 (AM32 firmware) | 48 kHz complementary PWM, sensorless 6-step |
| Gate driver × 4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | HS/LS drive |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN3.3×3.3 | 6S capable |

**Findings that affect the spec:**

1. **The "80 A burst" rating is only physically achievable for about 1 s on a nano (20×20 mm) board.** This is derived in §3. Either redefine it as "80 A / 1 s, 10 % duty" or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm board.
2. **Do not route motor outputs to TIMx_CHxN pins.** Complementary outputs cannot input-capture, so bi-directional DShot fails. Several commercial H743 boards made this mistake (e.g., PB0/PB1 on TIM8_CH2N/CH3N).
3. All MOSFET and driver parameters below are representative datasheet-class values. **Re-run the model with the selected part's datasheet and validate with thermocouple testing** before release.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

With synchronous 6-step commutation, exactly two FETs carry I_ph at any instant: the chopped leg (HS during D, LS during 1−D) plus the static LS of the return phase.

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,k_T\,R_{DS(on)} = 2(80)^2(1.5)(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{28.8\ W}
$$

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical period (1/3), so the average per FET is:

$$
P_{cond,FET} = \frac{I^2 k_T R_{DS}}{3} = \mathbf{4.8\ W}
$$

PCB copper (phase path ≈ 0.5 mΩ, 2 oz, 6-layer): P_Cu = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per channel**. This is not negligible on a nano board.

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Gate currents during the Miller plateau:

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10-3.5}{6} = 1.08\ \text{A}, \qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{3.5}{6} = 0.58\ \text{A}
$$

Transition times:

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = 12\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} = \frac{13\ \text{nC}}{0.58\ \text{A}} = 22\ \text{ns}
$$

V-I overlap loss (one chopped leg per channel at any time):

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_{on}+t_{off}) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(34\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{1.65\ W}
$$

|---|---|---|
| Output charge | Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.036 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.038 W (mostly in driver/R_g) |
