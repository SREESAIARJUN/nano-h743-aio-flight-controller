# Job #32: STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC

## Electrothermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Design Package

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Findings

**Architecture.** ArduPilot runs on an STM32H743 flight controller. ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation. The 4-in-1 ESC therefore uses four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This package assumes:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIH6 (LQFP100/TFBGA), 400 MHz (Rev V parts allow 480 MHz).
- **ESC:** 4 × STM32G071GBU6 running AM32, with 4 × FD6288Q three-phase gate drivers and 24 × N-MOSFETs (40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class).
- **Link:** FC → ESC over bidirectional DShot600, with eRPM telemetry fed into ArduPilot's harmonic notch.

**Key findings:**

1. **80A per channel on a 20×20 mm nano board is a transient rating only.** Under typical prop-wash airflow, the board-level thermal budget supports roughly 7–15 W sustained. That corresponds to about 20–30A continuous per channel, not 80A. At 80A on all four channels, the board reaches thermal limits in about 2.5–3 s (§3). The ESC firmware's thermal derating is therefore mandatory, not optional.
2. **Bidirectional DShot600 on the H743 can use a single timer (TIM1 CH1–4).** This consumes 5 DMA streams out of 16, which leaves enough for IMU SPI, OSD/flash SPI, and the UARTs.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per ESC Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The commutation scheme is six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. Each path is either the active FET or its synchronous partner. Total channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:
