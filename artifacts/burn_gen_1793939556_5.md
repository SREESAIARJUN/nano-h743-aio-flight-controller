# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller: Architecture, Loss, Thermal, and DShot DMA Model

Every number below comes from stated assumptions. Swap in the datasheet values for your chosen MOSFETs, driver, and stackup, and rerun the equations. I have flagged where the requested specification is physically marginal.

## 0. Architecture Assumptions

### 0.1 MCU partitioning
The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 (Cortex-M0+/M4, about 64–120 MHz, with a 3-phase complementary timer). The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600, one signal wire per motor.

### 0.2 Baseline electrical and mechanical assumptions

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max (V_bus), 22.2 V nominal |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, synchronous rectification (complementary) |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal; 2 phases conduct per sector |
| MOSFET (per switch, ×24) | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN class (see 0.3) |

### ⚠ Feasibility flag
A true "nano" board (20×20 mm mounting) cannot dissipate 4 × 80 A for more than a few seconds. The thermal model below shows this. With realistic sizing, a 20×20 board sustains about 30–35 A continuous per channel. 80 A is a short burst, on the order of 2–5 s, and is better suited to a 30×30 layout. The model is parameterized so you can see exactly where this limit comes from.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths per commutation sector
In each 60° sector, the current I_ph flows through two phases, A (PWM leg) and B (static low side):

- **High side A (Q_AH):** conducts for duty D. Loss = D · I² · R_ds
- **Low side A (Q_AL), synchronous rectification:** conducts for 1 − D. Loss = (1 − D) · I² · R_ds
- **Low side B (Q_BL):** conducts 100% of the sector. Loss = I² · R_ds

Summing these:

**P_cond,ch = I_ph² · R_ds(T_j) · [D + (1 − D) + 1] = 2 · I_ph² · R_ds(T_j)**

This result is independent of D because of synchronous rectification. This is why synchronous rectification matters at burst.
