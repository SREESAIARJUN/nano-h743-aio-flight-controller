# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage & DShot Architecture

**Reference design for Job #52, Rev A**

> **Scope note.** No MOSFET, gate driver, or stack-up was specified, so this document uses a **parameterized model with representative component values**. Every equation is kept symbolic first, so you can substitute datasheet values for your actual BOM. Before releasing to fab, verify all part numbers and pinouts against current datasheets.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification (affects everything below)

The STM32H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. In any 4-in-1 design running ArduPilot:

| Domain | Device | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743 (480 MHz, ArduPilot runs it at 400 MHz) | Attitude control, DShot600 command TX, bidirectional eRPM RX |
| ESC (×4 channels) | 4× ESC MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class) | 6-step commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection |
| Power stage (×4) | 3-phase gate driver + 6 N-MOSFETs per motor (24 FETs total) | Power conversion |

The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over a **single-wire, bidirectional DShot600 line**. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC firmware setting (AM32: `PWM frequency = 48`).

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM:

- Two phases are active at any instant.
- Current always flows through exactly **one high-side-position FET and one low-side-position FET**.
- During PWM on-time, the path is HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B).
- During off-time, current freewheels through LS(A) → motor → LS(B), via the synchronous FET rather than the body diode.

Conduction loss per channel is therefore independent of duty cycle, to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \, I_{rms}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With trapezoidal current at burst, I_rms ≈ I_pk = 80 A (flat-top approximation, conservative).

### 2.2 Temperature dependence (closed-loop)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta JC}$$

Because R depends on T and T depends on P, solve iteratively. The fixed point exists when $\alpha \cdot P \cdot R_{\theta} < 1$, which holds here.

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET (averaged over 6 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.13 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W | 2.85 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W | 3.09 W |

**Per-FET distribution caveat.** Each phase conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle. The HS/LS split depends on duty cycle D:

- HS RMS² share ∝ D
- LS RMS² share ∝ (2 − D)

At low throttle, the **low-side FETs carry most of the loss**. Size copper accordingly: low-side FET source pours should be equal to or larger than high-side pours.

### 2.3 PCB copper conduction loss (often neglected)

Assume 2 oz (70 µm) copper, phase trace 8 mm long × 4 mm wide, on 2 layers in parallel:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot 0.008}{0.004\cdot 70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.49\,\text{m}\Omega \;\;(\div 2 \text{ layers} = 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega)$$

Two phase paths plus the bus path give ≈ 0.6 mΩ, so **P_Cu ≈ 3.8 W per channel at 80 A**. This is comparable to a single FET's loss, which is why heavy copper and via stitching are mandatory.

## 3. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz)

Per commutation step, one hard-switched FET transitions each PWM period (two edges).

### 3.1 V-I overlap loss

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery (lands in the opposite FET)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 40\text{nC}\cdot 25.2\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

### 3.4 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET)

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2\cdot 45\text{nC}\cdot 10\cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-channel switching total

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.69\ \text{W}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:**

- At 24 kHz: P_sw ≈ 0.85 W
- At 96 kHz: P_sw ≈ 3.4 W

The 48 kHz setting is a reasonable trade-off between loss, current ripple, and audible/sync behavior on low-inductance (high-KV) motors.
