# STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal and Bidirectional DShot Architecture (Job #55)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Key Findings

**Architecture assumption.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and cannot also commutate four motors at 48 kHz. Each motor channel therefore gets its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. This split is how all practical integrated FC+ESC boards work.

**Placeholder parameters.** Every MOSFET, driver and PCB value below is a stated placeholder. Replace them with datasheet values from your chosen parts before release.

**Key findings:**

1. **The "80 A burst" figure is a transient rating, not a thermal operating point.** With all four motors at 80 A, the board absorbs about 100 W and can sustain it for roughly **2–4 seconds**. The physics allows nothing more on a nano-format PCB.
2. **4 × 80 A = 320 A of battery current.** No nano-class connector, pad or pigtail carries that. Size the input for realistic simultaneous current (about 120–160 A peak) and state the per-motor burst separately.
3. **Bidirectional DShot on the H743 is straightforward,** thanks to DMAMUX. Two non-negotiable rules:
   - Never use complementary (`CHxN`) outputs.
   - Place DShot DMA buffers in non-cacheable SRAM.
