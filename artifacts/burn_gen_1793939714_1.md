**Form-factor warning.** 80 A burst per channel on a "nano" (20×20) stack is not physically credible. There is not enough copper cross-section, MOSFET count, or capacitance. This design assumes a 30.5×30.5 ESC board stacked under the H743 FC. If 20×20 is a hard requirement, the burst rating should drop to about 40–45 A.

**Architecture.** The H743 does not commutate the motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32, which performs the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 to each ESC MCU and receives eRPM telemetry back.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path topology

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, phase A HS is PWM'd while phase B LS is held on:

- **During D·T_sw:** I flows through HS_A and LS_B.
- **During (1−D)·T_sw:** I freewheels through LS_A (synchronous) and LS_B.

So at every instant, exactly two channel resistances are in series, independent of D (dead time is handled separately in §2):

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{Cu} + I_{ripple,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}$$

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 110 °C

- R_ds(110) = 1.0 mΩ × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **1.38 mΩ**
- P_FET,cond = 6400 × 2 × 1.38 mΩ = **17.7 W per motor**
- Copper path (phase traces, solder, pads, motor wire pads), R_Cu ≈ 0.5 mΩ: P_Cu = 6400 × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per motor**
- Ripple term: with motor L ≈ 10 µH, ΔI_pp ≈ V·D(1−D)/(L·f) ≈ 13 A at D = 0.5. I_rms,ripple = ΔI/√12 ≈ 3.8 A, giving about **0.04 W** (negligible).

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 120° of every 360° electrical:

- **LS of the "sink" phase:** fully on for 1/3 of the cycle, so P_avg = 8.8 W × 1/3 ≈ **2.95 W**.
- **HS and LS of the PWM'd phase:** share 8.8 W × 1/3 in the ratio D : (1−D). That is ≤ 2.95 W combined, plus switching loss on the HS.

Electrical frequency example: 14-pole motor at 30 krpm gives f_e = 30000/60 × 7 = 3.5 kHz. That period is short compared with the junction thermal time constant (ms), so use **averaged** per-FET power for junction estimates.
