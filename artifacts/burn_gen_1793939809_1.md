## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

The request mixes two subsystems, so the architecture is stated explicitly first:

| Subsystem | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Flight Controller | STM32H743VIT6 (ArduPilot) | Generates bidirectional DShot600 for 4 motors and decodes eRPM telemetry |
| ESC ×4 | AM32-class MCU (STM32G071 / AT32F421) + 3-phase gate driver + 6 MOSFETs | Commutation and 48 kHz PWM |

**The H743 does not drive the bridge.** Bidirectional DShot is an FC-to-ESC protocol. Switching losses therefore belong to the ESC MCU's PWM, and DMA allocation belongs to the H743.

**Feasibility flags:**

1. **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is not credible.** That form factor uses 3.3×3.3 mm FETs and roughly 6 cm² of copper. Real 20×20 ESCs are rated 35–55 A. This model assumes **30.5×30.5 mm mounting with PDFN 5×6 FETs**. Section 2.6 shows that even at that size, 80 A is a **≤ 5 s burst** rating, not continuous.
2. **Supply:** 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum and 22.2 V nominal. Worst-case 25.2 V is used for switching losses.

## 2. Thermal / Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At a design T_j of 100 °C: R_DS = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 75) = **1.34 mΩ**.

This is self-consistent if the computed T_j stays at or below 100 °C, which Section 2.6 verifies.

### 2.2 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

**Commutation pattern.** In each 60° sector, one high-side (HS) FET is PWM'd at duty D, and one low-side (LS) FET in another phase is held fully on. With synchronous rectification, the complementary LS FET of the PWM'd phase conducts during (1 − D).

Each phase leg conducts current for 2/3 of the electrical cycle:

- 1/3 of the cycle as the PWM'd leg: HS carries the current for fraction D, the complementary LS for (1 − D).
- 1/3 of the cycle as the return leg: its LS is fully on.

**Per-FET RMS currents:**

$$I_{HS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\frac{D}{3}}, \qquad I_{LS,rms} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1-D}{3}}$$

**Total conduction loss per motor.** Exactly two FET channels carry I_ph at every instant, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At 80 A burst and full throttle (D → 1):

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I_HS,rms = I_LS,rms | 80 × √(1/3) = 46.2 A |
| P per HS FET | 46.2² × 1.34 mΩ = **2.86 W** |
| P_cond per motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.34 mΩ = **17.2 W** |

**PCB copper loss (often neglected and significant).** Assume a phase path of 15 mm length and 5 mm width, on 4 parallel 2 oz layers (70 µm each).

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w\,t\,n} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8} \cdot 0.015}{0.005 \cdot 70\times10^{-6} \cdot 4} = 0.18\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Two paths are active, plus the bus planes (about 0.1 mΩ). That gives P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.46 mΩ ≈ **2.9 W per motor**.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET switches at near-zero voltage after the dead time.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{DC}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ ns} \cdot 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance loss**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{DC} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovered by the HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{DC} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\,(2 t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 100\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive**

The loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET. Two FETs switch at f_sw:

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 60\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal per motor: about 2.37 W.**

**Sensitivity.** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, about 1.2 W would be saved per motor. At 96 kHz, it rises to 3.9 W. 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between acoustic noise, current ripple and efficiency for low-inductance motors.
