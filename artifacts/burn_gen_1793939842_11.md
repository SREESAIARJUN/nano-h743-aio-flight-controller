## 0. System Topology (Baseline Assumption)

The STM32H743 cannot drive 4×3 half-bridges at 48 kHz with closed-loop commutation by itself. The realistic architecture is therefore an FC/ESC stack or AIO:

```
[6S LiPo] ──► 4-in-1 ESC power stage ──► 4× BLDC motors
                 │  4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32)  [A]
                 │  4× 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class)                [A]
                 │  24× N-MOSFET 40 V, PQFN 3.3×3.3                       [A]
                 ▲  bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire per motor)
[STM32H743 FC, ArduPilot] ── IMU(SPI), Baro(I2C), OSD, Flash, UARTs
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.6 = **1.92 mΩ**.

This creates a positive feedback loop (loss → T_j → R → loss). Section 3.4 solves it self-consistently.

### 1.2 Six-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification

At any instant, two phases are active:

- The **PWM leg** carries I through its HS FET during D·T and through its LS FET during (1−D)·T (complementary switching).
- The **static leg** carries I through its LS FET continuously.

So, at every instant, exactly two FETs carry I:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W\ per\ motor\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, the loss spreads across the 6 FETs. Exact sharing depends on D and on which side PWMs, but on average:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} = 4.1\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 1.4 Phase current vs. battery current

$$I_{bat} \approx D \cdot I_{phase}$$

At full throttle (D → 1) these are equal. At partial throttle, the **phase** current sets the FET loss. Specify "80 A burst" as phase current, or the model underestimates losses.

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses (not negligible at 80 A)

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace,phase} + R_{shunt} + R_{bus\ share})$$

With 0.3 mΩ effective [A] per conducting path ×2 paths: **≈ 3.8 W per channel**. A 0.25 mΩ total-current shunt at 320 A total (4×80) dissipates **25.6 W**. A single bus shunt at full 4-motor burst is therefore unrealistic. Use per-ESC sensing or a lower-value shunt, or accept that 4×80 A simultaneous never occurs (see §3.5).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. The terms are:

**(a) V–I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Overlap time from gate drive:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}},\quad I_{gate} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}{R_g + R_{drv}}$$

**(b) Body-diode reverse recovery**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \approx 0.048\ \mathrm{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period)**

$$P_{dt} = 2\, V_f\, I\, t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 0.8 \times 80 \times 50\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$
