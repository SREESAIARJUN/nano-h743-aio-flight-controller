## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need settling before the math.

### 0.1 Partitioning

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates DShot600. It does **not** generate the 48 kHz motor PWM. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, and that MCU drives the 48 kHz bridge.

The reference ESC MCU here is the AT32F421K8U7. Each channel uses:
- 1× 3-phase gate driver (FD6288Q class)
- 6× N-channel MOSFETs

### 0.2 The 80 A requirement must be scoped

80 A on all four channels at once means 320 A from the battery. At 6S (25.2 V) that is about 8 kW. No nano-format (20×20 mm mount, ~27×27 mm outline) power path can handle that:
- An XT60 is rated about 60 A continuous.
- Battery leads and pads will not support it either.

This document therefore uses two cases:

| Case | Definition | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | 80 A on one channel, others at ~30 A | Realistic yaw/punch transient |
| **B** | 80 A on all four channels | Worst-case bound, shows the time limit |

Recommendation: the spec should read **"80 A burst per channel, ≤5 s, aggregate input limited by battery/connector (~150 A burst)."**

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

The bridge uses trapezoidal or six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At every instant, phase current flows through **exactly two MOSFETs in series**:
- the PWM leg's high-side FET during the on-time, or its low-side FET during the off-time (synchronous rectification), and
- the return leg's low-side FET.

Therefore:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

with the temperature dependence:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

### 2.2 Numbers at 80 A

At T_j = 100 °C:

$$R = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 75] = 1.41\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.41\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.1\ W}$$

> **Correction to the quick estimate.** The "~9.6 W" figure often quoted counts only one FET. The two-FET series path doubles it.

At T_j = 25 °C the same calculation gives 12.8 W.

### 2.3 Thermal runaway check

Conduction loss rises with temperature because R_DS(on) rises with temperature. The loop is stable only if the loss slope is smaller than the heat-removal slope:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha = 0.070\ \text{W/K}$$

The loop is stable if:

$$0.070 < \frac{1}{R_{\theta,total}}$$

As Section 5 shows, the board has almost no steady-state heat path. So at 80 A this is **not a steady-state operating point**. It is a transient only, and the firmware current limit must enforce that.

### 2.4 Distribution across the six FETs

Averaged over a commutation cycle, each FET carries the conduction loss for about one third of the time:

$$\bar{P}_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{18.1}{6} \times 2 \approx 6\ \text{W}$$

This holds for the two FETs active in each 120° interval. Averaged over the full electrical cycle it is about 3 W per FET.

The time scale matters. The electrical period at a typical 1–2 kHz is far shorter than the junction thermal time constant (~ms), so cycle-averaging is valid.
