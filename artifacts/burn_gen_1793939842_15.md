## 1. Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 does **not** commutate the motors. In any production 4-in-1 AIO, the topology is:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) ──DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor)──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32 class,
                                                     e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071)
                                                     │  6× PWM @ 48 kHz + BEMF comparators
                                                     ▼
                                                    3-phase gate driver ─► 6× MOSFET per motor
```

Two consequences follow:

- The **48 kHz switching loss** is set by ESC firmware (AM32 `PWM frequency`) and the gate-drive design.
- The **H743 timer/DMA budget** only has to cover the DShot side.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model at 80 A Burst

### 2.1 Current paths (6-step trapezoidal with synchronous rectification)

At any instant one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) FET of the commutating phase pair carry the current.

- During PWM **on-time** (duty D), current flows through HS_a and LS_b.
- During **off-time** (1−D), the HS turns off and the complementary LS_a freewheels synchronously with LS_b.

In both states, **two R_DS(on) are in series** with the motor current. Ignoring dead time, which is modelled separately:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad\text{(independent of D)}
$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At a design T_j of 115 °C:

$$
R_{DS(on)} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega\,[1 + 0.0065 \cdot 90] = 1.585\ \text{m}\Omega \approx 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ ESC}
$$

PCB copper adds:

$$
P_{pcb} = 80^2 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase is active for 2/3 of the electrical cycle, and HS/LS share that time according to D. The worst-case FET is an LS at low duty, because it carries both freewheel and return current:

$$
P_{LS,max} \approx I^2 R \cdot \tfrac{1}{3}\left[1 + (1-D)\right] \xrightarrow{D\to 0.5} 6400 \times 1.6\text{m} \times 0.5 = 5.1\ \text{W}
$$

The mean per FET is 20.5 W / 6 ≈ 3.4 W.
