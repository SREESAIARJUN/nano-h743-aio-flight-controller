## 1. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, per ESC channel)

### 1.1 Conduction topology

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, at any instant two phases carry the full phase current I:

- **On-state:** HS(A) + LS(B) conduct.
- **Off-state (complementary PWM):** LS(A) + LS(B) conduct. Current freewheels through the synchronous FET of the PWM leg.

Either way, exactly **two channels of resistance R_DS(on)** carry I at all times. Body diodes conduct only during dead time.

### 1.2 Conduction loss with thermal feedback

R_DS(on) depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. The resulting fixed-point equation is:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R(T_j)
$$

For flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_b. Add a current-ripple term:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_b^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph\text{-}ph}\, f_{sw}}
$$

With a typical 2306–2807 motor (L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH) at D = 0.5 and V_bemf ≈ 12 V:

ΔI_pp ≈ (13.2 × 0.5)/(20e-6 × 48e3) ≈ **6.9 A**, so the ripple term is ΔI²/12 ≈ 4 A². This is negligible against 6400 A².

**Worked numbers at I_b = 80 A:**

| T_j | R(T_j) | P_cond per motor | Per hot FET (≈ half, the HS/LS sharing the duty) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.10 mΩ | 14.1 W | ~7 W |
| 100 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.9 W | ~10 W |
| 125 °C | 1.71 mΩ | 21.9 W | ~11 W |

The loss is not spread evenly. Within one commutation sector, the PWM leg's HS and LS share I² by duty (D and 1−D), while the non-PWM leg's LS takes I² for the full sector. Each sector lasts only 1/6 of an electrical period (~17 µs at 10 krpm with 14 poles). That is far below FET thermal time constants (ms), so averaging over the electrical cycle is valid. **The average per FET is P_cond/6 × 2 = P_cond/3 over a full cycle**, about 7.3 W at 125 °C.

Thermal runaway is avoided when:

$$
\frac{\partial P_{cond}}{\partial T_j}\, R_{th,ja} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2 I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\, R_{th,ja} < 1
$$

At 80 A this gives 2·6400·1.1e-3·0.0055 = 0.0774 W/K, so the condition requires **R_th,ja < 12.9 K/W** for the whole motor channel. As shown in §2, this holds at steady state only with good airflow. At burst it is governed by thermal capacitance, not R_th.

### 1.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg (the PWM leg) switches per sector. Each term below is per motor.

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive clamp):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_b (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 33\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.60\ W}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I_b \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.85 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.65\ W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (charge dumped into the turning-on FET):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}
$$

Q_rr scales with di/dt and I_F, so derate the datasheet value. A realistic in-circuit range is 2–3× the spec, giving 0.1–0.15 W.

**(d) Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 35\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.04\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 45\text{nC} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.043\ W}
$$
