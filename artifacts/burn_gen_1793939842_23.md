## 0. Architecture Decisions and Key Finding

The H743 does **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs. Each ESC MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and drives its own 3-phase bridge at 48 kHz. All loss analysis below applies to those bridges.

| Parameter | Value / Assumption |
|---|---|
| Board | 30 × 30 mm outline, 25.5 × 25.5 mm usable copper. Treated as "nano" (20 mm mounting) for the thermal case |
| Stack-up | 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| MOSFETs | 40 V N-ch, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 PDFN, R_DS(on),25 ≈ 1.0 mΩ @ 10 V V_GS, Q_g ≈ 60 nC, Q_rr ≈ 50 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.0 K/W |
| Gate driver | 3× half-bridge driver per ESC (FD6288-class), 10–12 V gate rail |
| ESC MCU | Cortex-M0+/M4 class at 64 MHz+ (AT32F421 / STM32G071-class) |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal 6-step, complementary (synchronous) PWM, 48 kHz |
| Burst spec | **80 A per channel, phase current**, at near-full throttle |

**Executive conclusion.** At 80 A per channel × 4 channels, the board dissipates about **100–110 W**. A nano-scale board has a thermal capacity of about 3 J/K and roughly 8 K/W to prop-wash air. The burst limit is therefore about **2–2.5 s** before FETs exceed 125 °C. A sustained 10 s burst is only feasible at about **42 A/channel**. The math is in Section 3.

**Status of numbers.** Every MOSFET parameter above is a representative placeholder. Substitute datasheet values for the chosen part before freezing the design.
