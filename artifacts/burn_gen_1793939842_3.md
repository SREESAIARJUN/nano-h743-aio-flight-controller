## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Current distribution in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification
At any instant, current flows through exactly two FETs in series: one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS) path. With complementary PWM, the "off" phase conducts through the opposite FET in the same leg. The conducting-path count remains 2.

Total bridge conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Over a full electrical cycle, each of the 6 FETs carries current for 1/3 of the time. In time-averaged terms, this is shared uniformly:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{6} = \frac{I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}{3}, \qquad I_{rms,FET} = \frac{I_{ph}}{\sqrt{3}}$$

The HS/LS split depends on duty D. At low D the LS FETs carry more, since they conduct during the PWM-off sync-rect interval. The worst-case FET at D → 0 approaches \(I_{ph}^2 R \cdot \tfrac{2}{3}\) during its active 120° window.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence
$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,/\text{K} \Rightarrow R_{125} \approx 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega$$

This is a positive-feedback loop. Solve self-consistently with the thermal model in §4.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| I_rms per FET | 80/√3 = 46.2 A |
| P_cond per FET | 6400 × 0.0015 / 3 = **3.20 W** |
| P_cond per ESC (6 FETs) | **19.2 W** |
| P_cond whole 4-in-1 | **76.8 W** |

### 1.4 Copper losses (often overlooked, and large here)
On a 20×20 board, the phase and bus copper paths are about 0.3–0.6 mΩ each.

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{phase\_trace} + I_{bus}^2 R_{bus}$$

- Per phase, with 0.4 mΩ trace and 2 phases active: 2 × 6400 × 0.0004 = **5.1 W per ESC**.
- Battery bus at 4 × 80 A × D_avg, assuming D ≈ 0.8 at full throttle: about 256 A DC. Through 0.2 mΩ of pad, trace, and via, that is **13 W**.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

Per ESC, only one leg hard-switches per PWM period: the PWM'd HS FET against the commutating LS.

### 2.1 V–I overlap
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance
$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (2 edges per period)
$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (lost in driver and gate resistors, not the FET)
$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.6 Commutation-event losses
Commutation happens at f_comm = 6 · eRPM/60. At 14-pole and 30 krpm, that is 105 kHz eRPM/60 → 21 kHz. Each event costs roughly one extra hard edge.

$$P_{comm} \approx \tfrac{1}{2}V I t_{sw} f_{comm} \approx 0.42\ \text{W}$$

### 2.7 Switching summary per ESC

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Q_rr | 0.05 |
| Q_oss | 0.04 |
| Dead-time | 0.31 |
| Commutation | 0.42 |
| **Total P_sw** | **≈1.8 W** |

At 80 A, conduction dominates by about 10×. Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.9 W per ESC but improves current ripple and audible noise. Dropping to 24 kHz does **not** solve an 80 A thermal problem.

### 2.8 Total burst dissipation

| | Per ESC | 4-in-1 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 | 76.8 |
| Switching | 1.8 | 7.2 |
| Phase copper | 5.1 | 20.4 |
| Bus copper | — | 13 |
| **Total** | **~26 W** | **~117 W** |

Efficiency check: input power is about 25 V × 256 A ≈ 6.4 kW, so 117 W of loss is 1.8%. Electrically reasonable. **Thermally, it is the problem.**
