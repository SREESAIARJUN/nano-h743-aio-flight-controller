## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\text{–}0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS ≈ 1.0 mΩ × 1.47 ≈ **1.47 mΩ**. Use 1.5 mΩ for margin.

### 1.2 Six-step bridge with synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, two phases are active. The high-side (HS) FET of phase A switches with duty D. Its complementary low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1−D). The LS FET of the return phase conducts continuously.

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{DS}\,D + I^2 R_{DS}\,(1-D) + I^2 R_{DS} = \boxed{2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)}
$$

This is independent of duty, which is a key property of synchronous rectification.

At 80 A:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs takes on average 1/6 of the bridge conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Instantaneous** loss is 9.6 W in the conducting FET. At low eRPM (e.g., punch-out from hover, < 50 Hz electrical), the junction tracks the instantaneous value. Design to the instantaneous number for Z_th transients shorter than ~10 ms.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction

For 2 oz copper (70 µm), sheet resistance is about 0.25 mΩ/□ at 25 °C (×1.33 at 110 °C).

Assume ~2 squares of effective path per phase current loop across power planes, plus vias:

$$
R_{Cu} \approx 2 \times 0.33\,\text{m}\Omega + R_{via} \approx 0.8\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} \approx 80^2 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

Copper loss is often ignored and is a major error source. Mitigate with stacked power layers, via arrays (≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias per FET drain/source pad), and exposed copper with solder buildup on the phase pads.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (48 kHz, V_bus = 25.2 V, I = 80 A)

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (HS FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}
$$

The edge-time estimate is driven by the Miller plateau:

$$
t_{Miller} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_{gate}} = \frac{6\,\text{nC}}{0.8\text{–}1.2\,\text{A}} \approx 5\text{–}8\ \text{ns}
$$

Total transition time with the Q_gs2 portion and gate resistance is ~12–15 ns per edge.

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode, after dead time)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

The diode-recovery current also adds to HS turn-on loss, roughly +10–20% of P_sw. Use 0.2 W for both effects combined.

### 2.3 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

Use the energy-equivalent C_oss, with both FETs in the leg contributing:

$$
P_{oss} \approx C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 900\text{pF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.027\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
