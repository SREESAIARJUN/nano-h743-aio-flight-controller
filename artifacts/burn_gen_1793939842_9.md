## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology current paths

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM:
- One leg is PWM'd. Current flows through its high-side FET for fraction D and its low-side FET for fraction (1−D).
- A second leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.
- So at any instant, about **two FET channels** carry I_ph, independent of D (dead time ignored here; handled in §2.4).

The phase current RMS includes switching ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For typical 5" to 7" motors (L_ph ≈ 15–40 µH), ΔI²/12 is under 1 % of I_ph² at 80 A, so it is neglected below.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.5525 ≈ **2.33 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Instantaneous and averaged loss

**Total conduction loss per ESC:**

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6400 \times 2.33\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{29.8\ W}$$

**Peak loss in a single conducting FET:**

$$P_{FET,pk} = I_{ph}^2 R_{ds} = \mathbf{14.9\ W}$$

**Average loss per FET over an electrical cycle.** The load is shared across 6 FETs:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx 29.8/6 \approx 5.0\ \text{W}$$

The electrical frequency is roughly 1–3 kHz, which is much faster than the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms). So the die sees something between average and peak. Use about 0.6 × P_pk ≈ 9 W for junction-rise estimates.
