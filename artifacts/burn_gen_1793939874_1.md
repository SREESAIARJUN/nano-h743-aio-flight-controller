# Job #24: STM32H743 Nano FC + Integrated 4-in-1 ESC
## System Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

---

## 0. Feasibility Findings

Here are the conclusions the math below supports:

1. **"80A burst" on a nano-class board is a time-limited rating.** At 80A per motor, losses are about 39W per channel and about 155W per board. The board's lumped heat capacity is only about 5 J/K, so the adiabatic burst window is **about 1.5–2.5 s**. Continuous rating on this footprint is about 25–35A per channel with airflow. The spec sheet should say "80A burst (≤2 s, 25°C start)", not "80A".
2. **The H743 does not switch the FETs.** At 48kHz, commutation and PWM run on four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 running AM32 is assumed here). The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. Any design that puts gate PWM on the H743 conflicts with ArduPilot's architecture.
3. **The DC-link capacitor is the second-hardest constraint after thermal.** Ripple current reaches about 40A RMS per channel at D = 0.5. An external low-ESR bulk capacitor is mandatory.
4. **Form factor.** "Nano" (20×20mm mounting) cannot physically hold 24 power FETs plus an H743 at this current. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5mm board with 8 layers of 2oz copper**. Scaling to 20×20 cuts thermal mass by about 57% and the burst window to under 1 s.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125°C: R_ds = 1.8 × 1.6 = **2.88mΩ**.

### 2.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous rectification

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd (HS for D, LS for 1−D) and one leg's low-side is held ON. Current always flows through exactly two conducting FETs in series:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

The result is independent of D, which is the benefit of synchronous rectification.

Ripple correction: with phase inductance L ≈ 10µH,

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{10\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 13.1\,\text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 14.3
$$

The ripple adds only +0.2%, so it is negligible at 48kHz.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80A, T_j = 125°C

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.88\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{36.9\ W}
$$

Per-FET average: each of the 6 FETs carries 1/6 of the duty-weighted loss, giving about **6.1W/FET**. At D → 1 the HS FETs carry more, up to about 1/3 × I²R ≈ 6.1W each. The worst-case FET is therefore about 6.2W.

### 2.4 Copper and interconnect

**Board-level adder: about 3.8W per channel.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. That gives one turn-on and one turn-off per period, with hard switching on the HS FET and the LS FET acting as a synchronous rectifier.

### 3.1 V-I overlap

Gate driver source/sink is about 1.0A effective after R_g, so t_r ≈ t_f ≈ Q_gd / I_g ≈ 8ns, plus Miller and di/dt margin, giving about 10ns each.

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$
