### Architectural Clarification

In every production AIO of this class, the H743 is the flight controller. It talks to four dedicated ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The H743 does not run motor commutation. The rest of this document assumes that partition.

## 1. Feasibility Flags

Address these before layout.

1. **"Nano" vs 80 A.** A 20×20 mm board cannot physically place 24 PDFN5×6 FETs. It also cannot fit the H743 package with the bulk capacitance, gate drivers, and four ESC MCUs. This design assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm board. If 20×20 is a hard requirement, the realistic rating is roughly 35–45 A burst using 3.3×3.3 mm FETs.
2. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** As §3 shows, four channels at 80 A dissipate about 60 W. That load is thermally limited to a few seconds.
3. **6S at 48 kHz** is acceptable but not ideal. Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. 24 kHz would halve it, at the cost of ripple and acoustics.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The PWM leg alternates between its high-side (HS) FET, for duty fraction D, and its low-side (LS) FET by synchronous rectification, for 1−D.
- The return leg's LS FET is on continuously.

At every instant, the motor current therefore passes through exactly two channels in series. This holds regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent Resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)] \approx 0.70\text{ mΩ} \times 1.45 = 1.015\text{ mΩ}$$

### 2.3 Copper Parasitics

PCB copper is often larger than R_ds(on), so it must be included. Using 2 oz outer layers plus 2× 2 oz inner layers in parallel:

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho_{Cu}(T)\, L}{w\, t_{eff}}, \quad \rho_{Cu}(100°C) = 2.2\times10^{-8}\ \Omega\text{m}$$

With L = 12 mm, w = 4 mm, and t_eff = 4 × 70 µm = 280 µm:

R_Cu ≈ 0.24 mΩ per segment, and the path includes about 2 segments, giving 0.47 mΩ.

### 2.4 Per-Channel Result at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2·80²·1.015 mΩ | **12.99 W** |
| Copper | 80²·0.47 mΩ | **3.01 W** |
| Shunt (if used, 0.25 mΩ) | 80²·0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **≈ 17.6 W** |

### 2.5 Per-FET Distribution (Worst Case)

- The return-leg LS FET carries the full current continuously: 80²·1.015 mΩ = **6.5 W**.
- The PWM leg splits roughly 6.5 W between its HS and LS FETs according to D.

Commutation rotates these roles every 60° electrical, which averages per-FET dissipation to about 2/6 of the conducting total. Because the electrical period is short, junction ripple is negligible.

The time-averaged per-FET conduction is approximately:

$$\bar P_{FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{ds}}{6} = 4.3\text{ W}$$

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg's HS FET hard-switches. The LS FET switches with zero voltage after dead time.

### 3.1 V–I Overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 1.94\text{ W}$$

### 3.2 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.31\text{ W}$$

### 3.3 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{ k} = 0.06\text{ W}$$

### 3.4 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{ nC}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.05\text{ W}$$

### 3.5 Gate Charge

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{ nC}\times10\times48\text{ k} = 0.058\text{ W}$$

### 3.6 Per-Channel Switching Total

**≈ 2.4 W.** Halving f_sw to 24 kHz saves about 1.2 W per channel.

### 3.7 Grand Total at 80 A Burst, All Four Channels

$$P_{tot} = 4 \times (17.6 + 2.4) \approx \mathbf{80\ W}$$

Of this, about 62 W is in the FETs and copper, and the rest is in the shunts and drivers.
