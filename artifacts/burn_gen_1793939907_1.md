## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

Four points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor (or each pair, or all four) is commutated by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H743.
2. **80 A burst does not fit on a 20×20 mm "nano" board.** Twenty-four power FETs, 4 gate drivers, 4 MCUs, bulk capacitance and an H743 (LQFP100 or TFBGA) cannot be placed there at this current. This design therefore uses **30.5×30.5 mm mounting on a 36×36 mm board**, which is the smallest credible size. The thermal model in §3 shows that even this board can only sustain 80 A for about 1–2 s.
3. **Bus voltage:** 6S LiPo, so V_bus = 25.2 V maximum and 22.2 V nominal. The FETs are rated 40 V, leaving about 60% headroom for ringing and regen spikes.
4. **Commutation:** 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM on the active leg. This is AM32's default.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step complementary mode

In any 60° commutation sector, two legs carry the phase current I:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for a fraction D of each period. The low-side (LS) FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts for 100% of the sector.

At every instant, exactly **two FET channels** are in series with the motor. This holds whether HS or LS is on in the PWM leg, which is why synchronous rectification makes conduction loss independent of D.

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2\,(2R_{ds,hot} + R_{trace,path})$$

### 1.2 Per-FET average

Each phase spends one-third of the electrical cycle as the PWM leg, one-third as the return leg, and one-third floating.

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\frac{D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = I^2 R_{ds}\cdot\frac{1 + (1-D)}{3}$$

The worst-case FET is the LS with D → 0, giving P_LS → (2/3)·I²R.

At full throttle (D ≈ 1), P_HS = P_LS = (1/3)·I²R.

### 1.3 Numbers at I = 80 A, R_ds,hot = 1.6 mΩ

| Quantity | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| I²R (one FET) | 6400 × 0.0016 | 10.24 W |
| FET conduction per ESC | 2 × 10.24 | **20.5 W** |
| Trace / copper | 6400 × 0.0005 | 3.2 W |
| Per-FET average (D ≈ 1) | 10.24 / 3 | 3.41 W |
| Worst LS FET (low D, high I, e.g. punch-out from a stall) | 10.24 × 2/3 | 6.83 W |

The electrical frequency is 1–3 kHz at typical eRPM. That is far faster than the FET's thermal time constant (ms range), so averaged per-FET power is valid for junction calculations.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Per ESC there is one hard-switched HS/LS pair at any time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching (V–I overlap)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
