## 0. Design Reality Check

These findings constrain everything that follows.

| Issue | Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **80 A × 4 on a nano board** | A 20×20 mm stack (or even 25.5×25.5 "whoop" format) has roughly 1.5–2.5 J/K of heat capacity and almost no convective area. | 80 A is achievable only as a **≤1–2 s burst per channel**. It is not sustainable. Continuous rating is about 25–35 A per channel with prop-wash airflow. |
| **Battery current** | 4 × 80 A phase current ≠ 320 A from the battery. I_batt ≈ D·I_phase. | Size the input bus for about 120–160 A peak, not 320 A. An XT60 (≈60 A continuous) is undersized for sustained bursts. XT90 pads or direct solder are recommended. |
| **Architecture** | The H743 does not commutate motors. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running BLDC firmware (AM32 / BLHeli_32). | The FC→ESC interface is bidirectional DShot600 on 4 lines. The H743 never sees 48 kHz PWM. |
| **48 kHz PWM** | This is the ESC-side PWM frequency, set in AM32/BLHeli_32. | Switching-loss math below applies to the ESC power stage. |

**Recommended physical format:** a stacked design with a 30.5×30.5 mm 4-in-1 ESC and a 20×20 or 30.5 mm H743 FC. A true single-board "nano" all-in-one at 80 A burst is thermally marginal. Section 2 shows why quantitatively.

## 2. Mathematical Loss Model

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). At any instant, the motor current I_ph flows through exactly **two conducting MOSFET channels**:

- **PWM-on:** high-side A and low-side B.
- **PWM-off (freewheel):** low-side A, synchronously rectified, and low-side B.

Each FET position conducts during 1/3 of an electrical cycle (high- or low-side of its phase within the 120° window).

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 100) = **1.45 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Conduction Loss (per ESC channel)

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

For trapezoidal drive with flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I_ph during conduction. Taking 80 A burst at T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.6\ W}$$

At T_j = 25 °C (start of burst): 2 × 6400 × 1.0 mΩ = 12.8 W.

**Thermal feedback.** Because R rises with temperature, the steady state is

$$T_j = T_a + R_\theta\,P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_\theta P_0 \alpha}$$

where P_0 = 2I²R_25. Thermal runaway occurs when **R_θ·P_0·α ≥ 1**. Per FET, the average P_0 = 12.8/6 × 2 = 4.27 W. With conducting-pair weighting, use the per-FET average below.

### 2.4 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg, per ESC channel)

Only the PWM-ing half-bridge hard-switches. Using the linear V/I overlap model:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.5 Capacitive, Reverse-Recovery, Dead-Time and Gate Losses

| Loss | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Output capacitance | P_oss = Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | 30 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.036 W** |
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48k = **0.048 W** |
| Dead time (body diode) | P_dt = V_F·I_ph·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | P_g = 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw | 2 × 40 nC × 10 × 48k = **0.038 W** (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |

### 2.6 Loss Budget

| Component | Per Channel @ T_j = 125 °C | 4 Channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 18.6 W | 74.4 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 1.45 W | 5.8 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.46 W |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.08 W | 0.34 W |
| Gate drive | 0.04 W | 0.15 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, if low-side single shunt; I_rms ≈ I·√D at D = 0.8) | ≈2.6 W | 10.2 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.3 mΩ effective path, 2 oz) | ≈1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈25.3 W** | **≈101 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction loss is about 74% of the total. At 48 kHz, switching is a second-order effect. Reducing R_DS(on) (paralleled FETs or 0.6 mΩ devices) is worth roughly 10× more than faster switching. The shunt and copper path are non-trivial and frequently under-budgeted on nano boards.

**Sensitivity to f_sw.** P_sw + P_dt + P_oss + P_rr scales linearly at about 0.044 W/kHz per channel. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.06 W per channel. That is acceptable, and 48 kHz reduces audible noise and current ripple in low-inductance motors.
