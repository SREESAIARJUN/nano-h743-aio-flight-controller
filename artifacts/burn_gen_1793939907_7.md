# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC Nano Stack (Job #61): Electro-Thermal Model, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

**Document class:** Architecture baseline, Rev A (pre-layout)

> **Assumptions and corrections to verify before release**
> 1. **Architecture.** The H743 runs ArduPilot. The 4-in-1 ESC uses **four dedicated ESC MCUs** running AM32 or BLHeli_32, with 48 kHz PWM and 6-step synchronous commutation. The H743 does not commutate motors. ArduPilot has no native sensorless FOC/6-step motor control, so a "single-H743 ESC" is not a supported configuration.
> 2. **Form factor.** Twenty-four FETs plus drivers rated for 80 A do not fit honestly on a 20×20 mm board. The model below assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm stack** with a 6-layer, 2 oz ESC board. If marketing insists on "nano 20×20", the burst rating must drop to roughly 40–45 A.
> 3. **Electrical baseline.** 6S pack (25.2 V max), 80 A per channel for burst only.
> 4. **Part numbers and parameters.** Device parameters are representative of 40 V and 60 V DFN5×6 / DFN3.3 trench MOSFETs. **Replace them with the selected datasheet's values** before sign-off.

## 1. Power-stage topology and conduction model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

During any 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low. Current flows:

- **On-state (duty D):** HS_A → motor → LS_B
- **Off-state (1−D):** LS_A (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_B

So **two MOSFET R<sub>DS(on)</sub> are always in series with the phase current**, independent of duty cycle. Dead-time intervals are the exception and are treated separately in §2.3.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R<sub>DS(on)</sub>

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| Parameter | 40 V option (e.g. 0.8 mΩ class) | 60 V option (e.g. 1.4 mΩ class) |
|---|---|---|
| R<sub>DS,25</sub> (V<sub>GS</sub>=10 V) | 0.80 mΩ | 1.40 mΩ |
| R<sub>DS</sub> @ T<sub>j</sub>=125 °C | 1.28 mΩ | 2.24 mΩ |

### 1.3 Conduction loss per channel

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2\;\big[2R_{DS}(T_j)\big] + I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{Cu,ch}$$

Ripple at 48 kHz is small relative to 80 A when motor inductance is ≥ 10 µH, so I<sub>rms</sub> ≈ I<sub>DC</sub>.

**Copper term.** Power and phase planes use 2 oz copper (≈ 0.25 mΩ/sq at 100 °C), with roughly 4 squares per path split across 2 layers:

$$R_{Cu,ch} \approx 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

**Worked result at 80 A, T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C, 40 V FETs:**

$$P_{cond,FET} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.28\text{ m}\Omega = 16.4\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.5\text{ m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The FET term is split as **≈ 8.2 W in each of the two conducting FETs**. This peak per-device figure drives the junction gradient calculation in §3.

## 2. Switching losses at f<sub>sw</sub> = 48 kHz

Only **one half-bridge per channel switches** in a given sector, so the terms below are per channel.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t<sub>r</sub>+t<sub>f</sub> = 20 ns (2 A source / 3 A sink driver, R<sub>g</sub> ≈ 2.2 Ω):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$
