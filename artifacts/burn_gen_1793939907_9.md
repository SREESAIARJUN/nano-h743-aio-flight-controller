## 0. Design Basis and Feasibility Verdict

These assumptions drive every number below. Change any one and recompute.

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal | 8S is not viable with 40 V FETs (see §3.4) |
| Burst current | 80 A phase current, per motor, for t_burst | "80A burst" is a per-channel rating |
| ESC PWM | 48 kHz, complementary (synchronous rectification), 6-step | AM32 / BLHeli_32-class firmware |
| ESC MCU | Separate per-channel MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071) | **The H743 does not commutate motors.** It is the flight controller. |
| MOSFET | 40 V, PDFN 3.3×3.3, R_DS(on) = 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V | Representative of current 40 V shielded-gate parts |
| Gate driver | 3-phase bootstrap driver (FD6288Q class), 10 V gate rail | |
| Ambient | 40 °C, with prop-wash convection | |

**Feasibility verdict.** A true 20×20 mm "nano" board cannot place 24 power FETs plus drivers, shunt, and bulk capacitance. 24 × 3.3×3.3 mm FETs need about 260 mm² of the roughly 300 mm² usable area, before routing and keep-outs. The ESC is therefore specified as a **30.5×30.5 mm stack layer**, with the H743 FC on a 20×20 mm board using 20×20-to-30.5 standoffs, or with both on 30.5 mm.

Even at 30.5 mm, §4 shows that 80 A is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous one. Marketing must not label this an "80A ESC".
