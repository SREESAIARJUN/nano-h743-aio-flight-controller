# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Power-Stage, Thermal & BDShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture Clarification

I don't have a Job #42 spec sheet, so every derivation below is **parameterized**. Swap in your MOSFET datasheet values and the equations still hold. The worked numbers use these assumptions:

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I_ph | 80 A (DC-equivalent, trapezoidal) |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET (40 V, 5×6 or 3.3×3.3 class) | R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ @ V_gs = 10 V |
| R_ds(on) temperature coefficient | k_T | ≈1.6× at T_j = 125 °C |
| Rise / fall time (at driver strength used) | t_r, t_f | 20 ns / 20 ns |
| Effective Coss (energy-related) | C_oss,er | 2 nF |
| Body diode Qrr / V_f | Q_rr, V_f | 50 nC / 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC @ 10 V |
| Current shunt (per channel) | R_sh | 0.25 mΩ |

**Architecture note:** On real 4-in-1 stacks, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the **flight controller**. Each motor's commutation is handled by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically on an STM32G071, AT32F421, or GD32. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry. It does **not** drive gates directly. The model below follows that partition.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
6-step trapezoidal BLDC with synchronous (complementary) PWM:
- At any instant, **two phases conduct**:
  - The **PWM'd leg** alternates between its high-side FET (duty D) and low-side FET (1−D).
  - The **fixed leg** has one FET on for 100% of the 60° sector.
- Each FET is active for 120° (1/3) of the electrical cycle.

### 1.2 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\,\big[\underbrace{D + (1-D)}_{\text{PWM leg}} + \underbrace{1}_{\text{fixed leg}}\big] = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

Note that P_cond is **independent of duty** with synchronous rectification. That is why synchronous mode is mandatory at 80 A.

Temperature-dependent resistance:

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow 1.92\ \text{m}\Omega\ @\ 125\,°C
$$

### 1.3 Worked numbers (per channel, at 80 A)

| Condition | P_cond |
|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 2 × 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = **15.4 W** |
| T_j = 125 °C | 2 × 6400 × 1.92 mΩ = **24.6 W** |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt losses (often ignored, shouldn't be)

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | **1.6 W** |
| Phase/bus copper (2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective path) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | **≈1.9 W** |

**Thermal runaway check:** R_ds rises with T_j, so P_cond rises with T_j. Stability requires the following, which is easily met here:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\cdot R_{th} < 1
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches, so there is one hard-switched commutation pair per channel.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f_sw = 50 nC·25.2·48 k | **0.06 W** |
| Coss charge | 2 × ½ C_oss V² f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (lost in driver) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| **P_sw total** | | **≈2.7 W** |

**Sensitivity to f_sw.** All switching terms scale linearly with f_sw:
- At 24 kHz, switching loss drops to ~1.35 W.
- At 96 kHz, it rises to ~5.4 W.

At 80 A, conduction dominates by ~9:1. The argument for 48 kHz is lower current ripple and acoustic benefit for low-inductance motors, not efficiency.

**Dead time is the biggest secondary lever.** Cutting it from 100 ns to 50 ns saves ~0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch first.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 24.6 W | 98.4 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **≈30.8 W** | **≈123 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × D_eff. At full throttle, losses are about 1.5% of the ~2 kW per channel. The efficiency is fine. **The heat density is the problem.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Junction-to-case (per FET)
Average per-channel loss of ~27 W is spread over 6 FETs. The worst-case FET carries:
- Fixed-leg 100% conduction: 80² × 1.92 mΩ / 3 duty ≈ 4.1 W average
- Plus switching share

That gives roughly **5 W peak-average**. With R_th,jc ≈ 1 K/W, **ΔT_jc ≈ 5 K**, which is negligible. The bottleneck is the board.

### 4.2 Lumped board heat capacity (30.5 × 30.5 mm stack, 8-layer, 2 oz)

| Component | Calculation | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (≈50% fill) | 0.03² m² × 8 × 70 µm × 0.5 → 2.25 g × 385 J/kg·K | ≈ 0.9 J/K |
| FR4 | 1.44 cm³ × 1.85 g/cm³ → 2.7 g × 1100 J/kg·K | ≈ 2.9 J/K |
| FETs, caps, solder | | ≈ 1.2 J/K |
| **C_th total** | | **≈ 5 J/K** |

### 4.3 Transient response
Single-pole RC model, with convective R_th,board-air ≈ 8 K/W in still air or ≈ 2.5 K/W in prop-wash at ~10 m/s:

$$
T(t) = T_a + P\,R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/(R_{th} C_{th})}\right)
$$

With prop-wash, τ = 2.5 × 5 = 12.5 s. Early in the burst, the slope is approximately:

$$
\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{123}{5} \approx 25\ \text{K/s}
$$

| Scenario | ΔT_board | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| All 4 channels at 80 A, 1 s | ~24 K | OK |
| All 4 channels at 80 A, 3 s | ~65 K | Marginal (board ~100 °C from 35 °C ambient) |
| All 4 channels at 80 A, 5 s | ~100 K | Exceeds solder/FR4 limits |
| All 4 channels at 80 A, steady state | P × R_th ≈ 300 K | **Impossible** |

### 4.4 Engineering conclusion
**"80 A burst" on a nano stack is only credible as a ≤2–3 s rating**, or with an added aluminum heat spreader. A 3 g spreader adds ~2.7 J/K and roughly doubles the burst window. A realistic **continuous** rating is set by steady state:
