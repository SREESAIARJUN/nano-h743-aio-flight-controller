# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #25): Systems Architecture Analysis

## 0. Executive Finding

**An 80A per-motor burst rating cannot be supported on a nano (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 stack.** Sections 1 and 2 derive this, and Section 3 gives the derating that is physically supportable.

There is also an architectural correction. In standard 4-in-1 designs, the STM32H743 does not switch the FETs. It sends DShot600 frames to a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. The model below follows that split:

- **FC (H743):** DShot600 output, bidirectional eRPM capture, DMA allocation.
- **ESC:** power stage, conduction losses, switching losses, thermal behaviour.

Everything below is a first-order engineering model with stated assumptions. **It is not a fab-ready design.** Validate the netlist with ERC/DRC, check the MOSFET datasheets you actually select, and confirm the thermal results on hardware with thermocouples or IR.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Assumptions

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C | R₂₅ | 0.9 mΩ (40 V, 3.3×3.3 PQFN class) |
| R_DS(on) temperature factor at T_j ≈ 125 °C | k_T | 1.55 → R_hot ≈ 1.4 mΩ |
| Q_gs2 + Q_gd (plateau charge) | Q_sw | 12 nC |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 30 nC |
| Gate drive voltage, plateau voltage | V_drv, V_pl | 10 V, 3.0 V |
| Total gate resistance (driver + R_g + internal) | R_G | 5 Ω |
| Dead time | t_dt | 100 ns per edge |
| Body diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Reverse recovery charge, output charge | Q_rr, Q_oss | 40 nC, 30 nC |
| Commutation | — | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification, complementary PWM |

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In 6-step drive with complementary PWM, the motor current always flows through exactly two conducting FETs:

- One FET on the PWM leg: the high side (duty D) or its synchronous low side (1−D).
- The low side of the return leg, which is on for 100% of the sector.

The conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{hot} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

Averaged over the six FETs of the bridge (each leg is active for 2/3 of an electrical cycle), this is about **3 W per FET**. Peak instantaneous dissipation in a single FET is I²R_hot = 9 W.

Note that battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I. The 80A figure is phase current, so battery draw is lower at partial throttle.

### 1.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched leg, one per sector)

**Gate drive current during the plateau:**

$$I_{G,on} = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_G} = 1.4\ \text{A}, \qquad I_{G,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_G} = 0.6\ \text{A}$$

**Transition times:**

$$t_{on} = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,on}} \approx 8.6\ \text{ns}, \qquad t_{off} \approx 20\ \text{ns}$$

Add parasitic-inductance and ringing allowance to reach about **45 ns total** for t_r + t_f.

**V·I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(45\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction:**

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.02\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs per leg; dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 1.4 Totals

| Loss component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.9 W | 71.6 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 2.18 W | 8.7 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 20.8 W** | **≈ 83 W** |

Two observations follow from these numbers:

- **Conduction loss dominates at about 86%.** Lowering f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.4 W per channel. The real lever is R_DS(on), meaning paralleled FETs or a larger package.
- **Copper is a significant additional loss.** The sense shunt and copper add roughly 0.5–1 mΩ of loop resistance, or another 3–6 W per channel at 80A.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Junction-to-Board (fast transient)

Use a 3.3×3.3 PQFN with R_θJC ≈ 1.5 K/W and Z_θ(10 ms) ≈ 0.4 K/W.

Peak FET dissipation is about 9 W conduction plus about 2.5 W switching on the PWM FET, giving about 11.5 W:

$$\Delta T_{j-c}(\text{steady}) = 11.5 \times 1.5 \approx 17\ \text{K}$$

This is acceptable *if* the board copper underneath stays cool.

### 2.2 Board Heat Capacity (the binding constraint)

Assume a 20×20 mm board, 6 layers, 2 oz copper.

| Element | Mass | Heat capacity |
|---|---|---|
| Copper: 4 cm² × 0.42 mm total thickness | 1.5 g | ≈ 0.58 J/K |
| FR-4: 4 cm² × 1.6 mm | 1.2 g | ≈ 1.3 J/K (weakly coupled) |
| FETs, solder, capacitors | — | ≈ 0.3 J/K |
| **Effective total** | — | **C_th ≈ 2 J/K** |

With convection under prop wash (h ≈ 80 W/m²K, two faces of 8 cm²), the thermal resistance to ambient is R_θ,amb ≈ 1/(80 × 8×10⁻⁴) ≈ 15.6 K/W.

**Board rate of rise at 83 W:**

$$\frac{dT}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} = \frac{83}{2} \approx \mathbf{41\ K/s}$$

**Thermal time constant:**

$$\tau = R C \approx 31\ \text{s}$$
