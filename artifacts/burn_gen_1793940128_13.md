## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM'd (high-side HS / low-side LS complementary), one phase is held low (LS on 100%), and one phase floats.

- PWM-leg HS conducts for fraction D.
- PWM-leg LS (synchronous freewheel) conducts for fraction 1 − D, minus dead time.
- Return-leg LS conducts for the full period.

Instantaneous conduction loss per motor:

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D) + 1\big] = 2\, I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is the main benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{hot}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.007\ /\text{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_hot = 1.0 × 1.70 = 1.7 mΩ. This is the positive-feedback term in the thermal model (Section 4.4).

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.7\times10^{-3} = 21.76\ \text{W}
$$

Per FET, averaged over the electrical cycle: each switch carries current for 120° electrical, which is 1/3 of the cycle.

- Instantaneous peak per conducting FET: 80² × 1.7 mΩ = **10.9 W**. Use this for the junction transient (Section 4.5).
- Cycle-averaged per FET: 21.76 W ÷ 6 ≈ **3.6 W**.

### 1.4 PCB copper loss

Phase current path: input plane → HS → phase node → motor pad, plus return. Assume R_Cu,path ≈ 0.3 mΩ including vias and pads **[VERIFY by IR-drop sim]**.

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.92\ \text{W}
$$
