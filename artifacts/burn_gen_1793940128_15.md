**Feasibility warning.** On a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), **80 A per motor is a burst rating of about 1–2 seconds, not a continuous rating.** The thermal section quantifies this. Any marketing claim beyond that duration is not physically supportable without a heatsink.

**Architecture decision.** The H743 runs ArduPilot only. Each motor gets a dedicated ESC MCU (4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class) that handles commutation at a 48 kHz PWM rate. The H743 talks to these MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. Running 4× sensorless commutation on the H743 is not supported by ArduPilot and would destroy loop determinism.

## 1. Conduction Losses (80 A Burst)

**Topology.** In 6-step trapezoidal drive, two phases conduct at any instant. Current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

- During PWM-on: high-side (HS) of phase A and low-side (LS) of phase B.
- During PWM-off with synchronous rectification: LS of phase A (instead of its body diode) and LS of phase B.

So the series resistance is effectively constant across the PWM cycle, independent of duty.

$$R_{ds,hot}=k_T R_{ds25}=1.5\times1.3=1.95\text{ m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET}=2\,I^2R_{ds,hot}=2(80)^2(1.95\times10^{-3})=24.96\text{ W}$$

$$P_{cond,Cu}=I^2R_{cu}=6400\times0.5\times10^{-3}=3.2\text{ W}$$

$$\boxed{P_{cond}\approx28.2\text{ W per channel}}$$

**Per-device split.** Each of the six FETs in a leg carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. In each 60° sector, one FET holds about 12.5 W on average. The instantaneous peak is about 12.5 W on each of the two conducting FETs. That peak is what matters for T_j.

**Iterate if you need precision.** R_DS(on) depends on T_j, so solve these two equations self-consistently:

- R(T_j) = R_ds25 · [1 + α(T_j − 25)], with α ≈ 0.005 /K
- T_j = T_board + P_FET · R_θ(j-board)

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches, with one turn-on and one turn-off per period.

### 2.1 Gate-drive-limited transition times

Turn-on current:
$$I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{src}+R_g}=\frac{10-3}{4+2}=1.17\text{ A}$$

Turn-off current:
$$I_{g,off}=\frac{V_{pl}}{R_{snk}+R_g}=\frac{3}{2+2}=0.75\text{ A}$$

Transition times:
$$t_r=\frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}}=\frac{8\text{ nC}}{1.17}=6.9\text{ ns}$$

$$t_f=\frac{8\text{ nC}}{0.75}=10.7\text{ ns}$$

**Practical derating.** Common-source inductance in a PQFN layout typically adds 5–10 ns per edge. Use t_r + t_f ≈ 30 ns as the design value.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value |

### 2.3 Per-channel and board total

$$P_{ch}=28.2+2.2\approx\boxed{30.4\text{ W}}$$

$$P_{board}(4\times80\text{ A})\approx\boxed{122\text{ W}}$$

**Design insight.** Conduction losses are about 93% of the total. At 48 kHz, switching losses are minor. The levers that matter are:

- **Lower R_DS(on).** Parallel FETs or use 0.7 mΩ parts.
- **Thicker copper.** R_cu is worth as much as one FET.
- **Not f_sw.** Raising to 96 kHz adds only about 2 W per channel.
