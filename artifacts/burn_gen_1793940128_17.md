# STM32H743 Nano AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #7)
## Power-Stage Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and ArduPilot Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

---

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

Four constraints shape every number below.

1. **Who switches the FETs.** The H743 does not drive the 24 MOSFETs directly. ArduPilot sends DShot to four ESC MCUs, which run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and commutation are generated by those ESC MCUs. This architecture is assumed throughout.
2. **"80 A burst" is per motor channel.** Four channels at 80 A means about 320 A from the battery. No nano-format input connector or pigtail sustains that; XT60 is about 60 A continuous. Realistic aggregate burst is about 120–160 A. The thermal model is parameterised so you can evaluate either case.
3. **Board size makes 80 A a transient rating, not a continuous one.** On a 20×20 or 25×25 mm board, Section 3 shows 80 A is survivable for roughly 1–3 seconds.
4. **Part values are representative.** They describe typical 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm FETs and an FD6288-class 3-phase gate driver. Re-run the model with values from the datasheets you actually procure.

## 2. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current I flows through two legs:

- **Phase A, PWM leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1−D).
- **Phase B, return leg:** the low-side is on 100% of the 60° sector.

This means there are always exactly two R_ds(on) in series with the motor current, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Ripple correction: I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. With typical motor inductance (10–30 µH) at 48 kHz, ripple is ±3–6 A. That changes I_rms² by less than 1%, so the term is neglected.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss at 80 A

At an assumed T_j of 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}=1.2\,\text{mΩ}\times[1+0.0055\times85]=1.76\,\text{mΩ}$$

$$P_{cond}=2\times 80^2\times 1.76\times10^{-3}= \mathbf{22.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

The hottest single FET is the 100%-on return low-side: 6400 × 1.76 mΩ = **11.3 W**.

### 2.3 Thermal Self-Consistency and Runaway Criterion

Junction temperature depends on R_ds, which depends on junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_\theta P_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving for T_j:

$$T_j=\frac{T_{ref}+R_\theta P_0(1-25\alpha)}{1-\alpha R_\theta P_0}$$

The loop is stable only if **αR_θP₀ < 1**.

For the worst FET (P₀ = 6400 × 1.2 mΩ = 7.7 W at 25 °C) with only R_θJC:

- αR_θP₀ = 0.0055 × 1.5 × 7.7 = 0.063, which is stable.
- Junction rise above case is about 17 K.

The real limit is the case/board temperature, covered in Section 3.

### 2.4 Switching Loss at 48 kHz (One PWM Leg)

Only one leg per channel hard-switches.

| Mechanism | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Turn-on + turn-off V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·20n·48k | 0.968 W |
| Coss charge/discharge | Q_oss·V·f = 30n·25.2·48k | 0.036 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 20n·25.2·48k | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_SD·I·2t_dt·f = 0.8·80·100n·48k | 0.307 W |
| Gate drive, 2 FETs, mostly dissipated in the driver | Q_g·V_gs·f·2 = 40n·10·48k·2 | 0.038 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈1.37 W per channel** |

Switching is about 6% of conduction loss at 80 A. That means:

- Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.7 W per channel.
- The benefit is lower current ripple and acoustic noise.
- Burst thermal design is dominated by I²R. Prioritise lower R_ds, paralleled FETs and copper area over faster edges.

### 2.5 Other Burst Losses

**Shunt** (0.5 mΩ, if per-channel sensing is used):
6400 × 0.5 m = 3.2 W per channel. This is significant. Consider one shared 0.25 mΩ shunt on the DC bus.

**Copper** (phase plus bus path ≈ 0.6 mΩ effective with 2 oz copper):
≈3.8 W per channel.

**Input capacitor ripple.** The approximate formula is:

$$I_{C,rms}\approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}$$

At D = 0.5 this gives 40 A rms per channel. The capacitors cannot carry that alone. The bank needs low-ESR MLCCs on each half-bridge plus an external low-ESR electrolytic (≥1000 µF, 35 V) on the battery leads.

### 2.6 Per-Channel and Board Totals at 80 A

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.5 W | 90.0 W |
| Switching | 1.4 W | 5.5 W |
| Shunt + copper | ~7.0 W | ~28 W |
| **Total** | **~31 W** | **~124 W** |

Electrical efficiency at 25.2 V × 80 A = 2016 W per channel is about 98.5%. The problem is not efficiency but where 124 W goes on a 20×20 mm board.
