# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #49): Loss, Thermal and Bi-Directional DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Correction and Assumptions

**ArduPilot does not commutate motors.** No ArduPilot build drives the bridge FETs directly. The only workable architecture has two parts:

- **FC board:** an STM32H743 running ArduPilot. It outputs bidirectional DShot600.
- **ESC board:** four independent ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Examples are the STM32G071 and AT32F421. Each MCU drives its own three-phase gate driver and six FETs.

**Board size.** An 80A-per-channel bridge does not fit a "nano" (20×20 mm) board. The power stage below is a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC. It is stacked with a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm H743 FC, using an adapter if needed. A 20×20 4-in-1 at 80A burst would exceed junction limits within well under one second (see §3).

**The numbers below come from the stated parameters, not from a specific datasheet.** Before release, substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values. All equations are parametric for that purpose.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R_ds = 1.5 × 1.3375 ≈ **2.0 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current paths in complementary 6-step PWM

During each 60° sector, one phase is PWM-switched and one phase is held low.

- **On-time (D):** the PWM-phase high-side FET (HS) and the held phase low-side FET (LS) conduct.
- **Off-time (1−D):** the PWM-phase LS freewheels, and the held phase LS still conducts.

So exactly two FETs carry I_ph at all times, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This is **independent of duty cycle**.

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Average over one electrical revolution. Each phase is PWM-switched for 1/3 of the time and held low for 1/3.

$$P_{HS} = I^2R\cdot\frac{D}{3},\qquad P_{LS} = I^2R\cdot\frac{1+(1-D)}{3} = I^2R\cdot\frac{2-D}{3}$$

Check: 3·P_HS + 3·P_LS = I²R·(D + 2 − D) = 2I²R ✓.

| Case | I²R (80 A, 2.0 mΩ) | P_HS | P_LS (hottest) | P_cond per channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D = 1.0 (full throttle) | 12.8 W | 4.27 W | 4.27 W | 25.6 W |
| D = 0.5 | 12.8 W | 2.13 W | **6.40 W** | 25.6 W |

**Design point: the low-side FET is the thermal limiter at partial throttle under high load.** A typical example is a punch-out from hover.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg per channel switches at a time. Each term below is per channel.

**Hard-switching V·I overlap (on the HS FET):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (on the LS FET, two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph}\,(2t_{dead})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term is larger than the overlap loss. Reducing dead time to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. AM32 exposes this setting. Verify shoot-through margin on a scope first.

**Reverse recovery (on the HS FET):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ W$$

**Output capacitance (both FETs in the leg):**

$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f = 1\text{n}(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.030\ W$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET):**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ W$$

**Switching total:** P_sw,ch ≈ **2.3 W**.

### 2.1 Channel and board totals at 80 A burst, T_j = 100 °C

| Loss term | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 25.6 W | 102.4 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, I_bus ≈ D·I) | ≤ 1.6 W | ≤ 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 29.5 W** | **≈ 118 W** |

Efficiency at full throttle is 1 − 118 / (4 × 25.2 × 80) ≈ **98.5%**. The efficiency is fine; the heat density is the problem.

**Sensitivity to switching frequency.** Switching loss scales linearly with frequency, so 24 kHz saves about 1.15 W per channel. Conduction loss dominates either way, so 48 kHz is acceptable. Keep it for lower ripple and quieter motors.
