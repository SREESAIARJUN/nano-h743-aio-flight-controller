## 0. Architectural Corrections (Read First)

Three findings change the scope of this job before any math is done.

1. **The H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** Bi-directional DShot is a protocol *between* a flight controller and an ESC that runs its own commutation firmware. The H743 runs ArduPilot. Each ESC channel needs its own MCU, either 4× AT32F421 or 4× STM32G071 running AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class firmware). The netlist in §5 reflects this.

2. **"80 A burst" must be defined per channel and time-bounded.** At full throttle, 4 × 80 A gives roughly 320 A of battery current. No 20×20 mm "nano" board can survive this beyond a fraction of a second.
   - This document models **80 A per channel**.
   - It recommends a **30.5×30.5 mm ESC board** stacked under the FC.
   - §3 shows that burst duration is limited by board heat capacity, not by MOSFET ratings.

3. **"48 kHz" is the ESC MOSFET PWM frequency.** It is unrelated to DShot600 timing. The two are modeled independently.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths per commutation sector

In each 60° sector, phase X is PWM'd high and phase Y is held low.

- **On-time (D):** current flows through HS_X → motor → LS_Y.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through LS_X (synchronous rectification) → motor → LS_Y.
