# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Design Model

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

Several inputs were not specified, so the assumptions below are stated explicitly. Every result is given in parametric form so you can substitute datasheet values.

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the motors. Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. The H743 talks to the four ESC MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. The 48 kHz figure is the ESC's PWM frequency, not the DShot rate.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I | 80 A |
| ESC PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |
| MOSFET R_DS(on) at 25 °C | R₂₅ | 1.2 mΩ (40 V class) |
| R_DS(on) temperature coefficient | α | 0.0045 /K |
| Rise / fall times (~1 A gate drive) | t_r / t_f | 15 ns / 15 ns |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 30 nC |
| Output capacitance (energy-related) | C_oss(er) | 1.5 nF |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Dead time | t_d | 100 ns |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 50 nC |
| Gate drive voltage | V_GS | 10 V |
| Commutation scheme | | Six-step trapezoidal, complementary PWM on the active leg |

**Feasibility warning.** "Nano" (20×20 mm) combined with 80 A per motor is not a continuous rating in any physically realizable sense. The analysis below shows 80 A is a **multi-second burst** rating limited by board heat capacity. Treat the continuous rating as a separate, much lower number (Section 2.4).

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Per-motor conduction

In six-step drive, exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant: the PWM leg and the fixed low-side leg.

- On the PWM leg, current flows through the high side for a fraction D of each PWM period. With complementary switching, the synchronous low side carries it for the remaining (1 − D).
- So the PWM leg always contributes one R_DS(on) in the path (ignoring dead time).
- The return path is one more device.

The instantaneous conduction path is therefore always two R_DS(on) in series, independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

**Per-device RMS current over one electrical cycle** (each switch is in the 120° active window for one third of the cycle):

- High side: $I_{HS,rms} = I\sqrt{D/3}$
- Low side: $I_{LS,rms} = I\sqrt{(1-D)/3 + 1/3}$

The low side gets the extra term because it serves both as the fixed return and as the synchronous rectifier.

### 1.2 Numbers

| Condition | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 2·6400·1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 61.4 W |
| T_j = 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | **22.3 W** | 89.1 W |

### 1.3 Parasitic copper (do not ignore)

For each 2 oz outer layer with N paralleled layers, approximate the phase-path resistance as:

$$R_{Cu} \approx \frac{\rho\,L}{w\,t\,N}$$

Example: 10 mm path, 3 mm wide, 70 µm thick, N = 4 layers gives approximately 0.2 mΩ. That adds about **2·6400·0.2 m ≈ 2.6 W per motor**. Budget about 10 W extra for the board.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. In each event, the high side hard-switches and the low side commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Mechanism | Formula | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·30 n·48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 30 n·25.2·48 k = **0.036 W** |
| C_oss charge (2 devices) | 2·½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.046 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_d·f_sw |
