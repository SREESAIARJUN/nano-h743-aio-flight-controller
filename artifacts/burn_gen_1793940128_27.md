**Architectural partitioning.** A 4-in-1 ESC carries its own MCU per motor (or one shared MCU) running AM32 or BLHeli_32/Bluejay. That MCU generates the 48 kHz bridge PWM. The H743 does not drive the FETs. Its responsibilities are:

- generating 4× bidirectional DShot600 frames,
- capturing the GCR eRPM telemetry replies,
- feeding the eRPM data to the ArduPilot harmonic notch.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Commutation Topology

Under 6-step trapezoidal BLDC control with synchronous (complementary) PWM, the motor current at any instant flows through exactly two conducting channels:

- **Path A (PWM'd phase):** high-side for duty D, then synchronous low-side for (1−D). One FET conducts at all times.
- **Path B (return phase):** low-side held ON for the full 60° sector.

Each motor therefore always has 2·R_DS(on) in series with the winding.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

Evaluated at a design T_j of 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS}(110) = 0.9\,\text{m}\Omega \times [1 + 0.0055 \times 85] = 0.9 \times 1.4675 \approx 1.32\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Per-Motor Conduction Loss

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.32\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 2.64\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.9\ W}
$$

**Distribution among the six FETs:**

- **Per sector:**
  - The return low-side dissipates I²R = 8.45 W.
  - The PWM pair splits the remaining 8.45 W as D : (1−D).
- **Over one electrical revolution:** each FET is active for 120° (1/3) and idle for 240°. The averages are:
  - High-side: (1/3)·D·8.45 W
  - Low-side: (1/3)·[8.45 + (1−D)·8.45]·½ … (the half factor applies to the low-side's share between PWM-complement and return roles)
- **Simplified bound:** P_FET,avg ≈ P_cond,motor / 6 ≈ 2.8 W.
- **Worst-case FET** (low-side at low duty, e.g., D = 0.5): ≈ 4.2 W average.

**Thermal ripple check.** Electrical frequency at 30k eRPM is 500 Hz, giving a 0.67 ms sector. The die/package thermal time constant is about 1–5 ms. Junction ripple is therefore ±(4–8) K, and you should add this on top of the average.

### 1.4 Copper/Interconnect Conduction Loss

These losses are often larger than designers expect. Use:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace} + R_{pad} + R_{shunt}) \cdot k_{path}
$$

[ASSUMED] Inputs:

- 2 oz outer layers and 2×1 oz inner layers in parallel give ≈ 0.3 mΩ per motor current path, solder joints included.
- Shunt: 0.5 mΩ on the DC link (one per ESC, or shared).
- Current-path factor k_path ≈ 2.

Results:

- Motor path: 80² × 0.3m × 2 ≈ 3.8 W/motor.
- Shunt: I_DC,total² × 0.5 mΩ. At I_DC ≈ 4 × D × 80 ≈ 192 A (D = 0.6), this gives ≈ 18 W, which is clearly unacceptable. **Use per-motor shunts or a 0.2 mΩ shunt; current sense becomes amplifier-gain limited.**
