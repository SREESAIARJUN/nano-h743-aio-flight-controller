# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Model

**Scope and caveats**

- **Architecture:** The H743 does not drive the gate drivers directly. In every production 4-in-1 design, a local ESC MCU per channel runs AM32 or BLHeli_32 and does the commutation. The H743 (ArduPilot) sends bidirectional DShot600 commands to those MCUs. This model follows that topology.
- **Parameters:** Numeric values marked **[A]** are assumptions for a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PDFN MOSFET stage. Replace them with your actual BOM datasheet values before sign-off.
- **The 80 A figure:** At 6S, 80 A is about 2 kW per motor and 8 kW total. Section 3 shows a 20×20 mm board can survive this only for a few seconds. Treat "80 A burst" as a time-limited rating that must be specified, for example "80 A / 2 s @ 25 °C, airflow ≥ 5 m/s."

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, exactly two legs conduct at any instant. One is the active high side (or its complementary low side during PWM-off). The other is the static low side of the return phase. The current path therefore always crosses two R_DS,hot in series:

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS,hot}$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.25\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{28.8\ W}$$

Per-FET distribution over one electrical cycle:

- Each FET carries current for 120°, which is 1/3 of the cycle.
- The PWM-active high side carries current for a fraction D of that time. Its synchronous low-side partner carries it for (1 − D).
- The average works out to 28.8 W / 6 ≈ **4.8 W per FET**.
- The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is I²·R = **14.4 W**. This drives the transient junction rise in section 3.3.

Trace and copper loss is in addition to this. A typical 2 oz inner pour is about 0.3 mΩ per phase [A]:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.8\ W$$

### 2.2 Switching loss (hard-switched FET, one per PWM period)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ W$$

### 2.4 Output capacitance (both FETs in the switching leg)

$$P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1\text{nF}\cdot635\cdot48\text{k} = 0.03\ W$$

### 2.5 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I_{ph} \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.6 Gate drive (two switching FETs)

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot40\text{nC}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.04\ W$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FET junction.

### 2.7 Totals

| Loss term | Per channel | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 28.80 W | 115.2 W |
| Copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching (V·I overlap) | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 34.3 W** | **≈ 137 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 × (0.8 effective) ≈ 6.45 kW, so η ≈ 97.9%.

**Key insight:** At 80 A the stage is about 90% conduction-dominated. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per channel. Reducing R_DS(on) or paralleling FETs is the only effective lever.

**General expression for design sweeps:**

$$P_{ch}(I,f) = I^2(2R_{DS}k_T + 2R_{Cu}) + f\left[\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) + Q_{rr}V + C_{oss}V^2 + 2V_{SD} I t_{dt}\right]$$

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state network (per FET)

```
Tj --[Rθjc=1.2 K/W]-- Tc --[Rθcb=2 K/W (solder+vias)]-- Tboard --[Rθba]-- Tamb
```

Board-to-ambient resistance for a 20×20 mm, 6-layer, 2 oz board: Rθba ≈ 8 K/W in still air, ≈ 2.5 K/W at 10 m/s prop-wash [A].

Continuous capability with T_board,max = 100 °C, T_amb = 35 °C, and 10 m/s airflow:

$$P_{max} = \frac{65}{2.5} = 26\ W \Rightarrow I_{cont} = \sqrt{\frac{26/4}{2\cdot2.25\text{m}+2\cdot0.3\text{m}}} \approx \mathbf{36\ A\ per\ channel}$$

This is consistent with real "35 A / 80 A burst" nano ESC ratings.

### 3.2 Burst (lumped capacitance)

The thermal mass of the board, FETs, and connectors is C_th ≈ 3.5 J/K [A]. With airflow, the time constant is τ = Rθba·C_th = 2.5 × 3.5 ≈ 8.75 s.

$$T_b(t) = T_{amb} + P R_{\theta ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

With P = 137 W, the asymptote would be 35 + 342 °C, which is not survivable. Solving for T_b = 100 °C:

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\left(1 - \frac{65}{342}\right) = 8.75 \cdot 0.211 \approx \mathbf{1.8\ s}$$

Starting from a pre-heated board at 70 °C, the allowed burst drops to about 0.8 s.

### 3.3 Junction transient

Junction rise above the board while a FET conducts at 14.4 W:

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 14.4 \cdot (1.2 + 2) \approx 46\ K$$

At the commutation timescale this is a worst-case upper bound, because Zth(t) is lower than Rth.

Worst-case junction temperature: T_j ≈ 100 + 46 = **146 °C**. This is below the 150/175 °C rating, but the margin is thin.

**Firmware requirements:**

- Enable the ESC temperature limiter (AM32 `TEMP_LIMIT` around 110 °C).
- Enable the ArduPilot ESC telemetry temperature failsafe.
