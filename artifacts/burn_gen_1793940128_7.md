# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Form Factor): Engineering Model, Job #46

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Two problems with the requirement as written need to be raised before any math.

**1. The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.**
- In any 4-in-1 stack that runs ArduPilot, the H743 is the flight controller.
- It emits bi-directional DShot600. Each motor channel is commutated by its own ESC MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32 (or a BLHeli_S EFM8).
- The 48 kHz PWM and the switching losses therefore belong to the ESC MCUs. The DMA/timer model belongs to the H743.
- I model both, but they live on different silicon.

**2. 80 A burst on a nano (20×20 mm) board is thermally implausible beyond about 1 second.**
- Section 3 shows this quantitatively.
- Commercial "nano 4-in-1" parts quote burst figures (often 40–60 A, around 10 s) that rely on optimistic test conditions.
- Treat my result as a design-review finding, not a footnote.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.8 × 1.5 = **2.7 mΩ**.

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

$$\Delta I=\frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}=\frac{25.2\times0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times48\,\text{kHz}}=6.56\ \text{A (worst case, D = 0.5)}$$

$$I_{rms}^2=I_{pk}^2+\frac{\Delta I^2}{12}=6400+3.6\approx 6404\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple is negligible at 48 kHz, which is a benefit of the high switching frequency.

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

With synchronous rectification, exactly two FETs are in the current path at all times: the high side of one phase and the low side of another.

$$P_{cond,ch}=2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)=2\times6404\times2.7\,\text{m}\Omega=\mathbf{34.6\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during 120° of the 360° electrical cycle (1/3 duty).

- **Instantaneous loss while conducting:** 17.3 W.
- **Electrical-period average:** 34.6 / 6 × 2… which works out to **≈ 5.8 W per FET**.

The electrical frequency (~1–3 kHz) is much faster than the die thermal time constant (~ms), so averaging is valid at the die level. A ~1–2 K ripple is superimposed.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, only the PWM'd FET hard-switches. The synchronous FET commutates through its body diode during dead time.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap (hard switch) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = ½·25.2·80·35 ns·48 kHz | **1.69 W** |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz | **0.31 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 30 nC·25.2·48 kHz | **0.036 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.009 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw per FET | 0.014 W ×2 |
| **Total switching, per channel** | | **≈ 2.05 W** |

**Frequency trade-off:** switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz it would be about 1.0 W per channel.
- Ripple would rise to about 13 A, adding only about 14 A² to I²_rms.
- At 80 A, conduction dominates by about 17:1. Lowering PWM frequency barely helps; R_ds(on) is the lever.
