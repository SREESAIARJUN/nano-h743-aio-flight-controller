# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #23): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Scope and Architecture Corrections

Three problems in the brief would undermine the design if left unaddressed.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. A 4-in-1 ESC needs four dedicated ESC MCUs running AM32 or BLHeli_32, with one MCU per motor. Suitable parts are the AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6. The H743 talks to them over bidirectional DShot600. This document models the board as an H743 FC plus four AT32F421 ESC cores on a single PCB, which is the normal AIO topology.
2. **"48 kHz" is the ESC MCU's PWM frequency, not the H743's.** In AM32 it is a configurable parameter, and it sets switching loss. DShot600 is a separate 600 kbit/s command link.
3. **80 A burst on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 "nano" board is not thermally credible as a continuous figure.** The model in §3 shows it only works as a time-limited burst of about 1–3 s. The datasheet rating must say so explicitly.

All component values marked **[A]** are design assumptions. Replace them with the selected parts' datasheet values.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 does by default. Current therefore flows through exactly two devices at all times:

- the PWM'd high side (or its synchronous low-side partner during the off-time) on one phase, and
- the static low side on the return phase.

With PWM ripple included, the per-FET RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For typical 5-inch motors (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.5):

- ΔI ≈ 25.2·0.25 / (15e-6·48e3) ≈ 8.75 A
- The ripple term is 6.4 A², which is under 0.1 % of 6400 A². It is negligible at 80 A.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

The temperature-dependent on-resistance is:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

Per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond at 80 A | P_cond at 35 A |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ | 15.4 W | 2.9 W |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ | 20.6 W | 3.9 W |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ | 22.3 W | 4.3 W |

**Thermal runaway check.** dP/dT = 2I²R₂₅α ≈ 0.069 W/K at 80 A. The loop is stable only if R_θ(j-amb, effective) < 1/0.069 ≈ 14.5 K/W. This holds at the board level (§3.2), but the margin shrinks quickly if airflow is lost.

### 2.3 Switching Loss (hard-switched edge, at f_sw = 48 kHz)

**V–I overlap.** Only one FET per commutation state hard-switches per PWM cycle.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5·25.2·80·20\text{e-}9·48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction.**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8·80·200\text{e-}9·48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery.**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 25\text{e-}9·25.2·48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.030\ W}$$

**Output capacitance.**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** Two FETs switch per cycle. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2·40\text{e-}9·12·48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.046\ W}$$

### 2.4 Totals

| Loss term | 80 A burst (T_j = 125 °C) | 35 A continuous (T_j = 100 °C) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.3 W | 3.9 W |
| Switching (V·I) | 0.97 W | 0.42 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W | 0.27 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 W | 0.11 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ) | 3.2 W | 0.6 W |
| **Per motor** | **≈ 27.2 W** | **≈ 5.3 W** |
| **Board (4 motors)** | **≈ 109 W** | **≈ 21 W** |

Two design conclusions follow:

- **Conduction dominates at burst.** Raising f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 0.8 W per motor at 80 A, so 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for smoothness and acoustic performance.
- **The shunt is the second-largest loss.** Use 0.25–0.5 mΩ, or switch to a Hall sensor, if burst rating matters.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network

The heat path is modeled as a Cauer ladder:

```
P_FET ─┬─ R_jc(0.9 K/W) ─┬─ R_pad→PCB(2.5 K/W per FET, via array) ─┬─ R_PCB→air
       │                 │                                          │
      C_j(0.02 J/K)     C_case(0.15 J/K)                     C_board(≈5 J/K)
```

**Board heat capacity.** The board is roughly 4 g FR-4 plus 2 g copper (6-layer, 2 oz inner) plus 24 FETs.

- C_board ≈ 4·1.1 + 2·0.385 + 1.2 ≈ **6.4 J/K**

**Board-to-air resistance.**

| Condition | R_θ(PCB-air) |
|---|---|
| Still air, 26×26 mm, both sides | ≈ 18 K/W |
| Prop-wash, ~8 m/s | ≈ 4.5 K/W (h ≈ 80 W/m²K) |

### 3.2 Steady State, 35 A Continuous (prop-wash)

- P_total = 21 W gives ΔT_board = 21 × 4.5 ≈ 95 K.
- The ambient-to-board result is T_board ≈ 30 + 95 = **125 °C**.

**This is too hot.** The realistic continuous rating for this footprint is **about 30 A**, which gives P ≈ 15 W, T_board ≈ 98 °C, and T_j ≈ 98 + 1.3·(15/24)·(0.9 + 2.5) ≈ 101 °C. Alternatively, state 35 A with a mandatory airflow condition.

### 3.3 Burst Transient at 80 A (all four motors, from a 60 °C board)

The board node dominates the transient response:

$$\tau_{board} = R_{\theta}C = 4.5·6.4 ≈ 29\text{ s}$$

$$\frac{dT}{dt}\Big|_{t=0} ≈ \frac{P - (T-T_a)/R}{C} = \frac{109 - 30/4.5}{6.4} ≈ 16\text{ K/s}$$

The junction-to-board offset settles within about 50 ms
