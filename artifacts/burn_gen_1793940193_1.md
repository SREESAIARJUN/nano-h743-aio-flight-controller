# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #61): Electrical, Thermal & Firmware Architecture

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules and Assumptions

The H743 should **not** commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot only. Each motor channel uses a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 talks to these MCUs over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only architecture that ArduPilot supports for bidir DShot with eRPM telemetry, and it keeps the 48 kHz commutation loops off the flight controller.

Every result below depends on these assumptions. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values and re-run the equations.

| Parameter | Symbol | Assumed value |

| Battery | V_bus | 6S, 25.2 V max (worst case for switching) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Synchronous 6-step operation.** In any 60° sector, two legs are active:
- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high side conducts for duty D. The low side conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier. The current flows through exactly one R_ds(on) at all times, so the loss is I²R_hot, independent of D.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low side is held on for the whole sector, giving I²R_hot.

Per motor:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.28\ W}$$

**Copper and shunt additions.**
- A 0.25 mΩ shunt carrying the return current adds 80² × 0.25 mΩ = 1.6 W per motor.
- Phase pads and pours add an estimated 0.3 mΩ path, giving about 1.9 W per motor.
- Shunt and copper losses go into the board, not the junctions.

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts during 2 of the 6 sectors (about 1/3 of the electrical cycle). The electrical period at operating eRPM (more than 1 kHz electrical) is much shorter than the junction thermal time constant (ms range), so average power is valid:

$$P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} = 2.88\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak while a FET is conducting is 8.64 W. Use this peak value when you check Z_th at low eRPM or during startup.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. In each period, the high side hard-switches on and off. The low side switches with zero voltage (ZVS) after the dead time, but the body diode recovers.

**(a) Hard-switching V·I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ ns})(48\text{ kHz}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**(c) C_oss charging:**
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (2 edges per period):**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I (2t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{ ns})(48\text{k}) = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 switching FETs per sector; this loss goes mostly into the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2(40\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

**Switching subtotal:** about **2.66 W per motor**.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Terms (a) through (e) scale linearly with f_sw:
- 24 kHz: about 1.33 W
- 96 kHz: about 5.3 W

At 80 A, conduction dominates, at roughly 6.5 times the switching loss. Going to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per motor in exchange for smoother current and quieter motors. This is a reasonable trade.

### Total power budget at 80 A burst

| | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.28 W | 69.1 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.66 W | 10.6 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **23.4 W** | **≈ 93.7 W** |

ESC efficiency at this point: P_out ≈ 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8 kW, so efficiency is about 98.8%. The efficiency looks excellent, but **94 W dissipated into a 20×20 mm board is the binding constraint.**
