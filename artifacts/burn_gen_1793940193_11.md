# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

Some of these assumptions change the results materially, so please confirm or correct them.

| Item | Assumption |
|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S LiPo. 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. All switching math uses 25.2 V (worst case). |
| "80 A burst" | 80 A per channel, motor-phase current, near-full duty (D ≈ 1), lasting ≤ 10 s. |
| MOSFETs | 6 per channel, 24 total. Generic 40 V DFN3.3×3.3: R_DS(on) = 1.6 mΩ at 25 °C and V_GS = 10 V. Q_g = 40 nC, Q_gd = 8 nC, Q_rr ≈ 30 nC, Q_oss ≈ 40 nC, R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W. |
| Gate driver | 3× half-bridge driver per channel (FD6288Q class). Approximately 1.5 A source and 1.8 A sink. 100 ns dead time. |
| Commutation | 6-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM at 48 kHz, as in AM32 and BLHeli_32. |
| Board | Stacked "nano" form factor, 30×30 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper. |

**Architectural correction.** The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller in an ArduPilot system:

- ArduPilot does not perform motor commutation.
- Bidirectional DShot only makes sense if a separate ESC MCU receives the commands.

The model below therefore uses this topology:

> **H743 (ArduPilot FC) → 4× bidirectional DShot600 → 4× ESC MCUs (AT32F421 / STM32G071 running AM32) → 4× three-phase bridges at 48 kHz**

If you intended the H743 to drive the gates directly, that is a custom-firmware project, and Section 3 does not apply.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.60 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.14 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.32 mΩ (design value) |

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step with synchronous PWM

At any instant, two phases are active. Current always flows through two series FETs:

- High-side (HS) of phase A plus low-side (LS) of phase B during the on-time.
- LS of phase A plus LS of phase B during freewheel, because the synchronous rectifier is on.

As a result, per-channel conduction loss is essentially independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j)\;\;(+\ \text{ripple term } \tfrac{\Delta I^2}{12}\cdot 2R_{DS},\ \text{negligible for } L_{ph}\gtrsim 10\,\mu H)$$

| Condition | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| 80 A, T_j = 25 °C | 6400 × 3.20 mΩ | 20.5 W per channel |
| 80 A, T_j = 125 °C | 6400 × 4.64 mΩ | **29.7 W per channel** |

**Per-FET distribution.** Each FET conducts for 120° of each electrical cycle, i.e., one third of the time.

- Instantaneous loss while conducting: 80² × 2.32 mΩ = 14.85 W.
- Average loss per FET: 29.7 / 6 = **4.95 W**.

The electrical frequency is high: f_e = RPM · p/120. A
