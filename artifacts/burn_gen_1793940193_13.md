# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano Flight Controller: System Architecture Package (Job #54)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Design Flags

I don't have the Job #54 BOM, so this package uses a representative reference design. Each parameter below is marked as an assumption. Replace them with datasheet values from your chosen parts and the equations still hold.

**Architecture assumed:**
- **Flight controller:** STM32H743 running ArduPilot.
- **ESC side:** 4 × independent ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) running AM32 or BLHeli_32-style 6-step trapezoidal firmware.
- **Signalling:** each ESC MCU receives bi-directional DShot600 from the H743.
- **Power stage:** 24 MOSFETs (6 per motor) driven by 3-phase gate drivers.

**Design flags to resolve before layout:**

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 4 × 80 A simultaneous draws 320 A from the pack. XT60 or XT30 connectors and 20×20 mm input copper cannot support this. | Treat 80 A as a **per-channel, single-motor, sub-second transient**. Specify the pack-level limit separately (realistically about 120–160 A burst). |
| F2 | The math below shows about 25 W per channel at 80 A. A 20×20 board has a whole-board thermal mass near 2 J/K. | The burst duration limit is roughly **1 s**, not "burst rating" in the marketing sense. Section 3 gives the numbers. |
| F3 | A 20×20 board with an H743 (LQFP100 is 14×14 mm), 4 ESC MCUs, and 24 FETs is extremely dense. | Consider a 2-board stack (FC on top, ESC below). The netlists are partitioned so either option works. |

**Reference MOSFET parameters (assumption; 40 V N-ch, PDFN 3.3×3.3 class):**

| Param | Value | Param | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| R_DS(on) @ 25 °C, Vgs = 10 V | 1.2 mΩ | Q_g total | 40 nC |

**Operating point:**
- V_bus = 25.2 V (6S full charge; this is the worst case for switching loss).
- f_sw = 48 kHz.
- Gate drive V_drv = 10 V.
- External R_g = 2 Ω; driver output impedance about 2 Ω.
- Dead time t_d = 100 ns.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switched complementarily (HS/LS alternate). A second phase has its LS held statically ON, and the third phase floats for BEMF sensing. With synchronous rectification, motor current therefore always flows through exactly two conducting MOSFETs. Dead time is the exception, when the body diode conducts instead (Section 2.4).

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$
