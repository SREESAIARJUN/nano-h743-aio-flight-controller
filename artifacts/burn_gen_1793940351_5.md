# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion Design

I don't have the specification for "Job #25", so this design uses representative gimbal parameters. Everything is parameterized, so you can substitute your motor and frame values. The assumptions, which you should verify, are:

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Motor | Gimbal BLDC/PMSM, 12N14P (pole pairs p = 7), R ≈ 5–15 Ω, L ≈ 2–5 mH |
| Position sensor | 14-bit magnetic encoder (e.g., AS5048A / MT6701) on each axis |
| Driver | 3-phase half bridge, 24 V, center-aligned PWM at 20 kHz |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, mounted point-symmetric about the payload center of mass |
| MCU | Cortex-M4F/M7 with single-precision FPU (STM32G4/H7 class) |

---

## 1. Timing Architecture

| Loop | Rate | Ts | Bandwidth target | Feedback source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q) PI | 20 kHz | 50 µs | 1.5–2 kHz | Phase shunts |
| IMU sample, filtering, fusion | 8 kHz | 125 µs | — | 2 × ICM-42688-P (FIFO / DRDY) |
| Velocity PI + cogging feedforward | 4 kHz | 250 µs | 150–250 Hz | Fused gyro rate projected onto the axis |
| Angle P/PI + rate feedforward | 1 kHz | 1 ms | 30–50 Hz | Fused attitude (quaternion) |

Each loop runs at about 4–10× the bandwidth of the loop outside it. This lets each inner loop be treated as approximately unity gain when tuning the next loop out.

## 2. Field-Oriented Control (Innermost Loop)

### 2.1 PMSM model in the rotor (dq) frame

The electrical angle is θₑ = p·θₘ − θ₀, and the electrical speed is ωₑ = p·ωₘ.

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \frac{di_d}{dt} - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L_q \frac{di_q}{dt} + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac{3}{2}p\left[\lambda_m i_q + (L_d-L_q)i_d i_q\right] \;\overset{L_d\approx L_q}{=}\; K_t\, i_q,\quad K_t=\tfrac32 p\lambda_m
$$

The mechanical dynamics of each axis are:

$$
J\dot\omega_m = K_t i_q - B\omega_m - \tau_{cog}(\theta_m) - \tau_{dist}
$$

### 2.2 Coordinate transforms

**Clarke transform** (amplitude invariant, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

**Park transform:**

$$
\begin{bmatrix}i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

**Inverse Park with delay compensation.** The PWM duty is applied one period later and averaged over the next period, so the effective delay is 1.5·Ts. The angle used in the inverse transform is therefore advanced:

$$
\theta_e^{*} = \theta_e + 1.5\,\omega_e T_s
$$

### 2.3 Current PI with pole-zero cancellation and decoupling

The plant from voltage to current is 1/(Ls + R). Choose the PI zero to cancel the electrical pole at Ki/Kp = R/L. The closed loop then becomes first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_{p,i} = \omega_c L,\qquad K_{i,i} = \omega_c R
$$

$$
v_d^* = \text{PI}_d(i_d^*-i_d) - \omega_e L_q i_q,\qquad
v_q^* = \text{PI}_q(i_q^*-i_q) + \omega_e (L_d i_d + \lambda_m)
$$

Set i_d* = 0 (no field weakening is needed on a gimbal).

**Voltage limiting.** The voltage vector is limited to the circle inscribed in the SVPWM hexagon, |v| ≤ V_dc/√3. When limiting is active, d-axis voltage has priority:

$$
v_{q,\max}=\sqrt{(V_{dc}/\sqrt3)^2 - v_d^{*2}}
$$
