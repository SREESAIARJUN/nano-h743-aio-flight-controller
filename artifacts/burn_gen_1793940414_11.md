## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous PWM:
- At any instant, one phase is driven high and one is driven low.
- During the PWM **on** time (duty D), current flows through HS(A) → motor → LS(B).
- During the PWM **off** time (1−D), current freewheels through LS(A) → motor → LS(B).

So exactly **two FETs are always in the current path**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left[D\cdot R_{HS} + (1-D)\cdot R_{LS} + R_{LS}\right] \approx 2\,I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

This approximation holds because R_HS ≈ R_LS for identical FETs, so the result is independent of D.

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the T_j = 125 °C design point:

$$R = 0.95\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.5 = 1.43\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.43\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.3\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect loss

Assume the 80 A path (battery pad → bridge → phase pad) is about 0.30 mΩ per channel. This requires 2 oz outer layers, a 4–6 layer stack, and stitched internal power planes.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.30\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{1.9\ W}$$

### 1.5 Self-consistency check

Loss depends on T_j, and T_j depends on loss. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

This converges in 2–3 iterations. Using the 125 °C value directly is conservative.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches at f_sw. The freewheel LS pair switches complementarily, and the low-side "return" FET is static.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off)

Transition times from the gate charge plateau (Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 22 nC):

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{22\,\text{nC}}{\sim0.9\,\text{A (eff., with }R_g)} \approx 25\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f \approx 20\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{in} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 45\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery (LS diode → HS turn-on)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{in} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

This ignores Q_rr growth at 80 A di/dt. Multiply by about 2 if the datasheet Q_rr is specified at low current.

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two dead-time intervals occur per PWM cycle, with t_dt = 200 ns and V_f = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times400\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Dead time is the largest controllable switching term. Tune it down to 100–150 ns once the gate-drive edges are verified on a scope.

### 2.4 Output capacitance (Q_oss ≈ 60 nC)

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{in} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive (2 switching FETs, V_gs = 10 V)

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times100\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.10\ W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET junctions.

### 2.6 Loss summary

| Term | W / channel | W / 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 18.3 | 73.2 |
| Copper | 1.9 | 7.6 |
| V–I overlap | 2.18 | 8.7 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 | 0.5 |
| Gate drive | 0.10 | 0.4 |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.8 W** | **≈ 95 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 22.2 V × 4 (7.1 kW input) is about **98.7 %**. Conduction dominates (≈ 77 %), so **switching frequency is not the main lever**:
- Going from 48 → 24 kHz saves only about 1.7 W per channel.
- **Paralleling 2 FETs per switch** cuts conduction loss to about 9.2 W per channel, roughly halving the board heat.
