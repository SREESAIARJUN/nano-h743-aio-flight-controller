# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #13): Architecture, Loss Model, BDShot DMA Plan, and Netlists

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

Several points need to be settled before the math.

1. **MCU partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller and runs ArduPilot. ArduPilot does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel still needs its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8BB51 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. This document models the H743 as the DShot master and the ESC MCUs as BDShot slaves.
2. **"80A burst" on a nano board.** On a 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm board, 80A per channel is only physically sustainable for about 1 to 3 seconds. The model below quantifies this so the rating can be specified honestly, for example "80A / 2s burst, 35A continuous at 40°C ambient with airflow".
3. **Placeholder component parameters.** All MOSFET and driver parameters are representative values for 30–40V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values. The equations are parametric for this reason.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-step complementary PWM (AM32 default)

In each 60° sector, one phase is the PWM leg, one is the return leg, and one floats.

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The synchronous low side conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts continuously.

Per-motor instantaneous conduction loss:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hs} D + I^2 R_{ls}(1-D) + I^2 R_{ls} = 2\,I^2 R_{eff}
$$

The result is **independent of D** when R_hs = R_ls. Here R_eff = k_T·R_ds(on),25 + R_pcb = 1.8 + 0.3 = **2.1 mΩ**.

At 80 A:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0021 = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Over one electrical revolution, each FET is "active" (PWM-leg or return-leg) for 2/6 of the time.

- **Worst-case high-side FET at D → 1:** P = I²R_eff × (1/3) ≈ 4.5 W average.
- **Low-side FETs:** they carry the return interval plus the freewheel interval, so they run hotter at low D. Their average is ≈ (1/3)(1 + (1−D))/1 × I²R_eff, which is ≤ 9.0 W in the limit. In practice, high current coincides with D > 0.8, giving ≈ 5 W.

**Design rule:** size thermal relief for the low-side FETs.

### 1.3 Temperature feedback (self-consistency)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

Iterate with T_j = T_board + P·Z_th(t) (see §3). The loop converges if

$$
2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,Z_{th} < 1
$$

At 80 A with Z_th = 5 K/W: 2(6400)(0.0012)(0.005)(5) = 0.38. This is stable, but the margin shrinks quickly above 100 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per channel, two edges per period occur on the active PWM leg.

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching, inductive load)**

$$
P_{ov} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,\mathrm{k}) = 0.968\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** (the low-side body diode recovers when the high side turns on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\mathrm{n}(25.2)(48\mathrm{k}) = 0.036\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(c) Output capacitance** (E_oss ≈ ½ Q_oss V)

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.024\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2 t_{dead}\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\mathrm{ns})(48\,\mathrm{k}) = 0.614\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two FETs switching; this heat lands in the driver IC and gate resistors)

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2(40\mathrm{n})(10)(48\mathrm{k}) = 0.038\ \mathrm{W}
$$

**Switching subtotal:** P_sw ≈ **1.68 W per channel**.

### 2.1 Sensitivity notes

- **f_sw:** every term except dead-time conduction scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz, P_sw ≈ 0.84 W. At 96 kHz, it is about 3.4 W.
- **Dead time:** dead-time loss is about 37% of switching loss. Cutting t_dead to 50 ns, which is feasible with a fast driver and tight layout, saves about 0.3 W per channel.
- **Overlap time:** t_r and t_f are set by R_g and the driver source/sink current: t ≈ Q_gd·R_g,total / (V_drv − V_plateau). With Q_gd = 8 nC, R_g = 4.7 Ω, and a plateau of 3 V, t ≈ 5.4 ns plus driver delay. The 10 ns figure is a realistic in-circuit value.
