### ⚠️ Architectural red flags (read before layout)

1. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A burst is physically contradictory.** A 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate ~67 W (4 × 16.7 W, §2.5) for more than about 1 s. 80 A burst is realistic only on a **30.5×30.5 mm board or larger**, with 2 oz outer and inner copper and a bolted heat spreader. If "nano" means 20×20, the realistic rating is about 35 A burst / 20 A continuous per channel.
2. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 AIO, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each motor has its own ESC MCU doing commutation and the 48 kHz PWM. The DMA analysis (§3) concerns the FC→ESC link, not the gate PWM.
3. **Ground bounce from 320 A aggregate burst** (4 × 80 A) into the shared FC ground will corrupt IMU and baro readings and DShot edges unless you use the star/split ground topology in §4.

## 2. Power-Stage Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big]
$$

Evaluated at T_j = 125 °C using the max datasheet value:

$$
R_{ds}(125) = 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045 \times 100) = 1.305\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

Use **1.3 mΩ** as the worst-case hot design value. The typical-hot value (from 0.70 mΩ typ) is 1.02 mΩ.

### 2.2 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In any 60° commutation sector, three switches are active in one channel:

- **Phase A high-side** (Q_AH): PWM-switched, conducts for fraction D.
- **Phase A low-side** (Q_AL): complementary, conducts for fraction (1 − D), minus dead time.
- **Phase B low-side** (Q_BL): held fully ON, conducts 100%.

Instantaneous conduction power in the sector:

$$
P_{cond,sector} = I^2 R_{ds}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This result is **independent of D**, which is the key insight: with synchronous rectification there are always exactly two R_DS(on) drops in the current path.

**At 80 A burst, worst case hot (1.3 mΩ):**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.305\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{16.7\ W}
$$

**At 80 A, typical hot (1.02 mΩ):**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.02\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.1\ W}
$$

**Per-FET averaging.** Each switch is active for 120° of 360° electrical. Averaged over an electrical cycle, the 6 FETs share the load:

$$
\bar{P}_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6}\cdot 2 \;\;(\text{low-side FET, full-on portion}) \;\rightarrow\; \text{peak FET} \approx 80^2 \times 1.3\text{m} \approx 8.4\ \text{W during its window}
$$

**Averaging validity check.** Die thermal time constant τ_die ≈ 1–5 ms. The electrical frequency at hover for a 14-pole motor at 20,000 RPM is:

$$
f_e = \frac{20000}{60} \times 7 = 2.33\ \text{kHz}
$$

Since f_e ≫ 1/τ_die, the junction sees the **average** (≈ 16.7/6 × 2 ≈ 5.6 W for the worst FET, which carries both its full-on and PWM windows). At low eRPM (startup, desync, prop-strike stall), the averaging fails and the FET sees **8.4 W peak** for ms-scale windows. That is the stall-burn failure mode, and the ESC firmware's stall protection must handle it.
