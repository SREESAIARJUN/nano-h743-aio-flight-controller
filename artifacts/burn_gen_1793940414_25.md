# Job #45: STM32H743 Nano FC with Integrated 4-in-1 ESC
## System Model: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA

---

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Architectural Clarification

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and produces DShot600 frames. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the 4 ESC channels has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This document assumes an AT32F421 per channel (one quad-capable MCU is also possible). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The model therefore splits into two domains:

| Domain | Owner | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Power stage (losses, thermal) | ESC MCU + gate driver + 24 MOSFETs | 6S, 48 kHz, 80 A burst/phase |
| Motor command & telemetry | STM32H743 timers + DMA | DShot600, bi-directional (eRPM) |

**Baseline assumptions.** Replace these with datasheet values once the BOM is frozen.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° sector, two legs are active:
- **PWM leg:** HS conducts for D·T_s. LS conducts for (1−D)·T_s, through the channel (synchronous rectification), apart from dead time.
- **Return leg:** LS conducts for 100% of the sector.

The per-switch RMS current is affected by the phase-current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}
\;\Rightarrow\;
\Delta I_{pp,max}\big|_{D=0.5} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H}\times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ \text{A}^2 \quad(\text{ripple contributes } <0.06\%)$$

Ripple is negligible for conduction loss at 48 kHz. That is one benefit of the high switching frequency.

### 1.2 Hot R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds,hot} = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx 1.4\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 2.1\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-channel conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot}\,\big[\underbrace{D}_{HS} + \underbrace{(1-D)}_{LS\ sync} + \underbrace{1}_{return\ LS}\big]
= 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds,hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6404 \times 2.1\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{26.9\ W}$$

The total is independent of D. Only the distribution among the switches changes:

| Switch (at D = 0.5) | Loss |
|---|---|
| HS (PWM leg) | 6.7 W |
| LS (PWM leg) | 6.7 W |
| LS (return leg) | **13.4 W** ← hottest device |

The return-leg low-side carries 100% duty. It is the thermal limiter, not the switching device.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (often ignored, significant at 80 A)

- **Shunt:** 0.25 mΩ per channel gives 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**. A single bus shunt sees I_bus ≈ D·I_ph. At D = 1 that is 80 A. Size it for this.
- **Phase traces:** 2 oz copper is 0.245 mΩ/sq. A 10 mm × 4 mm pour = 2.5 sq ≈ 0.6 mΩ per lay
