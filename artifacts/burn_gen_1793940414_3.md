# STM32H743 AIO (4-in-1 ESC) Nano FC, Job #36: System Architecture Model

## 0. Feasibility Finding

Before the derivations, here is the result they lead to. **An 80 A per-motor burst rating on a nano-format (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) AIO is not thermally defensible.** The model below shows:

- About 110 W of total stage dissipation at 4×80 A.
- Adiabatic board heating of roughly 15–20 K/s.
- A realistic continuous rating of about 18–25 A per motor.

The derivations are written so you can substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values. Every assumed parameter is tagged **[A]**. Replace them with datasheet or measured values before sign-off.

---

## 1. System Partitioning

```
6S LiPo (25.2 V max) ──► VBAT bus ──► 4× 3-phase bridges (24 MOSFETs)
                              │            ▲ gate drive
                              │      4× FD6288Q-class 3-ph drivers
                              │            ▲ PWM @ 48 kHz
                              │      4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32/BLHeli_32)
                              │            ▲ Bidirectional DShot600 (1 wire each)
                              └─► BEC ──► STM32H743 FC (ArduPilot)
```

Two architectural points:

- **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz switching PWM.** The per-ESC MCUs do that.
- **The H743 owns the DShot link.** The DMA analysis in §6 applies to the FC side only.

## 3. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal, Synchronous Rectification)

During any commutation step, two legs carry current:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):** the high-side conducts for D·T. The low-side conducts for (1−D)·T as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Leg B (return leg):** the low-side is held on for the whole step.

The conduction loss per motor is:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2R_{hot} = 2\,I^2R_{hot}
$$

The result is independent of duty cycle, which is a key property of synchronous drive.

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.92\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{24.6\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

The stage also has copper losses. Assume a 2 oz external layer with an effective path of about 0.4 mΩ per motor **[A]**:

$$
P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.4\times10^{-3} = 2.6\ \text{W per motor}
$$

## 4. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 4.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (PWM leg high-side)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 4.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period)

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(400\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This is a large term. Tuning dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Validate shoot-through margin on the scope before making that change.

### 4.3 Reverse recovery

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 30\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$

### 4.4 C_oss discharge

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5(1.5\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = 0.023\ \text{W}
$$

### 4.5 Gate drive (two switching FETs active per period)

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2(50\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

This power is dissipated in the gate resistors and driver, not in the FET die.

### 4.6 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst

| Term | Per motor (W) | ×4 (W) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 24.6 | 98.4 |
| Copper | 2.6 | 10.4 |
| Overlap switching | 1.94 | 7.8 |
| Dead time | 1.23 | 4.9 |
| Q_rr + C_oss + gate | 0.11 | 0.4 |
| **Total** | **30.5** | **≈122 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 80% of the total. **Raising f_sw is cheap; R_DS(on) is what matters.** Each 48 kHz → 24 kHz halving saves only about 1.6 W per motor.

## 5. Thermal Model

### 5.1 Junction to board (per FET)

Commutation rotates the load around the bridge. The average dissipation per FET is about 30.5 / 6 ≈ 5.1 W. The worst case is the return-leg low-side at about 12.3 W during its step.

Assume an effective R_th(j-pcb) of 3 K/W **[A]**. This covers the clip-bonded DFN, solder, and a via array. With the step lasting about 1/6 of the electrical period (≪ τ_die), use the average:

$$
\Delta T_{j-pcb} \approx 5.1 \times 3 = 15\ \text{K}
$$
