# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Power Stage, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture (Job #6)

## 0. Architecture Corrections and Assumptions

### 0.1 Three corrections before any math

1. **The STM32H743 cannot be the ESC controller.** ArduPilot runs on the H743 and outputs DShot. Each motor still needs its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 running AM32, which supports 48 kHz PWM and bidirectional DShot.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A burst conflict.** On a 20×20 mm board, 24 power MOSFETs at 80 A per channel is not physically viable. The minimum credible format is 30.5×30.5 mm with an 8-layer, 2 oz copper stack and 3.3×3.3 mm FETs. Even then, the math below shows 80 A is a **2–4 second burst rating**, not a continuous one.
3. **Component values are representative, not authoritative.** Every FET and driver parameter below is an assumed representative value. Re-run the model with your selected part's datasheet values (R_DS(on) vs. T_j curve, Q_gd, Q_rr, Z_th(j-c)) before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 120° six-step operation, exactly two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification:

- During the PWM ON time, current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the OFF time, current flows through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous rectifier) and the low-side FET of phase B.

Either way, current always passes through **two** channel resistances in series.

### 1.2 Hot on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(125^\circ\text{C}) = 0.90 \times 1.60 = 1.44\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-ESC conduction loss

Include PCB copper and solder parasitics, estimated at R_pcb ≈ 0.25 mΩ per conduction path (phase pad to bus):

$$P_{cond} = I^2\,\big(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\big)$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET channels | 80² × 2 × 1.44 mΩ | 18.43 W |
| PCB copper | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W (lands in board, not junctions) |
| **Total** | | **20.03 W per ESC** |

### 1.4 Distribution among the six FETs

Each FET conducts during one third of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is above 1 kHz at load, which is fast compared with the die thermal time constant (about 5–20 ms), so the die sees roughly the cycle average.

- **Instantaneous** conduction loss while a FET is active: 80² × 1.44 mΩ = **9.22 W**
- **Cycle-averaged** conduction loss per FET: 9.22 / 3 = **3.07 W**

### 1.5 Thermal runaway check

R_DS(on) rises with T_j, which raises loss, which raises T_j. The loop is stable only if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta ja} < 1$$

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} = I^2 \cdot R_{25}\cdot\alpha = 6400 \times 0.9\times10^{-3}\times 0.006 = 0.0346\ \text{W/K}$$

The loop gain stays below 1 only for R_θja < 29 K/W, which this design easily satisfies. **No electrothermal runaway.** The real limit is total energy, not runaway.
