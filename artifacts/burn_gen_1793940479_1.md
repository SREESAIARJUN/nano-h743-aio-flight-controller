# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Timer Allocation, and Netlists

**Job #62, Design Basis Rev A**

---

## 0. Assumptions and Corrections

Several points must be fixed before the math is meaningful.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 as the flight controller (FC). Commutation runs on dedicated ESC MCUs executing AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. Those MCUs are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor. The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 and receives eRPM back. This document models that standard AIO architecture.
2. **80A is a burst rating, not continuous.** On a nano form factor (20×20 or 30.5×30.5 mm), 80A per channel is thermally impossible in steady state. Section 3 shows it is time-limited to seconds.
3. **All component values are assumptions.** They represent typical 40V, approximately 1 mΩ 3.3×3.3 mm power MOSFETs. Replace them with your selected part's datasheet values before release. The netlists are reference topology, not a verified layout.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous complementary PWM, two FETs carry the phase current at any instant: one high-side and one low-side. Over one electrical cycle, each FET conducts for 120° (one third of the period).

### 1.1 Per-FET RMS current

$$I_{rms,FET} = I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}} = 80 \times 0.577 = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET conduction loss (hot)

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on),25}\cdot k_T = 46.2^2 \times 1.0\text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 3.20\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss

Two FETs carry the full current at all times:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds,hot} = 2 \times 6400 \times 1.5\text{m} = 19.2\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Partial-duty generalization

At duty D, the following relations hold:

- Battery current: I_batt = D · I_ph.
- PWM'd high-side loss: D · I²R.
- Synchronous low-side loss: (1−D) · I²R.
- Static low-side loss: I²R.

Total conduction loss is therefore 2·I_ph²·R regardless of D. **Phase current, not battery current, sets conduction loss.**

### 1.5 Shunt and copper

- Shunt loss: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W per motor**.
- Copper, assuming about 0.3 mΩ of path per phase pair: 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per motor**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches. With complementary PWM, one hard-switched edge pair occurs per period, so the hard-switching term is counted once per motor.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value per motor |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f = ½·25.2·80·40n·48k | **1.94 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f = ½·1.5n·635·48k | 0.023 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_d·f = 0.8·80·200n·48k | 0.614 W |
| Gate drive (two switching FETs) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f = 2·60n·10·48k | 0.058 W (dissipated in driver and R_g) |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈ 2.70 W** |

**Sensitivity to frequency.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw: 24 kHz gives about 1.35 W and 96 kHz gives about 5.4 W. At 80A burst, conduction dominates (about 88%), so 48 kHz is a reasonable compromise for current ripple and acoustic noise.

**Sensitivity to temperature.** Diode V_f falls about 2 mV/K, while R_ds rises. The conduction term is already hot-corrected via k_T.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total loss at full four-motor burst

| Component | Per motor | × 4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.8 W |
| Shunt and copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| ESC MCU, drivers, LDO | ≈ 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.7 W** | **≈ 103 W** |

Overall efficiency: P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 80 ≈ 8.06 kW, so the ESC stage is about 98.7% efficient. However, 103 W is being dumped into roughly 13 cm² of PCB.

### 3.2 Lumped thermal capacitance (30.5×30.5 board, 36×36 mm outline, 8-layer 2 oz)

| Material | Volume | Mass | c_p | C_th |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FR4 | 2.07 cm³ | 3.8 g | 1.1 J/g·K | 4.2 J/K |
| Copper (8 × 70 µm) | 0.73 cm³ | 6.5 g | 0.385 J/g·K | 2.5 J/K |
| FETs, components, solder | | | | ≈ 0.8 J/K |
| **Total C_th** | | | | **≈ 7.5 J/K** |

### 3.3 Convective resistance under prop wash

Assumptions: h ≈ 100 W/m²·K and A ≈ 2 × 0.0013 m² = 0.0026 m².

$$R_{th,ca} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx 3.85\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\tau = R_{th,ca}C_{th} \approx 29\ \text{s}$$

### 3.4 Board temperature transient

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{th}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

The steady-state rise would be 103 × 3.85 ≈ 397 K. This is non-physical: the board fails long before reaching it. **80A × 4 is strictly a burst rating.**

Allowable burst time from T_a = 35 °C to T_board,max = 105 °C (ΔT = 70 K):

$$t_{burst} = -\tau \ln\!\left(1 - \frac{\Delta T}{P R_{th}}\right) = -29\ln(1 - 0.176) \approx 5.6\ \text{s}$$

| Scenario | Total P | Burst time to 105 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 4 × 80 A | 103 W | ≈ 5.6 s |
| 4 × 60 A | ≈ 60 W | ≈ 10 s |
| 1 × 80 A (yaw/punch on one arm) | ≈ 27 W | ≈ 25 s locally, with spreading |
| Continuous limit (ΔT = 70 K) | 18 W | → ~4 × 32 A
