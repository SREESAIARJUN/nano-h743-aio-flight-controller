## 0. Scope, Architecture and Stated Assumptions

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does not commutate the motors. Each of the four ESC channels has its own commutation MCU: an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 talks to each ESC MCU over one bidirectional DShot600 line. The design is a stacked or AIO nano board, about 30×30 mm with 20×20 mounting, on a 6S bus.

**Datasheet values must be substituted.** I have not been given a BOM. Every device parameter below is a representative value for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 5×6 / 3.3×3.3 mm class MOSFET. You must:
- replace each one with the actual datasheet value before sign-off;
- treat the netlist as a reference topology, not a fab-verified design.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32 default. At any instant the phase current flows through exactly **two** MOSFET channels:

- In the PWM'd leg, either the HS FET conducts (for D·T) or the LS FET conducts as a synchronous rectifier (for (1−D)·T).
- In the return leg, the LS FET conducts for the full commutation step.

So the conducting resistance is always about 2·R_ds,on, independent of duty cycle (dead-time intervals are ignored here and handled in §2.4):

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds,on}(T_j)
$$

For a flat-top trapezoidal burst, I_rms ≈ I_burst.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds,on: the self-consistent solution

$$
R_{ds,on}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\, R_{th,jc}
$$

Here P_FET is the loss in a single device. With two devices sharing the conduction loss over the commutation cycle, the per-FET conduction loss is:

$$
P_{FET} \approx I^2 R_{ds,on}(T_j)
$$

The current is assumed to dwell on one device pair (worst case, low eRPM).

**Fixed-point solution.** Solve with T_pcb = 90 °C, which is the end of a realistic burst (see §3):

$$
T_j = 90 + 6400 \cdot 10^{-3}\left[1 + 0.0055(T_j - 25)\right] \cdot 1.0
$$

$$
T_j = 90 + 6.4\,(0.8625 + 0.0055\,T_j)
$$

$$
T_j(1 - 0.0352) = 95.52 \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; T_j \approx 99.0\ ^\circ\text{C}
$$

$$
R_{ds,on} = 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.407 = 1.41\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

**Results at 80 A:**

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| R_ds,on (hot) | 1.41 mΩ |
| Per conducting FET | 80² × 1.41 mΩ = **9.0 W** |
| Per channel (2 conducting) | **18.0 W** |
| Thermal runaway check: α_R · I² · R25 · R_th,jc | 0.035, which is < 1, so it is stable |

The runaway margin is 1/(1 − 0.035).

### 1.3 PCB copper conduction (often forgotten)

Phase and bus traces on 2 oz (70 µm) copper, ~6 mm wide, ~15 mm current path:

$$
R = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.015}{0.006\cdot70\times10^{-6}} = 0.61\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

At 25 °C this is 0.61 mΩ, rising to about 0.8 mΩ hot. The loss is 80² × 0.8 mΩ ≈ **5.1 W per channel**, which is comparable to one FET.

**Mitigation:**
- Use 3 oz inner power planes, paralleled across layers.
- Solder-fill the bus bars.
- Target ≤ 0.3 mΩ total path, which brings this to about 2 W.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches; the return-leg LS FET is static per step. Per channel, at V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, f = 48 kHz:

### 2.1 V-I overlap (hard switching, HS FET)

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r + t_f) f = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Output-capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac12 C_{oss,eff} V^2 f = 0.5 \cdot 1.5\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.023\ W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (LS body diode → HS turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}
$$

Reverse recovery also adds roughly the same amount again to the HS FET turn-on loss. Budget **0.07 W** total.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

This is the dominant secondary loss. Cutting dead time to 50 ns halves it, but you must verify shoot-through margin against driver propagation-delay mismatch (FD6288: ~±20 ns).
