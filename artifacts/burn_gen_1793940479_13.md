# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

**Job #63, Rev A (design basis)**

## 0. Scope and Feasibility Constraints

Three constraints shape the rest of this document. The numbers below are worthless without them.

1. **The H743 does not run the ESC.** At 48 kHz with 6-step commutation, BEMF zero-cross detection and per-channel deadtime, each ESC channel needs its own MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Examples are the STM32G071 and the AT32F421. The H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600 to those four MCUs. "4-in-1 ESC" here means 4 ESC MCUs plus 12 half-bridges on the same PCB.
2. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** Section 3 shows that 4 × 80 A on a 25.5 × 25.5 mm "nano" board dissipates about 120 W. The board reaches its thermal limit in roughly 2 s. Marketing must state the burst duration and conditions.
3. **Device parameters are assumptions.** Values below are representative of a 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 mm PowerPAK/DFN MOSFET. Substitute datasheet values for the selected part before freezing the layout. Every equation is written so you can re-run it.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Trapezoidal 6-Step, Synchronous Rectification)

In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-driven high, one phase is held low, and one phase floats.

- The active high leg's HS FET conducts for D·T_sw.
- The same leg's LS FET (synchronous freewheel) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw.
- The return leg's LS FET conducts 100 % of the time.

So the instantaneous conduction loss of a channel is:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2R(D) + I^2R(1-D) + I^2R = 2I^2R_{hot}$$

This loss is **independent of duty cycle**, a useful property of synchronous 6-step.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

### Per-FET Distribution Over One Electrical Cycle

Each leg is driven-high for 120°, driven-low for 120°, and floats for 120°.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}D\,I^2R,\qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R$$

| D | P_HS | P_LS (worst FET) |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (burst, full throttle) | 3.84 W | 3.84 W |
| 0.5 | 1.92 W | 5.76 W |

The worst-case FET is the **low-side at mid throttle**. Commutation frequency (≥ several hundred Hz electrical) is fast compared with the die-to-board thermal time constant, so cycle averaging is valid.

### Copper Losses (Phase Pour + Ground Return)

$$P_{cu,ch} = 2I^2R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

These losses are spread over the board, not concentrated in the FET.
