## 0. Feasibility Findings

These findings constrain everything that follows, so they come first.

| # | Finding | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **The H743 cannot directly commutate 4 motors at 48 kHz while running ArduPilot.** Production 4-in-1 ESCs use a dedicated ESC MCU per motor (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). | The architecture is **H743 flight controller + 4× ESC MCUs**, linked by bi-directional DShot600. The DMA analysis below assumes this. |
| F2 | **80 A × 4 on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally sustainable.** At 80 A/channel, the board dissipates about 150 W. Its thermal mass is about 2 J/K. | 80 A is achievable only as a **≈1 s all-motor burst** or a **≈3–4 s single-motor burst**. See §3. |
| F3 | **Package fit.** The H743 LQFP100 is 14×14 mm. Twenty-four FETs plus 4 ESC MCUs plus 4 gate drivers will not fit on 20×20 mm. | Use the H743 in **TFBGA100 (8×8)** or UFBGA. Better still, use a **stacked 2-board design** (FC 20×20 over ESC 30×30), or derate the ESC to 40–45 A burst. |

**Recommendation:** 30×30 mm ESC board with DFN5×6 FETs for a genuine 80 A burst rating, or keep 20×20 mm with an honest rating of 45 A burst / 35 A continuous.

The math below is parametric. Substitute your final MOSFET datasheet values.

## 2. Loss Model (per ESC channel)

### 2.1 Commutation Topology

AM32 and BLHeli_32 use 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- **Switching leg:** HS conducts for duty D, LS conducts for 1−D. The current path always goes through exactly one FET of this leg.
- **Return leg:** LS is ON for the whole sector.
- **Floating leg:** OFF (used for back-EMF zero-crossing detection).

So the current always flows through **exactly two FETs in series**.

### 2.2 Conduction Loss

Temperature-corrected on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.8 \times [1 + 0.006 \times 85] = 2.72\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per channel:

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.72\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{34.8\ W}$$

Add copper loss. Assume a phase plus bus path of about 0.5 mΩ, using 4-layer 2 oz copper with paralleled planes:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

**Per-FET distribution:** each of the 6 FETs carries current for about ⅓ of the electrical cycle. The worst FET (the LS, which acts as both return and sync rectifier) sees about 1/3 + (1−D)/3 of the loss share. At D ≈ 0.5, a single LS FET averages about 8.7 W during the burst.

### 2.3 Switching Losses (switching leg only)

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.16\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction loss:**

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I_{ph}\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**Reverse recovery loss:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 20\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.015\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive loss** (dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):

$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 30\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.029\ \text{W}$$
