# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bi-directional DShot DMA, and Netlists

## 0. Architecture Review: Read This Before Layout

Three findings change the scope of this job. Each is resolved below.

| # | Issue | Consequence | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate 4 motors.** That needs 12 complementary dead-time PWM pairs. TIM1 and TIM8 supply 3 pairs each (6 total). HRTIM supplies at most 5 pairs. That is 11 pairs, and those resources would also be consumed by DShot. | A single-MCU design is not feasible. | Use a split architecture. The **H743 runs ArduPilot (the FC)**. **4× AT32F421 / STM32G071 run AM32** (the ESC), with one MCU per motor. The H743 talks to them over bi-directional DShot600. This is the industry-standard topology. |
| 2 | **"Nano" + "80 A".** A 20×20 mm board cannot carry 4×80 A on its copper. The 25.2 V supply rail alone would carry about 320 A worst case. | Thermal runaway in under 1 s. | 80 A is defined here as a **per-motor burst rating of ≤2 s**, with a 35–45 A continuous rating. The ESC needs a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint (8-layer, 2 oz). Only the FC can stay 20×20. |
| 3 | **48 kHz PWM** roughly doubles switching loss compared with 24 kHz. | About +1.3 W per motor at 80 A. | Retain it for acoustic and current-ripple reasons. The model below quantifies the penalty. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path (6-step / trapezoidal, complementary PWM)

During any commutation sector, the current always flows through:
- the **PWM phase**: high-side FET for duty D, then low-side synchronous FET for 1−D;
- the **return phase**: the low-side FET, which conducts for 100% of the sector.

Two FET channels are therefore always in series:

$$R_{path}(T_j) = 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}$$

### 1.2 Temperature coefficient

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \mathrm{K^{-1}}$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{DS(on)} = 0.9 \times 1.38 = 1.24\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.3 Per-motor conduction loss at 80 A

Take R_Cu = 0.30 mΩ (phase pours plus vias) and R_shunt = 0.25 mΩ.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times (2.48 + 0.30 + 0.25)\,\text{mΩ} = 6400 \times 3.03\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{19.4\ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET breakdown

This determines the worst-case junction. Commutation rotates the role among 6 FETs. Within a 60° sector:

| FET role | Loss expression | At D = 0.9 |
|---|---|---|
| High-side, PWM phase | D·I²R | 7.1 W |
| Low-side sync, PWM phase | (1−D)·I²R | 0.8 W |
| Low-side, return phase | I²R | **7.9 W** |

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each FET conducts for 1/3 of the time. Its average conduction loss is therefore:

$$\bar P_{FET,cond} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{DS}}{3} = 2.65\ \text{W}$$

**The sizing case is the instantaneous 7.9 W.** At low eRPM, for example at stall or prop-strike, the sector dwell time exceeds the junction thermal time constant (about 5–20 ms).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

The hard-switched FET is the PWM-phase high side. The low side switches near-ZVS after dead-time.

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{\text{V-I overlap}} + \underbrace{Q_{rr} V f_{sw}}_{\text{reverse recovery}} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss}V^2 f_{sw}}_{\text{C}_{oss}} + \underbrace{2V_F I\,t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{\text{dead-time diode}}$$

Evaluated at 80 A, 25.2 V, and t_dt = 100 ns:

| Term | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5·25.2·80·40 ns·48k | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery | 40 nC·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | 0.5·1.5 nF·25.2²·48k | 0.02 W |
| Dead-time body diode (V_F = 0.8 V) | 2·0.8·80·100 ns·48k | 0.61 W |
| **P_sw per motor** | | **2.62 W** |

Gate drive loss is dissipated in the driver, not the FET:

$$P_g = 6 \times Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot D_{active} \approx 6 \times 60\text{nC}\times10\text{V}\times48\text{k}/3 \approx 0.06\ \text{W per motor}$$

**Frequency sensitivity:** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw.
- At 24 kHz: 1.31 W.
- At 96 kHz: 5.24 W.

48 kHz therefore costs about 1.3 W per motor at burst current.

### 2.1 Total loss budget

| Quantity | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.4 W | 77.6 W |
| Switching | 2.6 W | 10.5 W |
| Driver, MCU, LDO | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total burst** | **22.3 W** | **89.3 W** |

Efficiency check: 89.3 W lost out of about 8 kW electrical, which is 98.9%. This is efficient, but 89 W into a ~10 g PCB is purely a thermal-capacitance problem.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Network (Cauer, 3-node)

```
P_FET ──[R_jc=1.2 K/W]──●(Tj)──[R_cb=4.5 K/W local via field]──●(T_board)──[R_ba]──● T_a
                        │C_j≈0.05 J/K                           │C_board
                       GND                                     GND
```

**Board thermal capacitance** (30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz):
- Copper is about 8 layers × 70 µm × 9.3 cm² × 70% fill × 8.96 g/cm³ ≈ 3.3 g. At 0.385 J/g·K, that is 1.27 J/K.
- FR-4 contributes about 1.9 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 2.1 J/K.
- 12 FETs plus solder add 0.6 J/K.
- **C_board ≈ 4.0 J/K.**

**Board-to-ambient resistance under prop-wash airflow:** h ≈ 60 W/m²K over 2×9.3 cm² gives R_ba ≈ 9 K/W, so τ_board ≈ 36 s.
