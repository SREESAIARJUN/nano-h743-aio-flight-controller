## 1. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

R_DS(on) rises with junction temperature. For trench FETs, α ≈ 0.4 %/K is typical.

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{hot} = 0.70\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.40 \approx 0.98\,\text{m}\Omega$$

Use **1.05 mΩ** to include package and bond-wire resistance.

### 1.2 Conduction loss

In 6-step operation, two phase legs conduct at any instant: one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, during PWM-off the complementary FET of the chopping leg carries the current. The current therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2 R_{hot}\quad\text{(independent of duty D)}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.05\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{13.44\ W}$$

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). The chopping-side FET is shared between itself and its complement in the ratio D : (1−D).

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{I^2 R_{hot}}{3} = \mathbf{2.24\ W}$$

The worst-case FET is the non-chopped low side at full current for its 1/3 window.

### 1.3 Switching loss at 48 kHz

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched edge, chopping leg only):**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$

With t_r + t_f = 20 ns (gate resistor R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω, ~1.5 A driver):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Output-capacitance loss:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(c) Body-diode reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.073\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (t_dt = 100 ns, two dead-time intervals per period, V_F = 0.8 V):

$$P_{dt} = V_F I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (two switching FETs per PWM cycle; dissipated mostly in R_g and the driver):

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\times60\text{nC}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

### 1.4 Loss summary

| Loss term | Per motor @ 80 A | Scaling law |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 13.44 W | ∝ I² |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 W | ∝ V·I·f |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | ∝ I·f |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.13 W | ∝ V·f |
| Gate drive | 0.06 W | ∝ f |
| **FET total** | **15.2 W** | |
| Copper / trace (estimated 0.4 mΩ path) | 2.6 W | ∝ I² |
| **Channel total** | **≈ 17.8 W** | |
| **4 motors simultaneous burst** | **≈ 71 W** | |

**Efficiency check.** Motor input power ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A = 1776 W. The ESC loss of 17.8 W gives about 99.0 % stage efficiency, which is plausible.

**Frequency sensitivity.** Conduction dominates at about 87 % of FET loss. Moving from 24 kHz to 48 kHz adds only about 0.9 W per channel. 48 kHz is therefore a reasonable choice for acoustics and current ripple.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Junction rise (steady-state, per FET)

The worst FET carries about 2.24 W conduction plus about 0.6 W (its share of switching and dead-time loss), so P_FET ≈ 2.9 W.

$$\Delta T_{JC} = 2.9 \times 1.2 = 3.5\ \text{K}$$

The junction-to-case drop is negligible. **The board-to-air path is the bottleneck.**

### 2.2 Board thermal capacitance (lumped burst model)

| Element | Mass | c_p (J/g·K) | C_th (J/K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper: 9.3 cm² × 8 × 70 µm × ~60 % fill → 0.31 cm³ × 8.96 g/cm³ | 2.8 g | 0.385 | 1.08 |
| FR-4 (1.6 mm) | 2.8 g | 1.1 | 3.08 |
| 24 FETs (~0.15 g each) + components | 4.5 g | ~0.7 | 3.15 |
| **Total** | | | **≈ 7.3 J/K** |

### 2.3 Board-to-air resistance

With prop wash (h ≈ 50–80 W/m²K) acting on both sides plus the motor wires as fins:

$$R_{\theta,BA} \approx \frac{1}{h A_{eff}} \approx \frac{1}{60 \times 0.0035} \approx 4.8\ \text{K/W}$$

This gives a thermal time constant:

$$\tau = R_{\theta,BA} C_{th} = 4.8 \times 7.3 \approx 35\ \text{s}$$

### 2.4 Burst response

The single-pole model is:

$$\Delta T(t) = P \cdot R_{\theta} \left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$$

Here P = 71 W for all four motors at 80 A, and the steady-state asymptote would be 340 K. That figure is not survivable; it means **the 80 A rating is burst-only by physics**.

| Burst duration | ΔT_board | T_board (from 45 °C cruise) | T_j (+3.5 K +spreading ≈ 8 K) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 9.6 K | 55 °C | ~63 °C |
| 2 s | 18.9 K | 64 °C | ~72 °C |
| 5 s | 45.4 K | 90 °C | ~98 °C |
| 10 s | 85 K | 130 °C | ~138 °C ❌ |

**Rating conclusions:**

- **Burst rating:** 80 A for 5 s is safe with T_j < 110 °C.
- **Continuous rating:** solve for P_max at ΔT = 60 K, which gives about 12.5 W total. Using I_cont ≈ 80 × √(12.5/71), that is **≈ 34 A per motor continuous with all four loaded**. A 50–55 A continuous label would require a heatsink or heat-spreader plate.
- **Nano 20×20 comparison:** C_th ≈ 3.2 J/K and R_θ ≈ 10 K/W. At 71 W the 5 s rise is about 110 K, so it fails. This confirms the form-factor warning.
