# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 80 A ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #9)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Baseline Assumptions

A design decision needs to be fixed first, because it shapes everything that follows.

**The STM32H743 should not commutate the motors directly.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack. It has no sensorless BLDC commutation, back-EMF zero-cross detection or per-phase PWM generation for 12 half-bridges. The architecture that matches the "bidirectional DShot600" requirement is therefore:

```
┌──────────────────────┐   DShot600 bidir (4 lines)   ┌──────────────────────────┐
│ STM32H743 (ArduPilot)│ ───────────────────────────► │ 4 × ESC MCU (AM32 / BLHeli_32-class) │
│  FC: IMU, baro, OSD  │ ◄─────── eRPM telemetry ──── │  e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 │
└──────────────────────┘                               │  48 kHz complementary PWM  │
                                                       │  3-ph gate driver + 6 FETs  │
                                                       └──────────────────────────┘
```

Replace the following assumed values with your selected components. Every equation is written so you can substitute them directly.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |

| Bus voltage (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |

> **Feasibility flag:** 80 A per motor on a 30.5 mm 4-in-1 board is at the extreme edge of what is achievable. Treat 80 A as a *transient burst* rating, not a continuous one. The model in §2 shows why. A 20 × 20 mm "nano" variant cannot meet 80 A burst with single 3.3 × 3.3 mm FETs per switch. It would need paralleled FETs, which it does not have room for.

## 1. Electrical Loss Model per ESC Channel (One Motor, Six FETs)

### 1.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification, at any instant two switch positions carry full phase current I:
- one high side plus one low side of the commutating phases, or
- during the PWM off-time, the complementary low side replaces the high side.

The duty-cycle split between the high and low side of the PWM'd leg sums to 1. The total conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}
$$

**Per-FET averaging:** each FET position is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle on average. This gives:

$$
\bar P_{FET} \approx 19.2/6 \times (\text{share-weighted}) \approx 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

The peak instantaneous dissipation in a conducting FET is I²R = 9.6 W.

**Temperature-coefficient iteration:** R_DS(on) depends on T_j. Iterate the calculation:

$$
R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + k(T_j - 25)], \qquad k \approx 0.005\ /K
$$

The calculation converges in two to three iterations. Starting from 25 °C, it lands near α ≈ 1.4–1.5 for the burst case.

### 1.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard switching:**
$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{0.968\ W}
$$

**(b) Reverse recovery:**
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}
$$

**(c) C_oss charge and discharge:**
$$
P_{oss} = C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.046\ W}
$$
The ½ CV² term is drawn from the supply. A further ½ is dissipated in the channel per cycle.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$
P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}
$$

**(e) Gate drive (two FETs per PWM'd leg; dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET):**
$$
P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{ nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}
$$

### 1.3 Total per channel and per board

| Loss term | Per motor (W) |
|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 |
| Switching overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_rr | 0.06 |
| C_oss | 0.05 |
| **FET total** | **≈ 20.9** |
| Gate drive (driver IC) | 0.06 |

**Four motors in simultaneous burst: ≈ 83.6 W** in the power stage. This draws about 320 A peak from the battery side. Size the XT60/XT90 connector, pads and battery C-rating accordingly.

**Key insight:** at 48 kHz, conduction dominates by ~20:1. Raising f_sw to 96 kHz adds only about 1.7 W per channel. Halving R_DS(on) by paralleling saves about 9.6 W. Spend the design budget on R_DS(on) and copper, not on switching speed.

**Optimisation knobs:**
- Reduce t_dt to 50 ns: saves 0.31 W per channel. This requires a verified shoot-through margin.
- Use a lower-Q_rr FET, or rely on the body-diode soft-recovery class.
- Use a gate resistor R_g ≈ 2–4.7 Ω. Faster edges than this worsen EMI and ringing for negligible efficiency gain.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Steady-state (why 80 A is burst-only)

The thermal path is junction → case → PCB copper → air.

- Board-to-ambient resistance with prop wash (~5 m/s): R_θBA ≈ 3–5 K/W for the whole 30.5 mm board.
- Steady state at 83.6 W: ΔT_board ≈ 83.6 × 4 = **334 K**. This is impossible. Continuous 80 A × 4 is not a valid operating point.
- Continuous rating instead: with T_board,max = 100 °C and T_amb = 40 °C, the board can dissipate ≈ 15 W total. That is ≈ 3.75 W per motor.
- Solving 2 I² R_hot ≈ 3.5 W gives **I_cont ≈ 34 A per motor** (≈ 30–35 A typical for this class).

### 2.2 Transient burst (lumped-capacitance model)

**Heat capacity of the board assembly:**
- FR4: ~4 g × 1.1 J/g·K = 4.4 J/K
- Copper (2 oz × 8 layers, ~60 % fill): ~5 g × 0.385 J/g·K = 1.9 J/K
- FETs, capacitors and solder: ~1.5 J/K

$$
C_{th} \approx 7.8\ \text{J/K}
$$

**Time constant:**
$$
\tau = R_{\theta BA} C_{th} \approx 4 \times 7.8 \approx 31\ \text{s}
$$

**Board temperature rise during a burst of duration t:**
$$
\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{\theta BA}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C_{th}} \quad (t \ll \tau)
$$

| Burst duration | ΔT_board (from pre-burst baseline) |
|---|---|
| 1 s | ≈ 10.6 K |
| 2 s | ≈ 21 K |
| 5 s | ≈ 50 K |

### 2.3 Junction rise above case

**Average component:**
$$
\Delta T_{JC,avg} = \bar P_{FET} R_{\theta JC} = 3.2 \times 1.2 \approx 3.8\ \text{K}
$$

**Commutation ripple:** each conducting interval lasts 1/3 of an electrical period. At 60k eRPM, the electrical period is 1 ms, so each interval is ~333 µs. From the datasheet transient curve, Z_θJC(333 µs, D = 0.33) ≈ 0.6 K/W. This gives a ripple of ≈ 9.6 × 0.6 ≈ 5.8 K peak.

**Worst-case junction temperature** (5 s burst after hover at board 70 °C):

$$
T_j \approx 70 + 50 + 3.8 + 5.8 \approx \mathbf{130\ °C}
$$

This is within the 150–175 °C rating. Keep the 20 K margin.

**Requirement:** ESC firmware must apply thermal derating. Use AM32 current limit plus a temperature sensor (NTC near the hottest FET), with a ramp-down starting at 100 °C.
