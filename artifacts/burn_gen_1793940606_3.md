# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bidirectional DShot600 Reference Design

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Assumptions

### Architectural corrections

These affect the rest of the model:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. Each motor is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU (STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class firmware). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM. The design is therefore two-tier:
   ```
   H743 (ArduPilot) ──BDShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AM32 @ 48 kHz) ──► 3-ph gate driver ──► 6× MOSFET/motor
   ```
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano/AIO form factor is a thermal-capacitance problem, not a steady-state rating.** The math below shows that a 30×30 mm board sustains roughly 25 A per motor. 80 A per motor is survivable only for about 1–3 s. Treat 80 A as a burst spec with a defined duration, and enforce it in firmware.
3. **No specific MOSFET was given.** I use a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN part. Substitute your selected datasheet values into the equations; every result is shown symbolically first.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Topology

AM32 uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. In every 60° sector, two legs carry the phase current I:

- **PWM on-time (D):** current flows through the high-side FET of leg A and the low-side FET of leg B.
- **PWM off-time (1−D):** current freewheels through the low-side FET of leg A (synchronous rectification) and the low-side FET of leg B.

Two R_DS(on) are always in series, so the conduction loss is independent of duty:
