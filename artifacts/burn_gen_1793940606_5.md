## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology
The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phases carry the motor current I:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is held on for the full 60° sector.

The current therefore always passes through exactly two channels in series, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

Ripple correction for the phase inductance L_ph:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{avg}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5" motor (L_ph ≈ 15 µH, D = 0.9, V_bemf ≈ 21 V), ΔI_pp ≈ 5 A. The ripple term is about 2 A², which is negligible against 6400 A².

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.6 × 1.6 = **2.56 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.56\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{32.8\ W\ per\ channel}$$

At 25 °C junction, this would be 20.5 W. Note the positive feedback: as T_j rises, R_DS(on) rises, which raises T_j further.

### 1.4 Other I²R paths (often ignored, but they are significant)

**Current shunt.** A 0.25 mΩ shunt carries the full current: 80² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**. If one shunt sits on the total 4-in-1 bus at 320 A, it dissipates **25.6 W**. A single-bus shunt at this current is not viable. Use per-channel shunts or a hall sensor.

**Copper.** For 2 oz copper (R_□ ≈ 0.25 mΩ/□ hot), a 10-square path costs 2.5 mΩ, or 16 W at 80 A. **Phase and bus currents must be carried in parallel across ≥4 inner planes with via arrays.** Target ≤0.5 mΩ total per phase path, which costs about 3.2 W.
