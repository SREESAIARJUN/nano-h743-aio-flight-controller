# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" FC: Electrothermal and DShot Architecture (Job #12)

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** On a production 4-in-1 AIO, the STM32H743 is the flight controller only. It does not commutate the motors, for two reasons:

- 12 phase legs at 48 kHz with sensorless BEMF zero-crossing detection would consume H7 timer and ADC resources needed elsewhere.
- Coupling an ESC firmware fault into the flight-control MCU is unacceptable.

The architecture used here is therefore:

| Block | Device (reference) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot, generates bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC MCU ×4 (or 1 multi-channel) | AT32F421K8U7 running AM32 | 48 kHz complementary PWM, sensorless commutation, eRPM reply |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, 3.3 V logic input, internal bootstrap diodes) | High-side and low-side drive |
| MOSFETs ×24 | 40 V N-ch, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | 6 per motor |
| Supply | 6S Li-ion/LiPo (25.2 V max, ~22 V under 80 A sag) | |

So the "48 kHz switching" lives in the ESC MCU, and the "DShot600 DMA" lives in the H743. Both are modeled below.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

In six-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous rectification (AM32 complementary PWM), exactly two phase legs carry current at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** current alternates between the high-side FET (fraction D of each period) and the low-side FET (fraction 1−D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for the full 120° sector.
