# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #15): Thermal, Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope Corrections and Assumptions

Several points in the brief need correcting before any numbers mean anything.

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** In every production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32 commutation firmware, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421. The H743 sends DShot600 to those MCUs and receives eRPM telemetry back. I model it that way.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A burst are in tension.** A 20×20 mm board cannot shed the ~90 W loss computed below for more than about a second. I assume a **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper** ESC board. Section 3 shows why 20×20 only works for sub-second bursts.
3. **Device parameters are representative, not from a specific datasheet.** I use a generic 40 V, ~1 mΩ, PDFN5×6-class MOSFET. Substitute your exact part's R_DS(on)(T), Q_g, Q_rr, C_oss and Z_th(j-c) curves. Every equation stays valid.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, which is the AM32/BLHeli_32 default. At any instant two phases conduct:

- **Switching leg:** the high-side FET conducts for D·T_sw. The low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1−D)·T_sw.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on for the whole 60° sector.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two FET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{on}(T_j) \;+\; I_{rms}^2 R_{sh} \;+\; I_{rms}^2 R_{Cu}$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (positive feedback)

$$R_{on}(T_j) = R_{on,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\,\text{K}^{-1}$$

Conduction loss and junction temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\cdot Z_{th}(t)$$

The fixed point stays stable only while dP/dT · Z_th < 1. The hot value R_on = 1.5 mΩ (T_j ≈ 125 °C) gives a conservative upper bound.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A, ripple neglected (I_rms ≈ I_dc)

| Term | Expression | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| FET channels | 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** |
| Shunt | 80² × 0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.15 mΩ phase path) | 80² × 0.15 mΩ | 1.0 W |
| **Per channel** | | **23.4 W** |

**Per-device averaging.** Each phase leg conducts 2/3 of the electrical cycle, so the FET loss spreads over 6 devices:

- Average per FET ≈ 19.2/6 = **3.2 W**.
- Instantaneous worst case, return-leg low side on 100% of the sector: 80² × 1.5 mΩ = **9.6 W**.

Because the sector period (~0.1–1 ms at burst eRPM) is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~10 ms), the average value governs junction temperature.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the switching leg commutates at f_sw. Five terms contribute.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard turn-on and turn-off of the high side:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}(25.2)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$
In practice Q_rr also lengthens the turn-on current overshoot. Budget about 2× this figure, roughly 0.12 W.

**(c) Output capacitance, both devices in the leg:**
$$P_{oss} = 2\cdot\tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 2(0.5)(2\text{n})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction, two edges per period:**
$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive, dissipated in the driver and gate resistors rather than the FETs:**
$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2(60\text{n})(10)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

| Term | Per channel |
|---|---|
| V–I overlap | 1.94 W |
| Reverse recovery (with margin) | 0.12 W |
| C_oss | 0.06 W |
| Dead time | 0.61 W |
| **FET switching total** | **2.73 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver) | 0.06 W |

**Design levers:**
- Dead time is the largest controllable term after V–I overlap. Dropping t_dt to 50 ns, if driver propagation matching allows, saves ~0.3 W per channel.
- Going from 48 kHz to 24 kHz halves P_sw, but increases current ripple and audible noise.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Total burst dissipation

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + shunt + Cu) | 23.4 W | 93.6 W |
| Switching | 2.73 W | 10.9 W |
| **Total** | **26.1 W** | **≈105 W** |

At 80 A × 25.2 V the system delivers about 8 kW, so this loss corresponds to ~98.7% efficiency. Losses are small relative to throughput but enormous relative to a 30 mm board.

### 3.2 Two-node RC network

```
Tj ──Rth_jc──┬── Tboard ──Rth_ba── Ta
             │      │
          (Cdie)  C_board
```

- **R_th,jc** ≈ 0.8 K/W per device.
