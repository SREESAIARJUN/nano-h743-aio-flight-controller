## 0. Architecture Clarification and Key Finding

### Architecture

- The H743 does **not** drive the MOSFET gates.
- In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071.
- The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those four MCUs.
- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. The DShot timer allocation lives on the H743.

### Key finding (stated up front)

On a 20×20 mm "nano" board, **80 A per channel is not a sustainable rating.** The model below shows:

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| Continuous, with prop-wash airflow | ≈ 33 A/channel |
| 80 A burst, all four channels at once | ≈ 2 s before T_j reaches 150 °C |
| 80 A burst, single channel | ≈ 4–6 s |

If 80 A burst is a hard requirement, move to a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint with 5×6 mm FETs or paralleled FETs. The math below lets you re-run that trade.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step operation, current flows through two phases at any instant:

- one high-side FET is on (or its complementary low-side FET during the PWM off-time, with synchronous rectification), and
- one low-side FET is on continuously for the 60° sector.

So there are always **two R_DS(on)** in series with the motor current.

**Hot on-resistance:**

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.6 = 1.92\,\text{mΩ}$$

**Per-channel conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I^2 (2R_{DS,hot} + R_{Cu}) = 80^2 (3.84 + 0.5)\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 4.34\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{27.8\ W}$$

This splits as 24.6 W in silicon and 3.2 W in copper.

**Per-FET loss:** the worst-case FET is the PWM-ing high-side device, at about 12.3 W of conduction loss.

*Note:* at high duty cycle, the R_DS split between high-side and low-side FETs shifts, but the sum stays at 2·R_DS. The battery current is I_bat ≈ D·I, which matters for the bulk capacitors and XT60 lead sizing, not for FET loss.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg hard-switches. Its complementary FET commutates through the body diode during dead time, so it switches at roughly zero voltage.

### (a) V–I overlap loss (hard-switched FET)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### (b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two transitions per period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### (c) Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### (d) Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

### (e) Gate drive (two switching FETs per leg)

This loss lands mostly in the gate driver and R_g, not in the FET.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.038\ W}$$
