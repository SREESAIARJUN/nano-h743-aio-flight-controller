## 1. Conduction loss model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.5\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.45 = 2.18\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Per-motor conduction loss

In 6-step drive, two legs are always conducting: one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the current flows through either the HS or the LS FET of the PWM leg, so the series resistance is always 2·R_ds. PWM ripple adds a correction term:

$$P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\left[1+\frac{1}{12}\left(\frac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2\right]$$

At 80 A, the ripple term is negligible below about 20 % ripple:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.18\,\text{mΩ} = \mathbf{27.9\ W\ per\ motor}$$

**Copper loss.** The phase and bus copper adds about 0.3 mΩ effective, which contributes roughly 1.9 W per motor [A]. Rather than add it separately, it is absorbed into the 2.18 mΩ worst case used in Section 3.

## 2. Switching loss model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. The other conducting leg is static during a sector.

| Term | Formula | Value at 25.2 V, 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 30 ns × 48 k = **1.45 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.048 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V·f_sw × 2 FETs | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate charge | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw × 2 | 0.038 W, mostly dissipated in the driver |

Summing the first four rows:

$$P_{sw} \approx \mathbf{2.16\ W\ per\ motor}$$

Dead-time loss is about 30 % of the switching budget. If the driver propagation skew allows it, cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.

### 2.1 Total burst dissipation

$$P_{motor} \approx 27.9 + 2.2 \approx \mathbf{30\ W}, \qquad P_{board} = 4 \times 30 = \mathbf{120\ W}$$
