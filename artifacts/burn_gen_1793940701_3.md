# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #5): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

The analysis depends on several assumptions. If any is wrong, the numbers change.

| # | Assumption | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Architecture.** The H743 is the flight controller. The 4-in-1 ESC has four dedicated ESC MCUs (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) doing 6-step commutation at 48 kHz. | The H743 cannot sensibly do the BEMF commutation for 4 motors and run ArduPilot at the same time. Bidirectional DShot also implies a separate ESC MCU. |
| A2 | Supply is 6S: V_bus = 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal. | Switching loss scales with V. |
| A3 | 80 A is a **per-motor burst** of 2 s or less, not continuous. | Thermal model is transient. |
| A4 | Reference MOSFET is a 40 V, 5×6 mm DFN with these parameters: R_ds(on) = 1.5 mΩ at 25 °C and V_gs = 10 V; Q_g = 50 nC; Q_oss = 40 nC; Q_rr = 50 nC; V_SD = 0.8 V; R_θJC = 1.2 K/W. | Substitute your real datasheet values. |
| A5 | T_ambient = 40 °C (inside the frame, rotor wash disrupted). | — |

> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** A true "nano" (20×20 mm) 4-in-1 board cannot physically carry 80 A per motor. Commercial 20×20 boards top out around 40–45 A burst, limited by 3.3×3.3 mm FETs and copper cross-section. Below I show that 80 A per motor puts about **135 W** into the ESC. This is only viable on a 30.5×30.5 mm (or larger) board with 5×6 mm FETs, 2–3 oz copper, and preferably paralleled low-side FETs. Treat "nano" as the flight controller form factor only.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right], \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.6 = **2.4 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM

During each 60° commutation sector:

- One high-side FET switches at duty cycle D.
- Its complementary low-side FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- One opposite-leg low-side FET is fully on.

The current I flows through two series conduction paths at every instant:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[\,D + (1-D) + 1\,] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Note that this is **independent of D**, because synchronous rectification replaces diode loss.

Add the copper path (pads, pours, phase wire joints). A realistic estimate is R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per motor path:

$$P_{cond} = 2 I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) + I^2 R_{cu}$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 2 × 6400 × 2.4 mΩ | 30.72 W |
| Copper | 6400 × 0.3 mΩ | 1.92 W |
| **Total P_cond per motor** | | **32.6 W** |

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution (worst-case sector)

The worst-case individual device is the statically-on low-side FET:

$$P = I^2 R_{ds} = 15.4\ \text{W}$$

It carries this for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

At high eRPM (e.g., 100k eRPM gives a 600 µs electrical period), the die thermal time constant (about 1–5 ms) averages this. Each of the 6 FETs then averages about **5.1 W** conduction.

At low eRPM or stall (punch-out from hover), the die sees the full 15.4 W. **Design to this case.**

## 2. Switching Losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Use V = 25.2 V, I = 80 A, and t_r + t_f = 40 ns. The 40 ns assumes about 2 A driver capability and gate resistance R_g ≈ 4.7 Ω.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V-I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = 1.94\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Assumes t_dt = 100 ns, with two transitions per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

Two FETs switch per PWM cycle, driven at 10 V:

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

### 2.6 Summary per motor at 80 A

| Loss | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FET + Cu) | 32.6 |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.11 |
| Gate | 0.05 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 35.3 W
