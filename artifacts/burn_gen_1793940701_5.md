**Architectural red flags. Resolve these before layout:**

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every real 4-in-1 design, the ESC has its own MCU(s), e.g. STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32. The H743 flight controller only emits DShot600 and captures the bi-directional eRPM telemetry. The DMA model in §3 reflects this split. If Job #33 truly intends the H743 to do field-oriented control (FOC) of 4 motors directly, that is a different and much harder architecture: 24 complementary PWM channels and 12+ ADC channels. Flag it for review.
2. **80 A continuous on a 25.5 mm board is not physically achievable.** §2.4 shows the 80 A rating is a **≈3–5 s burst** limited by thermal capacitance. Market it and set firmware current limits accordingly.
3. **80 A through the bus connector and copper** is a first-order loss term (§2.3), often larger than the MOSFET switching loss.

## 1. Electrical Topology Summary

```
6S BATT ──┬── TVS + bulk caps ──┬── ESC MCU ×1 or ×4 (AM32) ── 3× half-bridge gate drivers ── 6 MOSFETs/motor (24 total)
          │                     │
          └── FC BEC (5V/3A) ── STM32H743 FC ── DShot600 BDShot ×4 ──► ESC MCU signal pins
```

Per motor, six-step (trapezoidal) commutation applies. In any commutation sector, one phase is PWM-switching (high-side/low-side complementary), one phase has its low side held ON, and one phase floats for BEMF sensing.

## 2. Thermal / Loss Mathematical Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The current path in any sector runs through exactly **two conducting channels in series**:
- the active high-side (duty D) or its synchronous low-side complement (1−D), and
- the static low-side of the return phase.

Ignoring dead time (accounted for separately), the instantaneous series resistance is 2·R_ds(on) regardless of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

Temperature dependence of R_ds(on) for silicon trench MOSFETs:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left(1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right), \quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /\mathrm{K}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.55) = **1.55 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,motor} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.55\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.8\ W}$$

Because the burst current is effectively DC over a PWM period, the ripple correction (I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12) is small. With a typical motor inductance of 20 µH, the result is:

$$\Delta I \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \Big|_{D=0.5} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\mu \times 48k} = 6.6\ A_{pp}$$

This adds only +0.06 % to I_rms², so it is negligible.

**Electrothermal feedback note:** R_ds(on) depends on T_j. The coupled steady-state solution is therefore:

$$T_j = T_a + R_{\theta} \cdot P(T_j) \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_a + R_\theta (P_0 - 25\alpha P_0')}{1 - R_\theta \alpha P_0'}$$

Here P₀' = 2I²R₂₅. Thermal runaway occurs when R_θ·α·P₀' ≥ 1. With P₀' = 12.8 W and α = 0.0055, runaway occurs at R_θ ≥ 14 K/W per motor. **This is a real design constraint for nano boards** (see §2.4).

### 2.2 Switching Loss (One PWM-Switching Leg per Motor per Sector)

**(a) Hard-switching V-I overlap.** This occurs on the high-side only, since the low side switches at ≈ zero voltage after body-diode conduction.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode at high-side turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

Q_rr scales roughly with di/dt and I_F. At 80 A, derate pessimistically ×2, giving **0.11 W**.

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

The ½Q·V share is dissipated in the channel. The remaining share is dissipated in the opposite device and R_g during the transition. The total is used here, which is conservative.

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This is the largest switching-related term. Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves it, which is the single best knob available.

**(e) Gate drive.** Two switching FETs per active leg; most of this is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2 \times 55\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.053\ W$$

**Switching subtotal per motor:** ≈ 0.97 + 0.11 + 0.06 + 1.23 + 0.05 = **2.42 W**

### 2.3 Copper / Interconnect Loss

The current path is: battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad → motor wire pad, plus the return path. Assume an effective series copper path of **0.6 mΩ per motor**, including inner planes, vias and the shared bus.

$$P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.6\text{m} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}$$

The shared bus segment carries 4×80 = 320 A at the battery pads. Assume 0.15 mΩ of shared copper:

$$P_{bus} = 320^2 \times 0.15\text{m} = \mathbf{15.4\ W}$$

**This is a major term.** Battery current is lower than phase current when D < 1 (I_batt ≈ D·I_ph), so this is the D = 1 worst case. At D = 0.7, it falls to 7.5 W.

Bulk-capacitor ripple-current dissipation is minor relative to the other terms, at ≈0.3 W total.

### 2.4 Loss Budget and Thermal Results

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 125 °C) | 19.8 W | 79.2 W |
| Switching + dead time + gate | 2.4 W | 9.7 W |
| Phase copper | 3.8 W | 15.4 W |
| Shared bus copper (D = 1) | n/a | 15.4 W |
| **Total** | | **≈ 120 W** |

**Steady state is impossible.** A 25.5 mm board in prop wash has an effective board-to-ambient resistance of R_θ,ba ≈ 4–6 K/W with ~10 m/s airflow. At 120 W, ΔT = 480–720 K. The 80 A figure must therefore be treated as a **transient** rating.

**Transient (burst) model.** Two-node Cauer network:

```
P_j ──[R_jc = 1.2 K/W per FET]──● T_case ──[R_cb]──● T_board(C_board) ──[R_ba ≈ 5 K/W]── T_amb
             C_die ≈ 0.02 J/K                         C_board ≈ 5–6 J/K
```

**Board thermal capacitance:**
- FR4 + Cu, ~6 g at an effective 0.9 J/(g·K) ≈ 5.4 J/K
- 24 FETs at ~0.1 g each, plus the ESC capacitor bank, ≈ 0.6 J/K
- **C_th ≈ 6 J/K**

With τ = R·C ≈ 5 × 6 = 30 s, the burst behaves as nearly adiabatic for t ≪ τ:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) = P R_{ba}\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right) \approx \frac{P}{C_{th}} t = \frac{120}{6} t = 20\ \text{K/s}$$

**Junction rise above the board.** Each motor dissipates its loss over its 6 FETs, but only ~2–3 conduct heavily at a time. Take the worst-case FET at ≈ 9 W (half the motor conduction loss plus the switching loss on the PWM leg):

$$\Delta T_{jb} = 9\text{ W} \times (1.2 + 1.5_{\text{case→board spreading}}) = 24\ \text{K}$$

The die thermal time constant is ~10 ms, so this rise is reached almost immediately.
