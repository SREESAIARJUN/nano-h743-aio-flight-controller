## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and an Early Warning

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does **not** drive the inverters. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32) that commutates its 3-phase bridge. The 48 kHz switching frequency is an ESC-firmware parameter. ## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.6 × (1 + 0.0055×75) = **2.26 mΩ**
At T_j = 150 °C: R = 1.6 × 1.6875 = **2.70 mΩ**

### 1.2 Trapezoidal BLDC with complementary (synchronous) PWM

In any 60° commutation sector, two phases conduct:

- **PWM phase:** the high-side (HS) is on for fraction D, and the low-side (LS) is on synchronously for 1−D.
- **Return phase:** the LS is on for 100%.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}\cdot 1 = 2I^2R_{DS}$$

This result is **independent of duty** when the ESC uses synchronous rectification. Without it, the (1−D) term moves into the body diode, adding V_SD·I·(1−D), which is far worse.

At 80 A and T_j = 100 °C:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2(80)^2(2.26\times10^{-3}) = \mathbf{28.9\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each of the 6 FETs conducts in rotation. At flight eRPM, the commutation period is about 0.05–0.5 ms, which is much shorter than the die thermal time constant (~1–5 ms). Each FET therefore sees its time-average:

$$\bar P_{FET} = \frac{2I^2R_{DS}}{6} = \frac{I^2R_{DS}}{3} = \mathbf{4.82\ W\ @\ 80\ A}$$

**Exception:** near-stall or low-eRPM desync (commutation period > ~5 ms). Here a single FET can carry I²R = **14.5 W** instantaneously, so ΔT_JC alone reaches 17 K. Stall/desync protection must trip within **≤ 100 ms**.
