## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current paths in 6-step synchronous commutation

In each 60° electrical sector, one phase's high-side (HS) FET is PWM'd at duty D and a second phase's low-side (LS) FET is held on.
- **During the ON interval:** current flows HS(a) → motor → LS(b). Two FETs conduct.
- **During the OFF interval (synchronous rectification):** current flows LS(a) → motor → LS(b). Two FETs conduct.

So **two FETs always carry I_ph**, regardless of duty cycle. Total conduction loss per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j-25)\right]$$

where α ≈ 0.006 /K, which gives ×1.6 at 125 °C.

**At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C:**

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 2.08\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{26.6\ W}$$

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts during 1/3 of the electrical period, either as the switched device or as the held/rectifying device. For eRPM above about 1 kHz, the commutation period is far shorter than the package thermal time constant (ms range), so the averaged value applies:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{ph}^2 R_{DS(on)} \times 2 \;\Rightarrow\; \tfrac{26.6}{6} = \mathbf{4.4\ W/FET}$$

The instantaneous peak per FET is 13.3 W. This matters only at very low eRPM, such as start-up or a locked rotor (see §3.4).
