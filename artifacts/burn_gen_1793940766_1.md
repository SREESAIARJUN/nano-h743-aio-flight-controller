### Design flags to resolve at architecture review

1. **"Nano" form factor vs. 80 A.** 80 A per channel × 4 on a 20×20 mm board is not thermally credible (Section 2 shows ~80 W total at burst). I am modeling a **30.5×30.5 mm stacked or 35×35 mm integrated** board. Sustained ratings on 20×20 boards realistically land at 25–35 A per channel.
2. **MCU partitioning.** The H743 is the flight controller. Each ESC channel still needs its own motor-control MCU (e.g., AT32F421 running AM32, or the BLHeli_32 equivalent). The H743 does not drive gates directly. Bi-directional DShot runs between the H743 and those four ESC MCUs.
3. **Integrated FC + ESC.** Integration demands strict ground partitioning (Section 4.3). Otherwise gyro noise and DShot bit errors will dominate your bring-up time.

## 1. Loss Model per ESC Channel

### 1.1 Conduction topology
In 6-step drive, one high-side (HS) FET of one phase and one low-side (LS) FET of another phase carry the current at any time. With synchronous rectification, the PWM-off interval transfers the current to the complementary FET of the same leg. The motor current therefore always flows through **two FET channels in series**, independent of duty cycle D:

$$P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2\,(R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{cu})$$

The per-FET split matters for hotspot analysis. The HS of the PWM'd leg carries D·I²R, and its complementary LS carries (1−D)·I²R.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on), with a convergence loop
$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ /\text{K}$$

Because R depends on Tj and Tj depends on P, iterate the pair below until it converges:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}b}$$

### 1.3 Switching losses (hard-switched leg)
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}$$
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$$
$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}\ (\text{one hard-switched node})$$
$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw}$$
$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw}\ (\text{dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors})$$

### 1.4 Numerical evaluation at 80 A, 25.2 V, 48 kHz (Tj ≈ 125 °C)

**Conduction**
- R_ds(on),hot = 0.9 × 1.55 ≈ 1.40 mΩ
- P_cond,FET = 80² × 2 × 1.40 m = **17.9 W**
- P_cond,Cu = 80² × 2 × 0.4 m = **5.1 W**. This heat goes into the board, not the junctions.

**Switching**
- P_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W**
- P_rr = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W**
- P_oss = 0.5 × 1.5 nF × 25.2² × 48 k = **0.02 W**
- P_dt = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W**
- P_gate = 2 × 60 nC × 10 V × 48 k = **0.06 W**, in the driver

**Totals**

| | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor loss | ≈ 20.5 W | ≈ 82 W |
| Including copper | ≈ 25.6 W | ≈ 102 W |

At burst, ~140 W of FET loss would have to leave a 30×30 mm board. Section 2 therefore treats 80 A as a **time-limited** rating.

**Key sensitivity.** Conduction is about 87 % of semiconductor loss.
- Dropping f_sw from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only ~1.3 W per channel.
- Halving R_ds(on), for example with two paralleled FETs per switch, saves ~9 W per channel.
- **Spend the budget on R_ds(on) and copper, not on f_sw.**

### 1.5 Hottest-FET loss
At high throttle (D → 0.95), the PWM'd HS FET carries most of the conduction plus all switching loss:

P_HS ≈ 0.95 × 80² × 1.40 m + 1.94 + 0.61 ≈ **11.1 W** in a single 3.3×3.3 mm package. **This is the design-limiting component.**

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Network (per hottest FET → board → ambient)
```
Tj ──[Rθjc=1.0 K/W]── Tcase ──[Rθ,via+spread≈3.5 K/W]── T_board ──[1/G_amb]── T_amb
                                                   │
                                              C_board (lumped)
```

- Thermal vias under the pad: ≥ 20× 0.3 mm vias, plated or filled, tied to inner 2 oz planes.
- Junction time constant: τ_j ≈ ms scale, so the junction tracks the board almost instantly.

### 2.2 Steady junction offset over board
$$\Delta T_{j\text{-}b} = 11.1\ \text{W} \times 4.5\ \text{K/W} \approx 50\ \text{K}$$

### 2.3 Board transient during burst
Lumped board heat capacity for a 30.5 mm 8-layer board with 2 oz copper, FETs, and capacitors:
C ≈ m·c ≈ (6 g copper × 0.385) + (4 g FR4 × 1.1) + components ≈ **8 J/K**

Adiabatic heating rate at 102 W total: dT/dt ≈ 102 / 8 ≈ **12.8 K/s**

### 2.4 Burst-duration limit
Constraint: Tj,max = 125 °C (derated from 150 °C). This requires T_board ≤ 125 − 50 = 75 °C.

Starting from T_board = 40 °C (hover-heated):

$$t_{burst} \approx \frac{75 - 40}{12.8} \approx \mathbf{2.7\ s}$$

With prop-wash convection, the heat loss is G_amb ≈ h·A = 120 W/m²K × 2 × 0.00093 m² ≈ 0.22 W/K. Motor and battery leads add ~0.5 W/K. The ~25 W this removes during burst is small, so the result is t_burst ≈ **3.5 s**.

### 2.5 Continuous rating
Solve P_total(I) = G_amb,total × (T_board,max − T_amb) with T_board,max = 75 °C and T_amb = 35 °C:

P_allow ≈ 0.72 × 40 ≈ 29 W total ≈ 7.2 W per channel

$$I_{cont} \approx \sqrt{\frac{7.2 - P_{sw}(I)}{2(R_{ds}+R_{cu})}} \approx \mathbf{40\text{–}45\ A\ per\ channel}$$

**Recommended label:** 45 A continuous / 80 A for 3 s burst. Verify both with thermocouple and IR testing on a dyno.

### 2.6 Firmware protection
- ESC-side thermal model: I²t accumulator with the Section 2.3 time constant.
- NTC per FET bank.
- Current limit at 80 A via shunt.
- Report temperature through extended DShot telemetry (EDT) so ArduPilot can log it and react.
