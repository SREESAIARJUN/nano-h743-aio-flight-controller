## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Feasibility Check

### 0.1 Architecture

The STM32H743 cannot directly commutate four BLDC motors at 48 kHz PWM while also running ArduPilot. This design therefore uses a split architecture:

| Block | Device (reference choice) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC MCU | STM32H743VIH6 (480 MHz, TFBGA100) | ArduPilot. Generates DShot600, decodes bidirectional eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071GBU6 | AM32 firmware. 48 kHz complementary PWM, commutation, back-EMF sensing. |
| Gate driver ×4 | Fortior FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | Drives high-side and low-side MOSFETs. |
| MOSFET ×24 | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm PQFN class | 6 per channel. |

The 48 kHz switching loss is set by the ESC MCU PWM. The DShot600 and DMA allocation belong to the H743. The two are linked only by the DShot signal line.

### 0.2 Feasibility Check (Read First)

At 6S, 80 A on all four channels is about 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A ≈ **8 kW** of electrical throughput. Production ESCs in a nano (20×20 mm) footprint are typically rated 25–45 A continuous. The analysis below shows that:

- 80 A is achievable only as a **short burst (about 1–2 s)**, limited by thermal capacitance rather than steady-state dissipation.
- 80 A burst on all four channels simultaneously is marginal. A 30.5×30.5 mm footprint is strongly recommended if the 80 A figure must be guaranteed.

All device parameters below are stated assumptions. Replace them with datasheet values for the selected MOSFET before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step Synchronous Commutation

In each 60° sector, two legs carry current. The PWM leg alternates between its high-side (HS) switch (duty D) and its low-side (LS) switch (synchronous rectification, 1−D). The return leg holds its LS switch on continuously.

The current therefore always passes through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot \left[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} + R_{LS}\right] = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) \quad (R_{HS}=R_{LS})$$

Over one electrical revolution, each leg conducts for 240° of 360°. The average loss per MOSFET is therefore:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}$$

The distribution between HS and LS depends on D, but the channel total does not.

### 2.2 Temperature Dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,(1+\alpha)^{(T_j-25)}$$

| T_j | R_ds |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 2.10 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 2.34 mΩ |

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

Silicon loss:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.34\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.68\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{29.95\ W}$$

Copper loss (2 phases in path):

$$P_{Cu} = 6400 \times 2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 3.84\ \text{W}$$

**Conduction total per channel ≈ 33.8 W.**

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The return-leg LS switch is static during its sector.

### 3.1 V–I Overlap

Gate current during the Miller plateau, with V_pl ≈ 3.5 V at 80 A:

$$I_g \approx \frac{V_{drv}-V_{pl}}{R_{g,tot}} = \frac{10-3.5}{6} \approx 1.08\ \text{A}$$

Ideal transition time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_g} \approx 9.3\ \text{ns}$$

Common-source inductance and driver current limiting roughly double this in practice. Use **t_r = t_f ≈ 20 ns** as a design
