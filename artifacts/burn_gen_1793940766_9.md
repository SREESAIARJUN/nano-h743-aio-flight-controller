# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Assumptions

I don't have a parts list for Job #50, so this model uses **representative component parameters**. Each one is a named variable, so you can substitute datasheet values and re-run the math. Before the model, three design points need to be settled.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs (assumed: AT32F421 running AM32). Those MCUs handle six-step commutation at 48 kHz. Commutating four BLDC motors from the H743 under ArduPilot is not supported.

**2. "80A burst" is defined here as 80A per motor at the DC-equivalent phase current, for a few seconds.**
- Four motors at 80A means 320A from the battery, which exceeds any XT60/XT90 and most 6S packs.
- The board-level model below covers both cases: one motor at 80A, and all four at 80A.

**3. Form factor.** "Nano" is assumed to mean a 36×36 mm board (30.5 mm mounting pattern), 8 layers, 2 oz copper. A 20×20 board cannot thermally sustain this burst. Section 3 shows why.
