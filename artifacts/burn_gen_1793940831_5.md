## 1. Field-Oriented Control

### 1.1 Transforms

Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, assuming i_a + i_b + i_c = 0):

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

Park transform, with θ_e = p·θ_m − θ_off:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is the transpose of that matrix.

### 1.2 Space-vector modulation (min/max injection)

This form is equivalent to sector-based SVPWM:

$$
v_a = v_\alpha,\quad v_b = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha + \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} v_\beta,\quad v_c = -\tfrac12 v_\alpha - \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2} v_\beta
$$

$$
v_0 = -\tfrac12\big(\max(v_{abc}) + \min(v_{abc})\big),\qquad
d_x = \tfrac12 + \frac{v_x + v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$

The linear range is |v_αβ| ≤ V_bus/√3. Apply circular limiting to the dq vector: if |v_dq| exceeds V_max, scale it by V_max/|v_dq|. Give d priority so flux control is preserved.

### 1.3 Current loop (PI with decoupling)

Plant in the dq frame:

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

Use PI with pole-zero cancellation (zero at R/L). The closed loop is then first-order with bandwidth ω_c:

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_c,\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_c
$$

Choose ω_c ≤ 2π·f_s/20. At 20 kHz this gives 2π·1 kHz. Example: K_p = 2e-3 · 6283 ≈ 12.6 V/A and K_i = 5.5 · 6283 ≈ 34 600 V/(A·s).
