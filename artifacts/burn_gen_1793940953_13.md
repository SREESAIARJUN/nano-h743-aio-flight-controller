# STM32H743 AIO 4-in-1 ESC Nano FC: 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Architecture

I don't have prior context for Job #49, so this document starts from an explicit assumption set. Every result is parametric, so you can substitute real datasheet values and re-run the numbers.

**Key findings**

- **80 A per motor is a burst rating only (~0.5–1 s) on a 20×20 mm board.** The board dissipates ~124 W at that level, roughly 13× more than it can shed continuously. Continuous operation is limited to ~11 A/motor. Sustained 80 A/motor needs a larger, separate ESC.
- **Conduction loss dominates** (~93% of loss at 80 A). The FET's Rds(on) and temperature coefficient matter most. Switching speed, dead time, and PWM frequency are secondary.
- **A single shunt for 320 A total is infeasible.** Use a Hall-effect sensor or per-motor sensing.
- **The H743 does not run the motors directly.** It sends DShot600 to separate ESC MCUs. Assign all four motors to one timer (TIM1 CH1–CH4), never N-channels, and verify the timer and DMA allocation on hardware.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous (complementary) PWM. In each 60° sector:

- Phase A high-side (HS) chops at duty D. Its complementary low-side (LS) conducts for (1−D).
- Phase B LS is held fully on.
- Phase C floats.

Current therefore always flows through exactly **two channel resistances** in series, regardless of D.

Instantaneous conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot \big[D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS}\big] + I_{rms}^2 R_{LS} \approx 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Ripple contribution

Phase current ripple peaks at D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\times10^{-6} \times 48\times10^{3}} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

The resulting RMS current is:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.59 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds only 0.06%, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one argument for 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where the ripple term would be 4× larger.

### 1.3 Temperature-dependent resistance

The channel resistance rises with junction temperature:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\,\big[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\big], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{ds}(125\,^\circ\text{C}) = 1.5 \times 1.5 = 2.25\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

| Condition | P_cond per motor | P_cond, 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| T_j = 25 °C (start of burst) | 2 × 6404 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W** | 76.8 W |
| T_j = 125 °C (end of burst) | 2 × 6404 × 2.25 mΩ = **28.8 W** | 115.3 W |

Because R_ds rises with temperature, the loss is a positive-feedback loop. Self-consistent junction temperature, assuming T_case is held:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta jc}\cdot\frac{2 I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]}{n}$$

Here n is the number of FETs sharing the loss. Thermal runaway occurs if:

$$R_{\theta,total}\cdot\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \ge 1$$
