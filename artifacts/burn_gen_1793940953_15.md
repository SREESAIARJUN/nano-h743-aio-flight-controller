# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture & Analysis Package

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Three Critical Findings

**Topology.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and does not drive the bridges directly. Each motor has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 firmware at 48 kHz PWM, commanded over bidirectional DShot600. This is the only practical architecture: 6-step commutation with BEMF zero-cross detection needs a dedicated real-time core per motor.

**Device parameters.** The MOSFET values below are representative of a 30 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN device. Substitute values from your actual BOM.

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| On-resistance @ 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | R_ds,25 | 1.5 mΩ |
| R_ds temperature multiplier @ T_j = 100 °C | k_T | 1.4 → **2.1 mΩ** |
| Total gate charge | Q_g | 25 nC |
| Gate-source charge / gate-drain charge | Q_gs / Q_gd | 6 nC / 5 nC |
| Gate plateau voltage | V_pl | 3.5 V |
| Effective output capacitance | C_oss,eff | 800 pF |
| Reverse-recovery charge | Q_rr | 20 nC |
| Body-diode forward voltage | V_f | 0.8 V |
| Junction-to-case thermal resistance | R_θjc | 1.5 K/W |
| Bus voltage (6S, fully charged) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Phase current (burst) | I | 80 A |

| Dead time | t_dt | 200 ns |
| Gate driver (FD6288Q class) | V_drv, R_g | 10 V; 10 Ω on, 8 Ω off (total) |

### Critical findings

> ⚠️ **1. "80 A burst" cannot be sustained on a 20×20 mm nano board.** The physics, shown in §3, limits a nano-class AIO board to roughly **18–20 A continuous per motor**. 80 A is reachable for well under one second, and only when a single motor is loaded. Treat 80 A as a fault or desync surge rating, not a flight rating. A credible 80 A-burst design needs a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger standalone ESC with 2–3 paralleled FETs per switch position.
>
> **2. Conduction loss dominates; switching loss at 48 kHz is secondary.** The ratio is about 18:1.
>
> **3. The DShot/DMA plan is feasible.** The H743's DMAMUX removes the fixed stream mapping that constrains F4/F7 boards. The real limit is the total stream budget.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in AM32 6-step with synchronous rectification

During each 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase X** is PWM-switched, with the high side and low side complementary.
- **Phase Y** has its low side held fully on.
- **Phase Z** floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

$$
\text{ON interval } (D): \; HS_X + LS_Y \qquad\qquad \text{OFF interval } (1-D): \; LS_X + LS_Y
$$

Dead-time intervals are handled separately in §2. As a result, the per-motor conduction loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0053\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

### 1.2 Evaluation at 80 A

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 2.1\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 0.0042 = \mathbf{26.9\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

**Per-device distribution.** Each of the 6 FETs is active in a subset of sectors. The averaged load is about 26.9 / 6 ≈ **4.5 W per FET**. The LS_Y device is the hot spot during any given sector: it carries 2.1 mΩ × 6400 = 13.4 W instantaneous for 60° electrical.

**Copper loss.** The bus and phase current path (battery pad → bus plane → FET → phase pad) is about 0.4–0.6 mΩ on 2 oz copper. That adds **P_Cu ≈ 6400 × 0.5 mΩ ≈ 3.2 W**.

### 1.3 Thermal runaway check

R_ds rises with T_j, which raises P_cond, which raises T_j further. The loop converges only if:

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{\theta} = P_{cond,25} \cdot \alpha \cdot R_{\theta} < 1
$$

With P_cond,25 = 19.2 W and an effective board-to-air R_θ = 12.5 K/W (see §3), the loop gain is 19.2 × 0.0053 × 12.
