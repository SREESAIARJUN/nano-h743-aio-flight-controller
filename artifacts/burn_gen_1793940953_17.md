# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #22): Power-Stage Model, Bi-Directional DShot600 Timing, Netlist

## 0. Architecture and Assumptions

Two design issues shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In any real 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This document assumes an AT32F421K8U7 running AM32. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the four ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The switching model (§2) therefore belongs to the ESC MCU domain, and the DMA model (§4) belongs to the H743 domain.
2. **An 80 A per-motor burst on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is thermally marginal.** The adiabatic analysis in §3 shows about 2–3 s of headroom. Treat "80 A" as a ≤1–2 s transient rating, not a sustained one, and make sure the marketing claim matches what the hardware can survive.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Complementary PWM)

During any 60° commutation sector, two legs carry the phase current:

- **The PWM'd leg** conducts through the high-side (HS) switch for duty D and the low-side (LS) switch for 1−D. Because the drive is complementary (synchronous rectification), current always flows through exactly one FET in this leg.
- **The return leg** conducts through its LS FET continuously.

So exactly **two FETs conduct at all times**, regardless of duty cycle.

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = 9.6\ \text{W (whichever FET is on)}
$$

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = 19.2\ \text{W}
$$

**Per-device average.** Commutation rotates the duty across the six FETs.

- Each LS FET is the return path for 1/3 of the electrical cycle. It also freewheels during its own phase's PWM sectors at fraction (1−D) of another 1/3.
- At high throttle (D → 1), the steady average approaches **~3.2 W per FET**.
- During a sub-commutation-period burst, however, a single FET sees the full 9.6 W. This is the governing case for T_j.

**Copper and solder path.** Allow about 0.5 mΩ per motor current loop (2 oz inner planes, vias, motor pads):

$$
P_{Cu,motor} = 80^2 \times 0.5\ \text{m}\Omega = 3.2\ \text{W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

These losses occur once per PWM period, in the PWM'd leg only.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched HS:**
$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.94\ \text{W}
$$
For better accuracy, use the Miller plateau times:
$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\, R_{g,total}}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}
$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**
$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**(c) Reverse recovery of the LS body diode:**
$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.036\ \text{W}
$$
This term grows sharply with di/dt, so check it against the measured Q_rr at your actual gate resistor.

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\,\text{nF}(635)(48\,\text{kHz}) = 0.046\ \text{W}
