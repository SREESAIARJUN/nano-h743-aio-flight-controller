## 0. Architectural Clarification and Assumptions

### 0.1 Partitioning

ArduPilot running on the H743 **cannot commutate the motors directly**. ArduPilot has no BLDC commutation or FOC stack for 24 complementary gate signals. The architecture is therefore:

```
[ STM32H743 — ArduPilot ] --(4× bi-dir DShot600)--> [ 4× ESC MCU (AM32 or BLHeli_32) ] --> [ 4× 3-phase gate driver ] --> [ 24 MOSFETs ]
```

Each ESC channel uses an STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32 with a 48 kHz complementary PWM. "Single-PCB integration" means a stacked or combined board, not one MCU.

### 0.3 Feasibility Warning

The results below show that 80 A burst on a 20×20 or 30×30 nano footprint is a **seconds-scale, adiabatically limited rating**, not a sustainable one. Four channels at 80 A also implies roughly 300 A or more of battery current, which no nano connector or pad can carry. The math in Section 3 quantifies this so the spec can be negotiated rather than discovered at bring-up.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Six-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In any 60° sector, phase A is PWM'd (high-side HS_A on for D, synchronous low-side LS_A on for 1−D) and phase B's low-side LS_B conducts continuously:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}
$$

This is independent of duty cycle, which is a key result: synchronous rectification makes conduction loss throttle-invariant at a given current.

Including the hot resistance and PCB copper:

$$
R_{hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds(on),25} = 1.6 \times 1.0 = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2 I^2 R_{hot} + I^2 R_{cu} = 2(6400)(1.6\times10^{-3}) + 6400(0.3\times10^{-3})
$$

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 20.48 + 1.92 = \mathbf{22.4\ W\ per\ channel}
$$

### 1.2 Per-Device Distribution

Each phase conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Per sector, the worst-case device is the continuously-on low side:

$$
P_{LS,peak} = I^2 R_{hot} = 10.24\ \text{W (instantaneous, during its 120° window)}
$$

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R_{hot}}{6} = 3.41\ \text{W per FET (time-averaged over a revolution)}
$$

At low eRPM during a punch-out, the instantaneous value governs junction temperature, because the commutation period can exceed τ_jc (about 1–5 ms).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd high side hard-switches. The synchronous low side turns on and off after its body diode conducts, so it switches at near-zero voltage.

### 2.1 Gate Current and Transition Times

$$
I_{g,on} = \frac{V_{drv} - V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{10 - 4.5}{3.2} = 1.72\ \text{A}
\qquad
I_{g,off} = \frac{V_{pl}}{R_g} = \frac{4.5}{3.2} = 1.41\ \text{A}
$$

$$
t_{on} = \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}} = \frac{17\ \text{nC}}{1.72} = 9.9\ \text{ns}
\qquad
t_{off} = \frac{17\ \text{nC}}{1.41} = 12.1\ \text{ns}
$$

Your gate driver must actually source and sink these currents. An FD6288Q-class driver is weaker, around 1.0 A / 1.5 A, so t_on stretches to about 17 ns. Recompute with the driver's actual limit.
