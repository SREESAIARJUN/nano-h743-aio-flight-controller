## 0. Executive Summary

The headline result: **80 A per motor in a nano form factor is a short burst rating, not a sustained one.** The model below supports roughly:

| Rating | Value | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous | **~22–25 A per motor** | 40 °C ambient, prop-wash convection |
| Burst | **80 A per motor for ≈1.0 s** | All 4 motors simultaneously, starting from a 40 °C board |
| Burst | **80 A per motor for ≈3–4 s** | One motor at a time (yaw punch, single-motor saturation) |

**Recommendation:** Publish the spec as "25 A continuous / 80 A 1 s burst". Do not publish it as "80 A". If marketing needs a higher continuous figure, the board must grow to 30×30 mm or gain a heatsink. The derivation follows.

**System partitioning:**
- ArduPilot does not support driving motors directly from the H743's advanced-control timers as a field-oriented or six-step ESC.
- The architecture is therefore H743 FC → 4 bi-directional DShot600 lines → 4 independent ESC MCUs.
- The ESC MCUs are AT32F421-class running AM32, or BLHeli_32-class.
- Each ESC MCU drives a 3-phase gate driver and 6 N-channel MOSFETs at 48 kHz.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology-dependent current paths

The ESC uses six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as AM32 and BLHeli_32 do by default. In each 60° sector:

- **Phase A high-side (Q_AH):** conducts for duty D.
- **Phase A low-side (Q_AL):** conducts for 1 − D, carrying the synchronous freewheel current.
- **Phase B low-side (Q_BL):** conducts for 100% of the sector.

Total conduction loss per motor:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of duty cycle, which is a useful property of synchronous six-step.
