# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #42): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Budget, Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

## 0. Scope, Architecture Corrections, and Assumptions

### Architecture corrections

Two points in the brief need correcting before the math is meaningful.

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the power stage.** In any 4-in-1 design that runs bidirectional DShot, each ESC channel (or a pair) has its own small MCU. That MCU runs AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and executes the 48 kHz PWM and commutation. Typical parts are the AT32F421 and STM32G071. The H743 only:
   - generates the DShot600 frames,
   - decodes the eRPM telemetry.

   The switching-loss model (§2) therefore belongs to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA model (§4) belongs to the H743 domain.

2. **"Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict.** On a 20×20 mm board, 80 A per channel is not physically credible beyond about 1 s (see §3.4). I model a **30.5×30.5 mm board** as the baseline and give the 20×20 result as a sensitivity case.

I don't have access to a Job #42 design file. Every parameter below is a **stated assumption**. Replace them with your datasheet values; the equations are structured to accept that substitution directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

**Commutation scheme.** The scheme is six-step trapezoidal with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, two phases carry I_ph:

- **PWM phase:** high side for duty D, low side (synchronous rectification) for 1 − D.
- **Return phase:** low side fully on.

So the current always flows through exactly **two MOSFET channels in series**, ignoring dead time.

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right],\qquad \alpha=\frac{k_T-1}{100}=0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{ds}=1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Per-ESC conduction loss

$$P_{cond,ESC}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)\,\left(1-2t_{dt}f_{sw}\right)$$

The dead-time factor is 1 − 2·50 ns·48 kHz = 0.9952, which is negligible.

$$P_{cond,ESC}=2\cdot 80^2\cdot 1.5\times10^{-3}\cdot0.995 \approx \mathbf{19.1\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window, the current splits between the high side and low side according to duty. Averaged over one electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET}\approx\frac{P_{cond,ESC}}{6}\approx \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The per-device split is high-side ∝ D and low-side ∝ (2 − D)/2. This becomes asymmetric at low duty, where low-side FETs run hotter.

### 1.4 Electro-thermal coupling

Because R_ds depends on T_j, the loss and temperature must be solved together:

$$T_j = T_c + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}(T_j)$$

$$\Rightarrow\; T_j=\frac{T_c+R_{\theta JC}\,P_0\,(1-25\alpha)}{1-R_{\theta JC}\,P_0\,\alpha}$$

Here P_0 = I²·R_ds,25·(share). The solution is stable only if:

$$R_{\theta JC}\,P_0\,\alpha \ll 1$$

At these numbers the product is about 0.016, so the junction is not at risk of runaway. **The board is the bottleneck** (§3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-phase leg hard-switches. The return-phase low side is static between commutations.

| Loss term | Equation | Value per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I_ph·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 kHz = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V_bus·Q_rr·f_sw | 25.2·40 nC·48 kHz = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V_bus²·f_sw (×2 FETs in the leg) | 2·0.023 = **0.046 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I_ph·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·100 ns·48 kHz = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g, not the FET) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw ×2 | 2·0.024 = **0.048 W** |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.39 W** |
