## 0. Architecture Corrections (Read First)

Three points in the brief need to be resolved before any math is useful.

1. **ArduPilot does not run the ESC.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry back. Commutation at 48 kHz is done by four dedicated ESC MCUs. This design uses the AT32F421 running AM32, which supports bidirectional DShot, EDT, and PWM frequencies up to 48 kHz.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A per motor are physically incompatible.** On a 20×20 mm board, the copper cross-section and FET footprint cannot carry 4×80 A. Section 3 proves this. The baseline below therefore uses **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper on all layers**. A 20×20 "nano" variant should be rated at about 35–45 A burst.
3. **"80 A burst" must carry a duration.** The thermal model below is transient. The rating is defined as **80 A per motor for ≤ 3 s, starting from 40 °C board temperature, with propwash airflow.**

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step operation with synchronous rectification, two phases are active at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D, and the low-side FET conducts for 1−D. Between them, one device always carries I.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the time.

At any instant, therefore, exactly **two FET channels carry the phase current**, independent of duty cycle. Dead time is the only exception and is handled in §2.4.

### 2.2 Temperature-Dependent On-Resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0065\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 0.9 mΩ × 1.55 = **1.40 mΩ**.

### 2.3 Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.40\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{17.9\ W}$$

Add copper path loss. The power plane, phase pours, and motor pad together are about 0.35 mΩ per path:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{path} = 6400 \times 0.35\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{2.24\ W}$$

The Rds temperature coefficient creates positive feedback. The fixed-point condition, which defines the thermal runaway boundary, is:

$$T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\cdot 2I^2R_{DS,25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \;\Rightarrow\; T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th}\,k\,(1-25\alpha)}{1 - R_{th}\,k\,\alpha},\quad k = 2I^2R_{DS,25}$$

The system is stable only if R_th·k·α < 1. Section 3 checks this.

### 2.4 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs only in the PWM leg, with one commutation pair per period.

**V–I overlap:**
$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.05\ W}$$

**Body-diode reverse recovery:**
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (t_dt = 200 ns, two edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This term grows linearly with f_sw and dead time. At 48 kHz it exceeds the overlap loss. Tune AM32 dead time down to the minimum that is safe for the chosen FET and driver; 100–150 ns is typical.

**Gate drive** (6 FETs, dissipated mainly in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 6\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 6 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.17\ W}$$

### 2.5 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst, T_j = 110 °C

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 17.9 W | 71.6 W |
| Copper | 2.24 W | 9.0 W |
| Overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.11 W | 0.4 W |
| Gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (§2.6) | 0.4 W | 1.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 23.0 W** | **≈ 92 W** |

Conduction dominates at about 78%. Switching losses scale with f_sw: dropping from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.2 W per motor, so 48 kHz is affordable. Its benefits are lower audible noise and lower current ripple.
