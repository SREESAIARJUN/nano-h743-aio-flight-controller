# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Two Upfront Flags

I don't have the Job #19 requirements document, so this model rests on the assumptions below. Every equation is parameterized, so you can substitute real datasheet values.

**Flag 1: Architecture.** Bi-directional DShot is a protocol between two processors. Here the STM32H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot, and each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32-class firmware). The H743 does not commutate motors. The rest of this document assumes that split.

**Flag 2: Feasibility.** 80 A per channel on a "nano" footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is only achievable as a short burst, measured in seconds or less. Section 3 quantifies this. Treat 80 A as a transient rating, never a continuous one.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.0 × 1.75 = **1.75 mΩ**. I use 1.6 mΩ below as the realistic operating point (T_j ≈ 105 °C) and 1.75 mΩ as the bound.

### 1.2 Bridge current paths

In 120° six-step commutation, two phases conduct at any instant:

- **PWM leg:** The high-side FET conducts for duty D. Its complementary low-side FET conducts for (1−D) as a synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** The low-side FET is held on for the full 60° sector.

At every instant the motor current therefore flows through exactly two channels in series. The duty split only redistributes loss between FETs; it does not change the total.

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

Bound at 125 °C: 22.4 W.

**Per-FET average.** Each FET carries current for one-third of the electrical cycle. The worst-case FET sits on the PWM leg at high D, where it spends the most time hard-switching and conducting.

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} \approx \frac{20.5}{6} = 3.4\ \text{W}$$

The worst-case FET carries about 4.5 W including its switching share.

### 1.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period on the switching leg.

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect

Assume about 0.25 mΩ for the battery pad to bridge to phase pad path with 2 oz inner planes, plus 0.25 mΩ for the shunt (if a single low-side shunt is used per channel).

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt}) = 6400 \times 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

This term is frequently forgotten, and at 80 A it is about 15% of the FET conduction loss. A 0.25 mΩ shunt alone dissipates 1.6 W, so consider phase-current sensing via R_DS(on) or a 0.1 mΩ shunt with a high-gain amplifier.
