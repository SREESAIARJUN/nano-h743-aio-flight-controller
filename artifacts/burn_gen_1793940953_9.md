## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Architectural Correction

**The H743 does not commutate the motors.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot. Each motor channel needs a dedicated ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 or BLHeli_32, plus a 3-phase gate driver. DShot is the link between the two domains:

```
┌──────────────────────────┐   DShot600 bidir (4 lines)   ┌────────────────────────────┐
│ STM32H743 (ArduPilot)    │──────────────────────────────│ 4× ESC MCU (AM32)          │
│ IMU, baro, OSD, UARTs    │   eRPM telemetry (GCR)       │ 4× 3-ph driver, 24 MOSFETs │
└──────────────────────────┘                              └────────────────────────────┘
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Current path

In 120° trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at all times:

- **During t_on:** high-side of phase A, then the winding, then low-side of phase B.
- **During t_off:** low-side of A (synchronous freewheel), then the winding, then low-side of B.

Current therefore always flows through exactly two FETs, independent of duty D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Per-device split over one electrical cycle

Each leg conducts for 2/3 of the cycle:

- High-side FET: $P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R$
- Low-side FET: $P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R$

The low-side FETs run hotter at low throttle. Size the copper pour accordingly.

### 1.4 Numbers at 80 A

Self-consistent iteration, starting from T_j = 100 °C:

$$R_{DS}(100) = 1.2(1+0.005\cdot75) = 1.65\ \text{m}\Omega$$
$$P_{cond} = 2\cdot 6400\cdot 1.65\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.1\ W\ per\ motor}$$

| T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_cond / motor (W) | 4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 1.20 | 15.4 | 61.4 |
| 100 | 1.65 | 21.1 | 84.5 |
| 125 | 1.80 | 23.0 | 92.2 |

**Add parasitic copper.** Phase pads, vias and the bus add roughly 0.3 to 0.5 mΩ per path on a 20×20 board. With 0.4 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 \cdot 0.4\text{ m}\Omega \approx 2.6\ \text{W per motor}$$

This often rivals one FET's loss. Use 2 oz inner layers, stitched with via arrays (≥ 20 × 0.3 mm vias per phase).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In trapezoidal drive only one leg is PWM-switched at any instant, so switching loss is counted once per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot30\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

A more rigorous form uses gate-charge timing:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{g,on}},\quad I_{g,on}=\frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{g,ext}+R_{g,int}+R_{drv,on}}$$

### 2.2 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{DT} = V_{SD}\, I_{ph}\cdot 2t_{dead}\cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery, output charge and gate drive

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$
$$P_{gate} = 2\,Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

P_gate is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET.

### 2.4 Total switching loss

$$P_{sw,motor} \approx 2.18\ \text{W} \quad\Rightarrow\quad 8.7\ \text{W for 4 motors}$$

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales as f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.1 W per motor. At 96 kHz it rises to about 4.4 W per motor.

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between current ripple and loss. Check current ripple at minimum motor inductance (about 10 µH for low-kV 2207 motors):

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} \le \frac{25.2\cdot0.25}{10\mu\cdot48\text{k}} = 13\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

### 2.5 Bus capacitor ripple (often forgotten)

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I_{ph}\sqrt{D(1-D)} \le 40\ \text{A per motor at } D=0.5$$
