## 0. Architectural Clarifications and Design Risks

These points change the analysis, so they come first.

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 sends DShot600 frames and receives eRPM telemetry. One H743 cannot run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops at 48 kHz and also host ArduPilot. This document uses the standard split.
2. **"Nano" and 80A conflict thermally.** A 20×20 mm stack has about 6.5 cm² of board area. Section 3 shows that 80A is a **burst** rating of about 2–5 s for one motor, not a continuous rating. The continuous capability is roughly **25–30 A per motor** at realistic airflow.
3. **Assumed component parameters are flagged with ⚠.** Replace them with datasheet values for the parts you actually select before releasing the design.
4. **The netlist is a reference topology, not a sign-off netlist.** Treat it as input to schematic capture, ERC, and layout review.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Derivation

In 6-step commutation, two phases are active at any instant: one high-side (HS) and one low-side (LS).

- **PWM on:** current flows through HS_x and LS_y.
- **PWM off (synchronous rectification):** current freewheels through LS_x and LS_y.

In both states, exactly two MOSFETs carry the full phase current I. So, ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) \quad \text{(independent of duty cycle)}$$

The temperature dependence is:

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow \times1.6 \text{ at } 125\,^\circ\text{C}$$

Add the PCB copper path resistance, R_pcb. For 2 oz copper, about 10 mm of trace, and via arrays, estimate about **0.3 mΩ per conduction path**:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{pcb}\right)$$

### 1.2 Results at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | 2·I²·R_DS | I²·R_pcb | **P_cond per channel** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 1.9 W | **14.7 W** |
| 125 °C | 1.6 mΩ | 20.5 W | 1.9 W | **22.4 W** |

**Electro-thermal positive feedback:** loss raises T_j, which raises R_DS(on), which raises loss. To find the steady state, solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(T_j)\cdot R_{\theta,jb}$$

With R_θ,jb ≈ 1.5 K/W (junction to board, including solder and vias), each FET dissipates about 10.2 W at 125 °C. That gives a junction-to-board rise of about **15 K**. The loop converges as long as the board temperature stays below about 120 °C.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the **high-side FET of the PWM-ing phase hard-switches**. The synchronous LS FET turns on after its body diode is already conducting, so it switches at zero voltage.

### 2.1 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap)

The transition time is set by the switching charge and the gate drive current:

$$t_{sw} = \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{I_{G}} = \frac{20\text{ nC}}{1\text{ A}} = 20\text{ ns (each edge)}$$

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(40\times10^{-9})(48\times10^3) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

Q_rr also increases HS turn-on current. Budget for it in the gate-resistor selection in Section 5.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,C_{oss}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 0.5(2\text{ nF})(635)(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{ ns}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$
