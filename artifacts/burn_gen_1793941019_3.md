# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Flags

Four points shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot600 to the ESC. The ESC has its own commutation MCU(s), typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, or an EFM8 running Bluejay. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC firmware. The bidirectional DShot link is the interface between the two halves.
2. **The 80 A burst is assumed to be per motor channel, at 6S** (25.2 V maximum, 22.2 V nominal), with a burst of 1–10 s.
3. **Form-factor warning.** Section 3 shows that 80 A per channel on a 20×20 mm "nano" board is not thermally survivable beyond about 1–2 s without a heatsink. Commercial "80 A" 4-in-1 ESCs are 30.5×30.5 mm or larger, carry heatsinks, and have optimistic ratings. The math is given so you can choose a defensible rating.
4. **Component values are representative datasheet-class numbers**, not a specific vendor's guaranteed values. Substitute your selected MOSFET's actual R_DS(on), Q_g, Q_rr and R_θ values.

## 2. Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction losses (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous/complementary PWM)

At any instant one high-side and one low-side device carry the phase current in series. During PWM off-time, the complementary FET of the switching leg freewheels the current, so it is still two R_DS(on) in the path. That gives:

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

**Temperature-dependent R_DS(on):**

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

**At T_j = 125 °C (design point):**

$$R = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.6 = 1.92\,\text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = 6400 \times 3.84\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{24.6\ W/channel}$$

**Self-consistent solution.** P_cond depends on T_j, which depends on P_cond. The iteration is:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{ref} + R_\theta \cdot I^2\, 2R_{25}\left[1+\alpha(T_j^{(n)}-25)\right]$$

This converges only if:

$$R_\theta \cdot I^2 \cdot 2R_{25}\,\alpha < 1$$

At 80 A that requires R_θ < 1/(6400 × 2.4 mΩ × 0.006) ≈ **10.9 °C/W**. Above this value, steady state runs away thermally. A nano board without a heatsink sits near or above it.

### 2.2 Switching losses (hard-switched leg)

Only one leg PWMs at a time in 6-step operation.

**Turn-on and turn-off overlap:**

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I \cdot 2t_{dead} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

Note that recovery also adds to turn-on current overshoot (I_rr). Budget about 10 % extra on P_sw.

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx Q_{oss} V f_{sw}/2 \approx 0.021\ W$$

**Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET), two FETs per switching leg:**

$$P_g = 2 \cdot Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 2.3 Per-channel and board totals at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss | W/channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 24.6 | 98.3 |
| Switching (+10 % I_rr) | 2.13 | 8.5 |
| Dead-time | 0.61 | 2.4 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.12 | 0.5 |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, I_rms ≈ I_bus) | ~1.6 | — (one shared bus shunt ≈ 1.6 W total at 80 A bus) |
| PCB copper (2 oz, ≈0.3 mΩ path) | ~1.9 | 7.7 |
| **Total** | **≈ 29.3 W** | **≈ 119 W** |

**Key insight: conduction dominates at about 84 %.** Increasing f_sw from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W/channel. Lowering R_DS(on) is the only lever that matters at 80 A.

### 2.4 Frequency sensitivity

$$P_{freq}(f) = \left[\tfrac12 VI(t_r+t_f) + 2V_{SD}It_{dead} + Q_{rr}V + \ldots\right] f \approx 55\ \mu\text{J} \times f$$

| f_sw | Frequency-dependent loss |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.3 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.6 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.3 W |

At 48 kHz the f_sw choice is justified by lower current ripple and less audible noise.
