# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture Model (Job #3)

## 0. Assumptions and Design Flags

I don't have the Job #3 specification, so this model is built on the assumptions below. Please correct any that differ, because every number downstream depends on them.

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) |
| Board | 25.5 × 25.5 mm (nano AIO), 8-layer, 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner power planes |
| MOSFET | 40 V, R_DS(on) = 1.0 mΩ at 25 °C (V_GS = 10 V), PDFN 3.3×3.3, Q_g ≈ 50 nC |
| ESC MCU | AT32F421 per motor (or 1× multi-channel), running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware |
| Commutation | Trapezoidal, synchronous rectification, complementary PWM at 48 kHz |
| Burst current | 80 A per motor, 2 s burst; continuous rating to be derived |

**Three architectural flags before the math:**

1. **The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** The 48 kHz PWM, dead-time, and commutation run on the ESC MCU(s). The H743 only sends DShot and receives eRPM telemetry. The two domains are modeled separately below.
2. **80 A per motor on a 25 mm board is a 320 A aggregate burst.** As Section 2 shows, this is thermally feasible only as a short burst with heavy prop-wash cooling. It is not feasible as a continuous rating. A continuous rating of about 35–45 A per motor is the realistic target.
3. **The DShot DMA details are ArduPilot-version dependent.** The BIDIR pairing convention in Section 3 matches current ArduPilot hwdef practice as I understand it. Verify it against `libraries/AP_HAL_ChibiOS/hwdef/scripts/` and existing H743 hwdefs (e.g., MatekH743) for your target branch.

## 1. Power-Stage Loss Model (per motor, per 3-phase bridge)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{/°C}$$

At a design junction temperature of T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS}(125) = 1.0 \times (1 + 0.5) = 1.5\ \text{mΩ}$$

### 1.2 Conduction losses

In trapezoidal six-step drive, two phases conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, each active leg always has exactly one FET carrying the phase current: the high-side during the on-time, and the low-side (complementary) during the off-time. With a flat-top current, I_rms ≈ I.

**Total conduction loss per bridge:**

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET average:** each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical period.

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{19.2}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

During its 120° conduction window, a FET instantaneously dissipates about 9.6 W. This matters for the junction-ripple check in Section 2.4.

### 1.3 Switching losses at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched per PWM edge pair. The complementary low-side commutates at near-zero V_DS (body diode clamp), so it soft-switches.

**(a) V–I overlap loss**, with t_r + t_f ≈ 40 ns at about 2 A gate drive:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery**, with Q_rr ≈ 50 nC:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\, V_{bus}\, f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance**, with C_oss,eff ≈ 1.5 nF:

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2}\, C_{oss}\, V_{bus}^2\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{n} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, with t_dt = 100 ns per edge and V_f = 0.8 V:

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2\, t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive**, two FETs actively switched at V_GS = 10 V. This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FET:

$$P_{g} = 2\, Q_g\, V_{GS}\, f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 Loss summary at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Loss component | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.20 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching overlap | 1.94 W | 7.76 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.44 W |
| Q_rr + C_oss | 0.08 W | 0.33 W |
| Gate drive | 0.05 W | 0.19 W |
| **FET stage total** | **21.9 W** | **87.5 W** |
| Phase/bus copper (Section 1.5) | — | ~10–15 W |
| Shunt (Section 1.6) | — | ~2 W |
| **Board total** | | **≈ 100 W** |

**Key insight:** conduction dominates at 88% of FET loss. Raising f_sw from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs only about 1.3 W per motor. That is a good trade for motor current ripple and acoustic noise. Lowering R_DS(on) is the lever that matters most.

### 1.5 PCB copper loss

Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper (70 µm) at 100 °C:

$$R_\square = \frac{\rho}{t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\,(1+0.0039\times75)}{70\times10^{-6}} \approx 0.32\ \text{mΩ}/\square$$

Each phase path is about 3 squares when paralleled across 2 layers (≈ 0.5 mΩ). At 80 A:

$$I^2 R = 6400 \times 0.0005 = 3.2\ \text{W per motor}$$
