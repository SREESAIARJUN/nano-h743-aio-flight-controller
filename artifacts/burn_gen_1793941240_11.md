# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #10): Electrothermal Model, Switching Losses, and Bidirectional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Correction and Scope

**Correction:** the STM32H743 does not run the motor commutation. In every practical 4-in-1 design, the H743 is the **flight controller**. Each motor channel has its own small ESC MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32, or an EFM8 running BLHeli_S/Bluejay. A single H743 driving four 3-phase bridges with sensorless BEMF commutation is not supported by ArduPilot, and it would compete with the attitude loop for the same timers.

The architecture assumed here is:

```
6S LiPo ── 4-in-1 ESC board (4 × [ESC MCU + 3-ph driver + 6 MOSFETs + shunt])
              ▲  bidirectional DShot600 (×4), ESC telemetry/current/VBAT
              │
           H743 FC (ArduPilot) — stacked or AIO
```

**Feasibility warning:** "Nano" (20×20 mm) with **80 A burst per motor** is thermally marginal. The analysis in §3 shows it is viable for roughly 2–4 s bursts only, not sustained. This is the most important engineering conclusion in this document.

**Assumed operating point** (replace with your datasheet values):

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage (6S full) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current per motor | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation

In 6-step operation with synchronous (complementary) PWM:

- At any instant two phases conduct, so the current I flows through one high-side and one low-side path.
- The PWM'd leg alternates HS (duty D) and LS (1 − D) for that leg's position. Either way, one FET in that leg carries I, with the dead-time diode exception covered in §2.
- The other active leg's FET is held fully on.

This gives two FETs in conduction at all times, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Each FET conducts about 1/3 of the electrical cycle (120°). Per-FET average loss is therefore (2/6) of the bridge total, assuming equal sharing among 6 FETs.

### 1.2 Electrothermal self-consistency (runaway criterion)

The junction temperature depends on the loss, and the loss depends on the junction temperature:

$$T_j = T_a + P\cdot R_{th}, \qquad P = k\,I^2 R_0[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

Solving the two together gives a closed form:

$$\boxed{P = \frac{k I^2 R_0\,[1+\alpha(T_a-25)]}{1 - k I^2 R_0\,\alpha\,R_{th}}}$$

**Stability condition:** kI²R₀αR_th < 1. If this term approaches 1, the device is in thermal runaway.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

- At an assumed hot T_j ≈ 110 °C: R_DS = 0.90 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.32 mΩ**.
- Per FET (while conducting): 80² × 1.32 m = **8.45 W**.
- Per motor bridge: 2 × 8.45 = **16.9 W**.
- Shunt: 80² × 0.25 m = **1.6 W**.
- PCB copper, 2 oz, about 0.3 mΩ effective path: **≈1.9 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

There is one hard-switched commutation pair per PWM period (the PWM'd leg).

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30n·48k | **1.45 W** |
| C_oss | Q_oss·V·f_sw = 60n·25.2·48k | 0.073 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 50n·25.2·48k | 0.060 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·400n·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Gate drive (driver IC, 2 FETs/period) | 2·Q_g·V_GS·f_sw = 2·55n·10·48k | 0.053 W |
| **Total switching** | | **≈2.87 W/motor** |

Design levers:

- **Dead time is nearly as costly as the overlap loss.** Reducing it to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin with the driver's propagation delay mismatch first.
- At 24 kHz, switching loss halves to about 1.4 W, but current ripple rises. The 48 kHz choice is mainly for low-inductance (low-kV, large-stator) motors and quieter operation.
- Overlap loss scales with V_bus·I. At 8S (33.6 V) it becomes about 1.9 W.

### Per-motor and board totals (80 A burst, T_j ≈ 110 °C)

| | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 16.9 W | 67.6 W |
| Switching | 2.9 W | 11.5 W |
| Shunt + copper | 3.5 W | 14.0 W |
| **Total** | **23.3 W** | **≈93 W** |

Efficiency check: output is 25.2 V × 80 A × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW of input, so losses are 1.2%. The efficiency looks fine, but **dissipating 93 W on a 20×20 mm board is the actual problem.**

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Thermal network (per FET)

```
Tj ──[Rθjc ≈ 1.2 K/W]── Tcase ──[Rθc-pcb ≈ 0.8 K/W (thermal vias)]── T_pcb
                                                                    │
                                     C_board (lumped) ──┴──[Rθpcb-air]── T_air
```

Board-to-air resistance:

- Still air, 20×20 board: about 25 K/W.
- In prop wash (≈10 m/s): about 6 K/W.

### 3.2 Lumped heat capacity

Assume a 20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner board, with 24 FETs, solder, and caps:

- Copper ≈ 2.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 1.0 J/K
- FR4 ≈ 1.6 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 1.8 J/K
- FETs, caps, solder ≈ 2.0 J/K

**Total C ≈ 4.8 J/K.** Adding an aluminium heat-spreader clip of about 6 g would contribute roughly +5.4 J/K.

### 3.3 Burst response

The board time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 6 × 4.8 ≈ 29 s. Because τ is long compared with a burst, the burst is essentially adiabatic:

$$\Delta T_{board}(t) \approx \frac{P\,t}{C}$$

Junction temperature then follows:

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}(R_{\theta jc}+R_{\theta c\text{-}pcb})$$

Inputs:

- Per-FET average loss: 23.3 W / 6 ≈ 3.9 W, with a peak of 8.45 W during its conduction interval.
- Junction rise above board: 3.9 × 2.0 ≈ **+7.8 K** (thermal ripple at electrical frequency is small).
