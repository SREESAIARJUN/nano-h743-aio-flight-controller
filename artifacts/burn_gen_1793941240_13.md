# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Design Model and Feasibility Review

**Status:** Engineering design model, revision A. The numbers below rest on stated assumptions. Validate them against the datasheets of the components you select and against thermal-camera measurements before release.

---

## 0. Executive Summary

Two findings drive the rest of the design.

1. **An 80 A burst per channel is only feasible as a short transient on a nano-format board.**
   - Each motor dissipates about 31 W at 80 A, so the board totals about 123 W.
   - On a 30×30 mm 4-layer, 2 oz board this is limited to roughly **1.0–1.5 s bursts** before junctions reach 150 °C.
   - On a true 20×20 board, the safe burst is under 1 s.
   - **Recommendation:** rate the board at 45 A continuous and 80 A for ≤1 s, enforced in firmware (current limit and thermal derate). Do not market 80A as a continuous rating.

2. **The battery current is unrealistic at full load.** At 4×80 A and D≈1, the battery delivers about 320 A. This exceeds XT60 (60 A continuous) and XT90 limits, and pack C-ratings on most builds. A real-world peak is typically **≤200 A total**. The power entry needs short 10 AWG pigtails and soldered pads, not a connector-on-board design.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current Path in 6-Step Commutation with Synchronous Rectification

In each commutation sector, current flows through two series switches at all times:

- **Source phase:** the high-side FET conducts for fraction D. The complementary low-side FET (synchronous rectifier) conducts for fraction 1−D.
- **Sink phase:** the low-side FET conducts 100% of the sector.

The instantaneous conduction resistance is therefore independent of D:

$$R_{path}(T_j) = 2\,R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{25}[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

For trapezoidal drive with low current ripple, I_rms ≈ I. Ripple adds a correction factor:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2\left(1+\tfrac{1}{12}\left(\tfrac{\Delta I}{I}\right)^2\right)$$

With ΔI/I ≈ 0.2, the correction is about 0.3% and can be neglected.

### 2.2 Electrothermal Fixed Point

Because R_DS(on) depends on T_j and T_j depends on loss, the loss is found by iteration:

$$T_j^{(n+1)} = T_a + P_{FET}(T_j^{(n)})\cdot Z_{\theta}(t)$$

At steady state, a closed form exists when α·P₀·R_θ < 1:

$$P = \frac{P_0}{1-\alpha P_0 R_\theta},\qquad P_0 = \text{loss evaluated at }25^\circ\text{C}$$

**If α·P₀·R_θ ≥ 1, thermal runaway occurs.** For a single FET with P₀ ≈ 3.2 W, the runaway threshold is R_θ > 57 K/W. Keep the effective R_θJA well below this.

### 2.3 Numbers at 80 A

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 80² × 3.0 mΩ = **19.2 W** |
| 100 °C | 2.12 mΩ | 80² × 4.24 mΩ = **27.2 W** |
| 125 °C | 2.33 mΩ | 80² × 4.65 mΩ = **29.8 W** |

**Per-FET average.** Each of the 6 FETs carries roughly one-third of the electrical period, so the average is P/6 ≈ **4.5–5.0 W per FET**.

**Instantaneous peak.** While a FET is conducting, its dissipation is I²·R = 80² × 2.33 mΩ ≈ **14.9 W**. This matters for Z_θJC at the commutation frequency.

### 2.4 PCB Copper Loss (Often Forgotten)

Phase and bus copper per motor is about 20 mm of 2 oz copper, 4 mm wide, with 2 layers in parallel.

- Sheet resistance of 2 oz copper is about 0.25 mΩ/□.
- 20 mm / 4 mm = 5 squares, so 1.25 mΩ per layer. Two layers in parallel give 0.62 mΩ.
- Adding vias and pads brings the total to about 0.8 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.8\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 5.1\ \text{W per motor}$$

This is comparable to the MOSFET loss. Mitigations:
- Use 3 oz copper on the outer layers.
- Stitch with vias at ≤1 mm pitch.
- Add solder-mask openings for tinning.
