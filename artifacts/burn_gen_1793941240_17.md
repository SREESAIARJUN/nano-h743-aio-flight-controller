# STM32H743 AIO + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage, Thermal, and Bi-Directional DShot Design

**Scope:** This is a design-intent package with an analytical model, a worked numeric case, timer/DMA allocation, and a reference netlist for the critical blocks. Device parameters are representative values for this class of design. **Re-run the model with the datasheet values of the parts you actually select.**

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Three points shape the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On a 4-in-1 ESC, each motor (or the whole 4-in-1) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. This document assumes an AT32F421 per motor. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and gate signals. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The switching math (§2) belongs to the ESC MCU domain. The DMA math (§4) belongs to the H743 domain.
2. **80 A × 4 on a "nano" board (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) is a seconds-scale burst rating, not a continuous one.** The model below shows roughly 1–3 s to thermal limit from a warm start. Treat it as a burst spec with telemetry-based derating, or move to a 30.5×30.5 mm layout.
3. **"80 A" must be defined.** Here it means **80 A phase current per motor**. At high duty this is about 76 A of battery current per motor, so roughly 300 A total. That level also stresses the XT60, battery leads, and the bus capacitor ripple rating.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, then board)

### 2.1 Current distribution in 6-step complementary PWM

Over one electrical cycle, each phase leg spends:
- ⅓ PWM-ing, with HS on for D and LS (sync rectifier) on for 1−D,
- ⅓ with LS hard on (the return phase),
- ⅓ floating.

This gives the conduction fraction per FET:

$$
k_{HS} = \frac{D}{3}, \qquad k_{LS} = \frac{(1-D) + 1}{3} = \frac{2-D}{3}
$$

At every instant exactly **two FETs per motor** carry I_ph.

### 2.2 Conduction losses

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond,FET} = k \cdot I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**Numeric case at T_j = 110 °C:**
- R_DS = 1.6 × (1 + 0.0045 × 85) = **2.21 mΩ**
- I² R = 6400 × 2.21 m = **14.15 W** (instantaneous, per conducting FET)
- HS FET: 0.317 × 14.15 = **4.48 W**
- LS FET: 0.350 × 14.15 = **4.95 W**
- Per motor: 2 × 14.15 = **28.3 W**

**Copper path:** 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W per motor**.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches. Losses are charged to that leg.

| Term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard-switched HS) | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| C_oss | ½ C_oss V² f_sw | **0.015 W** |
| Reverse recovery (LS body diode) | Q_rr V f_sw | **0.048 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_SD I (2 t_dead) f_sw | 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (HS + LS) | 2 Q_g V_drv f_sw | **0.038 W** (dissipated mostly in driver and R_g) |
| **P_sw, motor** | | **≈ 2.65 W** |

Sensitivity: P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it drops to about 1.3 W, at the cost of higher current ripple and acoustic and efficiency effects on low-inductance motors.

### 2.4 Totals

| | Per motor | Board (×4) |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 28.3 W | 113.2 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Switching | 2.65 W | 10.6 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 34.2 W** | **≈ 137 W** |

Power-stage efficiency at burst is P_out ≈ 4 × 25.2 × 76 ≈ 7.66 kW, so η ≈ 98.2%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is power density.

Since conduction loss dominates (about 83%), **R_DS(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw**.
