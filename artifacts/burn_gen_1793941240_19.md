# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #23): Architecture & Design Package

## 0. Feasibility Constraints

A "nano" (20×20 mm mount, ~26×26 mm board) 4-in-1 ESC carrying **80 A burst per motor** is at or beyond the physical limit of the format. The math below shows that 80 A is achievable only as a **thermally-capacitive burst of a few seconds**, not as a continuous rating. That limit governs the rest of the design.

Partitioning assumptions:
- **STM32H743** is the flight controller MCU running ArduPilot.
- Each ESC channel runs its own MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 class) with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware supporting bidirectional DShot. The H743 does not commutate motors.
- Battery is 6S (25.2 V max). PWM frequency is 48 kHz, trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM.

### 1.2 Conduction Loss

In a 120° trapezoidal sector, current I flows through one high-side (HS) path and one low-side (LS) path:
- The active leg alternates HS (duty D) and synchronous LS (1−D).
- The return leg's LS conducts continuously.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R_{HS}D + I^2 R_{LS}(1-D) + I^2 R_{LS} = 2I^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)$$

with $R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + 0.0055(T_j - 25)]$ (≈ ×1.47 at 110 °C).

At I = 80 A and T_j = 110 °C (R = 1.5 mΩ):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Per-FET instantaneous dissipation during its conduction window is up to 9.6 W. Averaged over an electrical cycle (each FET carries current for 1/3 of the period), it is ≈ 3.2 W. The electrical frequency (hundreds of Hz to kHz) is fast compared with the die thermal time constant, so the average governs T_j, plus a small ripple term.

PCB copper and shunt conduction losses must be added separately:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt})$$

For 0.3 mΩ of total phase path per motor: P_Cu = 6400 × 0.3e-3 = **1.9 W/motor**.

### 1.3 Switching Loss at f_sw = 48 kHz

**Hard-switching V-I overlap** (active HS only):

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.036\ W$$

**Reverse recovery** (sync-LS body diode during dead time):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (t_dt = 100 ns, two transitions per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD} I \cdot 2t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs, V_drv = 10 V; dissipated in driver and gate resistors):

$$P_g = 2 Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \cdot 50\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ W$$

### 1.4 Loss Budget at 80 A Burst (Hot)

| Term | Per motor | ×4 |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| V-I switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 W | 2.4 W |
| Q_oss + Q_rr + Q_g | 0.13 W | 0.5 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 22.8 W** | **≈ 91 W** |

Conclusions from the budget:
- Conduction dominates (84%). The scaling law is P ∝ I²·R_ds(on).
- Switching loss at 48 kHz is under 7% of the total.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves only ~0.8 W/motor and costs audible noise, ripple current, and throttle resolution.
- **R_ds(on) and copper are the levers, not f_sw.**

General parametric form for scripting:

$$P_{tot}(I,T_j,f) = 2I^2R_{25}[1+\beta(T_j-25)] + I^2R_{Cu} + f\,[\tfrac12 VI t_{sw} + 2V_{SD}It_{dt} + (Q_{oss}+Q_{rr})V + 2Q_gV_{drv}]$$

Because R depends on T_j, solve this self-consistently with the thermal model by iterating T_j → R → P → T_j.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Steady State (Why 80 A Cannot Be Continuous)

Treat the nano stack as a convectively cooled plate, ~26×26 mm, two-sided, in prop wash:
- h ≈ 50–80 W/m²K
- A ≈ 2 × 6.8e-4 m² = 1.35e-3 m²

$$R_{\theta,BA} = \frac{1}{hA} \approx \frac{1}{65 \cdot 1.35\times10^{-3}} \approx 11.4\ \text{K/W}$$

With an allowable board rise of 70 K (40 °C ambient to 110 °C):

$$P_{cont,max} \approx 70/11.4 \approx 6.1\ \text{W total}$$

Inverting the loss model at ~1.5 W/motor gives roughly **I_cont ≈ 18–22 A per motor**. This sits in the range quoted for typical nano 4-in-1s, which confirms the model.
