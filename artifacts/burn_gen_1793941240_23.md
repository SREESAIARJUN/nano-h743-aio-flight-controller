# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package: 80 A Burst Electro-Thermal Model, 48 kHz Switching, Bi-Directional DShot600

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

I don't have Job #2's specific BOM, stack-up, or mechanical drawing. Everything below is parameterized, and the example values are clearly marked. Substitute your datasheet numbers into the equations, and the results scale directly.

**Architectural correction.** ArduPilot runs on the H743, but it cannot commutate the motors. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU(s), typically 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs over on-board traces. The design below reflects this.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step, complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Current path

During any 60° commutation sector with high-side PWM and synchronous rectification:

- **Phase A (PWM leg):** HS_A conducts for D·T. LS_A carries the freewheel current for (1−D)·T.
- **Phase B (low leg):** LS_B conducts 100 % of the sector.
- **Phase C:** floating (used for BEMF sensing).

Instantaneous conduction loss, independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{hot}\big[D + (1-D)\big] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected resistance

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)] \;\approx\; R_{25}\cdot k(T_j), \quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{hot} = 1.0\ \text{m}\Omega \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Averaged over one electrical revolution, each leg spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the low leg, and 1/3 floating.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} D I^2 R = 0.3 \times 9.6 = 2.88\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D) I^2 R + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R = (0.033 + 0.333) \times 9.6 = 3.52\ \text{W}$$

Check: 3 × (2.88 + 3.52) = 19.2 W ✓

**Design consequence:** the low-side FETs are the hot spots. Place them closest to the thermal via fields and the bottom-side copper pour.

### 1.4 Shunt and copper losses (add to the board budget)

$$P_{shunt} = I_{bus}^2 R_{sh}, \qquad I_{bus} \approx D \cdot I = 72\ \text{A}$$

With a 0.25 mΩ shunt: **1.3 W**.

For trace and plane copper, use R_Cu = ρL/(wt) with ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m × [1 + 0.0039(T−20)]. Budget roughly 0.5 mΩ per phase path, which is about **3.2 W per channel at 80 A**. On compact AIO boards this term is frequently underestimated.
