## 0. Scope and Architectural Corrections

Three assumptions in the brief need correcting before any numbers make sense.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no on-FC BLDC/FOC motor control. The working architecture is:
   - **H743 flight controller:** generates 4× bi-directional DShot600.
   - **4-in-1 ESC:** 4 independent ESC MCUs (AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32) that run 48 kHz PWM, gate drivers, and 24 MOSFETs.

   The design below follows this split. The math applies to the ESC board, and the DMA plan applies to the H743.

2. **"80 A burst" on a nano board is physically constrained by copper and heat, not by the MOSFET rating.** Section 3 shows that a 20×20 mm stack can sustain about 20 A/channel continuously. 80 A on all four channels is survivable for about 1 second. This must be written into the spec as a time-limited rating, not hidden.

3. **All component values are representative.** Substitute exact datasheet values (R_DS(on)(T), Q_gd, Q_rr, Z_th) during part selection. Every equation is parameterized so the numbers update directly.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

During each 60° electrical sector:
- One phase's high-side switch is PWM'd at duty D, with complementary synchronous rectification in its low-side.
- Another phase's low-side switch is held fully on.

So the motor current always flows through exactly **two** R_DS(on) in series:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \left[ D\,R_{HS} + (1-D)\,R_{LS} \right] + I_{rms}^2 R_{LS,on} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

This assumes identical high-side and low-side parts. The result is independent of D, which is a useful simplification for synchronous 6-step.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]
$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.3 × 1.45 = **1.89 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Ripple contribution

The worst case is at D = 0.5:

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.6\ A
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 \approx 6404\ A^2
$$
