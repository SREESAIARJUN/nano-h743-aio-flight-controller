## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor, 6-step synchronous)

### 2.1 Conduction topology

In any 60° commutation sector:

- **PWM leg:** the high-side (HS) FET conducts for fraction D. Its complementary low-side (LS) FET conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously.
- **Floating leg:** both FETs are off.

The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

This result is **independent of D**, which is the key advantage of synchronous rectification. Each of the six FETs carries 1/6 of this on average over an electrical revolution:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Include PWM ripple current in the RMS value:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12},\qquad \Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph-ph}\,f_{sw}}$$

Typical 5-inch motor: L ≈ 15 µH phase-to-phase, D = 0.5, V = 25.2 V.

- ΔI_pp = 25.2 × 0.25 / (15 µH × 48 kHz) = **8.75 A**
- ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². I_rms ≈ 80.04 A.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]$$

Because P depends on T_j and T_j depends on P, solve by fixed-point iteration. The closed form for the junction-rise portion is:

$$T_j = T_{Cu} + R_{\theta JCu}\,P_{FET}(T_j)
\;\Rightarrow\;
T_j = \frac{T_{Cu} + R_{\theta JCu}\left[\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}(1-25\alpha) + P_{sw,FET}\right]}{1 - R_{\theta JCu}\,\tfrac{1}{3}I^2R_{25}\,\alpha}$$

The thermal runaway condition is when the denominator reaches 0:

$$\tfrac{1}{3}I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_{\theta JCu} \ge 1$$

Evaluated at 80 A: (1/3)(6400)(0.8 mΩ)(0.007)(2.5) = **0.030**. This is far from runaway at the junction level. The real limit is the copper or board temperature T_Cu.

### 2.3 Switching losses (per motor, per switching event pair)

Only one leg switches at a time in 6-step. Per motor:

**(a) V–I overlap (hard-switched HS):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}
= 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**(b) Reverse recovery (LS body diode recovered by the HS turn-on):**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**(c) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw}\times 2 = 60\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.07\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET):**

$$P_{g} = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot60\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.058\ W}$$

The total switching-related loss at 80 A is:

$$P_{sw,motor} = 1.94 + 0.06 + 0.07 + 0.61 \approx \mathbf{2.68\ W}\ (+0.06\text{ W in driver})$$

**Scaling law.** P_sw scales with V·I·f_sw, while P_cond scales with I²·R. At 48 kHz versus 24 kHz, switching loss doubles (+2.7 W per motor at 80 A). This buys lower audible noise, a lower current ripple, and smoother low-throttle response.
