## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.425 = **1.71 mΩ**.

### 1.2 RMS current including PWM ripple

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{DC}^2\left(1 + \frac{r^2}{12}\right), \quad r = \frac{\Delta I_{pp}}{I_{DC}}$$

The motor phase-to-phase inductance L_ph sets the ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf})\,D}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor (L_ph ≈ 20 µH) at 80 A and D ≈ 0.9, r stays below 0.15. The ripple term is therefore under 0.2% and is neglected below.

### 1.3 Current path in synchronous 6-step drive

At every instant, exactly two FETs carry the phase current:

- **PWM-on interval:** high side of leg A plus low side of leg B.
- **PWM-off interval:** low side of leg A (synchronous freewheel) plus low side of leg B.

Total conduction loss per motor is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS}(T_j) = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.71\text{ m}\Omega = \mathbf{21.9\ W}$$

### 1.4 How the loss splits between FETs

Each leg is active for one third of the electrical cycle.

| FET | Conduction fraction | P at D = 0.9 | P at D = 0.3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| High side (PWM leg) | D/3 | 3.28 W | 1.09 W |
| Low side (PWM leg, freewheel) | (1−D)/3 | 0.36 W | 2.55 W |
| Low side (return leg) | 1/3 | 3.65 W | 3.65 W |

Averaged per FET position over a full rotation, the low-side FETs run hotter. If the ESC firmware alternates PWM between the high and low sides ("complementary rotation"), the load balances to about 3.65 W per FET.

### 1.5 Self-consistent junction temperature

$$T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}(T_j)\, R_{th,j\text{-}pcb}$$

$$T_j = \frac{T_{pcb} + I^2 k R_0 R_{th}(1 - 25\alpha)}{1 - I^2 k R_0 \alpha R_{th}}$$

Here k is the conduction fraction. With k = 1/3, R_th,j-pcb = 1.5 K/W and T_pcb = 95 °C, the result is T_j ≈ 101 °C conduction-only. That is consistent with the 110 °C assumption once switching loss is added (§2).

The thermal-runaway stability condition is:

$$I^2 k R_0 \alpha R_{th} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 6400 \times 0.333 \times 0.0012 \times 0.005 \times 1.5 = 0.019 \ll 1$$

The design is stable.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. In synchronous mode, hard switching lands on the high-side FET, and the low side commutates softly through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

Gate-driver-limited transition times (V_plateau ≈ 3 V, R_g,total ≈ 4 Ω):

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{pl}},\quad t_f \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_g}{V_{pl}}$$

With driver current limiting, this gives about 30 ns each, so t_r + t_f ≈ 60 ns.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 60\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{2.90\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery, output capacitance, and gate charge

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse recovery | P_rr = Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Output capacitance | P_oss = Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 0.048 W |
| Gate drive (2 switching FETs) | P_g = 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.038 W (dissipated in the driver/R_g) |

### 2.3 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f \cdot I \cdot 2 t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

This loss falls on the low-side FET of the PWM leg. Reducing dead time to 100 ns saves 0.6 W per motor, but you must verify shoot-through margin against driver propagation-delay skew.

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 21.9 W | 87.6 W |
| V–I overlap | 2.90 W | 11.6 W |
