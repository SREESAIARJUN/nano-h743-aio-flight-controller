## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology
In 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary PWM, during each 60° sector:
- the high-side (HS) of phase A conducts for duty fraction D;
- the low-side (LS) of phase A conducts for (1 − D) (synchronous rectification);
- the LS of phase B conducts for 100% of the sector.

The current always flows through exactly **two** R_ds(on) in series. The total channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0055\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\text{typical 40 V trench})$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 90) ≈ **1.50 mΩ**.

### 1.3 Parasitic copper and shunt
- **Phase copper.** For 2 oz copper: ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, t = 70 µm. A path of l = 8 mm and w = 4 mm on 2 layers in parallel gives R_cu ≈ ρl/(w·t·2) ≈ **0.25 mΩ**. Current flows through two such segments, so the effective copper resistance is 0.5 mΩ.
- **Low-side shunt.** R_sh = 0.5 mΩ, shared on the ground return. It conducts whenever an LS is on, which is ≈ 100% of the time.

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2\big[2R_{ds}(T_j) + 2R_{cu} + R_{sh}\big]$$

At 80 A and T_j = 115 °C:

$$= 6400 \times (3.0 + 0.5 + 0.5)\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{25.6\ W\ per\ channel}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per sector. Its HS commutates against the LS body diode during dead-time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw, with t_r + t_f = 40 ns | ½·25.2·80·40n·48k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40n·25.2·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw, with t_dt = 100 ns | 0.8·80·200n·48k = **0.61 W** |
| C_oss charge | 2 × ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | 1.5n·635·48k = **0.05 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver IC) | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw | 2·50n·10·48k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 2.70 W** |

**Rise/fall time derivation.** The overlap term is dominated by the Miller plateau:

$$t_{r} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}\,(R_{drv}+R_g)}{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_drv + R_g = 4 Ω, V_drv = 10 V and V_pl = 3.5 V, this gives t_r ≈ 7.4 ns intrinsic. Add PCB loop-inductance (L_loop ≈ 2 nH) di/dt limiting and a 3.3 Ω series gate resistor, and you arrive at ~20 ns per edge.

**Trade-off.**
- 48 kHz vs 24 kHz doubles P_sw: +1.35 W/ch at 80 A.
- In exchange it halves current ripple, which cuts motor copper loss and audible noise.
- At 80 A, switching is only ~10% of total loss. 48 kHz is therefore justified.

### 2.1 Total per-channel and board loss (80 A burst, T_j = 115 °C)

$$P_{ch} = 25.6 + 2.7 = \mathbf{28.3\ W}, \qquad P_{board} = 4 \times 28.3 + P_{FC,BEC} (\approx 2.5\ \text{W}) \approx \mathbf{116\ W}$$

The electrical input is 4 × 80 A × ~22 V ≈ 7 kW, so ESC efficiency is ≈ 98.4%. Losses are low in percentage terms, but the power density on 6.25 cm² is extreme.
