## 0. Feasibility Verdict

Two findings drive the rest of this document.

1. **The H743 cannot drive the bridges directly.** A Bi-directional DShot ESC needs a local commutation MCU per motor, or one MCU running 4 motors in specialised firmware. This design uses **4 × AT32F421 running AM32**, receiving DShot600 from the H743. AM32 supports up to 48 kHz PWM, which is where the 48 kHz switching requirement applies.

2. **80 A per channel is a burst rating, thermally limited to about 3–5 s with all 4 channels loaded simultaneously** on a 30.5 × 30.5 mm board.
   - A true 20 × 20 mm nano cannot carry 80 A. There is no room for adequate copper or FET packages.
   - **Recommendation:** use a 30.5 mm mounting pattern ("nano-AIO" class, about 37 × 37 mm outline) with 3.3 × 3.3 mm (DFN3333) 40 V FETs, 6-layer 2 oz copper. Accept a datasheet rating of **55 A continuous / 80 A burst (≤ 5 s, single channel ≤ 10 s)**.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct at any instant:
- The **PWM leg**: the HS FET conducts for duty D and the LS FET for 1−D. Under complementary PWM, exactly one of the two always carries I.
- The **return leg**: the LS FET is held on continuously for the 60° sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore independent of D (ignoring dead time):

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,(D) + I^2 R_{DS,hot}\,(1-D) + I^2 R_{DS,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

### 2.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1} \Rightarrow R_{125} = 1.5\,R_{25} = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 2.3 Values at 80 A

| Condition | R_DS | P_cond per channel | Per-FET average (6 FETs, each conducting about 2/3 of the electrical cycle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold (T_j = 25 °C) | 1.2 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.2 m = **15.4 W** | 2.56 W |
| Hot (T_j = 125 °C) | 1.8 mΩ | 2 × 6400 × 1.8 m = **23.0 W** | 3.84 W |

### 2.4 Copper path loss (often forgotten)

The current path is: battery pad → bus plane → HS FET → phase pour → motor pad, and back through the LS FET and GND plane. On 6 layers of 2 oz copper, estimate R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ per channel path (go plus return).

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 R_{Cu} = 6400 \times 0.3\,\text{m} = 1.9\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.5 Thermal runaway check

R_DS rises with temperature, so conduction loss feeds back into T_j. The loop is stable if:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}\,R_{\theta,eff} < 1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\alpha\,R_{\theta} = 2(6400)(1.2\text{m})(0.005)R_\theta = 0.077\,R_\theta < 1$$

This requires R_θ(junction to ambient, effective per channel) < 13 K/W, which is satisfied with large margin. There is **no runaway**; the limit is purely energy storage (Section 4).

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches. Every term is evaluated at V = 25.2 V and I = 80 A.

### 3.1 V–I overlap (hard-switched HS turn-on and turn-off)

Transition time per edge:

$$t_{tr} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2} + Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{12\,\text{nC}}{1\,\text{A}} = 12\ \text{ns}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 24\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.16\ W}$$
