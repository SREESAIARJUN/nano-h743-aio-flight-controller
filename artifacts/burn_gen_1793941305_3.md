# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #51): System Design Package

## 0. Architecture Decision and Feasibility

**The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** A production 4-in-1 AIO uses two compute tiers:

| Tier | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control, emits 4× bidirectional DShot600 |
| ESC (×4) | AT32F421K8U7 or STM32G071 (QFN32) | AM32 | 6-step/FOC commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drive (×4) | FD6288Q (3-ch half-bridge, 250 V, bootstrap) | n/a | Drives 6 N-FETs per motor |
| Power stage (×4) | 6× N-MOSFET, 40 V, ~1 mΩ, PDFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 | n/a | 24 FETs total |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is set in AM32's PWM config. ArduPilot only sets the DShot protocol and motor poles.

**Physical-reality flag.** The thermal model in Section 3 shows that a 30×30 mm "nano" board cannot sustain 80 A per motor. It sustains roughly 20–25 A continuous per motor and survives an 80 A all-motor burst for about 2 s. The "80A" figure must be specified as a time-limited burst rating, with firmware enforcement (Section 3.6).

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (Per Motor Channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

In 6-step commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM at duty D, current I flows through two phase legs:
- **Switching leg:** high-side conducts for D·T, low-side synchronous-rectifies for (1−D)·T.
- **Static leg:** one low-side FET stays on for the full period.

$$P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Conduction loss is therefore approximately independent of duty cycle. This is a key result: throttle position does not reduce burst conduction loss at a given current.

### 2.2 Temperature Coupling

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

$$T_j = T_{board} + P_{FET}\,Z_{\theta,J\text{-}B}(t)$$

This is a positive-feedback loop. Solve it iteratively; it converges when $2I^2R_{ds,25}\,\alpha\,Z_\theta < 1$.

At T_j = 115 °C, R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 90) = **1.405 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.405\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.98\ W}$$

### 2.3 Copper / PCB Loss

$$P_{cu} = 2\,I^2 R_{cu} = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.25\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{3.20\ W}$$

### 2.4 Switching Loss (Switching Leg Only)

**Hard-switching V–I overlap.** In synchronous rectification only the high-side hard-switches.

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\times10^{-9} \times 48\,000 = \mathbf{1.935\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (low-side body diode, dumped into the high-side at turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The Q_rr-induced turn-on current spike also adds overlap loss. Budget ~0.1 W total for this term.

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.614\ W}$$

**Gate drive.** Two FETs in the switching leg toggle; this loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2\times50\text{n}\times10\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

### 2.5 Loss Summary at 80 A Burst, T_j = 115 °C

| Loss term | Per motor (W) | ×4 motors (W) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 17.98 | 71.9 |
| PCB copper | 3.20 | 12.8 |
| V–I switching | 1.94 | 7.7 |
| Dead-time diode | 0.61 | 2.5 |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.20 | 0.8 |
| **Total** | **≈23.9** | **≈95.7** |

Efficiency at 80 A, D ≈ 0.9: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.9 × 80 ≈ 1814 W per motor, so η ≈ 98.7%. Efficiency is fine; heat density is the problem.

### 2.6 48 kHz Trade-off

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} = \left[\tfrac12VI(t_r+t_f) + 2V_{SD}It_{dt} + (Q_{rr}+Q_{oss})V\right] \approx 54\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz costs about 1.3 W per motor at 80 A, which is ~5% of total loss. The benefits are quieter operation, lower current ripple (which reduces RMS heating in low-inductance motors), and smoother low-throttle control. **48 kHz is justified.**
