> **⚠ Feasibility flag (architect's call):** An 80 A/motor burst rating is **not physically achievable on a 20×20 mm "nano" AIO**. The board would need 24 MOSFETs plus about 90 W of burst dissipation (see §1.5). The minimum credible form factor is a **30.5×30.5 stack with a separate ESC board**: 2 oz+ inner copper, 4–6 layers, and an airflow or heatsink path. The H743 FC can stay nano-sized. The ESC cannot. The rest of this document assumes that split.
>
> Note also that a 4-in-1 ESC has its own MCUs (typically STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32/BLHeli_32). The H743 does **not** generate the 48 kHz PWM. It sends DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.

## 1. Power Stage Loss Model (per motor)

### 1.1 Conduction Loss

With 6-step complementary PWM, current always flows through exactly **two** conducting MOSFETs:
- **On-state:** HS_x + LS_y.
- **Off-state:** LS_x (synchronous rectification) + LS_y.

The motor current I is the same in both states, so:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I^2 R_{pcb}$$

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C (R = 1.5 mΩ), and R_pcb = 0.3 mΩ per current path:

$$P_{cond} = 2(80^2)(1.5\text{m}) + 80^2(0.3\text{m}) = 19.2 + 1.92 = \mathbf{21.1\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for about 1/3 of the electrical cycle. The split between high-side and low-side duty depends on the PWM duty cycle D, so the average per FET is:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{2 I^2 R}{6} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}$$

The worst-case FET is the low-side FET at low D, which approaches **2I²R/3 × (1−D/2)** of its 1/3 conduction window.

### 1.2 Switching Loss (hard-switched edge, one leg at a time)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus}\, I\,(t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw}$$

$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 20\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.97\ W}$$

### 1.3 Dead-Time / Body-Diode Loss

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reverse recovery is bounded by $P_{rr} \approx Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw}$. With Q_rr = 50 nC, this gives **0.06 W**.

### 1.4 Capacitive and Gate Losses

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 60\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.058\ \text{W (dissipated in driver/R}_g)$$

### 1.5 Total Loss

| Component | Per motor @ 80 A | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (FET) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper | 1.9 W | 7.7 W |
| Switching | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss + gate | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| **Total** | **≈23.5 W** | **≈94 W** |

Efficiency at burst: P_out ≈ 22.2 V × 80 A × D. At full throttle (D ≈ 1), the electrical input is about 1776 W per motor, so η_ESC ≈ 98.7%. This looks excellent, but **the absolute heat is concentrated in about 1 cm² per phase leg.**

**Key insight:** Conduction loss dominates at 85% of the total. Moving from 48 kHz to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W per motor. Lowering R_ds(on) by paralleling 2 FETs per switch halves the dominant term. That is the correct lever to pull at 80 A.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Steady-State Network (per hottest FET)

$$T_j = T_a + P_{FET}\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CS} + R_{\theta SA})$$

| Element | Value |
|---|---|
| R_θJC (PDFN 5×6) | 1.0 K/W |
| R_θCS (solder + vias to inner planes) | 2.0 K/W |
| R_θSA, per-FET share of board to air, still air | 35 K/W |
| R_θSA, prop-wash airflow at about 5 m/s | 12 K/W |

The worst-case FET dissipates about 4.5 W (3.2 W conduction plus its share of switching and dead-time loss). At T_a = 40 °C:
- **Still air:** T_j = 40 + 4.5 × 38 = 211 °C → **FAIL** (continuous).
- **Airflow:** T_j = 40 + 4.5 × 15 = 107.5 °C → marginal pass.

**Conclusion:** 80 A is valid **only as a time-limited burst**, so the transient model below governs the rating.

### 2.2 Transient Model (Foster, 3-pole)

$$Z_{\theta}(t) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} R_i\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

| Pole | R_i (K/W) | τ_i | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0 | 5 ms | Die + package |
| 2 | 2.0 | 0.5 s | Copper spreading |
| 3 | 12.0 | 25 s | Board + airflow |

Burst limit for T_j,max = 150 °C, starting from the 55 A continuous steady state (P ≈ 2.1 W/FET, T_j0 ≈ 72 °C):

$$\Delta T(t) = (P_{burst} - P_{cont})\, Z_\theta(t) \le 150 - 72 = 78\ \text{K}$$

The step ΔP is 2.4 W. Solving gives Z_θ(t) ≤ 32.5 K/W, which is never reached (Z_θ,max = 15 K/W). **With airflow, 80 A is sustainable.**

Without airflow, use pole 3 = 35 K/W with τ = 40 s. Solving 2.4·Z(t) ≤ 78 combined with the rising baseline gives **t_burst ≈ 8–10 s**.

**Recommendation:** Specify "80 A / 5 s burst, 55 A continuous with prop-wash, 1 oz outer / 2 oz inner, 6-layer."

### 2.3 Bus Capacitor Ripple (often the real failure point)

$$I_{C,rms} \approx I\sqrt{D(1-D)}\ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \max = 0.5 I = 40\ \text{A rms per motor (at } D = 0.5)$$

Four motors share the bus but interleave imperfectly, so design for **about 50–60 A rms combined**. Use a 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR electrolytic plus 4× 10 µF/50 V X7R per phase bridge. Check the electrolytic's ripple rating, which must be derated with temperature.
