# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage Thermal Model, DShot DMA Allocation, and Reference Netlist

**Scope assumptions** (correct these if your Job #55 spec differs):

| Parameter | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| Form factor | 20×20 mm mounting (≈26×26 mm board), 6-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner copper |
| Battery | 6S LiPo, 25.2 V max, 22.2 V nominal |
| Burst rating target | 80 A per motor channel |
| PWM frequency | 48 kHz, center-aligned, complementary with active freewheeling |
| ESC architecture | 4× AT32F421 running AM32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to the ESC MCUs. |
| MOSFET class | 40 V, PDFN3.3×3.3, R<sub>DS(on)</sub> ≈ 1.2 mΩ at 25 °C (HYG/ON/Infineon class) |

> ⚠️ **Note on the architecture:** ArduPilot does not perform BLDC commutation, so the H743 cannot directly drive 24 MOSFETs under ArduPilot. The four ESC MCUs handle commutation. The H743 handles flight control and the DShot/telemetry link.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,\big[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\big],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T<sub>j</sub> = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous commutation

In any 60° sector, current flows through one high-side and one low-side FET. During the PWM off-time, the complementary FET of the active high-side phase carries the freewheel current. So the conducting path always contains exactly two channels regardless of duty D:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{ph,RMS}^2 \cdot 2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

With I<sub>ph</sub> ≈ 80 A (flat-top trapezoidal current, RMS ≈ DC within the sector):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET distribution

Each phase leg conducts for 120° of 360°, split between its HS and LS devices according to D. Averaged over an electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\;(\text{balanced}) \;\rightarrow\; \text{worst-case LS at low } D:\ \approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{3}$$

- Balanced: ≈ 3.8 W per FET
- Worst case: ≈ 7.7 W per FET

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses (per channel)

- **Phase copper.** A 3 mm × 8 mm run in 2 oz copper gives R ≈ 0.25 mΩ per path × 2 paths. Loss = 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**.
- **Shunt.** 0.25 mΩ in the low-side return carries the battery-side current I<sub>bat</sub> = D·I<sub>ph</sub>. At full throttle (D ≈ 0.95): 76² × 0.25 mΩ = **1.4 W**.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap (high-side FET)

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12\,V_{bus}\,I_{ph}\,(t_r + t_f)\,f_{sw}$$

With t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>f</sub> = 40 ns (≈1.5 A gate drive, 4.7 Ω R<sub>g</sub>):

$$P_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 50\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac12\,Q_{oss}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} \approx 0.5 \times 40\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

Two transitions per PWM period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\,I_{ph}\cdot 2\,t_{dt}\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 2 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

Reducing dead time to 100 ns halves this term. It is the cheapest loss to reduce at 48 kHz.

### 2.5 Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET)

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \times 6 = 50\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 6 = 0.14\ \text{W per channel}$$

### 2.6 Loss budget at 80 A, 6S

| Term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 23.0 W | 92.0 W |
| Phase copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Shunt | 1.4 W | 5.6 W |
| Switching (V–I) | 1.94 W | 7.8 W |
| Dead time | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q<sub>rr</sub> + C<sub>oss</sub> + gate | 0.22 W | 0.9 W |
| **Total** | **≈31 W** | **≈124 W** |

Conduction dominates at 81% of the total. Note that raising f<sub>sw</sub> from 24 to 48 kHz costs only about 3 W per motor.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Steady-state check: why this is not a continuous rating

Effective convective area: both board sides plus FET tops, ≈ 2 × 6.8 cm² ≈ 1.4×10⁻³ m². Prop-wash convection gives h ≈ 80 W/m²K.

$$\Delta T_{ss} = \frac{P}{hA} = \frac{124}{80 \times 1.4\times10^{-3}} \approx 1100\ \text{K}$$

**80 A on four motors simultaneously is thermally impossible in steady state on a nano board.** A realistic continuous rating is found by setting ΔT ≈ 70 K:

$$P_{allow} \approx 70 \times 80 \times 1.4\times10^{-3} \approx 7.8\ \text{W total}$$

This corresponds to roughly **18–22 A continuous per motor** with all four loaded. Expect about 30 A with heatsinking or strong airflow.

### 3.2 Burst model (lumped RC, Cauer two-node)

```
P_FET ──► [Rth_jc ≈ 1.5 K/W, Cth_j ≈ 0.05 J/K] ──► board node
          [C_board ≈ 2.2 J/K] ──► [R_conv ≈ 9 K/W] ──► ambient
```

C<sub>board</sub> combines FR4 (≈1.6 g × 1.1 J/gK) and copper (≈1.5 g × 0.385 J/gK).

**Board node (adiabatic for short bursts):**

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} \approx \frac{P}{C_{board}} = \frac{124}{2.2} \approx 56\ \text{K/s}$$

**Junction offset** (worst-case FET at 7.7 W + switching ≈ 9 W):

$$\Delta T_{jb} \approx 9 \times 1.5 = 13.5\ \text{K}\quad(\tau_j = R\,C \approx 75\ \text{ms, settles quickly})$$

**Burst duration**, from 45 °C start to a T<sub>j</sub> limit of 150 °C (with a 10 K margin, so T<sub>b</sub> ≤ 126 °C):

$$t_{burst} = \frac{(126 - 45)\times 2.2}{124} \approx \mathbf{1.4\ s}$$
