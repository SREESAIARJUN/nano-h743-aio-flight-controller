**Three architectural flags before the math:**

1. **The H743 does not drive the FETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the H743 flight controller sends DShot to four (or one quad-core) ESC MCUs. These are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32, and they generate the 48 kHz complementary PWM. ArduPilot has no field-oriented or 6-step motor commutation firmware for the FC MCU. The model below therefore splits into an ESC power stage (§1–§4) and an FC DShot/DMA layer (§5–§6).
2. **An 80 A burst on a "nano" form factor is thermally marginal.** "Nano" means 20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mounting. §3 shows it is survivable only as a short transient of a few seconds, never steady state. Rate it as a "≤ 2 s burst" on the datasheet, or move to 30.5×30.5.
3. **A 4 × 80 A burst means 320 A from the battery.** That is beyond any XT60 or XT90 and beyond a nano board's power entry. A realistic spec is 80 A on one motor with a total-board limit, or about 45–55 A per motor at 4× simultaneous load.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path

In 6-step with synchronous rectification, at any instant exactly two phases conduct. During the high-side ON interval, current flows through Q_H(x) → motor → Q_L(y). During the high-side OFF interval it freewheels through Q_L(x) (sync rect) → motor → Q_L(y).

Either way the motor current always flows through **two FET channels in series**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature-corrected R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.20 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.61 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.74 mΩ |
| 150 °C | 1.88 mΩ |

### 1.3 Ripple correction

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\ \text{A}_{pp}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{dc}^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

The ripple contribution is negligible (0.06 %). 48 kHz is more than adequate for low-inductance FPV motors.

### 1.4 Result at 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

$$P_{cond} = 6403.6 \times 2 \times 1.74\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.3\ W\ per\ motor}$$

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Each FET is active for one-third of the electrical cycle:

- **High-side:** conducts for D × 1/3 of the cycle, so P_QH ≈ I²·R·D/3.
- **Low-side:** conducts for 1/3 (as the return leg) plus (1−D)/3 (sync rect), so P_QL ≈ I²·R·(2−D)/3.
