# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC Control Loop, Anti-Cogging, and Dual-IMU Sensor Fusion

## 0. Assumptions

I don't have the original specification for Job #5, so this design rests on the assumptions below. Change the constants in `config.hpp` if your hardware differs.

| Item | Assumed value |
|---|---|
| MCU | STM32G4/H7-class Cortex-M4F/M7, single-precision FPU |
| Motors | Gimbal BLDC (GBM-type), 12N14P, so pole pairs p = 7 |
| Rotor sensor | 14-bit absolute magnetic encoder (AS5048A / MT6701), SPI |
| Current sensing | 2-shunt or 3-shunt low-side, sampled at PWM center |
| PWM | 20 kHz, center-aligned |
| IMUs | 2 × ICM-42688-P on the camera plate (rigid), SPI, ODR = 8 kHz |
| Joint order | Yaw (base) → Roll → Pitch (camera) |

### Loop rates and bandwidth separation

Each loop is roughly 5–10× slower than the loop inside it.

| Loop | Rate | Target closed-loop bandwidth |
|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q) PI | 20 kHz | ~1.5–2 kHz |
| IMU filtering | 8 kHz → decimated to 4 kHz | — |
| Velocity PI(D) | 4 kHz | ~150–250 Hz |
| Angle / attitude P(I) | 1 kHz | ~20–40 Hz |
| Cogging LMS adaptation | 1 kHz | very slow (time constant ≫ 1 s) |

### Feedback architecture

Gimbals perform **inertial** stabilization, so the feedback sources are split:

- **Angle and velocity loops:** use the fused camera IMU attitude and rate, projected onto each joint axis.
- **Encoder:** used only for commutation (electrical angle), the anti-cogging lookup, and joint-space kinematics.

## 1. Field-Oriented Control (Current Loop)

### 1.1 Electrical angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{\text{offset}}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

### 1.2 Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant)

With $i_a + i_b + i_c = 0$:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \frac{1}{\sqrt3}\,(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

### 1.3 Park transform

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} =
\begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix}
$$

### 1.4 PMSM model in the dq frame

$$
v_d = R i_d + L_d \dot i_d - \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q = R i_q + L_q \dot i_q + \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The electromagnetic torque (surface PM, so $L_d = L_q$) is:

$$
\tau_e = \tfrac32\,p\,\lambda_m\,i_q = K_t\, i_q
$$

### 1.5 Current PI with decoupling and back-EMF feedforward

The current error is $e_x = i_x^* - i_x$ for $x \in \{d, q\}$. The controller output is:

$$
v_d^* = K_{p,i}\,e_d + K_{i,i}\!\int e_d\,dt \;-\; \omega_e L_q i_q
$$

$$
v_q^* = K_{p,i}\,e_q + K_{i,i}\!\int e_q\,dt \;+\; \omega_e L_d i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

**Tuning by pole-zero cancellation.** The plant is $1/(Ls + R)$. Choosing

$$
K_{p,i} = L\,\omega_{c,i},\qquad K_{i,i} = R\,\omega_{c,i}
$$

makes the closed loop first order: $\dfrac{\omega_{c,i}}{s + \omega_{c,i}}$.

The PWM transport delay is about $1.5\,T_s$. Keep $\omega_{c,i}\cdot 1.5\,T_s \lesssim 0.2$ rad to retain at least 60° of phase margin. At 20 kHz this gives $\omega_{c,i} \approx 2\pi \cdot 2\text{ kHz}$.

### 1.6 Voltage limiting and anti-windup

Limit the voltage vector to the linear SVPWM region, $|v| \le V_{dc}/\sqrt3$, giving priority to the d-axis:

$$
v_d^{\text{sat}} = \text{clamp}(v_d^*, \pm V_{\max}),\qquad
v_q^{\text{sat}} = \text{clamp}\!\left(v_q^*, \pm\sqrt{V_{\max}^2 - (v_d^{\text{sat}})^2}\right)
$$

Use back-calculation anti-windup, with $K_{aw} \approx 1/K_{p}$:

$$
\dot I_x = K_i\,e_x + K_{aw}\,(v_x^{\text{sat}} - v_x^*)
$$
