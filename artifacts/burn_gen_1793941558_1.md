# STM32H743 Nano Flight Controller and 80A-Burst 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Package (Job #36)

## 0. Architectural Corrections

Three points in the request would cause a design failure if taken literally, so they are resolved first.

| Issue | Resolution |
|---|---|
| **The STM32H743 does not run the ESC.** 4-in-1 ESCs run their own MCU firmware, typically AM32 on AT32F421 or STM32G071, or BLHeli_32. | The H743 is the **flight controller**. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to an ESC stage with 4× AT32F421 running AM32. The analysis covers both. |
| **"80A" is a burst rating, not a continuous one.** | The thermal model below computes the burst duration the copper can absorb, rather than assuming a steady-state rating. |
| **"Nano" (20×20 mm) cannot dissipate 4×80A.** | The power stage is specified as **30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz**. The FC can stack on top as a 20×20 board, or the whole design can be built as a 30×30 AIO. |

All component parameters below are **design assumptions** representative of current parts. Substitute datasheet values before release.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005·100) = **1.5 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Six-step current path

At any instant, current flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path. With synchronous rectification, the freewheel interval uses the complementary MOSFET channel instead of the body diode. The current path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Ripple correction for phase inductance L and duty D:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus}-E_{bemf})\,D}{L\,f_{sw}}, \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

For a typical 5″/7″ motor (L ≈ 15 µH, D ≈ 0.8, E_bemf ≈ 18 V):

- ΔI ≈ 8 A
- Ripple term: ΔI²/12 ≈ 5.3 A² (negligible at 80 A)

### 2.3 Results

| Case | I | R_ds | P_cond per channel | P_cond per FET (averaged over 6 FETs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst, hot | 80 A | 1.5 mΩ | **19.2 W** | 3.2 W |
| Burst, cold start | 80 A | 1.0 mΩ | 12.8 W | 2.1 W |
| Continuous, hot | 50 A | 1.5 mΩ | 7.5 W | 1.25 W |

The per-FET average assumes each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical period. At low eRPM the instantaneous per-FET loss is up to 9.6 W during its conduction window, so junction ripple matters. See §4.3.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step operation, only one leg per channel is hard-switching at any instant. Each loss term below is per channel.

**(a) V-I overlap (hard switching):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.036\ W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction (two edges per period):**

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I \cdot 2t_d \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive (both FETs of the switching leg):**

$$P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

The gate-drive term is dissipated in the driver and gate resistors, not in the FETs.

### Switching loss total

| Term | W per channel |
|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.968 |
| Coss | 0.048 |
| Qrr | 0.036 |
| Dead time | 0.307 |
| Gate | 0.048 |
| **Total** | **1.41 W** |

### Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 29.4\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- At 24 kHz: switching loss falls to about 0.7 W.
- At 96 kHz: switching loss rises to about 2.8 W.

At 48 kHz, switching is only **~7 %** of total loss at 80 A. Conduction dominates, so R_ds(on) selection is the primary lever. Increasing PWM frequency for smoother throttle response is thermally cheap.

### 3.1 Total power stage loss at 80 A burst (hot)

$$P_{ch} = 19.2 + 1.41 = 20.6\ \text{W}, \qquad P_{4ch} = \mathbf{82.4\ W}$$

Add the bus capacitor ESR loss:

- I_cap,rms ≈ I·√(D(1−D)) ≈ 32 A per active channel, partially cancelling across channels.
- With 4× 35 V low-ESR caps (ESR 15 mΩ each in parallel, R ≈ 3.75 mΩ) and an effective 45 A aggregate RMS: **≈ 7.6 W**.

The capacitor bank is the hidden thermal killer. Use polymer/hybrid caps plus a 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR external electrolytic on the battery leads.

**System burst dissipation ≈ 90 W.**

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Lumped heat capacity (30.5×30.5 mm, 8 layers, 2 oz, ~55 % copper fill)

**Copper:**
- Volume = 0.0305² × 70 µm × 8 × 0.55 = 2.87×10⁻⁷ m³
- Mass = 2.56 g
- Heat capacity C = 2.56 × 0.385 = **0.99 J/K**

**FR-4 (1.6 mm):**
- Mass = 0.0305² × 1.6 mm × 1850 kg/m³ ≈ 2.75 g
- Heat capacity C ≈ **3.0 J/K**

Because FR-4 is poorly conductive (k ≈ 0.3), its effective coupling is about 50 % for bursts under 10 s.

**MOSFETs (24×) and capacitors:** ≈ **0.6 J/K**

**Effective board heat capacity:**

$$C_{eff} \approx 0.99 + 1.5 + 0.6 = 3.1\ \text{J/K}$$

### 4.2
