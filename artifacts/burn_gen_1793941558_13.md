# STM32H743 AIO (FC + 4-in-1 ESC) Design Package: Thermal, Switching, and Bidirectional DShot Architecture

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility

I don't have the Job #41 specification, so every value below is a stated assumption. Replace the parameters in the tables with your actual BOM values and the equations still apply. Two issues need resolving before any layout work:

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the FETs.** On every production AIO board, the H743 runs ArduPilot and sends DShot to four separate ESC MCUs (typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Each ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The netlist below follows that architecture.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano board (20×20 mm or 25.5×25.5 mm) is a burst rating only.** The math in §2 shows roughly 32 W per channel at 80 A, which totals about 130 W for four motors in a board region with a heat capacity of only a few J/K. That allows seconds of operation, not continuous use. Total battery current would be 320 A, about 8 kW on 6S. The rating should be specified as "80 A for ≤ X seconds at Y °C ambient", with X derived in §4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance
$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]
$$
At 125 °C: 1.5 mΩ × 1.5 = **2.25 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Commutation topology
With 6-step (trapezoidal) or FOC-like synchronous operation, two legs are in the current path at all times. In synchronous complementary PWM, current flows through either the high-side or the low-side FET of the PWM leg, so the path always contains exactly two channels' worth of R_ds(on):

$$
P_{cond,motor} = I^2 \left[ 2R_{ds}(T_j) + R_{trace} \right]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 80^2 \times (2 \times 2.25 + 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 4.8\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W\ per\ motor}
$$

Over a commutation cycle, each leg conducts 2/6 of the time per FET position. Per-FET average dissipation is therefore:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{P_{cond}}{6} \approx 5.1\ \text{W}
$$

The **instantaneous** dissipation in a FET during its conduction interval is about I²·R_ds = 14.4 W. This is relevant for short-time Z_th: at high eRPM the commutation period is well below the junction time constant, so the average governs. At low-speed or stall bursts, the peak governs.

### 1.3 Thermal runaway check
Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve self-consistently:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{\theta} \cdot I^2 R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]
$$

The solution exists only if R_θ · I² · R_ds,25 · α_R < 1. Example with R_θ (junction to board) = 20 K/W per FET and the per-FET effective current-squared share (I²/6 ≈ 1067 A²):

$$
20 \times 1067 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} \times 0.005 = 0.16 < 1
$$

This is stable, but the loop gain amplifies the temperature rise by a factor of 1/(1−0.16) ≈ 1.19.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per motor, one leg is PWM-switching at a time (a complementary HS/LS pair).

| Mechanism | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·20 ns·48 k = **0.968 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.048 W** |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw (×2 FETs) | 2 × 0.023 = **0.046 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD·I·2·t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.614 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs, 2 active/period) | Q_g·V_gs·f_sw per switching FET | 2·50 nC·10·48 k = **0.048 W** (lost in driver/R_g) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 1.72 W per motor** |

**Total per motor at 80 A:** 30.7 + 1.7 ≈ **32.4 W**. **Total for four motors:** ≈ **130 W**.

**Design levers:**
- Conduction dominates at about 95%, so lowering f_sw buys little.
- Dead time is the largest switching term. Cutting t_dt from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor.
- Paralleling FETs (two per position) cuts conduction loss by half. On nano boards this is usually impossible because of space.
