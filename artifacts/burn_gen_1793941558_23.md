# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #7): System Architecture & Mathematical Model

## 0. Architectural Clarification

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. In a "4-in-1" stack, the H743 is the flight controller running ArduPilot. It talks bi-directional DShot600 to four independent ESC MCUs, which run the commutation, PWM and gate drivers. The partition is:

| Domain | Device | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FC | STM32H743VIT6 @ 480 MHz | ArduPilot, DShot600 TX/RX via timer DMA |
| ESC ×4 | AT32F421 / STM32G071 (AM32 or BLHeli_32) | 6-step or FOC commutation, 48 kHz complementary PWM |
| Gate drive ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase bootstrap, ~12 V VCC) | Drives 6 N-FETs per motor |
| Power stage | 24 × 40 V N-MOSFET (3.3×3.3 or 5×6 mm) | 6S bus |

All numbers below are parametric. I used a representative 40 V low-RDS(on) FET. Substitute your actual datasheet values and re-run the equations.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 2.1 Current paths in 6-step with synchronous rectification

At any instant two phases conduct:
- The **PWM leg** is high-side ON for duty D. Its low-side (synchronous) FET is ON for (1−D), minus dead time.
- The **sink leg** has its low-side ON 100% of the 60° sector.

So the motor current always passes through exactly two FET channels in series, whatever D is. Ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)$$

Include inductor ripple:

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}$$

Take a typical 5-inch-class motor, L_ph-ph ≈ 20 µH, at D = 0.5, 25.2 V. The ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{20\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 6.6\,A$$

This gives ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.6 A², which is negligible against 6400 A². So I_rms ≈ 80 A.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on): self-consistent junction temperature

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha (T_j-25)\right]$$

Solve iteratively with the thermal model (Section 4). The converged burst value is around T_j ≈ 110 °C:

$$R_{DS}(110^\circ C) = 1.0\,m\Omega\,[1 + 0.0045 \cdot 85] = 1.38\,m\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,FET,ch} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.38\,m = 17.7\ W$$

$$P_{cond,Cu,ch} = 80^2 \cdot 0.3\,m = 1.9\ W$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Over one electrical cycle, each FET carries current for about 1/3 of the period:
- High-side: 120°, chopped at D.
- Low-side: 120° full, plus sync portions.

The average is about 17.7/6 ≈ **2.95 W per FET**. The instantaneous peak in the active sector is 80²·1.38 m = **8.8 W**.

The electrical period is short (e.g., 1 ms at 60k eRPM) compared with the FET's ~10–50 ms thermal time constant. The junction therefore follows the average, plus roughly 2–4 K of ripple.
