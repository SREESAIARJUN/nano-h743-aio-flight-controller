**Architectural flags. These should be resolved before layout.**

1. **The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 targets). The H743 generates DShot and decodes bidirectional telemetry. The H743 needs no 48 kHz PWM timers. 48 kHz is the ESC MCU's PWM setting.
2. **"Nano" form factor versus 80 A.** Four channels × 6 FETs is 24 MOSFETs plus 4 drivers, 4 MCUs, and bulk capacitance. That does not physically fit a 20×20 mm board. 25.5×25.5 mm is marginal. 30.5×30.5 mm or a 36×36 mm stack is realistic. The thermal model below uses a 36×36 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz board and still limits a 4-motor 80 A burst to about 2–3 s.
3. **80 A is a burst rating, not a continuous rating.** The physics in §3 shows why. Specify it as "80 A for ≤ X s from T_board,0 = Y °C".

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology current paths

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, two phases conduct in each 60° sector:

- **Switching leg:** current alternates between HS (for D·T_sw) and LS (for (1−D)·T_sw).
- **Static leg:** the opposite LS (or HS) conducts continuously.

At any instant, exactly two MOSFET channels carry I_ph, independent of duty cycle. Dead-time intervals are the exception and are handled in §2.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At the design-point T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{ds}(110) = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.006 \times 85) = 1.51\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 RMS current including PWM ripple

$$
\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L_{ph}\, f_{sw}}
= \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu\text{H} \times 48\,\text{kHz}} = 6.56\,\text{A}
$$

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\,\text{A}^2
$$

The ripple contribution is +0.06%, which is negligible at 48 kHz. This is one of the benefits of 48 kHz over 24 kHz, where ripple quadruples.

### 1.4 Per-channel conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,FET} = 2\, I_{rms}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.51\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.3\,W}
$$

The distribution is uneven, which matters thermally:

| Device | Duty share (one sector) | Loss during sector |
|---|---|---|
| Static-leg FET | 100% | 9.67 W |
| Switching-leg HS | D | 9.67·D W |
| Switching-leg LS | 1−D | 9.67·(1−D) W |

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs conducts 1/3 of the time. The average is therefore 19.3 / 6 ≈ 3.2 W per FET.

The hot spot is set by instantaneous power only at very low eRPM. At a 10,000 eRPM electrical frequency the sector period is about 1 ms, which is far shorter than the die thermal time constant (~10–50 ms). Use the average 3.2 W per FET for thermal sizing above about 1,000 eRPM. Use the peak 9.67 W for stall and startup.

### 1.5 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Resistance | Loss @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase path copper (2 phases × [0.30 mΩ]) | 0.60 mΩ | 3.84 W |
| Per-channel current shunt [0.25 mΩ] (if fitted) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.60 W |
| Bus copper share (at 4-motor burst, shared feed) | [0.15 mΩ] at I_bus ≈ 4×80×D | see §3 |

**Per-channel conduction total ≈ 19.3 + 3.84 + 1.6 ≈ 24.7 W.**
