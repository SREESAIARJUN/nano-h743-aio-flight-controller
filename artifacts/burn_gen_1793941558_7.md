# STM32H743 FC + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC: Power, Thermal & DShot Architecture

**Job #23, Rev A (engineering baseline)**

## 0. Assumptions and Feasibility Flags

**Topology.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It does not commutate motors. Each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421 class running AM32 or BLHeli_32), which generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 talks to the ESCs only through bidirectional DShot600. This split is the industry-standard architecture, and the 48 kHz switching-loss model below applies to the ESC power stage, not the H743.

**Form factor conflict.** An "80 A burst" rating does not physically fit a "nano" (20×20 mm) power stage. Section 3 shows the dissipation is about 84 W at full burst, which needs about 30.5×30.5 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz copper or better. The baseline is therefore:
- FC on a 20×20 or 30×30 mm board.
- ESC on a 30.5×30.5 mm board.
- The "nano" label applies to the FC only.

If the requirement really is a 20×20 mm AIO, derate to about 35–45 A burst (see Section 3.6).

- Bus voltage: 6S, V_bus = 25.2 V (worst case, full charge).
- Burst phase current: I = 80 A.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step Trapezoidal, Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Per-commutation-sector analysis

In each 60° sector, phase A is driven high (PWM), phase B is held low, and phase C floats. With synchronous (complementary) PWM at duty D:

| Device | Conduction interval | Current | Loss contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q_AH (high-side, PWM) | D | I | I²·R·D |
| Q_AL (sync rectifier) | 1 − D − 2t_dt·f_sw | I | I²·R·(1 − D) |
| Q_BL (static low-side) | 1 | I | I²·R |

Summed over the sector:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Under complementary PWM, conduction loss is **independent of duty cycle**. This is the key result.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At the design point T_j ≈ 135 °C: R_ds ≈ 1.0 × (1 + 0.0045 × 110) ≈ **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET average

Each FET carries current for one third of the electrical cycle. The electrical frequency is about 1–3 kHz, which is far faster than the FET thermal time constant, so averaging is valid. By symmetry:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6} = 3.2\ \text{W}$$

The instantaneous peak in the static low-side FET is I²R = 9.6 W.

### 1.4 Ancillary conduction losses

Assumed values:
- Copper path (pads, planes, vias): R_cu ≈ 0.25 mΩ per phase path, two paths active.
- Shunt: 0.5 mΩ, one per channel or one shared.

Losses:
- Copper path: P_cu = 2 × 80² × 0.25 m = **3.2 W per channel**.
- Shunt (per-channel case): P_shunt = 80² × 0.5 m = **3.2 W**.

The shunt loss is significant. Use a 0.2–0.25 mΩ shunt or Hall sensing at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 2.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap (one hard-switched FET per channel)

Transition times from switching charge and driver current:

$$t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{source}} = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 13.3\ \text{ns},\qquad t_f = \frac{20\,\text{nC}}{2.0\,\text{A}} = 10\ \text{ns}$$

Overlap loss:

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 23.3\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.13\ W}$$

### 2.2 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 60\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.073\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery (low-side body diode, hard turn-on of high-side)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

At high di/dt, Q_rr typically rises 2–3×. Budget 0.15 W.

### 2.4 Dead-time diode conduction (two dead times per PWM period)

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

Reducing dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns halves this. This is an AM32/BLHeli setting, constrained by driver propagation skew.
