# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 80 A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #59): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, Bi-Directional DShot600 DMA Plan, and Reference Netlists

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification

In any production 4-in-1 stack, the STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFET gates. The partitioning is:

| Block | Device | Firmware | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flight controller | STM32H743VIT6 (480 MHz, LQFP100) | ArduPilot | Attitude control. Generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry. |
| ESC MCU ×4 | e.g. AT32F421 / STM32G071 | AM32 (or BLHeli_32-class) | Commutation, 48 kHz PWM, dead-time insertion, current limiting |
| Gate driver ×4 | FD6288Q (3-phase, bootstrap) | n/a | Half-bridge drive |
| Power stage ×4 | 6 × N-MOSFET per motor (24 total) | n/a | Inverter |

The 48 kHz switching frequency is therefore an ESC firmware parameter, not an H743 timer setting. Sections 1–3 model the ESC power stage. Section 4 covers the H743 DMA and timer plan.

**Form-factor reality check.** "Nano" with "80 A" is not a continuous-rating combination. A 20×20 mm board cannot reject the losses derived below. This design assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm board (6-layer, 2 oz outer, 2 oz inner power planes) with 80 A as a ≤5 s per-motor burst rating and about 45 A continuous with prop-wash airflow. The calculations below justify this.

## 2. Conduction-Loss Model

### 2.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j)=R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R = 1.0 × (1 + 0.0055 × 85) = **1.47 mΩ**.

### 2.2 Per-device RMS current under 6-step synchronous PWM

Each phase leg is active for 120° of each electrical period, i.e. 1/3 of the time. Within an active interval at duty D:

- The high-side FET conducts for a fraction D.
- The low-side synchronous FET of that leg freewheels for a fraction (1 − D).
- The opposite leg's low-side FET conducts continuously.

This gives the per-device RMS currents:

$$I_{rms,HS}=I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS}=I_{ph}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}}\;(\text{worst case, incl. sync-rect})$$

Because synchronous rectification is used, exactly two channels conduct I_ph at every instant (ignoring dead time). The total bridge conduction loss is therefore:

$$\boxed{P_{cond,bridge}=2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}$$

This holds independently of D, because the freewheel path also sees R_DS rather than a diode drop.

### 2.3 Copper and shunt contributions

| Element | Resistance | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase trace and bus path (2 oz, ~0.25 mΩ effective per conduction path) | 2 × 0.25 mΩ | 3.2 W |
| Low-side shunt (if used; 0.25 mΩ, per motor) | 0.25 mΩ × duty-weighted | ≈1.6 W |

### 2.4 Burst result (per motor, T_j = 110 °C)

$$P_{cond}=2\times 80^2\times 1.47\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.8\ W}$$

Per FET (heaviest, low-side), the time-averaged loss is about 80² × 1.47 m / 3 ≈ **3.1 W**.

## 3. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches hard. That is one hard-switched node per motor at any time.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ V_bus I_ph (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw = 30 n × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.036 W |
| Coss charge | ½ Q_oss V_bus f_sw | 0.024 W |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F I_ph (2 t_dt) f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (6 FETs; dissipated in driver and R_g) | 6 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 0.115 W |
| **Switching subtotal** | | **≈2.73 W** |

### Sensitivities

- P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor but increases current ripple and acoustic noise.
- Cutting dead time from 100 ns to 50 ns saves about 0.3 W per motor. Verify shoot-through margin against the driver's propagation-delay mismatch (FD6288: ~20 ns).

### 3.1 Total burst loss

| Item | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 18.8 W | 75.2 W |
| Copper + shunt | 4.8 W | 19.2 W |
| Switching | 2.7 W | 10.9 W |
| **Total** | **26.3 W** | **105 W** |

Efficiency at burst: output ≈ 25.2 × 80 × 4 ≈ 8.06 kW, so η ≈ 98.7%. The efficiency is fine; the problem is rejecting 105 W from a 30×30 mm board.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Electrothermal fixed point

R_DS depends on T_j, so P depends on T_j. Iterate:

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_a + P\!\left(T_j^{(k)}\right)\, Z_{th}(t)$$

The iteration converges in 3–4 steps when the loop gain α · P_cond · Z_th < 1. If that gain approaches 1, the condition is **thermal runaway**, and it sets the hard limit on burst duration.

### 4.2 Foster network (per FET, junction → ambient)

| Stage | R_i (K/W) | τ_i | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.2 | 0.5 ms | Die → package (R_θJC ≈ 1.2 K/W) |
| 2 | 6.0 | 0.8 s | Solder + local copper spreading |
| 3 | 8.0 | 25 s | Board bulk (shared by all 24 FETs; scale by heat-sharing) |

$$Z_{th}(t)=\sum_i R_i\left(1-e^{-t/\tau_i}\right)$$

**Board lumped heat capacity:** about 12 g of FR4 + copper + FETs + capacitors at ≈0.9 J/(g·K) gives C_board ≈ 11 J/K.

**Board-to-air resistance:** R_board,a ≈ 1.2 K/W with prop wash, or about 4 K/W in still air.
