# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #69)

## 0. Feasibility Issues to Resolve First

Four issues affect the architecture and should be resolved before layout.

| # | Issue | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The H743 cannot drive the motors directly. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 running AM32, or BLHeli_32 targets). The H743 only generates DShot. | The design is a **two-board stack**: an H743 FC and a 4-in-1 ESC with 4 local MCUs. |
| 2 | "Nano" (20×20 mm, M3/M2 holes) at **80A burst per motor** is beyond realistic copper and thermal capacity. Commercial 20×20 boards top out around 40–60A burst. 80A boards are typically 30.5×30.5. | Section 3 quantifies this. Without a heatsink, an 80A burst on a 20×20 board is limited to roughly 2–4 s. |
| 3 | The H743VI in LQFP100 (14×14 mm) is a tight fit on a 20×20 FC. | Use TFBGA100 (8×8 mm), or accept a single-sided, dense layout. |
| 4 | "80A" is ambiguous. I interpret it as **phase current per motor**. | Battery current is roughly D·I_phase. Four motors at once means 320A of phase current. |
