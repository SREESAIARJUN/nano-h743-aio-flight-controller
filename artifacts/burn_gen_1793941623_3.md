## 0. Scope, Architecture, and Assumptions

Three facts shape the rest of this analysis:

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz motor PWM.** In every production 4-in-1 design, each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32/BlueJay. Typical parts are the AT32F421, STM32G071, or GD32. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bidirectional DShot600. The switching-loss model applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **"80 A burst" on a nano (20×20 mm) board is not thermally realistic.** I model a 30.5×30.5 mm ESC footprint (≈36×36 mm board). I show quantitatively where the 80 A rating breaks down.
3. **The MOSFET values are representative, not a specific part number.** They describe a 40 V, ≈1 mΩ, PQFN 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 class device. Before freezing the BOM, substitute the parameters of your chosen part.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Conduction topology
In 6-step drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM, every commutation sector has the same structure:
- One leg is PWM'd. Its high-side conducts for duty D, and its low-side synchronously rectifies for (1−D).
- One leg has its low-side held statically ON.
- One leg floats and is used for BEMF sensing.

The phase current therefore always passes through exactly **two R_DS(on) in series**, regardless of D. Ignoring dead time:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right]$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per motor @80 A |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 100 °C | 1.34 mΩ | 17.1 W |
| 125 °C | 1.45 mΩ | 18.6 W |

### 1.3 Per-device distribution
Each of the 6 FETs spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle conducting. Averaged over a cycle:

$$\bar P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6} \approx 3.1\ \text{W @125 °C}$$

The instantaneous dissipation within its sector is ≈ I²R ≈ 9.3 W. At electrical frequencies above about 200 Hz, the die and package thermal mass filter out that ripple.

### 1.4 Copper and interconnect conduction
Assume 2 oz (70 µm) outer layers and 2 oz inner planes. Phase path: 4 mm wide × 10 mm long, two layers in parallel.

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho L}{w t} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.01}{0.004\cdot70\times10^{-6}\cdot2} \approx 0.31\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Including the bus return path, add ≈0.5 mΩ effective, so I²R_Cu ≈ **3.2 W per motor**. This loss heats the board directly, not the junction. It is often neglected, and that is a mistake at 80 A.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches in each sector, so these terms apply to one leg per motor.

### 2.1 V–I overlap (high-side hard switching)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = \tfrac12(25.2)(80)(35\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{kHz}) = 1.69\ \text{W}$$

**Gate-drive check.** Assumptions: V_drv = 10 V, V_plateau ≈ 3 V, R_g,ext + R_g,int = 4 Ω, and switching charge Q_gs2 + Q_gd ≈ 10 nC.

$$t_{on} \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/R_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{1.75\,\text{A}} \approx 5.7\ \text{ns}$$

$$t_{off} \approx \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{V_{pl}/R_g} \approx 13\ \text{ns}$$
