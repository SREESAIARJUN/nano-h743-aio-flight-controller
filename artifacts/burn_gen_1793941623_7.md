# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #57): Power, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Decisions and Feasibility Flags

Three issues must be settled before deriving the math. Each one changes the design.

**1. The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.**
ArduPilot outputs bidirectional DShot. It does not run motor commutation. The standard architecture is therefore:

```
H743 (ArduPilot) --DShot600 BDShot x4--> 4x ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 firmware)
                                          --> 4x 3-phase gate driver --> 24x N-MOSFET
```

The ESC firmware sets the 48 kHz PWM frequency (AM32 supports 24–48 kHz+). The H743 only governs the DShot timing.

**2. "Nano" and "80 A" conflict.**
- A 20×20 mm board can fit only 3.3×3.3 DFN FETs.
- Its copper thermal mass is about 1.3 J/K.
- Section 3 shows that four channels at 80 A would saturate it thermally in under 1 s.

This design therefore assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm "compact" form factor, 8-layer, 2 oz copper**. Results for 20×20 are noted alongside.

**3. "80 A burst" must be defined.**
- **Per-motor (assumed):** 80 A peak per motor, ≤2 s, with not all four motors at 80 A for the full duration.
- **All four simultaneously:** 320 A from the pack. No XT60/XT90 connector or 6S pack wiring on a nano frame supports that.

The bus is modeled at 6S, V_bus,max = 25.2 V.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous-rectified) PWM. At any instant, two phases conduct the motor current I_m:

- **PWM ON:** HS(phase A) → winding → LS(phase B)
- **PWM OFF (freewheel):** LS(phase A, sync-rectified) → winding → LS(phase B)

In both states, exactly **two FET channels** are in series with I_m. The instantaneous bridge conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\,I_m^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

### 1.2 Per-FET Distribution

The per-FET split matters for hotspot analysis. Over one electrical period, each phase spends:
- 1/3 sourcing,
- 1/3 sinking,
- 1/3 floating.

For duty D, the RMS currents are:

$$I_{HS,rms}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I_m^2$$

$$I_{LS,rms}^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I_m^2 + \tfrac{1}{3} I_m^2 = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I_m^2$$

The low-side FET is always the hotter device. Its worst case is at low duty, which also carries the highest freewheel current.

### 1.3 Numbers at I_m = 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

R_DS(on) at 125 °C = 1.0 × 1.5 = **1.5 mΩ**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

At D = 0.9, typical for an 80 A burst at full throttle:

| Device | I_rms² | Loss per FET |
|---|---|---|
| HS FET | 0.300 × 6400 = 1920 A² | 2.88 W |
| LS FET | 0.367 × 6400 = 2347 A² | 3.52 W |

Check: 3 × (2.88 + 3.52) = 19.
