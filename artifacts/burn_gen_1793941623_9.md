# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #53): Reference Engineering Model

## 0. Scope and Assumptions

### Architectural assumptions
These matter for correctness:

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** On every real 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU, for example an AT32F421 running AM32 or BLHeli_32.
   - The H743 sends bidirectional DShot600 commands and receives eRPM telemetry.
   - The ESC MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
   - The models below cover both domains.
2. **"80 A burst" means per-channel motor phase current (RMS), not battery current.** Phase and battery current relate as:

   $$I_{batt} \approx D \cdot I_{phase}$$

   At full throttle (D ≈ 1) the two converge, which is the worst case.
3. **"Nano" form factor.** I assume a 30.5×30.5 mm stack. A 20×20 mm board cannot dissipate 80 A-class burst losses for any useful duration; Section 3 shows why.
4. **Bus voltage:** 6S, 25.2 V maximum. The MOSFETs are 40 V rated, giving about 37% derating headroom for ringing.
5. **Netlists are reference-design level.** Pinouts are verified against the H743 LQFP100 AF table. Before fabrication, the final release still needs an ERC/DRC pass and a datasheet cross-check of the specific MOSFET and driver part numbers.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in six-step commutation with synchronous rectification
At any instant, two phase legs are active, say A (PWM) and B (low side held on). Exactly **two MOSFETs carry the phase current at all times**:

| PWM state | Conducting FETs |
|---|---|
| ON (duration D·T) | Q_AH + Q_BL |
| OFF (duration (1−D)·T), synchronous | Q_AL + Q_BL |

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore independent of D to first order:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \cdot I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence of R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\text{–}0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C with α = 0.005:

$$R = 0.90(1 + 0.5) = 1.35\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.3\ W}$$

Losses are not evenly distributed across the bridge:
- **Per-FET worst case:** Q_BL conducts 100% of the commutation interval, so P = 80² × 1.35 m = **8.64 W** while active.
- **Rotation through steps:** each leg is active for 2 of the 6 commutation steps, which averages out the instantaneous hotspot. Over one electrical cycle, each FET averages about 2.9 W conduction.
- **Design to the instantaneous value.** The thermal time constant of a 5×6 die/clip (~ms) is comparable to the electrical period at low eRPM. Use instantaneous values for low-speed/high-torque (punch-out) conditions.

### 1.4 Other resistive losses (per channel)

| Element | Expression | @ 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ) | I²R | 1.60 W |
| PCB copper (≈0.15 mΩ effective, 2 oz inner + 3 oz outer) | I²R | 0.96 W |
| Motor pad/solder joints (≈0.1 mΩ) | I²R | 0.64 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **3.2 W** |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The synchronous FET commutates through its body diode during dead time.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

A more exact method replaces t_r and t_f with the Miller-plateau intervals
