# STM32H743 FC + 80 A Burst 4-in-1 ESC: System Architecture Model

## 0. Assumptions and Scope

No component datasheets or board constraints were supplied, so this model uses a **representative parametric baseline**. Every number below is a function of the variables defined in §1, so you can substitute your real MOSFET, PCB and airflow data.

**Architectural correction up front.** The STM32H743 belongs on the **flight controller** side. A 4-in-1 ESC needs its own commutation MCU, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 cannot run four sensorless BLDC commutation loops and ArduPilot at the same time. This document therefore covers:

- **FC (H743, ArduPilot)**: the bidirectional DShot600 timer and DMA allocation.
- **ESC (4× power stages)**: conduction, switching and thermal models.

**Baseline assumptions**

| Parameter | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Supply (6S, full charge) | V_bus | 25.2 V |
| Burst phase current | I | 80 A |
| PWM frequency | f_sw | 48 kHz |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_0\,[1 + \alpha\,(T_j - 25)]$$

Add a series parasitic for copper and solder: R_par ≈ 0.3 mΩ per conduction path (2 oz, ~15 mm of 4 mm-wide trace, layered).

### 1.2 Per-FET average over one electrical cycle

In 6-step commutation each phase leg is "high" for 120° and "low" for 120°. With complementary PWM on the active high leg:

- **High-side FET (leg PWMing):**
$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{DS}$$
- **Low-side FET** (synchronous rectification during its own leg's high interval, plus fully on during its 120° low interval):
$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)\,I^2 R_{DS} + \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{DS} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2 R_{DS}$$
- **Per motor (6 FETs):** at every instant exactly two FET channels carry current, so
$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}$$
This holds for any D, because the synchronous FET replaces the diode.

### 1.3 Self-consistent junction temperature (fixed-point iteration)

$$T_j^{(k+1)} = T_{ref} + P_{FET}\!\left(R_{DS}(T_j^{(k)})\right)\cdot R_{\theta,j\text{-}ref}$$

The iteration converges if $\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_\theta = \tfrac13 I^2 R_0 \alpha R_\theta < 1$. Otherwise thermal runaway occurs.

With I = 80 A, R_θ,j-board ≈ 2 K/W, and the board at 80 °C:

$$\tfrac13 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.001 \cdot 0.005 \cdot 2 = 0.021 \ll 1$$

So the loop is locally stable. Iterating:

| k | T_j (°C) | R_DS (mΩ) | P_FET at D=1 (W) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 80 | 1.275 | 2.72 |
| 1 | 85.4 | 1.302 | 2.78 |
| 2 | 85.6 | 1.303 | 2.78 |

The result is a **2.78 W** per-FET conduction loss.

Adding parasitic copper loss (6400 × 0.3 mΩ × 2 paths = 3.84 W per motor) gives:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot (1.303 + 0.3)\,\text{m}\Omega \approx \mathbf{20.5\ W}$$

$$P_{cond,4\times} \approx \mathbf{82\ W}\ \text{(all four motors at 80 A)}$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWMing leg hard-switches. Its synchronous low-side commutates softly through the body diode.

| Mechanism | Equation | Value (per motor) |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw | 40 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½·C_oss·V²·f_sw | **0.015 W** |
| Gate drive (mostly in driver) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw·2 | 40 nC·10·48 k·2 = **0.04 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.65 W/motor, ≈ 10.6 W for 4×** |

**Duty dependence.** Overlap loss is present only when 0 < D < 1. At 100 % throttle, switching loss collapses to roughly 0. Worst-case total loss therefore occurs at D ≈ 0.85–0.95 with current near peak.

**Sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per motor but increases current ripple. With phase inductance L_ph ≈ 10 µH, the ripple is:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{2L_{ph}f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2 \cdot 0.25}{2 \cdot 10\mu \cdot 48k} \approx 6.6\ \text{A at D=0.5}$$
