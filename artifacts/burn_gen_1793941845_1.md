> **Feasibility flag:** A sustained 80 A per motor on a 20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm "nano" board is not thermally achievable. The math in §3 shows roughly 87 W of semiconductor loss at full burst on all four motors. Treat 80 A strictly as a time-limited burst rating. The board must also enforce a current/temperature foldback derived from the model.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R_ds = 1.5 mΩ × 1.45 = **2.175 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

In each 60° sector, one phase is PWM'd and one phase is held low. Current flows through exactly two channel-conducting devices at all times:

- During D·T_sw: HS(A) and LS(B) conduct.
- During (1−D)·T_sw: LS(A) (sync-rectifier) and LS(B) conduct.

So the instantaneous channel loss is independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Each FET is in a conducting path for 120° of the electrical cycle, with its share split by D. The averaged loss over the six devices is:

$$P_{cond,FET,avg} = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\quad\text{(worst single FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}P_{cond,ch}\text{ at D}\to 1\text{)}$$

### 1.3 Numbers

| T_j | R_ds | P_cond per channel | Avg per FET | Worst FET (D≈1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 2·80²·1.5m = **19.2 W** | 3.2 W | 6.4 W |
| 100 °C | 2.01 mΩ | 25.7 W | 4.3 W | 8.6 W |
| 125 °C | 2.18 mΩ | **27.8 W** | 4.6 W | 9.3 W |

### 1.4 Parasitic copper and shunt resistance

Add these to the series path:

$$P_{Cu} = I^2 (R_{trace} + R_{shunt}) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 80^2 \times (0.3\,\text{m}\Omega + 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega) = 3.5\ \text{W per channel}$$

- R_trace assumes 2 oz outer layers plus inner-layer stitching.
- R_shunt of 0.25 mΩ applies only if per-phase sensing is used. With a single bus shunt, compute it once on the battery current instead.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd half-bridge switches in any sector, so these terms apply once per channel.

### 2.1 Hard-switching V–I overlap

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery

The sync-FET body diode recovers at HS turn-on:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.036\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output-capacitance energy

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,er} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2 = 0.046\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

There are two edges per cycle:

$$P_{dt} = V_F\, I\, (2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not the FET junction:

$$P_g = 2\, Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

For all four channels, the gate rail must supply about 0.19 W (≈20 mA at 10 V). Size the gate-drive LDO or buck to ≥ 50 mA.

### 2.6 Switching-loss summary per channel

$$P_{sw,ch} \approx 1.94 + 0.04 + 0.05 + 0.61 = \mathbf{2.64\ W}$$

### 2.7 Frequency sensitivity

$$\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} = 55\ \mu\text{W/Hz}$$

- At 24 kHz: ≈1.3 W.
- At 96 kHz: ≈5.3 W.

48 kHz is a reasonable compromise between ripple current and audible noise.
