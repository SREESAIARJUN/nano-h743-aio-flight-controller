## 0. Architectural Clarifications

These points drive everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own MCU (AT32F421, STM32G071 or EFM8BB51) running AM32, BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 runs ArduPilot and talks to the ESCs over bidirectional DShot600. The loss model applies to the ESC board. The DMA model applies to the FC.
2. **"80 A burst" means per channel, short duration, not all four at once.** 4 × 80 A = 320 A at 6S is about 8 kW, which exceeds any battery lead or connector that fits a nano stack. I model per-channel burst and treat simultaneous burst as a thermal-mass event of a few seconds.
3. **"Nano" form factor.** I use the 20 × 20 mm (M2) FC with a 30.5 × 30.5 mm (M3) ESC. True 80 A burst on a 20 × 20 ESC is not credible thermally. The math in §3 shows why.
4. **Component values are representative.** MOSFET and driver parameters below are typical of 40 V, 3.3 × 3.3 mm DFN parts. Substitute datasheet values for the chosen BOM; every equation is parametric.

## 2. Loss Model, One ESC Channel

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step synchronous PWM, two phase legs carry current at any time:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts during D. The low-side complementary FET conducts during (1 − D).
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the 60° sector.

The conducting path therefore always contains two FET channels in series:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot}\cdot[D + (1-D)] + I^2 \cdot R_{ds,hot} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

$$
R_{ds,hot} = k_T \cdot R_{ds,25} = 1.5 \times 0.90\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.35\,\text{m}\Omega
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.35\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{17.28\ W}
$$

**Per-FET distribution:** each FET conducts for 120° of 360° electrical.
- Return-leg low-side, worst instantaneous: I²R = **8.64 W** while active.
- Average per FET over a rotation: 17.28 / 6 = **2.88 W**.

The transient model in §3 uses the 8.64 W figure because a sector at low eRPM can last milliseconds.

**Copper / shunt term.** Use 2 oz outer, 70 µm, with about 0.15 mΩ of effective path resistance per channel, including the phase pad and bus pour:

$$
P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.15\,\text{m}\Omega = 0.96\ W
$$

### 2.2 Switching losses at 48 kHz

**Switching times:**

$$
t_r = \frac{Q_{sw}}{I_{G,src}} = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{1.5\,\text{A}} = 10\,\text{ns},\qquad
t_f = \frac{15\,\text{nC}}{2.0\,\text{A}} = 7.5\,\text{ns}
$$

Add about 50 % for loop inductance and the Miller plateau with real R_g. That gives t_r ≈ 15 ns and t_f ≈ 11 ns, so Σ ≈ 26 ns.

**V–I overlap (hard-switched leg only):**

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\times25.2\times80\times26\times10^{-9}\times48\times10^{3} = \mathbf{1.26\ W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.048\ W
$$

**Reverse recovery** (the complementary FET's body diode is forced off at high-side turn-on):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\times25.2\times48\text{k} = 0.060\ W
$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead-times per PWM period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\, I\, (2t_{dt})\, f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times200\text{n}\times48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
