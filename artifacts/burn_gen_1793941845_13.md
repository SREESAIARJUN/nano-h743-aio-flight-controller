**Architecture note 1 – who generates the 48 kHz PWM.** The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. The 48 kHz six-step/complementary PWM, BEMF zero-cross detection, and eRPM telemetry run on **four dedicated ESC MCUs** (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). Do not attempt to close 4 sensorless commutation loops (12 half-bridges) on the H743 alongside ArduPilot. The timer budget and real-time jitter make this unworkable, and bidirectional DShot then becomes meaningless.

**Architecture note 2 – the 80 A / 10 s burst is not thermally achievable on a nano/30.5 mm board without a heatsink.** See §3.4.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current waveform

Commutation is trapezoidal six-step with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM, as AM32/BLHeli_32 use).

In any commutation sector, two phases carry the phase current I:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The complementary low-side conducts for 1−D (minus dead time).
- **Static leg:** the low-side FET is on continuously for the sector.

So at every instant, **exactly two R_ds(on) paths** carry I, regardless of D.

### 1.2 RMS current including ripple

Peak-to-peak ripple in the PWM leg:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{(V_{bus}-V_{bemf})\,D}{L\,f_{sw}} \approx \frac{V_{bus}\,D(1-D)}{L\,f_{sw}}$$

Worst case is D = 0.5:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{20\,\mu H \times 48\,kHz} = 6.56\ \text{A}$$

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12} = 6400 + 3.6 = 6403.6\ \text{A}^2$$

Ripple adds 0.06 %, so it is negligible at 48 kHz. The 48 kHz choice is justified on audible noise and ripple, not on loss.

### 1.3 Conduction loss per channel

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds(on)}\,k_T$$

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 6403.6 \times 1.0\,m\Omega \times 1.5 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

Because R_ds rises with T_j, solve this self-consistently with §3:

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0067/K$$

### 1.4 Copper and shunt loss

Assume an effective 0.3 mΩ per channel current path (phase pad + bus planes + shunt share):

$$P_{Cu,ch} = 80^2 \times 0.3\,m\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

If you use a 0.25 mΩ low-side shunt per channel, add 80² × 0.25 mΩ × (sector duty) ≈ 0.5–1.6 W depending on topology. **Recommendation:** use a single DC-link shunt for 4-in-1 total current. Rely on ESC firmware current estimation per channel, or use a Hall sensor (e.g. ACS7xx) on the bus.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. The static low-side commutates at electrical frequency (≪ f_sw), so its switching loss is negligible.

### 2.1 Hard-switching overlap (V–I crossover)

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,V_{bus}\,I\,(t_r+t_f)\,f_{sw}$$
$$= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,ns \times 48\,kHz = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

### 2.2 Body-diode reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}\,V_{bus}\,f_{sw} = 40\,nC \times 25.2 \times 48\,kHz = 0.048\ \text{W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance (C_oss) energy, two devices per leg

$$P_{oss} = 2 \times \tfrac{1}{2}\,C_{oss,er}\,V_{bus}^2\,f_{sw} = 1\,nF \times 635 \times 48\,kHz = 0.030\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\,I\,(2\,t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,ns \times 48\,kHz = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

This is a large, controllable term. Reducing dead time to 50 ns saves 0.3 W per channel. Verify shoot-through margin first with a double-pulse test
