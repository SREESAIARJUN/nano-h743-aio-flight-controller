### 0.1 Architectural clarification

The STM32H743 belongs on the **flight controller**. Bidirectional DShot implies the ESC side has its own MCUs, one per channel, running AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware (e.g., 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071). Using a single H743 to commutate four motors directly would remove the need for DShot entirely. So the baseline is:

```
[STM32H743 FC] ──4× bidir DShot600──► [4× ESC MCU] ──► [4× 3-phase gate driver] ──► [24× MOSFET]
```

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current waveform

The ESC uses 6-step trapezoidal commutation with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM). Each high-side FET carries phase current for 1/3 of the electrical period while its phase is driven high. The corresponding low-side FET carries it for 1/3 while that phase is the return path.

During PWM off-time, the low-side FET in the driven leg conducts in sync-rectification mode. At duty D, the high-side share is D and the low-side share is (1 − D). The *sum* of conduction across the pair is therefore always the full 1/3 window.

Per-FET RMS current (high side shown):

$$
I_{rms,HS} = I_{pk}\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS,complementary} = I_{pk}\sqrt{\tfrac{1-D}{3}}
$$

Total conduction loss per channel is independent of D under synchronous rectification. At any instant, exactly two FET channels carry current:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{pk}^2\, R_{ds}(T_j),\qquad R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] \approx k_T R_{ds,25}
$$

### 1.2 Numbers at 80 A burst

- R_ds,hot = 1.5 × 1.5 mΩ = **2.25 mΩ**
- P_cond,ch = 2 × 80² × 2.25 m = **28.8 W per channel**
- Worst single FET (D → 1, high side): I_rms = 80/√3 = 46.2 A, giving P = 46.2² × 2.25 m = **4.8 W**
- **4 channels: 115 W conduction**

### 1.3 Electro-thermal coupling

R_ds rises with T_j, so the loss model and the thermal model must be solved together. Iterate:

$$
T_j^{(n+1)} = T_{board} + R_{\theta JC}\,P_{FET}\big(T_j^{(n)}\big)
$$

For the worst FET: 4.8 W × 1.5 K/W ≈ 7 K rise above board temperature. This converges in 2–3 iterations. At this power level the loop is stable and there is no thermal runaway at the FET level. **The board itself is the bottleneck, not the junction** (see §3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg is PWM-switched at any instant. That is the high side of the active leg, with its complementary low side.

| Component | Formula | Value / channel |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap (hard switching) | ½ V_bus I_pk (t_r + t_f) f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 n·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr V_bus f_sw | 50 n·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss discharge | ½ Q_oss V_bus f_sw | 0.5·40 n·25.2·48 k = **0.02 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_F I_pk (2 t_dt) f_sw | 0.8·80·100 n·48 k = **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated mainly in the driver) | 2 Q_g V_GS f_sw | 2·50 n·10·48 k = **0.05 W** |
| **Total** | | **≈ 2.4 W / channel, ≈ 9.5 W for 4 ch** |

### Observations

1. At 48 kHz, switching loss is roughly 8% of conduction loss at 80 A. **Conduction dominates.** Gaining 10% on R_DS(on) is worth more than halving f_sw.
2. V-I overlap scales with t_r + t_f. That makes **gate resistor selection the main lever** you control. Lower R_g speeds the edges but makes ringing worse at the 25 V bus with ~2–5 nH of loop inductance. Check the overshoot margin: V_DS,pk = V_bus + L_loop · di/dt must stay below about 80% of the 40 V rating.
3. At D = 100% (full-throttle burst), there is effectively **no PWM switching**. Only commutation edges remain, at the e-rev rate. Worst-case total loss therefore occurs at about D ≈ 0.85–0.95 with maximum current.

**Combined burst loss: about 31 W per channel, about 125 W for the whole ESC.**

- Copper: 4 cm² × 8 × 70 µm = 0.224 cm³, giving 2.0 g × 0.385 J/g·K = **0.77 J/K**

- **C_th ≈ 2.4 J/K** (no heatsink)
