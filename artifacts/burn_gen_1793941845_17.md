# Nano AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Architecture, Loss Model, Thermal Budget, and DShot DMA Plan

## 0. Architectural Correction and Key Finding

**The STM32H743 cannot be the motor-commutation MCU.** Four 3-phase bridges need 12 complementary PWM pairs with dead-time. The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1 and TIM8) plus HRTIM, and it would also have to run sensorless BEMF zero-cross detection for four motors while executing ArduPilot. The architecture is therefore:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) --bi-dir DShot600 x4--> 4x AT32F421 (AM32) --> 4x FD6288Q --> 24x N-MOSFET
```

The 48 kHz figure is the AM32 PWM carrier, not an H743 timer.

**Key finding: 80 A on all four motors cannot be sustained on a nano board.** The math below shows:
- It is thermally limited to roughly 1–2 seconds.
- Continuous capability is about 30–40 A per motor, depending on airflow.

The firmware limits in §5 are mandatory, not optional.

**Design assumptions** (substitute your datasheet values):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Board | 30.5×30.5 mm footprint (~32×32 mm PCB), 8-layer, 2 oz outer / 1 oz inner (≈ 8 × 50 µm avg) |

| Burst definition | 80 A per motor (phase/DC current), all four motors simultaneously (worst case) |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 × 1.45 = **1.74 mΩ**. Use **1.8 mΩ** for margin.

### 1.2 Six-step commutation with synchronous rectification

During each 60° sector, three FETs carry current:

- **High-side A** conducts for duty D.
- **Low-side A** (complementary) conducts for 1 − D.
- **Low-side B** is continuously on.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I^2 R\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2 I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

This result is independent of D, which is a useful property of synchronous rectification. Including inductor ripple, use $I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I_{pp}^2/12$:

$$P_{cond} = 2R\left(I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}\right)$$

For low-inductance racing motors (L ≈ 15 µH) at 48 kHz, ΔI ≲ 10 A, so the ripple adds less than 0.2 %. It can be neglected.

### 1.3 Numerical result at 80 A

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.8\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{23.0\ W\ per\ motor}$$

The single worst-case FET is the continuously-on low-side, which carries 100 % of a sector:
- Over one sector: 80² × 1.8 mΩ = **11.5 W**.
- Averaged over the electrical cycle (each low-side is on for 2 of 6 sectors): ≈ 3.8 W average, but the 11.5 W peak drives junction ripple at low eRPM.

### 1.4 Electrothermal stability check (runaway)

The loop gain is:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T} = 2I^2R_{25}\alpha = 2(6400)(1.2\text{m})(0.0045) = 0.069\ \text{W/K}$$

Stability requires $\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\cdot R_{\theta,total} < 1$, so R_θ < 14.5 K/W per motor share. That is satisfied (see §3), but the effective gain multiplies the base temperature rise by 1/(1 − 0.069·R_θ), which is about 1.6× at R_θ = 5.5 K/W.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

In each sector only one leg is PWM-switched. The terms are:

| Term | Formula | Value @ 80 A, 25.2 V |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ V I (t_r + t_f) f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·30n·48k | **1.45 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_F · I · 2t_dt · f_sw = 0.8·80·400n·48k | **1.23 W** |
| Reverse recovery | V · Q_rr · f_sw = 25.2·50n·48k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | V · Q_oss · f_sw | 0.05 W |
| Gate drive (from 12 V rail) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f_sw | 0.04 W |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.83 W per motor** |

### 2.1 Frequency trade-off

$$P_{sw}(f) \approx 59\ \mu\text{J} \times f_{sw}$$

| f_sw | P_sw |
|---|---|
| 24 kHz | 1.4 W |
| 48 kHz | 2.8 W |
| 96 kHz | 5.7 W |

At 80 A, switching is only about 11 % of total loss. Conduction dominates, so 48 kHz is justified for smoother low-throttle behavior and less audible noise.

**Mitigation:** reduce dead time to 100 ns if the FD6288Q propagation mismatch allows, verified on a scope. This saves about 0.6 W per motor.
