# STM32H743 AIO FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #3): 80A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching Losses, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

---

## 0. Executive Summary

1. **The STM32H743 does not commutate the motors.** Each ESC channel uses a dedicated motor MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and emits bi-directional DShot600. This document models both sides.
2. **80A continuous on four channels is not achievable on a nano board** (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm). At 80A/channel with all four motors loaded, the power stage dissipates about **110 W**. That heats the board at roughly **30 K/s**, which gives only about **1.5–2 s** of burst before the FETs reach their junction limit.
3. **A defensible rating for this form factor is about 45A continuous / 60A for 5 s per channel.** An "80A burst" label must be qualified as ≤1 s, single-motor, with airflow. If the 80A figure is a hard customer requirement, recommend a 30.5×30.5 mm board or a split FC/ESC stack.
4. **Datasheet values in this model are representative placeholders.** Replace every value marked ⚠ with the actual datasheet figure before sign-off.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with complementary PWM, two phase legs carry current I at all times:
- **PWM leg:** the high-side FET conducts for duty D. The synchronous low-side FET conducts for 1−D.
- **Return leg:** the low-side FET conducts for the whole 120° sector.

The series resistance in the current loop is therefore always 2·R_DS(on), independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ch} = I^2 \cdot \left(2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) + R_{sh}\right)
$$

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.006\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

### 2.2 At 80 A, T_j = 125 °C

- **FETs:** P_cond = 80² × 2 × 1.92 mΩ = 6400 × 3.84 mΩ = **24.6 W**
- **Shunt:** 6400 × 0.25 mΩ = **1.6 W**
- **PCB copper** (≈0.3 mΩ phase and bus path): about **1.9 W**
- **Conduction total:** about **28.1 W per channel**

### 2.3 Per-FET breakdown

Each FET conducts for about one third of the electrical period. Its averaged loss is:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \frac{24.6}{6} \approx 4.1\ \mathrm{W}
$$

While a FET is actually conducting, it dissipates I²R = 12.3 W. The junction thermal time constant is in the millisecond range, while one electrical cycle at 30k eRPM lasts about 2 ms. The junction therefore ripples by roughly 3–5 K above the averaged value.

## 3. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching V·I overlap

$$
