# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 80A ESC: System Architecture Model (Job #8)

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Architecture Boundary

The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs. In a 4-in-1 stack, each ESC channel has its own motor-control MCU, typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or an EFM8/BB51 running BLHeli. That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM. The H743 generates bidirectional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.

| Domain | Owner | Key Parameter |
|---|---|---|
| Flight control, DShot, telemetry | STM32H743 (FC) | DShot600, bidirectional, DMA |
| Commutation, 48 kHz PWM | ESC MCU ×4 | Center-aligned PWM, dead time |
| Power stage | 6 N-FETs/channel, 3-phase gate driver | 6S (25.2 V max) |

Assumed power-stage parameters are listed below. Replace them with your BOM datasheet values; every equation is parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S fully charged |
| I_burst | 80 A | Phase current, per channel |
| R_ds(on),25 | 1.2 mΩ | 40 V FET, 3.3×3.3 or 5×6 package |
| α_R | ≈ 1.5 at T_j = 125 °C | R_ds(on) temperature multiplier |
| t_r, t_f | 20 ns each | At driver R_g ≈ 5–10 Ω |
| Q_rr | 50 nC | Body-diode reverse recovery |
| Q_oss | 40 nC | Output charge |
| Q_g | 40 nC at 10 V | Total gate charge |
| t_dead | 200 ns | Per edge |
| V_f | 0.8 V | Body-diode forward voltage at 80 A |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Junction to case |

**Reality check.** 80 A per channel on a 20×20 or 30×30 nano board is a burst rating only. Section 3 shows it is thermally sustainable for roughly 2–4 seconds, not continuously.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current Path

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation and synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), two phases conduct at any instant:

- During the PWM on-time, current flows through the high-side FET of phase A and the low-side FET of phase B.
- During the PWM off-time, current flows through the low-side FET of phase A (synchronous) and the low-side FET of phase B.

The motor current I therefore always flows through exactly two FET channels, regardless of duty cycle D.

### 1.2 Temperature-Dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\left[1 + k_T (T_j - 25)\right], \quad k_T \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

$$R_{ds}(125^\circ C) = 1.2 \times 1.5 = 1.8\ \text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,FET} = I^2 \cdot 2R_{ds}(T_j) = 80^2 \times 2 \times 1.8\text{m} = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

At a cold start (25 °C), the same calculation gives 15.4 W. Because loss rises with temperature and temperature rises with loss, this is a positive-feedback loop. Section 3.4 resolves it.

### 1.4 Parasitic Copper and Shunt Losses

| Element | R | P = I²R at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad to FET copper (2 oz, 6-layer, ×2 paths) | 0.25 mΩ ×2 | 3.2 W |
| Current shunt (if per-channel) | 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| **Subtotal** | | **4.8 W** |

### 1.5 Per-FET Distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° electrical, which is 1/3 of the cycle. The low-side FETs also carry the freewheeling (1 − D) interval, so they are the hot devices at low throttle with high current. Examples are punch-outs and prop-wash recovery.

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds} D, \qquad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{ds}(2 - D)$$

At D = 0.85, I = 80 A: P_HS ≈ 3.3 W and P_LS ≈ 4.4 W average. The instantaneous peak is I²R_ds = 11.5 W per FET.

## 2. Switching Loss Model (f_sw = 48 kHz)

### 2.1 Hard-Switching V–I Overlap

Only the active (high-side PWM) FET hard-switches. The synchronous FET switches at near-zero voltage after dead time.

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.060\ \text{W}$$

Reverse recovery also adds a turn-on current spike of roughly Q_rr / t_r. That spike drives ringing and EMI, which is why the RC snubber in section 5 is fitted.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 40\text{n} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = 0.024\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

There are two dead-time intervals per PWM period.

$$P_{dt} = V_f I \cdot 2t_{dead} f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 400\text{n} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.23\ W}$$

At 48 kHz, dead time is the second-largest switching term. Trimming it from 200 ns to 100 ns saves about 0.6 W per channel. Only do this if the gate driver's propagation-delay matching guarantees no shoot-through across temperature.

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g, not the FET.

$$P_g = Q_g V_{gs} f_{sw} \times 2 = 40\text{n} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = 0.038\ \text{W}$$

### 2.6 Why 48 kHz Instead of 24 kHz

At 48 kHz, f_sw-proportional loss is about 3.3 W per channel versus 1.6 W at 24 kHz. The benefit is lower current ripple:

$$\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5-inch motor, L_ph is about 15–30 µH line-to-line. At 48 kHz, ripple stays near 2–4 A. This reduces copper/iron loss
