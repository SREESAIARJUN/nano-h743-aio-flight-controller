# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Systems Architecture Package (Job #15)

## 0. Architecture Clarification

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and generates bidirectional DShot600. It does **not** commutate the motors directly. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, which performs the 48 kHz complementary PWM. A typical part is the AT32F421 or STM32G071. This split is standard because:

- ArduPilot's scheduler cannot guarantee microsecond-level commutation timing for four sensorless BLDC motors.
- The bidirectional DShot protocol assumes a separate ESC MCU on the far end of the link.

The 80 A figure below is treated as a **per-motor burst rating**.

### Baseline Assumptions

|---|---|---|
| Bus voltage | 6S, 25.2 V max (22.2 V nominal) | Worst case used for switching loss |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Six-Step Conduction Topology

At any instant, two phases carry current.

- **Phase A, active high-side:** the HS FET conducts for fraction D of each PWM period. The LS FET synchronously rectifies for (1 − D).
- **Phase B, return leg:** the LS FET conducts continuously for the full 60° sector.

The instantaneous conduction loss in the current path is:

$$P_{cond,inst} = I^2 R_{hot}\left[D + (1-D)\right] + I^2 R_{hot} = 2 I^2 R_{hot}$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** when synchronous rectification is used. This is a key simplification.

### 1.2 Including Ripple Current

The phase current ripple is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{(V_{bus} - V_{bemf}) D}{2 L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

For a typical 5″ motor, L_ph ≈ 15 µH. At D = 0.5 and V_bus − V_bemf ≈ 6 V, ΔI ≈ 2.1 A peak-to-peak.

The RMS current becomes:

$$I_{rms} = \sqrt{I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}} \approx I \quad (\text{error} < 0.01\%)$$

Ripple is therefore negligible at 48 kHz.

### 1.3 Burst Value Per Motor

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

Each FET conducts for at most 1/3 of an electrical cycle:

- 120° as a high side, split between HS and the synchronous LS.
- 120° as a return low side.

For a single low-side FET, the electrical-cycle average loss is:

$$P_{LS,avg} = \frac{1}{6}I^2R(1-D)\cdot 2 + \frac{1}{3}I^2 R \approx \frac{18.56}{6}\,(\text{evenly spread}) \approx 3.1\ \text{W}$$

The **peak** in-sector dissipation is I²R = **9.3 W**. This matters at low eRPM, where the electrical period approaches the junction thermal time constant (see §4.3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching phase leg has hard transitions. The return leg is static within a sector.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap (Hard-Switched HS Turn-On and Turn-Off)

$$P_{VI} = \frac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

A refined model with gate-driver-limited Miller plateau time is:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} R_G}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}$$

With Q_gd = 12 nC, R_G = 4.7 Ω (including internal resistance), V_drv = 10 V and V_pl = 4.5 V, this gives t_sw ≈ 10 ns per Miller transition. This is consistent with the assumed 18–22 ns total edge times.

### 2.2 Reverse Recovery (LS Body Diode During Dead Time, Then HS Turn-On)

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 45\text{nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.054\ W}$$

Q_rr scales roughly with I and di/dt. At 80 A, use about 1.5× the datasheet value, giving **≈0.08 W**.

### 2.3 Output Capacitance

$$P_{oss} = C_{oss(er)} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 1.6\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.049\ W}$$

This term covers charging and discharging both FETs in the leg.

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I\, (2 t_d) f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 100\text{ns} \times 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.31\ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate Drive

This loss is dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors, not the FETs.

$$P_{gate} = Q_g V_{drv} f_{sw} \times N_{switching} = 55\text{nC} \times 10 \times 48\text{k} \times 2 = \mathbf{0.053\ W}$$
