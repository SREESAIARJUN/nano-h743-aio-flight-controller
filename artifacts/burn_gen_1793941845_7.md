# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #45): Power-Stage, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and Feasibility

### 0.1 Architectural correction

The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. It does **not** commutate the bridges.

- Each motor needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware, such as an STM32G071, AT32F421 or GD32E230.
- The H743 cannot close four sensorless BEMF/commutation loops at 48 kHz PWM while also running the EKF3 at 400 Hz with deterministic timing.
- The H743 talks to the ESCs through bidirectional DShot600 (Section 3).

### 0.3 Feasibility flags

These must be resolved before layout.

1. **Total battery current.** 4 × 80 A means up to about 320 A peak phase current. Battery current is lower (I_dc ≈ D·I_ph) but can still exceed 150 A. This is outside what XT30, or XT60 on a 20×20 or 30×30 board, can carry.
2. **Duty and heat removal.** "80 A" is treated here strictly as a **burst rating of ≤ 2–3 s at ≤ 25 % duty**. A nano board cannot reject roughly 100 W continuously (Section 2.3).

## 1. Loss Model, Per Motor (One 6-FET Bridge)

### 1.1 Conduction loss

With 6-step trapezoidal drive, high-side PWM and complementary synchronous rectification, exactly **two FETs carry I_ph at all times**:

- PWM on-time: HS_x + LS_y.
- PWM off-time: LS_x + LS_y.

So:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
$$P_{cond} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

- R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.71 mΩ**
- P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.71×10⁻³ = **21.9 W**

Phase-current ripple adds a small term. With phase inductance L_ph ≈ 5 µH, the ripple peak-to-peak is:

$$\Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph}f_{sw}} \approx \frac{25.2 \times 0.25}{5\mu \times 48k} = 26\ \text{A}_{pp}\ (D=0.5)$$

The RMS correction is ΔI²/12 = 56 A². Including it:

- I_rms² = 6400 + 56 = 6456 A²
- P_cond = **22.1 W**

### 1.2 Copper loss

The phase path includes pad → plane → FET → plane → motor pad. With 2 oz copper on 4 layers, assume about 0.3 mΩ per conduction path.

- P_Cu = 2 × 80² × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W**

This is not negligible. Use 2 oz copper on all layers and stitch the planes.

### 1.3 Switching loss (hard-switched high side)

**Gate-drive currents through the Miller region:**

- Turn-on: I_G,on = (10 − 4) / 5 = 1.2 A, so t_on = 15 nC / 1.2 A = 12.5 ns.
- Turn-off: I_G,off = 4 / 5 = 0.8 A, so t_off = 15 nC / 0.8 A = 18.75 ns.

| Loss term | Formula | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V_bus · I_ph · (t_on + t_off) · f_sw = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 31.25 ns × 48 kHz | 1.51 W |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V_bus · f_sw = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | 0.06 W |
| Coss | ½ · Q_oss · V_bus · f_sw | 0.04 W |
| Dead-time diode | V_F · I_ph · 2t_d · f_sw = 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 kHz | 0.61 W |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_drv · f_sw | 0.06 W (mostly in driver/R_G) |
| **Total switching-related** | | **≈ 2.3 W** |

### 1.4 Per-motor and board totals at 80 A burst

| Loss term | Per motor | ×4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 22.1 W | 88.4 W |
| PCB copper | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Switching + dead time | 2.3 W | 9.2 W |
| Shunt (0.25 mΩ, I_dc ≈ 40 A) | 0.4 W | 1.6 W |
| **Total** | **28.6 W** | **≈ 114 W** |

**Efficiency check.** At D = 0.5, P_out ≈ 25.2 × 40 = 1008 W per motor, so bridge efficiency is about 97 %. The problem is heat density, not efficiency.

**Design lever.** Conduction dominates at about 80 % of losses, which makes 48 kHz a reasonable choice. Halving R_DS(on), either by paralleling FETs or using a sub-0.8 mΩ part, gives more benefit than any change to f_sw.

### 1.5 Per-FET distribution

Over an electrical cycle, each leg's FETs share the bridge loss. Two hot cases matter:

- **Average case:** about 4.0 W per FET.
- **Worst case: stalled or very low eRPM.** One HS/LS pair carries the full current continuously:
  - LS FET: about 80² × 1.71 m ≈ 10.9 W.
  - HS FET: 10.9 W + 1.5 W switching = 12.4 W.
