# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Power-Stage and DShot Design Model (Job #24)

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

Two constraints in the request need to be addressed before the model, because they change the design.

**1. The H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC controller.**
- A 4-in-1 ESC runs its own MCU per channel (or one shared MCU) with AM32 or BLHeli_32 firmware, typically on an STM32G071 or AT32F421.
- That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.
- The H743 running ArduPilot generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes the eRPM telemetry.
- The model below follows this split.

**2. 80 A burst per channel on a nano (20×20 mm) board does not close thermally.**
- Total battery current would be 4 × 80 A = 320 A.
- That exceeds any nano connector (XT30 ≈ 30 A, XT60 ≈ 60 A continuous) and the copper.
- The equations below show the feasible envelope is roughly 1–2 s bursts at 80 A per channel, from a thermally soaked board.
- I recommend either a 30.5×30.5 mm footprint or redefining "80 A" as a ≤1 s peak per channel, with a total battery limit of ~120–160 A.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

R_DS(T_j) = R_DS,25 · [1 + α (T_j − 25)]

At T_j = 100 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.36 = **1.63 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During any commutation sector, phase current I_ph flows through two conduction paths:

- **PWM'd leg:** the high-side conducts for D, and the synchronous low-side conducts for (1 − D). Together they carry I_ph 100 % of the time.
- **Static return leg:** the low-side conducts 100 % of the time.

Per-channel conduction loss is therefore:

P_cond = 2 · I_ph² · R_DS(T_j)

### 1.3 Converting battery current to phase current

I_ph ≈ I_bat / D

At burst with D → 1, I_ph = 80 A.

P_cond = 2 × 80² × 1.63 mΩ = **20.9 W per channel**

That is about 10.4 W per conducting FET.

### 1.4 Copper and shunt losses

| Element | Value | Loss at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| Phase pad/trace, 2 oz, ~0.3 mΩ effective | 0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Current-sense shunt (if per channel) | 0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |
| Shared bus plane at 320 A, ~0.1 mΩ | 0.1 mΩ | **10.2 W** (whole board) |

### 1.5 Self-heating feedback

Because R_DS rises with temperature, T_j and P_cond must be solved together:

T_j = T_case + R_θJC · P_FET(T_j)

Solving this iteratively converges in 3–4 steps. If the condition below is violated, the solution diverges, which means thermal runaway:

α · R_θJC · P_FET < 1

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg switches. Each loss term, per channel at I = 80 A, V = 25.2 V:

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V·I overlap (high-side) | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery (low-side body diode) | Q_rr · V · f_sw | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k = **0.06 W** |
| C_oss energy (2 FETs) | 2 · ½ · C_oss · V² · f_sw | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time body-diode conduction | V_f · I · 2 t_dt · f_sw | 0.8 × 80 × 200 ns × 48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (dissipated in the driver, not the FET) | 2 · Q_g · V_g · f_sw | **0.06 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.7 W per channel** |

**Sensitivity.** Overlap loss scales linearly with f_sw. Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.3 W per channel, at the cost of more current ripple and audible noise.
