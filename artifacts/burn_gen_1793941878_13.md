# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 ESC: Engineering Model (Job #34)

## 0. Architecture and Key Assumptions

The STM32H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It runs ArduPilot and talks bi-directional DShot600 to four dedicated ESC MCUs, each running AM32 at 48 kHz complementary PWM. That split is how every production AIO with this MCU is built. Dead-time insertion, 6-step commutation and BEMF sensing are hard-real-time tasks that do not belong on the flight-control core.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ \text{K}^{-1}$$

At T_j = 115 °C: R_ds = 1.2 mΩ × 1.405 ≈ **1.68 mΩ**.

### 1.2 Current-path topology

In 6-step trapezoidal commutation, exactly two phase legs conduct at any instant.

- Each leg is active for 120° of every 180° half-cycle, i.e. 2/3 of the electrical period.
- With synchronous (complementary) PWM, the current always flows through exactly one FET per active leg. That FET is either the high-side or the low-side, depending on the PWM state.
- So at any instant there are two R_ds(on) in series with the load current.

$$\boxed{P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)}\qquad
P_{cond,FET} \approx \tfrac{1}{3}\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

The per-FET value is a time-average over symmetric commutation.

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond per FET | (1/3)(6400)(1.68 mΩ) = **3.58 W** |
| P_cond per channel | 2 × 6400 × 1.68 mΩ = **21.5 W** |
| P_cond, 4 channels | **86 W** |

**Copper path loss:** add the copper resistance from bus to phase pad (R_cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ effective for 2 oz plus vias). This contributes 2 × 6400 × 0.3 mΩ ≈ **3.8 W per channel**. It is often forgotten, and it heats the same board.
