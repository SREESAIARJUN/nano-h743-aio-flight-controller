## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A burst)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,\big[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\big],\qquad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /\mathrm{K}$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) |
|---|---|
| 25 °C | 0.95 mΩ |
| 100 °C | 1.27 mΩ |
| 125 °C | 1.38 mΩ (design value) |

Add PCB copper and solder in the current path: about 0.25 mΩ per phase leg **[A]**. This gives an effective R_leg ≈ 1.63 mΩ at a hot junction.

### 1.2 Conduction topology

In 6-step operation exactly two phases carry current at any time. The current path always includes one high-side position and one low-side position.

- **Active (PWM) phase:** the high-side FET (HS) conducts for D·T_sw. The synchronous low-side FET (LS) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, less dead time.
- **Return phase:** its LS is held fully on.

Two R_DS are therefore always in series, regardless of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{DS}(T_j) \;+\; I_{ph}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

At I = 80 A and T_j = 125 °C:

$$P_{cond} = 6400 \times 2 \times 1.38\,\mathrm{m\Omega} + 6400 \times 0.5\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = 17.66 + 3.2 = 20.9\ \mathrm{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-device distribution

Each phase spends 1/3 of the electrical cycle as the PWM phase, 1/3 as the return phase, and 1/3 floating.

Worst-case single-FET average (an LS that serves as both the return device and the synchronous rectifier):

$$P_{LS,avg} \approx I^2R_{DS}\left[\tfrac13 + \tfrac13(1-D)\right]$$

At D = 0.9: 8.83 W × 0.367 ≈ **3.2 W average**.

The instantaneous peak while conducting is I²R = **8.8 W**. Use this peak for the junction transient (Section 3.3).

### 1.4 Ripple correction

The phase-current RMS within a PWM period is

$$I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \Delta I^2/12, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}$$

With L_ph = 15 µH, D = 0.5 and V = 25.2 V: ΔI = 8.75 A. The correction term is ΔI²/12 = 6.4 A², about 0.1 % of 6400 A². It is negligible at 80 A, but it matters for low-inductance motors under 5 µH.
