# STM32H743 4-in-1 ESC Flight Controller (Job #62): Systems Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan and Netlist

## 0. Feasibility Problems to Resolve First

The analysis below shows that the brief, as written, cannot be built. Three issues need decisions before layout.

1. **80 A × 4 channels on a 20×20 mm nano board is not thermally viable.** Section 2 calculates about 88 W of dissipation at full burst. A 20×20 board has roughly 8 cm² of surface area. Even with prop wash, the steady-state temperature rise would be several hundred kelvin. You need one of the following:
   - (a) a 30.5×30.5 mm or larger board with a clamped heatsink plate,
   - (b) a lower burst rating (40–55 A) at 20×20, or
   - (c) "80 A" redefined as a short burst on one channel rather than all four at once.
2. **The H743 should not commutate the motors.** Production 4-in-1 boards run each bridge from a dedicated small MCU, such as the AT32F421 running AM32 or the EFM8/STM32G0 family. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot to those MCUs. Running FOC or 6-step for four motors on the H743 alongside the ArduPilot scheduler is not supported upstream. The architecture below assumes the split design.
3. **Assumed MOSFET.** I have parameterised the analysis on a representative 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm or 5×6 mm N-channel part. Substitute your selected part's datasheet values.

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$
