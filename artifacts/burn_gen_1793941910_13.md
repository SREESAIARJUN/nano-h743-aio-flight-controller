**Architectural flags to resolve before layout:**

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In every practical 4-in-1 design, each motor (or the 4-in-1 as a whole) has its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, e.g. 4× AT32F421 or STM32G071. That MCU runs the 48 kHz commutation. The H743 only sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. The model below keeps those two domains separate.
2. **80 A is a burst rating only on this form factor.** Section 3 shows the continuous rating is roughly 34 A per channel. Market it as "35 A continuous / 80 A for ≤5 s", or add paralleled FETs.
3. **A true 20×20 "nano" board cannot carry this current.** The thermal mass math below assumes a 30.5 mm board.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (Six-Step, Synchronous Complementary PWM)

### 1.1 Conduction pattern

In each 60° commutation sector, one leg is PWM'd and one leg's low side is held fully on:

- **On-time (duty D):** current flows through HS_x and LS_y.
- **Off-time (1−D):** current flows through LS_x (synchronous rectification) and LS_y.

The current therefore **always flows through exactly two FETs**. That gives a clean closed form for the whole channel:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I^2\,R_{ds,on}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Per-device distribution

Each leg spends 1/3 of the electrical period as the PWM'd leg and 1/3 as the held-low leg.

$$P_{HS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,D}{3}, \qquad P_{LS} = \frac{I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,\left[(1-D)+1\right]}{3}$$

- The worst-case low-side FET appears at D → 0: P_LS = (2/3)·I²R. This only matters when D is low *and* current is high (low-throttle stall or desync).
- At full-throttle burst (D → 1), every FET carries I²R/3.

### 1.3 Temperature dependence

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds} = 1.0\ \text{mΩ}\times(1+0.0045\times85) = 1.38\ \text{mΩ}$$
