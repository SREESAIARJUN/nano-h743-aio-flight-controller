## 0. Architecture Clarification and Design Basis

**Topology note.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, the STM32H743 does not commutate the motors. It runs ArduPilot and generates DShot. Each motor channel needs its own ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32, for example an STM32G071 or AT32F421, plus a 3-phase gate driver. The model below follows that partitioning.

The 80 A figure is treated as a **time-limited burst**, not a continuous rating. Section 3 quantifies the burst duration.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation Model

The ESC runs synchronous (complementary) 6-step commutation. At any instant, one high-side FET and one low-side FET carry I_ph.

- During the PWM off-time, the low-side FET of the PWM leg conducts by synchronous rectification.
- The total conduction path is therefore always two R_ds(on) in series, independent of duty cycle D.

**Bridge conduction loss per channel:**

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds,hot} = 2 (80)^2 (1.8\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{23.0\ W}$$

**Per-FET conduction loss.** Each FET is active for 1/3 of the electrical period. At D → 1 this gives:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \tfrac{1}{3} I_{ph}^2 R_{ds,hot} = \mathbf{3.84\ W}\ \text{(average)}$$

The value above is a time average. The instantaneous dissipation while a FET conducts is 11.5 W. Section 3.3 shows this matters for junction ripple.

At partial duty, the PWM-leg high-side FET conducts for a fraction D of its 1/3 window, and its low-side partner for (1 − D).
