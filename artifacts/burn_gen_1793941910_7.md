## 0. Architecture Corrections and Key Findings

Three issues shape every derivation below.

### 0.1 The H743 cannot commutate the motors

ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It does not run six-step or FOC commutation. The correct partition is:

```
┌────────────────────────── 20×20 / 30.5×30.5 stack or single PCB ─────────────────────────┐
│  STM32H743 (ArduPilot) ──BDShot600 ×4──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32/BLHeli_32)  │
│                                          └─► 3-ph gate driver ─► 6 MOSFETs per motor (24 total)│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs. It is set in the AM32/BLHeli_32 configuration, not in ArduPilot.
- The H743 owns DShot generation, bidirectional eRPM capture, and the notch filters fed by eRPM.

### 0.2 80 A burst on a nano footprint is thermally marginal

As §3 shows, 80 A burst on all four channels dissipates roughly 100 W.

- A 20×20 board absorbs that adiabatically for only about 1–1.5 s.
- Continuous capability is roughly 15–20 A per channel without a heatsink.

The rating must therefore be specified as a time-limited burst, for example 80 A for 1 s and 20 A continuous. Otherwise the board must grow to 30.5×30.5 or larger.

## 2. Loss Model per Channel

### 2.1 Conduction topology

The model assumes six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM.

- During the ON interval, current flows through HS(phase x) and LS(phase y).
- During the OFF interval, current freewheels through LS(phase x) and LS(phase y). The synchronous FET carries it, not the diode.

At every instant except dead time, exactly two FETs carry the full current I. Duty cycle therefore cancels out of the total, although it does redistribute loss between the HS and LS devices.

### 2.2 Conduction loss

Junction-temperature-dependent resistance:

$$
R_{ds}(T_J) = R_{ds25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_J - 25)]
$$

$$
P_{cond} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_J) + I^2 R_{cu}
$$

At T_J = 125 °C, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ:

$$
P_{cond} = 2(80)^2(1.5\text{m}) + (80)^2(0.5\text{m}) = 19.2 + 3.2 = \mathbf{22.4\ W}
$$

Per-FET split at duty D:

- HS(x): $I^2 R_{ds}D$
- LS(x): $I^2 R_{ds}(1-D)$
- LS(y): $I^2 R_{ds}$

**The always-on low-side FET is the hottest device: 9.6 W at 80 A.**

**Thermal-runaway check.** The model has a self-consistent solution only if

$$
\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\,R_{\theta} = 2I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta < 1
$$

With an effective junction-to-ambient R_θ ≈ 12 K/W:

$$
6400 \times 1\text{m} \times 0.005 \times 12 = 0.38 < 1
$$

The system is stable, but the 1/(1−0.38) ≈ 1.6× multiplier on the temperature rise must be included.

### 2.3 Switching losses at 48 kHz

One device per channel hard-switches per PWM edge: HS(x).

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\text{n})(48\text{k}) = 1.45\ \text{W}
$$

**Reverse recovery** (the LS body diode conducts during dead time):

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}
$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$
P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two dead times per period):

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot200\text{n}\cdot48\text{k} = 0.61\ \text{W}
$$

**Gate drive** (two switching FETs; this loss lands in the driver and gate resistors):

$$
P_g = 2\,Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2\cdot50\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

$$
P_{sw,total} \approx \mathbf{2.2\ W/channel}
$$

**Frequency sensitivity.** Switching loss scales linearly with f_sw.
