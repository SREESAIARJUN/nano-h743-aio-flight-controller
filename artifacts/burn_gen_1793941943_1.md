## 1. Kinematics: Camera Rates to Joint Rates

The axis order is yaw ψ (z) → roll φ (x) → pitch θ (y), from base to camera. Let ω_b be the base angular velocity, R_x(φ) and R_y(θ) the elementary rotations, and e_x, e_y, e_z the unit axes.

The camera-frame angular velocity is:

$$
\boldsymbol\omega_c = R_y^\top(\theta)R_x^\top(\phi)\,R_z^\top(\psi)\,\boldsymbol\omega_b \;+\; \underbrace{\big[\,R_y^\top R_x^\top \mathbf e_z \;\big|\; R_y^\top \mathbf e_x \;\big|\; \mathbf e_y\,\big]}_{J(\phi,\theta)}\begin{bmatrix}\dot\psi\\ \dot\phi\\ \dot\theta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The rate loop therefore regulates joint rates, with the base motion as a feedforward term:

$$
\dot{\mathbf q}_{cmd} = J^{-1}\big(\boldsymbol\omega_{c,cmd} - \boldsymbol\omega_{b\to c}\big),\qquad
\dot{\mathbf q}_{meas} = J^{-1}\big(\boldsymbol\omega_{c,meas} - \boldsymbol\omega_{b\to c}\big)
$$

- **Without a base IMU,** set ω_{b→c} = 0. Then J⁻¹ω_c,meas is the "effective" joint rate the camera sees. This is the standard stabilization approach.
- **Singularity:** det J = cos φ. Clamp |φ| < 80° or saturate the inverse near gimbal lock.

## 2. Field-Oriented Control

### 2.1 Electrical angle

$$
\theta_e = p\,(\theta_m - \theta_{m,0}) \bmod 2\pi
$$

Calibrate the offset θ_m,0 by applying v_d > 0, v_q = 0 at θ_e = 0 and reading the encoder once the rotor has settled.

### 2.2 Clarke transform (amplitude-invariant, i_a + i_b + i_c = 0)

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

### 2.3 Park and inverse Park transforms

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\begin{bmatrix} v_\alpha\\ v_\beta\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & -\sin\theta_e\\ \sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} v_d\\ v_q\end{bmatrix}
$$

### 2.4 Plant and current PI design
