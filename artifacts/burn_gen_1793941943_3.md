# 3-Axis Gimbal: FOC, Cascaded Control, Anti-Cogging, and Dual-IMU Vibration Rejection

**About the hardware assumptions:** I don't have the spec sheet for Job #38. The design below assumes a typical brushless gimbal:

- **Motors:** 12N14P outrunner (GBM2804-class), 7 pole pairs.
- **Encoders:** 14-bit magnetic, one per motor (MT6701 / AS5047-class), used for commutation.
- **IMUs:** Two ICM-42688-P on the camera plate, on SPI at 8 kHz ODR.
- **MCU:** STM32G4/H7-class with an FPU.

Every hardware-dependent constant is gathered in a single `Config` struct (Section 7). Replace those values with your measured R, L, Kt and J before closing any loop.

## 1. Architecture and Loop Rates

```
          ┌────────────────── 1 kHz ─────────────────┐ ┌──── 4 kHz ────┐ ┌──── 20 kHz ────┐
 q_ref ─► Attitude P ─► ω_ref ─► Velocity PI ─► iq_fb ─►(+)─► Current PI (d,q) ─► SVPWM ─► Motor
            ▲    (joint-space)       ▲                     ▲ iq_ff (cogging + inertia)   ▲
            │                        │                     │                             │
   Dual-IMU fusion ─► Mahony AHRS    gyro rate (projected)   Cogging FF (Fourier/LMS)    encoder θ_e, ia, ib
   (notch + LPF + inverse-variance)  + encoder PLL blend
```

The design keeps a bandwidth separation of at least 5× between nested loops, so each inner loop looks like unity gain to the loop above it:

| Loop | Rate | Bandwidth target |
|---|---|---|
| Current (d/q) | 20 kHz | ω_c ≈ 2π·1500 rad/s |
| Velocity | 4 kHz | ω_v ≈ 2π·150 rad/s |
| Attitude/angle | 1 kHz | ω_p ≈ 2π·25 rad/s |
| IMU ODR | 8 kHz | decimated ×2 to 4 kHz after filtering |

## 2. FOC Current Loop

### 2.1 Transforms

The Clarke transform is amplitude-invariant and assumes balanced phases, so i_c = −i_a − i_b:

$$
i_\alpha = i_a,\qquad i_\beta = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}(i_a + 2 i_b)
$$

The Park transform uses the electrical angle θ_e = p·θ_m + θ_off:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} i_d\\ i_q\end{bmatrix}=
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e\\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha\\ i_\beta\end{bmatrix}
$$

The inverse Park transform is

$$
v_\alpha = v_d\cos\theta_e - v_q\sin\theta_e,\qquad v_\beta = v_d\sin\theta_e + v_q\cos\theta_e
$$

### 2.2 Plant and decoupling

For a surface PMSM (L_d ≈ L_q = L):

$$
v_d = R i_d + L\dot i_d - \omega_e L i_q,\qquad
v_q = R i_q + L\dot i_q + \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e \lambda_m
$$

The controller adds a decoupling feedforward:

$$
v_d^{ff} = -\omega_e L i_q,\qquad v_q^{ff} = \omega_e L i_d + \omega_e\lambda_m
$$

With this feedforward in place, each axis reduces to $G_i(s) = 1/(Ls+R)$.

### 2.3 PI gains by pole–zero cancellation

Choose the PI so that its zero cancels the electrical pole:

$$
C_i(s)=K_p + \frac{K_i}{s},\quad K_p = L\,\omega_c,\quad K_i = R\,\omega_c
\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{i}{i^*}=\frac{\omega_c}{s+\omega_c}
$$

### 2.4 Discrete PI with back-calculation anti-windup

Using forward Euler with sample time $T_s$:

$$
u_k^{\text{raw}} = K_p e_k + I_k,\qquad u_k = \operatorname{sat}(u_k^{\text{raw}})
$$

$$
I_{k+1} = I_k + K_i T_s e_k + K_{aw} T_s\,(u_k - u_k^{\text{raw}}),\qquad K_{aw}\approx K_i/K_p
$$

### 2.5 Voltage limiting and SVPWM

The voltage vector is limited circularly to $|v|\le V_{bus}/\sqrt3$, with d-axis priority:

$$
v_{q,\max}=\sqrt{V_{max}^2-v_d^2}
$$

Duties are then computed by min-max zero-sequence injection, which is equivalent to SVPWM:

$$
v_{abc}=\text{invClarke}(v_\alpha,v_\beta),\quad v_0=-\tfrac12(\max v_{abc}+\min v_{abc}),\quad D_x=\tfrac12+\frac{v_x+v_0}{V_{bus}}
$$
