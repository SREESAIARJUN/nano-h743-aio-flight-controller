# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano): Power-Stage, Thermal & DShot Architecture

**Job #5: Reference Design Analysis, Rev A**

---

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Feasibility Verdict

Three issues need resolving before the math, because they change the design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot is flight-control firmware. It has no sensorless FOC or trapezoidal ESC firmware. The correct partition is:
   - The **STM32H743** runs ArduPilot and emits 4× bidirectional DShot600.
   - **4× ESC MCUs** run AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware and handle 48 kHz PWM commutation. Typical parts are the AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor.

   An "all-in-one" board puts both on one PCB, but they remain separate firmware domains.

2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is not continuously achievable.** As shown in §3, an 80 A channel dissipates about 23–30 W. The board's lumped thermal capacitance is only about 5–6 J/K. Treat 80 A as a burst rating of 1–3 s on a single channel, with all four channels limited to about 35–45 A continuous. A rating sheet that claims "4×80 A" for this footprint is marketing, not physics.

3. **All numbers below rest on stated assumptions.** Parameters are representative of 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN MOSFETs. Netlists use **pin function names**, not package pin numbers. They must be bound to the final part datasheets before layout. This is a reference design, not a release package.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current path in 6-step, synchronous complementary PWM

During any commutation sector, phase A is PWM'd and phase B is low-side ON:

| Device | Conduction fraction |
|---|---|
| Q_AH (high side, PWM leg) | D |
| Q_AL (sync rectifier, same leg) | 1 − D |
| Q_BL (return leg) | 1 |

The conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

**Key result:** with synchronous rectification, conduction loss is independent of duty cycle. It is set by motor current alone.

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (self-consistent)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

$$
T_j = T_{pcb} + P_{FET}\,R_{\theta,j\text{-}pcb}
$$

These two equations are coupled. Iterate them, because thermal runaway exists if $2I^2R_{25}\alpha\,R_\theta \ge 1$.

**Worked case at 80 A:**

| T_j | R_DS | P_cond = 2·6400·R_DS |
|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.00 mΩ | 12.8 W |
| 85 °C | 1.33 mΩ | 17.0 W |
| 110 °C | 1.47 mΩ | 18.8 W |
| 125 °C | 1.55 mΩ | 19.8 W |

Design value: **P_cond ≈ 19 W per channel at 80 A, T_j ≈ 110 °C.**

### 1.3 PCB copper loss (frequently ignored, not negligible)

Assume 2 oz copper on 4 parallel layers. The phase path plus the bus path to the bridge is about 0.3 mΩ effective, with 2 legs in the current path:

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 I^2 R_{Cu} = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.3\text{ mΩ} \approx 3.8\text{ W}
$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per sector.

### 2.1 V-I overlap loss (dominant)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Reverse recovery (Q_AL body diode → Q_AH turn-on)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 40\text{nC} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\text{ W}
$$

### 2.3 C_oss charge/discharge (two devices per leg)

$$
P_{oss} = 2 \cdot \tfrac12 C_{oss} V^2 f_{sw} = 1.5\text{nF} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.046\text{ W}
$$
