## 0. Architecture Decision and Assumptions

### 0.1 Critical architecture point

The STM32H743 should **not** commutate the motors directly.

- Driving four 3-phase bridges with complementary PWM needs 24 gated outputs with dead-time. The H743 has only two advanced timers (TIM1 and TIM8). HRTIM could stretch this, but it would be a custom firmware path.
- ArduPilot does not support it.

The production-sane topology, and the one assumed throughout this document, is:

```
STM32H743 (ArduPilot FC) ──4× bi-dir DShot600──► 4× ESC MCU (AT32F421 / STM32G071, AM32 or BLHeli_32-class)
                                                  └─► 3-phase gate driver ─► 6× N-MOSFET per motor
```

The 48 kHz PWM is generated by each ESC MCU. The H743 owns only the DShot links, telemetry ingestion (eRPM to harmonic notch), and flight control.

## 1. Commutation and Current-Path Model

Assume 6-step trapezoidal control with synchronous rectification (complementary PWM), the default for AM32/BLHeli_32.

**During the PWM on-time (fraction D):** current flows from V_bus through the high-side FET of phase A, through the motor (A to B), then through the low-side FET of phase B.

**During the off-time (1 − D):** the high-side turns off. After dead time, the low-side of phase A turns on, and current freewheels through LS_A and LS_B.

**Consequence:** at every instant, **exactly two FETs carry I_ph**, independent of duty cycle. Duty cycle only redistributes which FETs heat. Over a full electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs carries current about 1/3 of the time in some state.

Phase current I_ph is the motor current, not the battery current:

$$I_{bat} = D \cdot I_{ph} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{80 A phase at } D=0.8 \to 64\text{ A per ESC from battery}$$

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 MOSFET conduction

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{ds(on)}(T_j) \approx R_{ds,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right]$$

with α ≈ 0.005 /K, which gives k_T = 1.5 at 125 °C.

| Condition | R_ds(on) | P_cond per ESC |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (25 °C) | 1.0 mΩ | 80² · 2 · 1.0e-3 = **12.8 W** |
| Hot (125 °C) | 1.5 mΩ | 80² · 2 · 1.5e-3 = **19.2 W** |

### 2.2 Copper conduction

Copper resistance rises about 0.39 %/K, so at +60 K the 0.5 mΩ path becomes ≈ 0.62 mΩ.

$$P_{Cu} = I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}(T) = 6400 \cdot 0.62\text{e-}3 \approx \textbf{4.0 W}$$

This term is routinely forgotten. On a 20×20 or 30×30 nano board it is a significant fraction of the FET loss.

### 2.3 Per-FET distribution

The averaged per-FET dissipation is P_cond/3 ≈ 6.4 W hot.

However, the thermal design must use the **instantaneous** value of 9.6 W per conducting FET. The commutation period at high eRPM (≈ 0.3–1 ms) is far shorter than the package thermal time constant, so the average governs the board, and the instantaneous value plus Z_th governs the junction ripple.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Per commutation interval, only the PWM-switched leg hard-switches. That means one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off per period, with the synchronous-rectifier FET soft-switching via dead time.

### 3.1 V–I overlap

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \textbf{0.97 W}$$

### 3.2 Reverse recovery

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 30\text{e-}9 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = 0.036\text{ W}$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature, so derate ×2–3, giving ≈ **0.1 W**.

### 3.3 Body-diode dead-time conduction

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{e-}9 \cdot 48\text{e}3 = \textbf{0.61 W}$$
