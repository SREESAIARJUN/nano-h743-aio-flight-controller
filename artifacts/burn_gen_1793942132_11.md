# STM32H743 Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Nano Stack): Design Package, Job #12

## 0. Scope, Architecture Assumptions and Feasibility Flag

The H743 does not drive the MOSFETs directly. It runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs, which commutate the bridges at 48 kHz. Those MCUs are typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 parts running AM32 or BLHeli_32.

```
[STM32H743 FC] --4x Bidir DShot600--> [4x ESC MCU (AM32)] --6x PWM--> [4x 3-ph gate driver] --> [4x 6-FET bridge]
```

**Engineering flag:** 80 A per motor on a nano footprint (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) is thermally achievable only as a short burst of a few seconds. The model below quantifies that limit. Every component parameter is a **representative assumption**. Substitute datasheet values before release.

| Symbol | Value | Basis (assumption) |
|---|---|---|
| V_bus | 25.2 V | 6S, full charge |
| I_burst | 80 A per ESC (phase RMS ≈ DC-equivalent) | Spec |
| f_sw | 48 kHz | Spec |
| R_DS(on),25 | 1.0 mΩ | 40 V, 3.3×3.3 mm DFN-class FET |
| k_T | 1.5 at T_j = 125 °C | Typical PTC of R_DS(on) |
| t_r + t_f | 30 ns total (at 80 A) | ~1–2 Ω gate path, 1 A-class driver |
| t_dt | 100 ns per edge | Dead time |
| V_SD | 0.8 V | Body diode |
| Q_rr | 40 nC | At 80 A, high di/dt |
| Q_oss | 50 nC | At 25 V |
| Q_g | 50 nC at V_GS = 10 V | |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W | Per FET, to board |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

**Topology.** Synchronous-rectified 6-step (or FOC) drive conducts current through one high-side and one low-side path at all times. During the PWM off-time, the active high-side FET hands off to its complementary low-side FET, so the current path count stays at two.

$$R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)],\quad \alpha\approx0.005\,\text{K}^{-1}\Rightarrow k_T\approx1.5$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = 2(80)^2(1.5\,\text{m}\Omega) = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per-FET distribution.** Each phase is active 2/3 of the electrical cycle. Within that window, the high-side and low-side split the time by duty cycle D (high-side) and 1−D (low-side).

$$P_{HS} = I^2 R\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}D,\qquad P_{LS}= I^2 R\cdot\tfrac{2}{3}(1-D)\ \ (\text{plus the full low-side share of the returning phase})$$

Averaged over all phases, the per-FET load is 19.2 W / 6 = **3.2 W**. The worst instantaneous FET at D → 1 sees I²R = **9.6 W**.

**Copper losses** are not negligible at 80 A. Assume 2 oz copper, 35 µm × 2 layers, and a phase trace of 10 mm × 4 mm on 4 layers in parallel, giving R_Cu ≈ 0.3 mΩ:

$$P_{Cu}\approx 2\cdot I^2\cdot R_{Cu} = 2(6400)(0.3\,\text{m}\Omega)\approx 3.8\ \text{W}$$

Add solder and pad resistance (≈0.1 mΩ per path) for a further ≈1.3 W.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only one leg hard-switches per PWM period in 6-step (complementary PWM on the active high-side).

**(a) V–I overlap:**
$$P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(30\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**(b) Dead-time body-diode conduction** (2 edges per period):
$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw} = 0.8(80)(200\,\text{ns})(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** (diode Q_rr dumped into the opposite FET):
$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 40\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**(d) Output capacitance:**
$$P_{oss} = Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\,\text{nC}(25.2)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.060\ W}$$

**(e) Gate drive** (2 switching FETs per period, dissipated in the driver and gate resistors):
$$P_g = 2\,Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw} = 2(50\,\text{nC})(10)(48\,\text{k}) = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total}\approx \mathbf{2.2\ W\ per\ ESC}$$

**Ripple check.** Assume a motor phase inductance of L ≈ 15 µH, typical for a 2207-class motor at a 0.5 duty point:

$$\Delta I_{pp} = \frac{V_{bus}D(1-D)}{L f_{sw}} = \frac{25.2(0.25)}{15\,\mu\text{H}\cdot48\,\text{k}} \approx 8.75\ \text{A}$$

The resulting RMS correction is I_rms² = I² + ΔI²/12, about +6.4 A² or 0.1 %. This is negligible.

## 3. Total Loss Budget

| Loss term | Per ESC | 4-in-1 (all motors at burst) |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (hot) | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching + dead time + Q_rr + Q_oss | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Copper / solder | 5.1 W | 20.4 W |
| Gate drive, LDO, MCU | 0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈26.8 W** | **≈107 W** |

At 4 × 80 A × 25.2 V ≈ 8 kW input, the board-level efficiency is ≈98.7 %. That figure looks good, but 107 W concentrated in roughly 6 cm² is the binding constraint.

## 4. Thermal Model

### 4.1 Steady State (Proof That 80 A Continuous Is Infeasible)

The thermal path runs junction → R_θJC → board → R_θBA (convective) → ambient.

Assume a 25.5 × 25.5 mm board, two sides, A ≈ 13 cm², with prop-wash forced convection h ≈ 60 W/m²K:

$$R_{\theta BA} = \frac{1}{hA} = \frac{1}{60\times1.3\times10^{-3}} \approx 12.8\ \text{K/W}$$

$$\Delta T_{BA} = 107 \times 12.8 \approx 1370\ \text{K}\ \Rightarrow\ \textbf{not survivable}$$

The steady-state capacity at T_board = 100 °C and T_amb = 35 °C is P_max ≈ 65/12.8 ≈ 5 W total. Since P ∝ I², this corresponds to roughly **17–20 A per motor continuous**.

### 4.2 Transient (Burst) Model: Lumped RC Foster Network

The board heat capacity, assuming FR4 + Cu ≈ 9 g, a 6–8 layer stack, and 2 oz inner copper, is:

$$C_{board} = m_{FR4}c_{FR4} + m_{Cu}c_{Cu} \approx 5(1.1) + 4(0.385) \approx 7.0\ \text{J/K}$$

$$\tau_{board} = R_{\theta BA}C_{board} = 12.8\times7.0\approx 90\ \text{s}$$

For bursts of t ≪ τ, the board heating is essentially adiabatic:

$$T_{board}(t) = T_{amb} + P\,R_{\theta BA}\left(1-e^{-t/\tau}\right)\approx T_{amb}+\frac{P\,t}{C}$$

The rate is 107 W / 7 J/K ≈ **15.3 K/s**.

### 4.3 Junction Over Board

Use the worst-case FET at D → 1: 9.6 W conduction plus about 1 W switching share, so ≈10.6 W.
