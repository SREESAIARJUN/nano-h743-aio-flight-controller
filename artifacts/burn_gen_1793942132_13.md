# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #38): Power, Thermal, and DShot Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions, and a Feasibility Warning

**Architecture.** The STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and does not commutate motors. Each motor channel has its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM and talks bidirectional DShot600 back to the H743. The design therefore splits into three domains:

1. **Power stage.** 4 × 3-phase bridges with 24 MOSFETs, gate drivers, and bulk capacitance.
2. **ESC control.** 4 × ESC MCUs running AM32 at 48 kHz center-aligned PWM with complementary (synchronous) switching.
3. **Flight controller.** STM32H743 sends DShot600 out and receives eRPM telemetry back over a GCR-encoded line.

Specify the burst duration explicitly, e.g. "80 A / 1 s, 1 channel" or "55 A / 10 s". Validate it with thermocouple and IR testing before publishing.

## 1. Electrical Operating Model

AM32 uses 6-step (trapezoidal) commutation with complementary PWM. In each 60° commutation sector:

- **Phase A (PWM phase).** The high-side switch conducts for duty D. The low-side switch conducts for (1 − D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Phase B (return phase).** The low-side switch is on for 100% of the sector.
- **Phase C** is floating, used for BEMF zero-crossing sensing.

At any instant, current flows through exactly two MOSFET channels in series:

$$R_{path} = 2\,R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + R_{Cu} + R_{shunt}$$

Battery-side current is I_bat ≈ D · I_phase. At full-throttle burst, D → ~0.9–1.0.

## 2. Thermal Model

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

The hot channel resistance is

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha(T_j - 25)\right] \approx k_T\,R_{ds(on),25} = 1.6\ \text{m}\Omega$$

Conduction loss per motor channel, independent of D with synchronous rectification, is

$$P_{cond} = I^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\underbrace{[D + (1-D)]}_{\text{phase A}} + I^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j)\cdot\underbrace{1}_{\text{phase B}} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds}(T_j)$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.6\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{20.5\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper and shunt losses.** Assume 0.3 mΩ of total phase-path copper on 2 oz inner layers, plus a 0.25 mΩ shunt if the shunt sits in the phase path. Most 4-in-1 boards use one shared DC-link shunt instead.

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$
