# STM32H743 FC + 4-in-1 80 A ESC (Job #9): Thermal, Switching, DShot DMA, and Netlist Reference

## 0. Architecture Corrections

Three assumptions in the brief would break the design. They are corrected here, and the rest of the document is built on the corrections.

| Issue | Problem | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **H743 driving the ESC** | The H743 is the flight controller MCU. A 48 kHz 6-step commutation loop with BEMF zero-cross detection on 4 motors does not belong on it. | Use 4 dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 or STM32G071) running AM32 with bidirectional DShot. The H743 sends DShot600 and receives eRPM telemetry. |
| **"Nano" (20×20 mm) at 80 A ×4** | 24 MOSFETs rated for 80 A burst (5×6 mm PDFN) physically do not fit. Section 2 also shows ~85 W of dissipation, which a 20×20 board cannot survive even transiently. | Use a 30.5×30.5 mm stack minimum: FC board on top, separate ESC board below. The 80 A figure is a ≤5–7 s burst rating, not continuous. |
| **Single-board "nano"** | Switching noise at 85 W next to the IMU degrades gyro data. | Use a two-board stack with an 8-pin JST-SH interconnect. Details are in Section 5. |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)],\quad \alpha \approx 0.0045\ /^\circ C$$

At the design point T_j = 125 °C:

$$R_{DS} = 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega \times 1.45 = 1.45\,\text{m}\Omega$$

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° sector, the phase current always flows through exactly **two FETs in series**:
- the always-on low-side FET of the sinking phase, and
- either the HS FET (during D) or the LS FET (during 1−D) of the PWM leg.

Because synchronous rectification is used, duty cycle cancels out of the conduction total:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j), \qquad I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}$$

Ignoring ripple (ΔI ≪ I at 80 A with typical motor inductance):

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.45\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{18.56\ W}$$

Averaged over the electrical cycle, this is about 3.1 W per FET (six FETs per channel).

PCB copper adds loss. With 2 oz copper, a ~0.3 mΩ phase path gives 80² × 0.3 m ≈ **1.9 W per channel**. This is budgeted separately in Section 3.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Hard switching occurs on the PWM leg only.

| Term | Equation | Value per channel |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f_sw = ½ · 25.2 · 80 · 40 ns · 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Output capacitance | Q_oss · V · f = 40 nC · 25.2 · 48 k | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f · I · 2t_dt · f = 0.8 · 80 · 200 ns · 48 k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_GS · f = 2 · 60 nC · 10 V · 48 k | 0.06 W (mostly dissipated in the driver) |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.72 W** |

**Sensitivity.** P_sw scales linearly with f_sw. At 24 kHz it falls to about 1.4 W, but motor current ripple and audible noise rise. 48 kHz is acceptable because conduction loss dominates by roughly 7:1.
