## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths per Commutation Sector

In any 60° sector with complementary PWM on the switching leg (phase A high side switching, phase B low side held on):

- **Q_AH** conducts for fraction D.
- **Q_AL** conducts for fraction (1−D) as the synchronous rectifier.
- **Q_BL** conducts 100% of the sector.

So the instantaneous conduction loss per bridge is:

$$P_{cond,bridge} = I^2 R_{hot}\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

This is **independent of duty**, which is the key benefit of synchronous rectification. Without it, Q_AL's share becomes the body diode loss V_SD·I·(1−D), which is far worse.

### 1.2 Hot Resistance

$$R_{hot} = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)] \approx 1.2\,\text{mΩ} \times 1.6 = 1.92\,\text{mΩ} \quad (T_j = 125\,°\text{C})$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A Burst

$$P_{cond,bridge} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.92\times10^{-3} = 24.6\ \text{W}$$

### 1.4 PCB Copper Loss

Assume a phase-path resistance (pad → plane → motor pad) of about 0.5 mΩ, with two phases carrying current:

$$P_{Cu} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.5\times10^{-3} = 6.4\ \text{W per channel}$$

The DC bus-plane loss is separate and is covered in §5.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Q_AH turns on into Q_AL's body diode after the dead time.

### 2.1 V–I Overlap

$$E_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} = 30.2\ \mu\text{J}$$
$$P_{sw} = E_{sw} f_{sw} = 30.2\,\mu\text{J}\times 48\,\text{kHz} = 1.45\ \text{W}$$

### 2.2 Dead-Time Body Diode Conduction

Two dead times per period, t_dt = 50 ns each:

$$P_{dt} = V_{SD}\,I\,(2 t_{dt}) f_{sw} = 0.8\times80\times100\,\text{ns}\times48\,\text{kHz} = 0.31\ \text{W}$$
