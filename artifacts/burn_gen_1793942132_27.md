## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Risks

Three points shape everything that follows.

1. **The H743 does not generate the 48 kHz PWM.** In any practical 4-in-1 ESC, each motor channel (or the whole ESC) has a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32-class firmware. That MCU generates the 48 kHz bridge PWM. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071. The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. The loss model (Sections 1–3) belongs to the ESC side; the DMA model (Section 5) belongs to the FC side.

2. **80 A per motor on a nano board (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) can only be a short burst.** The math in Section 3 shows roughly 85–90 W of dissipation at 4×80 A on 6S. Board-level thermal mass gives about 3–5 seconds before the junctions reach their limits. It must be specified, firmware-limited, and tested as a burst rating, not a continuous one.

3. **All component parameters are representative placeholders.** They are assumed values for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, 3.3×3.3 mm-class MOSFET and a 3-phase gate driver. Substitute datasheet values before sign-off. The equations are structured so that this is a parameter swap.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Paths in 6-Step Synchronous PWM

Within one 60° sector, current enters through phase A and returns through phase B.

- **Phase A leg (PWM leg):**
  - The high-side FET conducts I for a fraction D of each PWM period.
  - The low-side FET conducts I for the remaining (1−D), as the synchronous freewheel path.
- **Phase B low side:** fully on for the whole sector, carrying I continuously.
- **Phase C:** floating (it is the back-EMF sensing phase).

At every instant, therefore, exactly two R_ds(on) elements are in series with the motor current. This holds regardless of D, with dead-time intervals handled separately in Section 2.

### 1.2 Per-Channel Conduction Loss

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{ds(on)}(T_j) + I_{ph}^2 R_{Cu}$$

The temperature dependence of R_ds(on) is:

$$R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\left[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)\right]$$

At T_j = 125 °C, R_ds(on) = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 100) = **1.5 mΩ**.

$$P_{cond,FET} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{19.2\ W\ per\ channel}$$

**Copper loss.** Phase, bus, and ground copper add series resistance. Take ~0.3 mΩ effective per channel, from 2 oz inner layers and short pours:

$$P_{Cu} = 80^2 \times 0.3\times10^{-3} = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

### 1.3 Per-FET Distribution (Hotspot Analysis)

Over one electrical revolution, each high-side FET is the PWM high side for 1/3 of the time. Each low-side FET is the freewheel path for 1/3 and the return path for 1/3.

$$P_{HS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3} D\, I^2 R,\qquad P_{LS,avg} = \tfrac{1}{3}(1-D)I^2R + \tfrac{1}{3}I^2R$$

| Throttle | P_HS (per FET) | P_LS (per FET) | Hotspot |
|---|---|---|---|
| D = 1.0 (full burst) | 3.2 W | 3.2 W | Even split |
| D = 0.5 | 1.6 W | 4.8 W | **Low side** |

At partial throttle with high current (heavy prop, low RPM, punch-out start), the low-side FETs run hottest. Layout must give the low-side FETs equal or better copper than the high-side FETs.
