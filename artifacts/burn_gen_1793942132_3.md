## 0. Feasibility Findings

Before the math, three architecture-level issues:

1. **80 A per channel on a nano form factor is a burst rating that lasts seconds, not tens of seconds.** Section 3 shows a 20×20 mm stack reaches its thermal limit in about 2–5 s at 4×80 A. Treat 80 A as a transient spec, enforced by firmware current and temperature limiting.
2. **The total battery current is 4 × 80 A = 320 A.** This exceeds XT60/XT90 ratings and any practical nano-board copper. Most likely 80 A is a per-motor *peak* that is never simultaneous. Specify a total-current limit, e.g. 120–160 A continuous.
3. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In a 4-in-1 ESC, each channel runs its own ESC MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to those MCUs. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by the ESC MCUs, not the H7.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model

### 2.1 Conduction loss

In 6-step commutation, two phase legs carry the full current at any instant. With synchronous rectification, each conducting leg routes current through either its high-side (HS) or low-side (LS) FET, so one R_DS(on) is in path per leg:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2\,I^2 R_{hot}$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 2 \times 80^2 \times 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Per leg.** Each leg conducts 240° of every 360° electrical cycle (120° sourcing, 120° sinking):

$$P_{cond,leg} = \tfrac{2}{3} I^2 R_{hot} = 6.4\ \text{W}$$

**Split between HS and LS** at duty D, while the leg is the PWM'd "high" phase:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot} D, \quad P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}(1-D) + \tfrac{1}{3} I^2 R_{hot}$$

At full-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.95), the loss divides roughly evenly at about 3.1–3.3 W per FET averaged over the electrical cycle.

**Self-heating correction.** Because R_DS(on) rises with temperature, solve iteratively with

$$R(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}.$$

Thermal runaway occurs if

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \cdot R_{\theta,ja} \ge 1.$$

### 2.2 Switching loss at 48 kHz (one hard-switched leg per ESC)

**Overlap (V–I crossover):**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus}\, I\, (t_r + t_f)\, f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

Here t_r and t_f are approximately Q_sw / I_g.

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus}\, Q_{rr}\, f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\,\text{nC} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\,\text{nF} \times 635 \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.023\ \text{W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\,\text{ns} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.61\ \text{W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs, dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors):

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} = 2 \times 40\,\text{nC} \times 10\,\text{V} \times 48\,\text{kHz} = 0.04\ \text{W}$$

**Total:**

$$P_{sw,ESC} \approx \mathbf{2.18\ W}$$

**Design notes on 48 kHz:**
- Overlap and dead-time losses scale linearly with f_sw. Dead time is the second-largest switching term, so trim it to the minimum safe value; at 50 ns, P_dt halves.
- 48 kHz is a reasonable choice for low-inductance quad motors: ripple current stays acceptable without excessive switching loss.

### 2.3 PCB copper and shunt loss

**Copper resistance:**

$$R_{Cu} = \frac{\rho\, L}{w\, t}$$

For 2 oz (70 µm) copper, w = 5 mm, L = 15 mm, ρ = 1.72×10⁻⁸ Ω·m: R ≈ 0.74 mΩ per layer. Parallel four power layers to get ≈ 0.18 mΩ per path.

$$P_{Cu,ESC} \approx 2 \times 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega \approx 3.2\ \text{W}$$

This is non-trivial; use heavy copper and via stitching.

**Shunt (0.25 mΩ, if a per-ESC shunt is fitted):**

$$P_{shunt} = 80^2 \times 0.25\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.6\ \text{W}$$

### 2.4 Loss summary at 80 A burst

| Term | Per ESC | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.2 W | 8.7 W |
| Copper | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |
| Shunt | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 26 W** | **≈ 105 W** |

**Efficiency:** the electrical input is 4 × 25.2 V × 80 A ≈ 8.06 kW, so stage efficiency is about 98.7%. However, about 105 W is dumped into a board that weighs a few grams.
