## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three points shape the rest of the analysis.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot runs on the STM32H743 and emits DShot600. Each motor needs a dedicated ESC MCU running AM32 or BLHeli_32. This design assumes one AT32F421 or STM32G071 per motor, each driving a 3-phase gate driver plus 6 MOSFETs. The 48 kHz figure is the **ESC MCU's PWM frequency**. It is unrelated to the DShot rate.

2. **An 80 A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is thermally marginal.** Section 2 shows that the burst is achievable only for about 1 s, and the continuous rating is set by convection rather than by the silicon. A 30.5×30.5 mm layout is recommended if 80 A is a firm requirement.

3. **"80 A" is defined here as the per-motor phase current.** The corresponding battery current is lower and depends on duty cycle.

## 2. Mathematical Model of Losses

### 2.1 Conduction Topology

The ESC uses 6-step (trapezoidal) drive with synchronous rectification. At any instant, two phases carry current:

- One high-side FET is PWM-switching.
- One low-side FET is on continuously.
- During PWM off-time, the complementary FET in the switching leg freewheels the current.

As a result, the current always flows through **two FET channels in series**, regardless of duty cycle D:

$$
P_{cond} = I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{ds(on)}(T_j)\cdot\big[D + (1-D)\big]_{\text{sw leg}} + I_{rms}^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)_{\text{static leg}} = 2\,I_{rms}^2 R_{ds(on)}(T_j)
$$

The on-resistance rises with temperature. A linearized form is:

$$
R_{ds(on)}(T_j) = R_{ds(on),25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\mathrm{K}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C, this gives R_ds(on) = 1.2 mΩ.

**Current ripple correction.** The RMS current includes the PWM ripple:

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I^2}{12}, \qquad \Delta I = \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

Example: L_ph = 20 µH (typical 2207-class motor) and D = 0.5 give ΔI ≈ 6.6 A, so ΔI²/12 ≈ 3.6 A². Compared with I² = 6400 A², this is negligible.

**Result at 80 A:**

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.2\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{15.36\ W}
$$

$$
P_{cu} = 80^2 \cdot 0.5\,\mathrm{m\Omega} = \mathbf{3.2\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-switching leg incurs switching losses.

| Term | Expression | Value at 80 A |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V_bus·I·(t_r + t_f)·f_sw | ½·25.2·80·40 ns·48 k = **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V_bus·f_sw | 50 nC·25.2·48 k = **0.06 W** |
| Output capacitance | ½·Q_oss·V_bus·f_sw | ≈ **0.04 W** |
| Dead-time diode conduction | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw | 0.8·80·200 ns·48 k = **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive | 2·Q_g·V_gs·f_sw (lost in the driver) | **0.06 W** |
| **Total P_sw** | | **≈ 2.71 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$
\frac{\partial P_{sw}}{\partial f_{sw}} \approx 56\ \mu\text{W/Hz}
$$

- Dropping to 24 kHz saves about 1.35 W per motor.
- The cost is higher ripple and acoustic noise.

### 2.3 Per-Motor and Board Totals

$$
P_{motor} = P_{cond} + P_{sw} + P_{cu} \approx 15.36 + 2.71 + 3.2 = \mathbf{21.3\ W}
$$

$$
P_{board}(4\times 80\,\text{A}) \approx \mathbf{85\ W}
$$

Adding the gate drivers, the 4 ESC MCUs, and the H743 (~0.5 W) gives about 86 W.

**Hottest single FET.** The static low-side FET carries the full current for its 120° commutation interval:

$$
P_{FET,max} \approx I^2 R_{ds(on)} + P_{sw}\ (\text{if it's the switching leg}) \approx 7.7 + 2.7 \approx 10.4\ \text{W (worst instantaneous)}
$$

Averaged over a full electrical revolution, 6-step rotation spreads the load:

$$
P_{FET,avg} \approx \tfrac{1}{6}\,(2\cdot 7.68 + 2.71)\cdot 2 \approx 6\ \text{W per FET}
$$
