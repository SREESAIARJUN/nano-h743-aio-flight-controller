# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC: Electrothermal and Timing Design Package (Job #6)

## 0. Architectural Corrections and Assumptions

Three issues affect the scope of this design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** Even in an AIO design, each motor bridge is driven by a dedicated ESC MCU (assumed 4× AT32F421 running AM32). The 48 kHz PWM is generated there. The H743 only generates bi-directional DShot600 and decodes eRPM telemetry.
2. **"Nano" and 80 A per channel conflict physically.** Four channels at 80 A draw 320 A from 6S, which is about 8 kW. That cannot fit on a 20×20 mm board. This package assumes a 30.5×30.5 mm board, 8-layer, with 2 oz copper on the outer and power layers. **80 A is treated as a burst rating of seconds, not a continuous rating.**
3. **Component values are representative placeholders.** The figures below are not from a qualified BOM. Replace every parameter marked ⚠ with datasheet values before release.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Topology

With trapezoidal 6-step commutation and synchronous (complementary) PWM, two phases conduct at any instant. Current flows through one high-side and one low-side path. On the PWM leg, the current alternates between the HS FET (duty D) and the sync-rectifying LS FET (1−D). Some device therefore always carries the current, so the duty cycle cancels out of the totals:

$$P_{cond,ch} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.2 Temperature dependence (solved iteratively)

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At a design point of T_j = 100 °C:

- R_DS = 1.2 mΩ × 1.30 = **1.56 mΩ**
- Loss per conducting device: 80² × 1.56 mΩ = **9.98 W**
- **P_cond,ch ≈ 20.0 W**

At T_j = 125 °C, R_DS = 1.68 mΩ, which gives **21.5 W per channel**. This positive feedback is why the burst duration is bounded (Section 4).

### 1.3 Copper and shunt losses

| Loss source | Calculation | Per channel |
|---|---|---|
| Phase/bus copper, 2 oz, ~0.25 mΩ effective path | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Current shunt (if per-channel), 0.1 mΩ ⚠ | 80² × 0.1 mΩ | 0.64 W |

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

The switching loss components per channel (one hard-switched leg) are:

$$P_{sw} = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{2}V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}}_{V\text{-}I\ overlap} + \underbrace{Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}}_{recovery} + \underbrace{2V_f I t_{dt} f_{sw}}_{dead\text{-}time} + \underbrace{\tfrac12 C_{oss}V_{bus}^2 f_{sw}}_{C_{oss}} + \underbrace{2Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw}}_{gate\ (driver)}$$

| Term | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | 0.5 × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 k | **1.94 W** |
| Q_rr | 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 k | 0.06 W |
| Dead-time body diode | 2 × 0.8 × 80 × 50 ns × 48 k | 0.31 W |
| C_oss (≈2 nF ⚠) | 0.5 × 2 nF × 25.2² × 48 k | 0.03 W |
| Gate drive (dissipated in driver/R_g) | 2 × 50 nC × 10 × 48 k | 0.05 W |
| **Total P_sw,ch** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:** dP_sw/df ≈ 50 µW/Hz. Going from 24 kHz to 48 kHz therefore costs about 1.2 W per channel. That is small compared with conduction loss, so 48 kHz is justified for smoother current and quieter motors.

## 3. Loss Budget

| Loss | Per channel | 4 channels |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction (T_j = 100 °C) | 20.0 W | 80.0 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.6 W |
| Copper + shunt | 2.2 W | 9.0 W |
| **Total** | **24.6 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

Efficiency at burst is P_out ≈ 4 × 80 × 25.2 × D_eff. Losses amount to roughly 1.5% at full throttle, which is efficient but still very concentrated heat.

### 3.1 Input capacitor ripple (critical at 80 A)

$$I_{C,rms} = I\sqrt{D(1-D)}\ \xrightarrow{D=0.5}\ 40\ \text{A}_{rms}\ \text{per channel}$$

- No single electrolytic capacitor can handle this.
- **Specify per channel:** 6× 10 µF/50 V X7R 1210 MLCC placed within 3 mm of the bridge, plus a shared 1000 µF/35 V low-ESR external bulk capacitor on the pigtail.
- MLCC DC-bias derating at 25 V is about −60%. ⚠ Verify the remaining effective capacitance.
