# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Reference Design Model

The numbers below are worked with the representative component values in §0. Substitute your actual MOSFET datasheet values before using any result for design sign-off. The equations are general. The specific numbers are only as good as the inputs.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Assumptions

**Partitioning.** The STM32H743 is the flight controller. It runs ArduPilot and generates bi-directional DShot600. Motor commutation runs on four dedicated ESC MCUs, typically AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. The H743 does not commutate motors directly. Running FOC or 6-step for 4 motors at 48 kHz alongside ArduPilot's scheduler is not supported. Even on an AIO board, the FC-to-ESC link is still DShot.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

The model assumes 6-step trapezoidal drive with complementary (synchronous) PWM. At any instant, current flows through exactly two FET channels:
- **PWM ON:** high-side of phase A, then the motor, then low-side of phase B.
- **PWM OFF:** low-side of phase A (synchronous freewheel), then the motor, then low-side of phase B.

Per ESC channel:

$$P_{cond,ESC} = I^2\,(2R_{DS,hot} + R_{sh} + R_{cu})$$

$$R_{DS,hot} = R_{25}\,k_T = 1.0 \times 1.5 = 1.5\ \text{m}\Omega$$

$$P_{cond,ESC} = 80^2 \times (3.0 + 0.25 + 0.3)\ \text{m}\Omega = 6400 \times 3.55\ \text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{22.7\ W}$$

The terms break down as follows:
- **MOSFETs:** 19.2 W
- **Shunt:** 1.6 W
- **Copper:** 1.9 W

### 1.2 Per-FET distribution (duty-dependent)

For duty cycle D, each phase is active for 1/3 of the electrical cycle:

$$P_{HS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,D\,I^2 R_{DS,hot}$$

$$P_{LS} = \tfrac{1}{3}\,(1 + (1-D))\,I^2 R_{DS,hot} = \tfrac{1}{3}(2-D)\,I^2R_{DS,hot}$$

At full-throttle burst (D ≈ 0.95), with I²R = 9.6 W:
- P_HS ≈ 3.04 W
- P_LS ≈ 3.36 W

The low-side FETs are the thermal limit at partial throttle. At D = 0.5, P_LS = 4.8 W while P_HS = 1.6 W.

### 1.3 Temperature feedback

R_DS rises with temperature, so solve iteratively:

$$T_j = T_a + P(T_j)\,R_{\theta,ja},\quad R_{DS}(T)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T-25)],\ \alpha \approx 0.005/\text{K}$$

Thermal runaway is avoided only if:

$$\alpha\,P_{cond}\,R_{\theta,ja} < 1$$

## 2. Switching Loss Model @ 48 kHz

Only one half-bridge switches per PWM cycle. That is the PWM-ed phase.

| Loss term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V–I overlap | ½·V·I·(t_r+t_f)·f_sw = 0.5·25.2·80·35 ns·48k | **1.69 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr·V·f_sw = 40n·25.2·48k | 0.05 W |
| C_oss | ½·C_oss,er·V²·f_sw ×2 FETs | 0.05 W |
| Dead-time body diode | V_f·I·2t_dt·f_sw = 0.8·80·200 ns·48k | **0.61 W** |
| Gate drive (in driver, not FET) | Q_g·V_GS·f_sw ×2 | 0.06 W |
| **Total switching per ESC** | | **≈ 2.46 W** |

**Frequency sensitivity:**

$$\partial P_{sw}/\partial f_{sw} \approx 51\ \mu\text{J per cycle}$$

- **24 kHz:** saves about 1.2 W per ESC.
- **96 kHz:** costs about 2.5 W more per ESC.

At 80 A, conduction dominates (≈90%). This makes 48 kHz a reasonable choice for ripple and current control.

## 3. Total Dissipation and Thermal Analysis

### 3.1 Totals at 80 A burst

| | Per ESC | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | 22.7 W | 90.8 W |
| Switching | 2.4 W | 9.8 W |
| ESC MCU, driver, LDO | ~0.3 W | 1.2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 25.4 W** | **≈ 102 W** |

Efficiency at 80 A × 25.2 V × 4 = 8.06 kW input works out to ≈ 98.7%. That figure looks good, but 100 W in a nano footprint is the real problem.

### 3.2 Junction thermal network (Foster model)

The thermal path runs from the junction through the case, PCB, and spreading resistance to the ambient airflow:

```
Tj ─[Rθjc 1.0 K/W | τ≈5 ms]─ Tc ─[Rθcb 2 K/W]─ Tboard ─[Rθba (airflow)]─ Ta
```

$$Z_{th}(t)=\sum_i R_i\,(1-e^{-t/\tau_i})$$

**Per-FET junction rise above board temperature, worst low-side, 3.4 W + switching share ≈ 4.2 W:**

$$\Delta T_{j-b} = 4.2 \times (1.0 + 2.0) \approx \mathbf{12.6\ K}$$

This is fine. **The board is the bottleneck.**

### 3.3 Board-level adiabatic burst model

Assume a 30.5 × 30.5 mm 8-layer board with 2 oz internal copper.

**Heat capacity:**
- Copper ≈ 4.5 g × 0.385 J/g·K
- FR4 ≈ 3 g × 1.1 J/g·K
- FETs, caps, and solder ≈ 2 g × 0.7 J/g·K
- **Total: C_th ≈ 5.0 J/K**

**Board-to-air resistance** in prop wash (~10 m/s, both sides):

$$h \approx 80\ \text{W/m}^2\text{K} \rightarrow R_{\theta,ba} \approx \frac{1}{80 \times 2 \times 0.00093} \approx 6.7\ \text{K/W}$$

The time constant is τ = R·C ≈ 34 s.

$$T_{board}(t) = T_a + P\,R_{\theta,ba}\,(1-e^{-t/\tau})$$

| Burst duration (all 4 at 80 A, T_a=35 °C) | T_board | T_j (worst FET) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | 35 + 102·6.7·(1−e^{−1/34}) ≈ **55 °C** | ~68 °C |
| 3 s | ≈ **93 °C** | ~106 °C |
| 5 s | ≈ **128 °C** | ~141 °C |
| Steady state | ~720 °C (impossible) | — |

### 3.4 Conclusions

1. **80 A per motor simultaneously is a ~2–3 s burst rating** on a 30×30 board with these FETs. Specify it as such, for example "80 A / 2 s, 45 A continuous."
2. **Sustainable continuous current at T_board ≤ 100 °C:** solve 4·I²·R_tot,eff·R_θ,ba = 65 K. This gives I ≈ 40–45 A per channel, which matches real-world 30×30 4-in-1 ratings.
3. **The H743 must be thermally isolated from the ESC stack.
