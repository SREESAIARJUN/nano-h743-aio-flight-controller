# STM32H743 + 4-in-1 ESC "Nano" Stack: Architecture, Loss Model, and DShot Allocation

**Document scope:** This is a reference design derivation, not a released design. Part choices are representative. Every number below must be re-checked against the datasheets of the parts you actually procure, then validated on the bench with a thermocouple or IR camera.

---

## 0. Architecture Clarification and Feasibility Flags

Two points need to be settled before any math.

**Flag 1: The STM32H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.**
In every production 4-in-1 stack, the H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot. Each ESC channel has its own small MCU (for example an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz complementary PWM.

The design therefore splits into three domains:

| Domain | Owner | Key parameters |
|---|---|---|
| FC (H743 @ 480 MHz) | ArduPilot | DShot600 bidirectional, DMA, eRPM telemetry |
| ESC MCU ×4 | AM32 | 48 kHz PWM, dead time, commutation |
| Power stage ×4 | Hardware | 6 MOSFETs per channel, gate driver, shunt, bulk caps |

**Flag 2: 80 A on a 20×20 mm board is physically marginal.**
- **Area:** 24 MOSFETs in 5×6 mm packages need about 720 mm² of footprint. A 20×20 board has 400 mm² per side, before the gate drivers and MCUs.
- **Consequence:** a true 20×20 board forces 3.3×3.3 mm DFN parts. These have roughly 2–3× higher R_DS(on).
- **Model choice:** the calculations below use DFN3333 parts on a 20×20 board. The 30.5×30.5 alternative is shown for comparison.
- **What "burst" means here:** it is governed by the board's thermal mass, not by steady-state dissipation. Section 3 derives the allowable burst duration.

**Assumed operating point**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Battery | 6S LiPo, V_bus = 25.2 V (full charge, worst case for switching loss) |
| Burst current | I = 80 A phase current per channel, at ~100% duty |
| Switching frequency | f_sw = 48 kHz |
| Control | 6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification ("complementary PWM", AM32 default) |
| Dead time | t_dt = 100 ns per edge |
| Ambient | T_a = 40 °C |

**Representative MOSFET (40 V, DFN3333 class):**

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| R_DS(on) at 25 °C, V_GS = 10 V | 2.0 mΩ |
| Temperature coefficient | ×1.5 at T_j = 125 °C, so R_hot = 3.0 mΩ |
| Q_g | 35 nC |
| Q_rr | 40 nC |
| C_oss | 1000 pF |
| V_SD (body diode) | 0.8 V |
| t_r + t_f with gate driver | 40 ns |
| R_θJC | 1.5 K/W |

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Current paths

In 6-step drive, two phase legs carry current at any instant: one sourcing, one sinking.

- **With synchronous rectification**, the PWM'd leg alternates between the high-side (HS) FET and the low-side (LS) FET. The complementary leg stays on.
- **Effect:** at every instant, exactly **two MOSFET channels** carry the phase current.

The conduction loss per ESC channel is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \, I_{ph}^2 \, R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

This expression is essentially **independent of duty cycle** (dead-time diode intervals are excluded and treated in Section 2.4).

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ \text{K}^{-1}
$$

At T_j = 125 °C this gives R = 2.0 × 1.5 = **3.0 mΩ**.

### 1.3 PCB copper loss

This term is often forgotten. Model the phase and bus path as about 0.5 mΩ per current path, using 2 oz outer layers plus 2 oz inner layers, paralleled and stitched.

$$
P_{Cu} = 2 \, I^2 \, R_{trace} = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 0.5\,\text{m}\Omega = 6.4\ \text{W}
$$

### 1.4 Results at 80 A

| Term | Expression | Per channel | ×4 channels |
|---|---|---|---|
| FET conduction (125 °C) | 2·80²·3.0 mΩ | **38.4 W** | 153.6 W |
| FET conduction (25 °C, start of burst) | 2·80²·2.0 mΩ | 25.6 W | 102.4 W |
| PCB copper | 2·80²·0.5 mΩ | 6.4 W | 25.6 W |
| Shunt (0.5 mΩ, single DC-link shunt) | 80²·0.5 mΩ | 3.2 W | 12.8 W |

**Per-FET average.** Each FET conducts for about one third of the electrical cycle at high eRPM. The PWM'd FET pair shares its leg's conduction, so the average per FET is about 38.4 / 6 ≈ **6.4 W**.

- **Peak instantaneous per-FET loss:** 19.2 W
- **Junction rise over case:** ΔT_jc = 6.4 × 1.5 ≈ 10 K. This is not limiting.
- **The real limit is board temperature** (Section 3).

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

In 6-step drive only one leg is actively PWM'd at a time. Each period has one hard turn-on and one hard turn-off of the HS FET (or the LS FET, depending on the PWM scheme).

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$
P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} \, I \, (t_r + t_f) \, f_{sw}
= 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\times10^{-9} \cdot 48\times10^{3}
= \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

**Gate-driver-limited rise and fall times.** The rise and fall times are set by the driver's ability to move the Miller charge:

$$
t_{r,f} \approx \frac{Q_{gd} \, R_g}{V_{drv} - V_{plateau}}
$$

With Q_gd = 8 nC, R_g = 4.7 Ω (driver plus external), and 10 − 4.5 = 5.5 V of overdrive, each edge takes about 7 ns ideally. Real layouts with ringing land around 15–20 ns per edge. The model uses 40 ns total, which is conservative.

### 2.2 Output capacitance (C_oss) loss

The C_oss energy is dissipated at each hard turn-on:

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss,eq} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} \times 2
= 0.5 \cdot 1\text{n} \cdot 635 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2
= 0.061\ \text{W}
$$

### 2.3 Reverse recovery loss

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} \, V_{bus} \, f_{sw} = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.048\ \text{W}
$$

The model also adds about 0.1 W of extra turn-on loss in the opposing FET, caused by I_rr overlap.

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_{SD} \, I \, (2 t_{dt}) \, f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 200\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
