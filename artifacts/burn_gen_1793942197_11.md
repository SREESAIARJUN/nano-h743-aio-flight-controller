> ⚠️ **Feasibility flag.** 80 A per motor × 4 motors on a nano-class board is a **burst-only** rating. The steady-state analysis in §3 shows this board cannot sustain it. Do not publish "80A continuous."

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation topology

Assume six-step trapezoidal commutation with complementary (synchronous) PWM, as used by AM32 and BLHeli_32-style firmware. In any 60° sector, current flows from phase A's half-bridge to phase B's low-side, and phase C floats.

| Device | Conduction during the sector |
|---|---|
| A-high | On for duty D |
| A-low | On for (1 − D), synchronous rectification |
| B-low | On for 100% of the sector |

### 1.2 Instantaneous bridge conduction loss

$$
P_{cond,bridge} = I_{rms}^2 R_{ds}(T_j)\,[D + (1-D) + 1] = 2\,I_{rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)
$$

This loss is **independent of duty cycle**. This is a key result: full-throttle and part-throttle burst currents heat the bridge equally.

### 1.3 Ripple-corrected RMS current

$$
I_{rms}^2 = I^2 + \frac{\Delta I_{pp}^2}{12}, \qquad
\Delta I_{pp} \approx \frac{V_{bus} D(1-D)}{L_{ph} f_{sw}}
$$

Example: line-to-line inductance L = 20 µH, D = 0.5 gives ΔI_pp ≈ 6.6 A. The correction is 3.6 A², which is negligible compared with 6400 A². Low-inductance motors (< 5 µH) are a different case: the ripple becomes significant and argues for a higher f_sw.

### 1.4 Temperature dependence (positive feedback)

$$
R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)]
$$

At T_j = 110 °C: R_ds = 1.0 × (1 + 0.005 × 85) = **1.425 mΩ**.

### 1.5 Worked numbers (80 A, T_j = 110 °C)

| Quantity | Expression | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Bridge conduction loss | 2 × 80² × 1.425 mΩ | **18.24 W per motor** |
| Per-FET peak (while conducting) | 80² × 1.425 mΩ | 9.12 W |
| Per-FET average over electrical cycle | 18.24 / 6 | **3.04 W** |
| PCB copper loss (est. 0.3 mΩ phase + bus path) | 80² × 0.3 mΩ | ~1.9 W per motor |

The per-FET average assumes a symmetric six-step: each FET conducts for 1/3 of the cycle, split between high-side, synchronous, and full-sector
