# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC (Job #51): Power, Thermal and DShot Design Package

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before the Math

Three architectural issues change the math, so they come first.

| Issue | Correction |
|---|---|
| **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot runs on the FC. The 48 kHz complementary PWM is generated by the ESC MCU, typically AM32 or BLHeli_32 on an AT32F421 or STM32G071, one per motor or one quad MCU. | The design has two compute domains: the FC (H743, ArduPilot) and the ESC (4× AT32F421, AM32). They are linked by 4× bidirectional DShot600. |
| **"Nano" (20×20 mm) at 80A burst cannot run steady-state.** Section 3 shows ΔT ≈ 1000 K at steady state with 4×80A on a 20×20 board. | Rate it as 80A for ≤1–2 s per motor (single-motor event). For sustained high current, move to 30.5×30.5 mm. |
| **"80A" must be defined.** | Here it means **phase current I_ph = 80A**, the worst case. Battery current is I_dc ≈ D·I_ph. |

**Baseline assumptions** (treat as representative and replace with datasheet values):

- 6S pack, V_bus = 25.2 V maximum.
- 40 V N-MOSFET, PDFN 5×6:
  - R_ds(on),25 = 1.0 mΩ, temperature coefficient α = 0.0055 /K, giving ×1.5 at 115 °C.
  - t_r + t_f = 40 ns at the chosen R_g.
  - Q_rr = 50 nC, C_oss(er) = 1.5 nF, Q_g = 50 nC at 10 V.
  - V_f(body diode) = 0.8 V.
- Dead time t_dt = 100 ns. Ripple ratio r = ΔI/I_ph = 0.2.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (6-step trapezoidal, synchronous rectification)

In any commutation sector, current flows through one high-side FET of phase X and one low-side FET of phase Y.

- During PWM-on, the path is HS_X → winding → LS_Y.
- During PWM-off with synchronous freewheel, the path is LS_X → winding → LS_Y.

Either way, **two FETs always carry I_ph in series**.

**Temperature-dependent resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**RMS current including ripple:**

$$I_{rms}^2 = I_{ph}^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)$$

**Conduction loss per motor:**

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\,I_{ph}^2\left(1+\frac{r^2}{12}\right)R_{ds}(T_j)$$

Numbers (I_ph = 80 A, T_j = 115 °C, R_ds = 1.5 mΩ):

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2(6400)(1.0033)(0.0015) = \mathbf{19.3\ W}$$

**Per-FET loss.** Each FET conducts during 2 of 6 sectors, with HS/LS duty sharing. Averaged over an electrical cycle, P_FET ≈ 19.3/6 ≈ **3.2 W**.

The instantaneous loss while conducting is **9.6 W**. This instantaneous value matters for Z_th(transient) once the electrical period exceeds about 10 ms, i.e., at low eRPM under load such as a punch-out from hover.

**Shunt loss** (0.25 mΩ low-side or DC-link shunt):

$$P = I^2 R = 6400 \times 0.00025 = 1.6\ \text{W/motor}$$

**Copper loss.** This is the hidden killer. Take a 2 oz (70 µm), 5 mm wide, 10 mm long segment:

$$R = \frac{\rho L}{wt} = \frac{1.72\times10^{-8}\cdot0.01}{0.005\cdot70\times10^{-6}} = 0.49\ \text{m}\Omega \;\rightarrow\; 3.1\ \text{W at 80 A}$$

Mitigation:
- Use a 4–6 layer stack with 2 oz outer and 2 oz inner layers.
- Run parallel power planes.
- Stitch with ≥0.3 mm vias at ≥30 per pad.
- Keep FET-to-pad paths under 5 mm.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at f_sw = 48 kHz

Only one leg is hard-switched at a time: the PWM'd phase.

**V–I overlap loss:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph}(t_r+t_f)f_{sw} = 0.5(25.2)(80)(40\text{n})(48\text{k}) = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (LS body diode recovers at HS turn-on):

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw} = 50\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$
