# STM32H743 AIO Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #55): 80 A Burst Thermal, 48 kHz Switching, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Design

This design rests on several engineering assumptions, and the conclusions depend on them:

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** In every production AIO, ArduPilot runs on the H743, and each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU running AM32 or BLHeli (e.g., AT32F421 or STM32G071). The H743 talks to the ESC MCUs over bi-directional DShot600. This architecture is assumed below.
2. **80 A per channel on a nano (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm) board is a burst rating, not continuous.** The math below shows it is thermally sustainable for about 1–2 s. Realistic continuous rating is about 25 A per channel.
3. **The MOSFET parameters are representative values** for a 40 V, ~1 mΩ, DFN3.3/5×6-class part. Replace them with your chosen part's datasheet values. All equations are parametric for that reason.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Topology insight

Assume 6-step trapezoidal drive with synchronous complementary PWM. At any instant, two phases carry the current I:

- **PWM'd phase:** the high-side (HS) conducts for a fraction D of the period. The low-side (LS) synchronous rectifier conducts for (1−D).
- **Return phase:** the LS is on for 100% of the interval.

The total resistive conduction loss is therefore:

$$
P_{cond} = I^2 R_{ds}\,[D + (1-D)] + I^2 R_{ds} = 2\,I^2 R_{ds,hot}
$$

This result is **independent of duty cycle** (dead-time is ignored here and handled in §3.3). Rip
