# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #16): Design Model

## 0. Architecture Assumptions and Critical Flags

### Assumed architecture

The STM32H743 is the **flight controller** MCU running ArduPilot. Each of the four ESC channels has its own small commutation MCU (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32). The H743 cannot practically run four sensorless FOC/6-step loops while also running ArduPilot, and no ArduPilot ESC firmware exists for that. If you meant the H743 to commutate directly, that is a different and much harder project. Tell me and I'll re-scope.

### Flag 1: "80A burst" on a nano-format board is a thermal-transient rating, not a capability

Section 3 shows ~32 W of dissipation per channel at 80 A. With all four channels loaded, that is about 128 W on roughly 4 J/K of board heat capacity. The board heats at **~30 K/s**. The rating is only defensible as a **time-limited burst (≤1–2 s)** with a stated starting temperature and duty cycle. Four channels at 80 A also means a **320 A battery draw**, which no nano-class XT30/XT60 pigtail, pad, or 6S pack chemistry realistically supports. Size the burst spec per-channel and non-simultaneous, or derate.

### Flag 2: Netlists and part values are illustrative

They are engineering starting points, not verified production data. Every part number, pin assignment, and DMA mapping must be checked against current datasheets, the H743 reference manual (RM0433), and the ArduPilot version you build.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, phase current at any instant flows through **one high-side-equivalent path and one low-side path**. During the PWM ON-time, the active HS FET and the opposite-phase LS FET conduct. During OFF-time, the complementary LS FET (synchronous rectifier) and the opposite LS FET conduct. In both states, **two FET channels** carry the full current.

$$
P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I_{rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS(on)}(T_j)
$$

With trapezoidal current near flat-top at burst, I_rms ≈ I_burst = 80 A.

### 2.2 Temperature-dependent resistance

$$
R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{25}\,[1 + \alpha_R (T_j - 25)]
$$

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond (2 FETs) | Per FET |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.50 mΩ | 19.2 W | 9.6 W |
| 75 °C | 1.88 mΩ | 24.0 W | 12.0 W |
| 125 °C | 2.25 mΩ | 28.8 W | 14.4 W |

### 2.3 Electrothermal feedback

Conduction loss and junction temperature depend on each other:

$$
T_j = T_{ref} + R_{th}\cdot P(T_j), \qquad P(T_j) = I^2 R_{25}[1+\alpha_R(T_j-25)]
$$

Solving for T_j:

$$
T_j = \frac{T_{ref} + R_{th} I^2 R_{25}(1 - 25\alpha_R)}{1 - R_{th} I^2 R_{25}\alpha_R}
$$

**Stability criterion (thermal runaway):** R_th · I² · R₂₅ · α_R < 1.

Per FET at 80 A: I²·R₂₅·α_R = 6400 × 1.5e-3 × 0.005 = 0.048 W/K. Runaway therefore occurs if the **total junction-to-ambient** R_th exceeds ~21 K/W. A nano board in free air without prop-wash typically has R_th,JA of 15–40 K/W per FET. **Continuous 80 A is thermally unstable without airflow.** This is the mathematical proof that 80 A must be a burst rating.

### 2.4 Copper and interconnect conduction loss

Phase and bus copper resistance is estimated as:

$$
R_{cu} = \frac{\rho_{cu}(T)\, L}{w\, t\, n_{layers}}, \qquad \rho_{cu}=1.72\times10^{-8}[1+0.0039(T-20)]
$$

Example: a 10 mm phase path, 4 mm wide, 2 oz (70 µm), 4 paralleled layers gives R ≈ 0.15 mΩ, so P = 6400 × 0.15e-3 ≈ **1.0 W per phase path**. The total board copper loss per channel is about **1.5–2 W** including the bus. Do not ignore it; it heats the same board mass.

## 3. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

### 3.1 Hard-switching transition loss (active HS FET)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw}
= 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 40\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ kHz} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}
$$

The more accurate gate-charge form is:

$$
t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}+Q_{gs2}}{I_{G}}, \quad I_G = \frac{V_{drv}-V_{plateau}}{R_{G,total}}
$$

Use this to set R_G. The trade-off is EMI and ringing (dV/dt) against loss.

### 3.2 Output capacitance loss

$$
P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} C_{oss} V_{bus}^2 f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 1.5\text{ nF} \times 635 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.023\ W
$$

### 3.3 Reverse recovery loss (LS body diode, hard turn-on of HS)

$$
P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{ nC} \times 25.2 \times 48\text{ k} = 0.060\ W
$$

Q_rr rises with di/dt and temperature. Budget 2× margin, so **0.12 W**.

### 3.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{ ns} \times 48\text{ k} = \mathbf{0.61\ W}
$$
