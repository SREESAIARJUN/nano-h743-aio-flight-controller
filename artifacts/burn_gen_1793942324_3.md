### ⚠️ Architect's findings to resolve before layout

1. **Form factor.** A "nano" (20×20 mm) board cannot carry 80 A burst per channel.
   - Twenty-four 5×6 FETs alone occupy about 720 mm² against 400 mm² of board.
   - Section 2 shows roughly 110 W of burst dissipation, which a 20×20 stack cannot handle.
   - **Recommendation:** 30.5×30.5 mm ESC at minimum. Keep the H743 FC at 20×20 or 30×30 as a stacked board.
2. **Two-processor split.** Switching losses at 48 kHz belong to the ESC's own MCUs (e.g. AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32), not the H743.
   - The H743 runs ArduPilot and only generates and receives bidirectional DShot600.
   - The model below treats these as two separate domains.
3. **What "80 A" means.** Here 80 A is per-channel motor phase current.
   - Battery current per channel is I_bat = D·I_ph, about 72 A at D = 0.9.
   - Four channels at full burst would draw about 288 A from the pack. That is a pack, connector (XT60 is rated ~60 A continuous) and wiring problem regardless of the ESC.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model (per channel)

### 1.1 Temperature-dependent on-resistance

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 125 °C: R = 1.2 mΩ × 1.5 = **1.8 mΩ** (use 1.9 mΩ with margin for PCB spreading and solder).

### 1.2 Current path in 6-step synchronous PWM

During each 60° sector (example: phase A high, phase B low, phase C floating), the current path is:

| Device | Conduction fraction of the sector | Current |
|---|---|---|
| Q_AH (high side, switching) | D | I_ph |
| Q_AL (sync rectifier) | 1 − D | I_ph |
| Q_BL (low side, static) | 1 | I_ph |

The sum of conduction fractions is always 2. Two FETs are therefore always in series:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)$$
