# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #12): Architecture, Loss Model, DShot DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Architectural Ground Rules

Three design constraints shape everything below.

1. **The H743 does not commutate motors.** ArduPilot is a flight-control stack and contains no BLDC commutation firmware. The architecture is therefore:
   - an STM32H743 running ArduPilot, which outputs bidirectional DShot600, feeding
   - four dedicated ESC MCUs (AT32F421 class, running AM32), each driving one 3-phase bridge through a gate driver.

   The "4-in-1" is the power stage plus four ESC MCUs on a shared board.
2. **80A is per-motor phase current for a burst of a few seconds, not continuous.** On a nano form factor (20×20 or 25.5×25.5 mm), 4×80A continuous is not thermally achievable. Section 3 quantifies the burst time budget.
3. **The netlists are reference-level, not fab-ready.** Exact MOSFET and driver parameters must be pulled from the selected parts' datasheets and the model re-run. Every assumed value is flagged with **[A]**.

## 2. Loss Model (per motor, per 3-phase bridge)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step commutation, current at any instant flows through exactly one high-side path and one low-side path:

- In the PWM leg, the high-side FET conducts for duty D. With synchronous rectification, the complementary low-side FET carries the freewheeling current for (1−D).
- In the return leg, the low-side FET is held on for the whole 60° sector.

The current path therefore always contains two R_DS(on) in series, regardless of D (neglecting dead time):

$$P_{cond} = 2\, I_{ph}^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = k_T R_{25}$$

$$P_{cond} = 2 \times 80^2 \times (1.5 \times 1.0\,\text{m}\Omega) = 2 \times 6400 \times 0.0015 = \mathbf{19.2\ W}$$

**Distribution across FETs:**
- Over one electrical revolution, each of the 6 FETs averages 19.2/6 = **3.2 W**.
- During a single sector, the active pair can see up to 9.6 W each.
- At 48 kHz with eRPM well below f_sw, sector duration (≈ hundreds of µs to ms) is comparable to die thermal time constants. Use the **peak 9.6 W per FET** for junction-to-case transient checks, and the 3.2 W average for board-level heating.

**Copper and shunt add-ons:**
- Phase trace and pad resistance: assume ~0.3 mΩ round-trip, giving 80² × 0.3 mΩ = **1.9 W**.
- Current-sense shunt (if per-ESC, on the battery return): 0.25 mΩ at average battery current. At D = 0.8, I_bat ≈ 64 A, giving **1.0 W**.

### 2.2 Switching Loss at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. Each PWM period has one turn-on and one turn-off hard transition (high-side, while the low side does ZVS-ish synchronous commutation).

**Overlap (V·I) loss.** Miller-plateau transition time:

$$t_{sw} \approx \frac{Q_{gd}}{I_g} = \frac{10\,\text{nC}}{0.7\,\text{A}} \approx 14\,\text{ns} \;\;\Rightarrow\;\; t_r + t_f \approx 30\,\text{ns (with current-rise time)}$$

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \times 25.2 \times 80 \times 30\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.45\ W}$$

**Dead-time body-diode conduction** (two dead-time intervals per period):

$$P_{dt} = V_f I_{ph} \cdot 2 t_{dt} \cdot f_{sw} = 0.8 \times 80 \times 200\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.61\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery:**

$$P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2 \times 50\text{e-}9 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.06\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \times 2 = 25\text{e-}9 \times 25.2 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.03\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (two FETs switching per period; this power is dissipated mostly in the driver and R_g):

$$P_{gate} = 2\, Q_g V_{dr} f_{sw} = 2 \times 50\text{e-}9 \times 10 \times 48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

$$P_{sw,total} \approx 1.45 + 0.61 + 0.06 + 0.03 + 0.05 = \mathbf{2.2\ W}$$

**Sensitivity:** P_VI scales linearly with f_sw and with t_sw.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves ~1 W per motor.
- Doubling R_g roughly doubles P_VI but reduces ringing and EMI. Tune with a scope on the switch node.

### 2.3 Per-Motor and Board Totals at 80A Burst

| Loss term | Per motor | 4 motors |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Switching (all terms) | 2.2 W | 8.8 W |
| Copper + shunt | 2.9 W | 11.6 W |
| Bulk capacitor ESR (see 2.4) | ~0.5 W | ~2 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 24.8 W** | **≈ 99 W** |

Inverter efficiency at this point, assuming D ≈ 0.8: P_out ≈ 25.2 × 0.8 × 80 ≈ 1.6 kW per motor, so η ≈ 98.5%. Efficiency is good; the problem is power density, not efficiency.
