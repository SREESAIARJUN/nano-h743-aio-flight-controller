## 1. Conduction Loss Model (80 A Burst)

### 1.1 Current-path analysis for 6-step complementary PWM

In each 60° commutation sector, one phase leg is PWM-active (high side at duty D, its low side complementary at 1−D). A second leg has its low side held fully on. The third leg floats.

At every instant, the motor current therefore flows through exactly **two** conducting channels.

Over one electrical period, each leg spends 1/3 of the time as the PWM leg, 1/3 as the static-low leg, and 1/3 floating. The per-device RMS-squared current factors are:

$$
I_{HS,rms}^2 = \frac{D}{3}I^2,\qquad
I_{LS,rms}^2 = \frac{(1-D)}{3}I^2 + \frac{1}{3}I^2 = \frac{2-D}{3}I^2
$$

$$
P_{cond,leg} = I^2 R_{DS}(T_j)\left[\frac{D}{3}+\frac{2-D}{3}\right] = \frac{2}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j)
$$

$$
\boxed{P_{cond,motor} = 3\cdot\frac{2}{3}I^2R_{DS}(T_j) = 2\,I^2\,R_{DS}(T_j)}
$$

This result is independent of D. That is the benefit of synchronous rectification.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_DS(on)

$$
R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{25}\left[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)\right]
$$

At the design-limit junction T_j = 110 °C:

$$
R_{DS} = 1.2\,\text{m}\Omega \times (1 + 0.0045\times85) = 1.66\ \text{m}\Omega
$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A

$$
P_{cond,motor} = 2\times 80^2 \times 1.66\times10^{-3} = \mathbf{21.2\ W}
$$

**Hot-spot FET:** at full-throttle burst (D → 1), the static-low FET carries the worst per-device load:

$$
P_{LS,max}=\frac{2-D}{3}I^2R \xrightarrow{D=1} \frac{1}{3}(6400)(1.66\text{m}) = \mathbf{3.54\ W}
$$
