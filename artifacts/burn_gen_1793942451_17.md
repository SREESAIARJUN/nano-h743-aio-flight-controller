# STM32H743 Nano FC + 80A 4-in-1 ESC: Electro-Thermal Model and DShot Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture and Feasibility

Two points shape everything below.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot sends DShot from the H743 (the FC) to four ESC MCUs (for example AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32). These drive the 3-phase gate drivers. ArduPilot has no in-tree FOC or 6-step path that drives power-stage gates from the FC MCU. The model below assumes this split.

**2. An 80A burst on a "nano" (20×20 mm) board is thermally capacity-limited, not resistance-limited.** The math shows a few seconds of full-board burst at most. Treat 80A as a ≤1–3 s per-channel rating, not continuous.

## 1. Conduction Losses (80A Burst)

### 1.1 Current path

With 6-step trapezoidal drive and complementary (synchronous) PWM, current always flows through exactly two MOSFETs in series:

- **ON-time:** HS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)
- **OFF-time:** LS(phase A) → motor → LS(phase B)

The per-channel conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$P_{cond,ch} = 2\,I_{ph,rms}^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

With a flat-top trapezoidal current, I_rms ≈ I_pk during the conduction interval.

### 1.2 Temperature-dependent R_ds(on)

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{ds25}\,[1+\alpha\,(T_j-25)]$$

At T_j = 110 °C:

$$R_{ds}=1.0\,\text{m}\Omega\,(1+0.005\cdot85)=1.425\text{ m}\Omega$$

### 1.3 Numbers

$$P_{cond,ch}=2\cdot80^2\cdot1.425\times10^{-3}=18.24\text{ W}$$

### 1.4 Per-FET distribution

Each FET conducts for 120° of the electrical cycle. During that window, the HS conducts D·T_sw and the LS conducts the rest. Averaged over commutation, the 6 FETs share the load:

$$\bar P_{FET}\approx \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\cdot\frac{6}{3}\cdot\frac12 = \frac{P_{cond,ch}}{6}\approx 3.04\text{ W (average)}$$

During its active 120° window, a FET can see up to I²R = 9.1 W. The die thermal time constant is about 1 ms, and the electrical period at high RPM is about 0.3–1 ms, so the peaks partially average out. Design to roughly 1.5× the average, about 4.6 W per FET.

### 1.5 Parasitic copper and shunt losses (often forgotten)

| Element | R | Loss @ 80A |
|---|---|---|
| Per-phase shunt 0.5 mΩ | 0.5 mΩ | **3.2 W** |
| Phase trace and pad, 2 oz × 4 layers | ~0.3 mΩ | 1.9 W |
| Bus plane share | ~0.2 mΩ | 1.3 W |

**Recommendation:** use a single bus-return shunt of 0.25 mΩ, or a Hall sensor on the bus. Avoid per-phase shunts.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Its low-side partner commutates softly through the body diode.

### 2.1 V–I overlap loss

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I_{pk}(t_r+t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=0.968\text{ W}$$

The overlap times follow from the gate drive:

$$t_r \approx \frac{Q_{gs2}+Q_{gd}}{(V_{drv}-V_{pl})/(R_{drv,src}+R_g)}$$

With V_drv = 10 V, V_pl = 3 V, and R_src + R_g ≈ 7 Ω: t_r ≈ 12 nC / 1 A ≈ 12 ns.

### 2.2 Reverse recovery loss

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=30\text{n}\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{k}=0.036\text{ W}$$

### 2.3 Output capacitance loss

$$P_{oss}\approx\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=0.024\text{ W}$$

### 2.4 Dead-time body-diode conduction

$$P_{dt}=V_{SD}\,I_{pk}\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=0.307\text{ W}$$

### 2.5 Gate drive loss

This loss is mostly dissipated in the driver and R_g:

$$P_g=2\,Q_gV_{drv}f_{sw}=2\cdot40\text{n}\cdot10\cdot48\text{k}=0.038\text{ W}$$

### 2.6 Switching loss total

$$P_{sw,ch}\approx 1.37\text{ W}$$
