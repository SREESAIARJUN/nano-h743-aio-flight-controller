# STM32H743 Nano AIO with 4-in-1 ESC (Job #9): Power-Stage, Thermal and Bidirectional DShot600 Architecture

## 0. Scope, Assumptions and Feasibility Findings

Every number below comes from a **parametric reference MOSFET**. Before release, replace these values with the datasheet values of the part you actually select.

### Architecture assumption

The H743 does not commutate the motors directly. ArduPilot has no on-FC BLDC commutation, so the H743 cannot drive the bridges itself. The architecture is therefore:

- **FC:** STM32H743VIT6 running ArduPilot. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four ESC MCUs.
- **ESC MCUs:** 4 × STM32G071 or AT32F421 running AM32. Each has its own 3-phase gate driver and 6 MOSFETs.

### Findings flagged up front

1. **80 A is a burst rating only, about 1–2 s.** A 20×20 or 25×25 mm board cannot reject about 80 W continuously. Section 3 quantifies this.
2. **Four motors at 80 A at full throttle would draw about 320 A from the battery.** XT60 connectors, 12 AWG leads and the pack are not rated for this. Specify the burst rating as "per motor, non-simultaneous", or as a total battery-current limit enforced by AM32 current limiting plus the ArduPilot `BATT_*` limits.
3. **Nano form factor plus 80 A means low-side heat is concentrated under one FET per position.** If R_th validation fails, the only real fixes are to parallel FETs (larger board) or to derate.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

### 1.1 Commutation-state current paths

In 6-step drive with synchronous rectification, during each 60° sector:

- **Leg A (PWM leg):**
  - The high-side FET (HS_A) conducts for D·T_sw.
  - The low-side FET (LS_A) conducts for (1−D)·T_sw, minus dead time.
- **Leg B (return leg):** LS_B conducts 100 % of the time.
- **Leg C:** floating (back-EMF sensing).

At every instant the current I_ph flows through **exactly two R_DS(on)** values: either HS_A + LS_B, or LS_A + LS_B.

### 1.2 Equations

Temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{DS}(T_j) = R_{DS,25}\left[1 + \alpha (T_j - 25)\right],\quad \alpha \approx 0.005\ /\text{K}$$

Per-motor conduction loss:

$$P_{cond,motor} = 2\, I_{ph,rms}^2\, R_{DS}(T_j)\;\;(\text{independent of }D\text{ with sync rect})$$

Per-FET average loss over one electrical cycle:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{P_{cond,motor}}{6}$$

The actual FET loss is non-uniform. The return-leg low-side FET in a given sector carries the full current. The rotation-averaged value is P/6 per FET.

A peak sector-local loss also matters, because it drives the junction ripple at low eRPM:

$$P_{FET,peak} = I_{ph}^2 R_{DS}(T_j)$$

### 1.3 Numbers at 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C (R_DS = 1.5 mΩ)

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| P_cond,motor | 2 × 6400 × 1.5 m = **19.2 W** |
| P_cond,FET (average) | **3.2 W** |
| P_FET,peak (sector) | **9.6 W** |
| Four motors simultaneously | **76.8 W** |

Ripple correction: I_rms² = I_avg² + ΔI²/12. With a typical ΔI of about 10 A p-p (low-L racing motor at 48 kHz), the correction is under 0.2 %, so it is neglected.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg switches hard. The high-side FET sees the hard turn-on and turn-off. The low-side FET switches at zero voltage (ZVS) after its body diode has conducted, so its switching loss is negligible.
