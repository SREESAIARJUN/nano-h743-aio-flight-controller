## 0. Architectural Corrections Up Front

Three points in the brief need correcting before the math.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production AIO design, the H743 runs ArduPilot as the flight controller. Each motor channel is driven by a small dedicated ESC MCU, which handles BEMF zero-cross detection and 48 kHz complementary PWM.
   - This design uses 4× AT32F421K8U7 (or STM32G071) running AM32. A single shared ESC MCU is also possible.
   - Bidirectional DShot600 is the link between the H743 and these ESC MCUs.
2. **"Nano" and "80 A burst" conflict thermally.** The four DFN3.3 FET sets needed for 80 A do not fit with adequate copper on a 20×20 mm board. This design assumes a **30.5×30.5 mm mount, 36×36 mm board**. The math in §3 shows that even this board is burst-limited to about 2–3 s at 80 A per motor.
3. **System current.** Four motors at 80 A each is 320 A of phase current. Battery current is lower at partial throttle, but the battery lead, XT60/XT90 connector, and bus capacitors must be sized for it (see §2.5).

## 2. Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction Losses

With 6-step trapezoidal commutation, two phase legs conduct at any instant. With synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly one FET in each of those two legs, whether the high side or the complementary low side is on.

Each FET carries current for 1/3 of the electrical cycle, so:

$$I_{FET,rms} = \frac{I_{ph}}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{80}{\sqrt3} = 46.2\ \text{A}$$

$$P_{cond,FET} = I_{FET,rms}^2 \cdot R_{DS,hot} = 46.2^2 \times 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = 5.12\ \text{W}$$

$$P_{cond,motor} = 6 \cdot P_{cond,FET} = 2 I_{ph}^2 R_{DS,hot} = 30.7\ \text{W}$$

**Copper and shunt losses**

| Element | Calculation | Loss |
|---|---|---|
| Copper path (≈0.25 mΩ round trip, 2 oz inner pours) | 80² × 0.25 mΩ | 1.6 W |
| Shunt (0.2 mΩ) | 80² × 0.2 mΩ | 1.28 W |

$$P_{cond,total} \approx 33.6\ \text{W per motor}$$
