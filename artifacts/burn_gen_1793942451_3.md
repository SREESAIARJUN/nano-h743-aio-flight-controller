# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #35): Thermal, Switching and Bi-directional DShot Model

## 0. Scope Corrections and Stated Assumptions

Three architectural facts need to be clear before the derivations:

1. **The H743 does not switch the MOSFETs.** In a 4-in-1 stack, each motor channel (or a single quad-capable MCU) runs ESC firmware such as AM32 (e.g., on AT32F421), BLHeli_32 or Bluejay. That firmware drives the gate drivers at 48 kHz. The H743 runs ArduPilot, sends DShot600, and receives eRPM telemetry. The loss model below therefore applies to the ESC power stage. The DMA model applies to the H743.
2. **80 A per motor on a "nano" (20×20 / 25.5×25.5 mm) board is far beyond what the copper and thermal mass support for more than about 1–2 s.** The math below shows this quantitatively. If the spec means 80 A *total* (4×20 A), every loss figure scales by (20/80)² ≈ 1/16 for conduction.
3. **All component values are assumptions until parts are selected.** Each equation is parameterized so real datasheet values can be substituted.

## 1. Conduction Loss Model

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, current always flows through exactly **two conducting MOSFETs**:

- one phase-leg FET, which alternates between high side (fraction D of the period) and synchronous low side (fraction 1−D), and
- the static low-side FET of the return phase.

**Per-motor conduction loss:**

$$P_{cond} = I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot \left[ D \cdot R_{HS}(T_j) + (1-D) \cdot R_{LS}(T_j) + R_{LS}(T_j) \right] + I_{ph,rms}^2 \cdot R_{Cu}$$

**Temperature-dependent on-resistance:**

$$R_{ds}(T_j) = R_{ds25}\left[1 + \alpha_T (T_j - 25)\right], \quad \alpha_T \approx 0.0059\ \text{K}^{-1}\ (\Rightarrow 1.5\times \text{ at } 110\,°\text{C})$$

With R_HS = R_LS = 1.5 mΩ, the D-terms collapse:

$$P_{cond} = 2 \cdot I^2 \cdot R_{ds}(T_j) = 2 \cdot 80^2 \cdot 1.5\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{19.2\ W/motor}$$

**Copper loss.** Assume a phase plus return path of about 0.3 mΩ (4 oz equivalent, about 15 mm path at nano scale):

$$80^2 \times 0.3\,\text{m}\Omega = 1.9\ \text{W}$$

**Battery bus and shunt.** The shared bus carries 4 × 80 × D. At D = 0.9 that is about 288 A peak DC, which is not physically realistic on XT30/XT60 leads or nano-board pads (see §6).

**Thermal runaway check.** R_ds rises with T_j, so losses must converge:

$$P_{cond}(T_j) \cdot R_{\theta} \text{ must satisfy } \frac{dP}{dT_j} \cdot R_\theta < 1$$

$$\frac{dP}{dT} = 2 I^2 R_{ds25}\,\alpha_T = 2 \cdot 6400 \cdot 0.001 \cdot 0.0059 = 0.076\ \text{W/K}$$

The product 0.076 × R_θ is less than 1 for R_θ < 13 K/W, so the stage is stable against electrothermal runaway. The real limit is thermal capacity (§3), not runaway.

## 2. Switching Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM-ing leg switches: one high-side/low-side pair per motor.

**(a) V–I overlap, hard-switched high side:**

$$P_{VI} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 20\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.97\ \text{W}$$

**(b) Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss} = \tfrac{1}{2} Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 40\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.05\ \text{W}$$

**(c) Reverse recovery** of the low-side body diode, absorbed by the high side:

$$P_{rr} = Q_{rr} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 50\text{n} \cdot 25.2 \cdot 48\text{k} = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

**(d) Dead-time body-diode conduction**, two edges per period:

$$P_{dt} = V_F \cdot I \cdot 2 t_{dt} f_{sw} = 0.8 \cdot 80 \cdot 400\text{n} \cdot 48\text{k} = 1.23\ \text{W}$$

**(e) Gate drive**, dissipated in the driver and R_g rather than the FET:

$$P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot 2 = 60\text{n} \cdot 10 \cdot 48\text{k} \cdot 2 = 0.06\ \text{W}$$

### Per-Motor Burst Loss Summary

| Term | W |
|---|---|
| Conduction (FETs) | 19.2 |
| Copper / traces | 1.9 |
| V–I overlap | 0.97 |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 |
| Q_oss + Q_rr | 0.11 |
| Gate drive | 0.06 |
| **Total per motor** | **≈ 23.5 W** |
| **4 motors** | **≈ 94 W** |

**Design insights:**

- Conduction dominates (about 82%). Switching losses at 48 kHz are secondary.
- Dropping to 24 kHz saves only about 1.1 W per motor.
- Halving R_ds(on) by paralleling FETs or using a 5×6 package saves about 9.6 W per motor. **Spend board area on R_ds(on), not on lowering f_sw.**
- Reduce dead time to 100 ns if the driver and FET allow it. This recovers about 0.6 W per motor.
