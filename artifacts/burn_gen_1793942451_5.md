# STM32H743 AIO "Nano" FC + 4-in-1 ESC: Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, BDShot Timer/DMA Plan and Netlists

## 0. Feasibility Findings

These two points change the design, so they come first.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every production 4-in-1 design, the FETs are driven by a dedicated ESC MCU per motor or per pair, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32. That MCU produces the 48 kHz PWM, dead-time and back-EMF sensing. The H743 runs ArduPilot and sends bidirectional DShot600 to those MCUs. This document is partitioned that way. Commutating four motors directly from an H743 under ArduPilot is not supported firmware.

2. **80 A on a 20×20 mm nano board cannot be sustained.** As shown in §3, it is survivable only as a short burst of about 1–2 s, and only if the controller enforces it. Continuous ratings on this form factor are roughly 1.5–6 W of total dissipation. I quantify this below rather than hide it. If 80 A must be sustained, the board needs to be 30.5×30.5 mm or larger with paralleled FETs, or the "80 A" spec must be reworded as a burst rating.

All component values below are **representative assumptions** (marked ⚙). Replace them with your selected FET's datasheet values. The equations stay the same.

## 2. Electrical Loss Model (per motor channel)

### 2.1 Conduction losses

In 6-step commutation, each phase is high for 120° and low for 120° per electrical cycle. With complementary PWM at duty D:

- **High-side FET** conducts for a fraction D/3 of the time.
- **Low-side FET of the same leg** freewheels for (1−D)/3 and is fully on for 1/3.

RMS current in each FET:

$$I_{rms,HS}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{D}{3}},\qquad I_{rms,LS}=I\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{3}+\tfrac{1-D}{3}}$$

Summed over the bridge, the conduction loss is effectively two FETs in series carrying I at all times:

$$P_{cond,bridge}=2\,I^2\,R_{ds}(T_j)$$

with the temperature-dependent on-resistance:

$$R_{ds}(T_j)=R_{25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)]$$

**Worked numbers (D → 1, 80 A burst, T_j = 125 °C):**

- R_ds = 0.9 × 1.45 = **1.31 mΩ**
- I_rms per FET = 80/√3 = **46.2 A**
- P_cond per FET = 46.2² × 1.31 m = **2.79 W**
- P_cond per motor (6 FETs) = 2 × 80² × 1.31 m = **16.8 W**

**Electro-thermal stability.** Because R_ds rises with temperature, conduction loss feeds back on itself. Requiring the loop gain to stay below 1 gives:

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}R_{\theta}<1 \;\Rightarrow\; 2I^2R_{25}\,\alpha\,R_\theta<1$$

At 80 A this becomes R_θ < 1/(2·6400·0.9e-3·0.0045) ≈ **19 K/W** of effective bridge-to-ambient resistance. The nano board's steady-state R_θ is about 12.5 K/W for the whole board (§3), so the system is stable but close to the limit. Expect rapid temperature creep.

### 2.2 Switching losses (only the PWM'd high-side FET switches at f_sw)

**Hard-switching V·I overlap:**

$$P_{VI}=\tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r+t_f) f_{sw}=\tfrac12(25.2)(80)(20\text{ns})(48\text{k})=\mathbf{0.968\ W}$$

**Output capacitance:**

$$P_{oss}\approx\tfrac12 Q_{oss}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.018\ W}$$

**Reverse recovery** (the low-side body diode recovers on every high-side turn-on):

$$P_{rr}=Q_{rr}V_{bus}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.048\ W}$$

**Dead-time diode conduction** (two edges per period):

$$P_{dt}=V_f\,I\,(2t_{dt})\,f_{sw}=0.8\cdot80\cdot100\text{ns}\cdot48\text{k}=\mathbf{0.307\ W}$$

**Gate drive** (dissipated mostly in the driver and gate resistors). Switching FETs dominate:

$$P_g=Q_gV_{GS}f_{sw}=\mathbf{0.024\ W}$$

per switching FET, or about 0.05 W per motor.

**Switching total per motor ≈ 0.968 + 0.018 + 0.048 + 0.307 + 0.05 ≈ 1.39 W.** This is the worst case at D ≈ 0.5–0.9 with 80 A flowing. At D = 1 the PWM switching terms vanish and only commutation edges remain.
