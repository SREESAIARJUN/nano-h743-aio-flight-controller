# STM32H743 Nano FC + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #37): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Allocation

## 0. Assumptions and Architecture

**Topology.** The H743 does not drive gates directly. Each motor channel uses a local ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), a 3-phase gate driver, and 6 N-MOSFETs. The H743 sends bi-directional DShot600 to the four ESC MCUs. This is the only architecture that meets 48 kHz PWM with commutation-level timing and ArduPilot compatibility.

**Interpretation of "80A burst".** I take this as 80 A per motor phase current for ≤ 2 s, which is the industry convention. Sections 2 and 3 show this is the binding constraint on a nano (20×20 mm) form factor.

**Reference parameters.** Replace these with your BOM datasheet values. All equations are parametric.

| Symbol | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|

## 1. Conduction Losses

With synchronous rectification in 6-step drive, current at any instant flows through exactly one FET in the high-side leg path and one FET in the low-side return path. The high side conducts for duty D and its complementary FET for (1−D), so the loss is **duty-independent**:

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2\,k_T\,R_{ds,on} + I_{ph}^2 R_{cu}$$

At 80 A:

- FET conduction: 80² × 2 × 1.5 mΩ = **19.2 W**
- Copper: 80² × 0.5 mΩ = **3.2 W**
- **Total: 22.4 W per motor**

**Per-FET worst case:** the conducting pair splits ~9.6 W, which concentrates in one device for 1/3 of the electrical cycle.

For FOC/sinusoidal drive, replace I_ph with I_rms and use 3 legs: P = 3 · I_rms² · k_T · R_ds,on. The result is similar at equal torque.

## 2. Switching Losses at 48 kHz

Only the PWM'd leg hard-switches. Each term below is per motor.

| Term | Equation | Value |
|---|---|---|
| V-I overlap | ½ · V · I · (t_r + t_f) · f = ½ × 25.2 × 80 × 40 ns × 48 kHz | **1.94 W** |
| Reverse recovery | Q_rr · V · f = 50 nC × 25.2 × 48 kHz | **0.06 W** |
| C_oss (2 FETs) | 2 × ½ · C_oss · V² · f | **0.05 W** |
| Dead-time diode | V_SD · I · 2 · t_dead · f = 0.8 × 80 × 100 ns × 48 kHz | **0.31 W** |
| Gate drive (2 FETs) | 2 · Q_g · V_gs · f (dissipated in driver/R_g) | **0.05 W** |
| **Total switching** | | **≈ 2.4 W** |

**Total at burst:**

- Per motor: P_motor(80 A) ≈ 22.4 + 2.4 = **24.8 W**
- Board: P_board = 4 × 24.8 + P_H743 (~0.5 W) + LDO/BEC losses (~1 W) ≈ **101 W**

**Continuous scaling.** Conduction scales with I², switching with I:

$$P(I) = 3.5\text{ mΩ}\cdot I^2 + 0.030\,I \quad \text{[W/motor]}$$

At 35 A this gives **5.3 W/motor**, or about 21 W per board.

**Lever for 48 kHz vs 24 kHz.** Halving f_sw saves ~1.2 W/motor at 80 A. That is only 5% of burst loss. Conduction dominates, so FET R_ds,on and copper weight are the real levers.

## 3. Thermal Model

### 3.1 Two-node RC network

```
P_FET → [Z_th,jc(t) ≈ 1.2 K/W] → T_board (C_b) → [R_ba] → T_amb
```

**Board heat capacity (20×20 mm, 8-layer, 2 oz Cu, 1.6 mm):**

- Copper: 0.224 cm³ × 8.96 g/cm³ × 0.385 J/g·K ≈ 0.77 J/K
- FR4: ~1.2 g × 1.1 J/g·K ≈ 1.3 J/K
- FETs, caps, and solder: ~0.25 J/K
- **C_b ≈ 2.3 J/K**

**Board-to-ambient resistance:**

- Still air: R_ba ≈ 25 K/W
- In prop wash: R_ba ≈ 10 K/W

This gives τ = R_ba · C_b ≈ 23 s in airflow.

### 3.2 Burst case (adiabatic, since t ≪ τ)

$$\frac{dT_b}{dt} = \frac{101}{2.3} \approx 44\ \text{K/s}$$

$$T_j = T_{b0} + 44\,t + P_{FET}\cdot Z_{th,jc}(t)$$

With T_b0 = 60 °C (hover-soaked) and the worst FET at ~11.5 W:

| Burst duration | T_board | T_j |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 s | 104 °C | ≈ 118 °C |
| 1.5 s | 126 °C | ≈ 140 °C |

**Burst limit ≈ 1.2 s from a 60 °C soak** if T_j,max = 125 °C is design-limited, which is consistent with the k_T = 1.5 assumption. The ESC firmware must enforce an I²t limit with a ~1 s time constant, plus an NTC fold-back at 110 °C.

### 3.3 Continuous rating

With airflow, an allowable board rise of 70 K (40 → 110 °C) gives:

$$P_{max} = \frac{70}{10} = 7\text{ W board} \;\Rightarrow\; 1.75\text{ W/motor} \;\Rightarrow\; I_{cont} \approx 20\text{ A per motor}$$

**Engineering verdict.** On a true 20×20 nano, "80A burst" is achievable only as a ~1 s rating, and the honest continuous rating is **~20 A**. Options to reach 35–45 A continuous:

- Move to 30.5×30.5 mm.
- Use 3 oz outer copper with via-farm thermal pads.
- Use dual-side-cooled FETs.
- Bond an aluminium heat spreader to the FET backs. Each of these roughly halves R_ba.
