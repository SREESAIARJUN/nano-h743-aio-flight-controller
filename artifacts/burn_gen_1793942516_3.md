# STM32H743 AIO Flight Controller with 4-in-1 80A ESC (Job #62): Power-Stage Loss Model, Thermal Analysis, and Bi-Directional DShot600 Timer/DMA Allocation

## 0. Architecture Clarifications and Design Risks

Three points need to be settled before any equations, because they change the model.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In an ArduPilot AIO design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot and emits DShot600. Each ESC channel has its own commutation MCU (typically an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32/BLHeli_32), or a single MCU per 4-in-1. The 48 kHz PWM is generated by those ESC MCUs, not the H743. The netlist below reflects this split.

2. **80A per motor on a "nano" footprint is a burst-only rating.** It is not a continuous one. Section 2.5 shows the board heats at roughly 15–20 K/s adiabatically at full 4×80A. The rating must be tied to a defined burst duration, e.g. "80A / 2 s, 55A continuous with airflow". Please confirm the intended burst profile.

3. **All values below are engineering estimates** based on stated assumptions (MOSFET part class, copper weight, board size). They must be validated by:
   - double-pulse testing,
   - IR thermography under a load-bench burst profile, and
   - checking ArduPilot's `dma_resolver.py` output for the final hwdef.

## 1. Conduction-Loss Model

### 1.1 Current Path

In 6-step commutation with synchronous rectification, the motor current always flows through exactly two MOSFET channels:

- the PWM leg, i.e. the high-side FET for D, then the complementary low-side FET for 1 − D, and
- the fixed return leg's low-side FET.

The instantaneous conduction loss is therefore independent of duty cycle:

$$
P_{cond,ESC} = 2\, I^2\, R_{DS(on)}(T_j), \qquad R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on),25}\,[1+\alpha (T_j-25)]
$$

with α ≈ 0.005 /K, giving k ≈ 1.5 at 125 °C.

### 1.2 Numbers

| T_j | R_DS(on) | P_cond per ESC | Per FET (time-averaged, ÷6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 °C | 1.0 mΩ | 2·80²·1.0 m = **12.8 W** | 2.13 W |
| 125 °C | 1.5 mΩ | 2·80²·1.5 m = **19.2 W** | 3.20 W |

Note the positive feedback: R_DS(on) rises with T_j. The coupled electro-thermal fixed point is

$$
T_j = T_a + R_{th,ja}\cdot P(T_j)
$$

This diverges (thermal runaway) if

$$
R_{th,ja}\cdot \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j} \ge 1, \qquad \frac{\partial P}{\partial T_j}= 2I^2 R_{25}\,\alpha
$$

- At 80 A: ∂P/∂T ≈ 2·6400·1m·0.005 = 0.064 W/K per ESC.
- Runaway therefore occurs if the ESC-level R_th exceeds about **15.6 K/W**.
- A nano board in still air sits around 15–25 K/W.

**80A continuous is thermally unstable without airflow. This is why the rating must be burst-only.**

### 1.3 Copper / Interconnect Conduction

These losses are not negligible at 80 A.

**Trace/plane resistance** (2 oz Cu, 70 µm, ρ = 1.72e-8 Ω·m at 20 °C):

$$
R = \rho \frac{L}{w\,t}
$$

For a 10 mm × 6 mm phase pour: R ≈ 1.72e-8·0.01/(0.006·70e-6) = 0.41 mΩ per layer. Paralleling two layers gives about 0.2 mΩ.

**Path budget:** battery pad → bus → FET → phase pad → return ≈ 0.6 mΩ effective per motor current path.

$$
P_{Cu} \approx I^2 \cdot 0.6\text{ m}\Omega = 3.8\text{ W per ESC}
$$

**Shunt** (if per-phase or bus current sensing is used): 0.25 mΩ × 80² = 1.6 W per ESC. Many nano 4-in-1s instead use a single 0.25 mΩ bus shunt for the combined current, which gives (4·I)²·R; at 320 A that is 25.6 W. **Do not use a single combined shunt at this current level.** Use per-ESC shunts, or a lower-value shunt plus a gain-adjusted amplifier.

## 2. Switching-Loss Model at 48 kHz

Only the PWM leg hard-switches. One FET turns on hard, and its complementary partner commutates through the body diode during dead time. That gives one hard-switching pair per ESC at any time.

### 2.1 Voltage–Current Overlap (Hard Switching)

$$
P_{sw} = \tfrac12 V_{bus} I (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot25.2\cdot80\cdot20\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{0.97\ W}
$$

### 2.2 Body-Diode Reverse Recovery

$$
P_{rr} = V_{bus} Q_{rr} f_{sw} = 25.2\cdot50\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.060\text{ W}
$$

Q_rr increases with di/dt and T_j; use 2× margin, giving **0.12 W**. The recovery current also adds to turn-on overlap; this is included in the margin.

### 2.3 Output-Capacitance Loss

$$
P_{oss} \approx \tfrac12 Q_{oss} V_{bus} f_{sw} = 0.5\cdot80\text{e-}9\cdot25.2\cdot48\text{e}3 = 0.048\text{ W}
$$

### 2.4 Dead-Time Body-Diode Conduction

$$
P_{dt} = V_F\, I \cdot 2 t_{dt}\, f_{sw} = 0.8\cdot80\cdot400\text{e-}9\cdot48\text{e}3 = \mathbf{1.23\ W}
$$

This term exceeds the overlap loss. Reducing dead time from 200 ns to 100 ns, which requires driver propagation-delay matching, saves about 0.6 W per ESC.

### 2.5 Gate-Drive Loss

This loss is dissipated in the driver and R_g, not in the FET die:

$$
P_g = Q_g V_{GS} f_{sw} \cdot N_{switching} = 60\text{e-}9\cdot10\cdot48\text{e}3\cdot2 = 0.058\text{ W}
$$

Add driver quiescent current and the bootstrap regulator for about 0.1 W per ESC.

### 2.6 Loss Summary at 80A Burst, T_j = 125 °C

| Term | Per ESC | 4-in-1 total |
|---|---|---|
| FET conduction | 19.2 W | 76.8 W |
| Copper + interconnect | 3.8 W | 15.2 W |
| Shunt (per-ESC) | 1.6 W | 6.4 W |
| V·I overlap | 0.97 W | 3.9 W |
| Dead-time diode | 1.23 W | 4.9 W |
| Q_rr + Q_oss | 0.17 W | 0.7 W |
| Gate drive + driver | 0.10 W | 0.4 W |
| **Total** | **≈ 27.1 W** | **≈ 108 W
