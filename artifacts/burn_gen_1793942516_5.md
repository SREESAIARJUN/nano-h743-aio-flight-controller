# STM32H743 Nano Flight Controller + 4-in-1 ESC (Job #60): Power-Stage, Thermal and DShot Architecture

## 0. Architecture Corrections Before Any Math

Two premises in the brief need correcting first, because the rest of the design depends on them.

**1. The H743 does not commutate the motors.** In every ArduPilot-compatible 4-in-1 design, the STM32H743 runs ArduPilot on the flight controller. It sends bidirectional DShot600 to four (or one shared) ESC MCUs, such as an AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32. Those MCUs generate the 48 kHz PWM. The architecture below follows that split:

```
[6S Pack]──XT60──[Bulk C]──VBAT bus──┬── 4× (3 half-bridges + FD6288Q + AM32 MCU) ── motors
                                     │         ▲ DShot600 bidir (1 wire/motor)
                                     └─ BEC ── [STM32H743 FC: ArduPilot] ─ IMU, baro, OSD, UARTs
```

**2. "Nano" (20×20 mm) and 4×80 A burst are physically incompatible.** Section 3 proves this. I give the full model so you can evaluate it yourself. My architectural recommendation is either a 30.5×30.5 mm board, or a re-rating to about 4×45 A burst on 20×20 mm.

All component values below are representative engineering placeholders. Verify every MOSFET, driver and pin alternate function against the current datasheets before layout.

## 2. Loss Model (Per Motor, Then Per Board)

### 2.1 Conduction Loss

In 6-step complementary PWM, the current path always runs through one high-side-position FET and one low-side-position FET. The high-side switch and its synchronous partner share the duty: D on one, (1–D) on the other. The series path resistance is therefore always ≈ 2·R_ds, independent of D.

$$P_{cond,motor} = I_{ph}^2 \cdot 2R_{ds,hot} = 80^2 \cdot 2 \cdot 2.4\,\text{m}\Omega = \mathbf{30.7\ W}$$

Each switch position conducts for 120° of each 360° electrical cycle. The average loss per FET (positions are symmetric) is:

$$P_{cond,FET} = \frac{30.7}{6} \approx \mathbf{5.1\ W\ avg}, \quad \text{instantaneous peak} \approx 15.4\ \text{W}$$

At ~30 k eRPM the electrical period is ~2 ms. That is much shorter than the board's thermal time constant, so averaging is valid for the board. It is only marginal for the die (Z_th,jc rise ≈ 1–2 K ripple, which is acceptable).

### 2.2 Hard-Switching (V–I Overlap) Loss

One switch hard-switches per motor at any instant:

$$P_{sw} = \tfrac{1}{2} V_{bus} I_{ph} (t_r + t_f) f_{sw} = 0.5 \cdot 25.2 \cdot 80 \cdot 40\text{ns} \cdot 48\text{k} = \mathbf{1.94\ W}$$
