## 0. Architecture Clarifications

Three facts constrain this design.

1. **The H743 does not commutate the motors.** ArduPilot has no 6-step or FOC motor-drive firmware. Each of the four channels needs its own ESC MCU (AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32 or BLHeli_32). That MCU generates the 48 kHz PWM, and the H743 talks to it over bidirectional DShot600. "STM32H743 4-in-1" here means an AIO board: H743 flight controller plus a 4-in-1 ESC on one stack or PCB.

2. **"80 A burst" on a 20×20 mm board is a thermal-transient number, not an electrical rating.** Section 4 shows it is sustainable for roughly 0.3–1 s per channel, and only if all four channels are not loaded simultaneously from a warm start. The datasheet should state this explicitly, e.g. "80 A, 1 s, Tboard,start ≤ 50 °C."

3. **Parameter values are representative, not sourced.** MOSFET parameters below are typical 40 V PDFN3.3×3.3 values. Before release, replace them with the chosen part's datasheet values. All equations are parameterized for that substitution.

## 2. Conduction Loss Model

### 2.1 Current path

With 6-step commutation and complementary PWM, current flows through exactly two FET positions at every instant:

- During PWM on-time: high-side of phase A, then low-side of phase B.
- During PWM off-time: low-side of phase A (synchronous rectification), then low-side of phase B.

Ignoring dead time, the series path therefore always contains two R_ds(on) plus copper, independent of duty cycle:

$$R_{path}(T_j) = 2\,R_{ds,25}\,[1+\alpha(T_j-25)] + R_{cu}, \qquad \alpha \approx 0.006\ \text{K}^{-1}$$
