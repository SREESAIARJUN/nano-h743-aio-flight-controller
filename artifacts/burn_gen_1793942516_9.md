## 0. Feasibility Findings

**F1. Architecture correction: the H743 is the flight controller, not the ESC MCU.**
A 4-in-1 ESC needs a dedicated commutation controller per motor. Typical choices are the AT32F421 or STM32G071 running AM32, or the EFM8 running BLHeli_S. These handle zero-cross detection, PWM generation at 48 kHz, and the bi-directional DShot (BDShot) telemetry reply. The H743 runs ArduPilot and drives four DShot lines. All math below assumes this split.

**F2. "80 A" can only be a short burst rating on a nano (20×20 mm mount, roughly 30×30 mm board) layout.**
- The model below gives about 23 W dissipated per channel at 80 A, or about 93 W with all four channels loaded.
- With no heatsink and no airflow, the board reaches its limit in about 2–3 s with all four channels at 80 A.
- A defensible spec is roughly **40–45 A continuous with prop-wash airflow, 80 A for ≤1 s per channel**.
- Also, 4 × 80 A is 320 A from the battery. That exceeds what an XT60 and nano-scale input copper can carry. The battery-side burst budget must be capped (see §5.4).
